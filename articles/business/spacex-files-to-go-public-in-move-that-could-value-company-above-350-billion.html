SpaceX files to go public in move that could value company above $350 billion

5:00 PM ET

SpaceX has filed paperwork for an initial public offering that would make Elon Musk's rocket and satellite internet company one of the largest publicly traded corporations in the world. The company will trade under the ticker symbol SPCX.

The filing comes as SpaceX dominates the commercial space industry through two main businesses: launching rockets for NASA, private companies, and foreign governments, and operating Starlink, a satellite internet service now used by millions of subscribers globally. The company currently handles the majority of global orbital launches and holds contracts worth billions with the US government.

Analysts estimate the public listing could value SpaceX above $350 billion, potentially making it more valuable than most Fortune 500 companies. For Musk personally, who owns approximately 42 percent of SpaceX according to recent disclosures, a successful IPO at expected valuations could push his net worth past $1 trillion, making him the first individual to reach that threshold.

The decision to go public marks a significant shift for SpaceX, which Musk has long kept private to maintain control over long-term projects like Mars colonization that might not satisfy quarterly earnings pressures. The company last raised private funding at a $210 billion valuation in 2024.

Several factors appear to be driving the timing. Starlink has reached profitability and now generates steady revenue, providing the kind of predictable income stream public investors favor. The satellite internet service reported over 4 million subscribers as of early 2025 and continues expanding globally.

SpaceX also faces growing capital needs. The company is developing Starship, a fully reusable rocket system intended for Moon and Mars missions, which requires substantial ongoing investment. Going public would provide access to capital markets to fund these ambitious projects without diluting existing shareholders through private funding rounds.

The IPO also comes as competition in the space industry intensifies. Blue Origin, backed by Jeff Bezos, is ramping up operations, while Chinese companies are developing reusable rockets. A public listing would give SpaceX additional resources to maintain its market lead.

Investors will be watching how SpaceX structures the offering. Technology companies often use dual-class share structures that give founders voting control even after going public. Musk has used such arrangements at other companies to maintain decision-making authority.

The offering is expected to be one of the largest IPOs in history. Investment banks have not yet been formally announced, though SpaceX has previously worked with Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs on private fundraising.

Timing for the actual public debut remains unclear, as the regulatory review process typically takes several months.
