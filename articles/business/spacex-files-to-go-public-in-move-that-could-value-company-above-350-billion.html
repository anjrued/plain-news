SpaceX files to go public in move that could value company above $350 billion

6:00 PM ET

SpaceX has filed paperwork for an initial public offering that would allow the general public to buy shares in Elon Musk's rocket and satellite internet company for the first time. The company will trade under the ticker symbol SPCX.

The move comes as SpaceX dominates the commercial space industry. The company launches more rockets than any other organization globally, operates the Starlink satellite internet network serving millions of customers, and holds billions of dollars in contracts with NASA and the US military. SpaceX is currently valued at roughly $350 billion in private markets, making it one of the most valuable private companies in the world.

An IPO would transform that private valuation into publicly traded stock, allowing everyday investors to own a piece of the company. For Musk personally, who owns an estimated 42 percent stake in SpaceX, a successful public offering at current valuations could push his net worth above $1 trillion, according to financial analysts.

SpaceX has resisted going public for years. Musk has previously said he prefers private ownership because it allows the company to focus on long-term goals like Mars colonization without pressure from quarterly earnings reports. The decision to file now suggests either a need for capital to fund ambitious projects or pressure from early investors seeking liquidity.

The filing comes at a complex moment for Musk, who also runs Tesla, X (formerly Twitter), Neuralink, and The Boring Company. Tesla's stock has faced volatility, and X has struggled financially since Musk's acquisition. A SpaceX IPO would give Musk access to public market capital while diversifying his wealth beyond Tesla shares.

For the space industry, a SpaceX public offering represents a watershed moment. No pure-play space company has achieved this scale in the public markets. Previous space ventures that went public through mergers with special purpose acquisition companies largely disappointed investors. SpaceX's profitability and market dominance present a different proposition.

The timing also reflects broader market conditions. After years of elevated interest rates that suppressed IPO activity, companies are beginning to test public markets again. SpaceX would be among the highest-profile offerings in years.

Investors will scrutinize several factors: the sustainability of Starlink's growth, the reliability of SpaceX's government contracts, competition from companies like Blue Origin and United Launch Alliance, and regulatory risks. The company has faced FAA investigations over launch procedures and environmental concerns at its Texas facility.

The offering size and exact timing remain unclear. SpaceX has not yet released a prospectus with detailed financials. The company did not immediately respond to requests for comment.
