SpaceX files for stock market debut that could value company above $350 billion

6:00 PM ET

SpaceX has filed paperwork for an initial public offering that would make Elon Musk's rocket and satellite company publicly traded for the first time. The company will trade under the ticker symbol SPCX.

The move comes as SpaceX has grown into the dominant force in commercial spaceflight, launching more rockets than any other company and operating Starlink, a satellite internet service now available in over 100 countries. The company holds contracts worth billions with NASA and the U.S. Department of Defense.

SpaceX was last valued at roughly $350 billion in private fundraising rounds, making it one of the most valuable private companies in the world. A successful public offering at or above that valuation would significantly increase Musk's personal wealth. Some analysts have suggested the company could be valued higher in public markets, though no price range has been disclosed in the initial filing.

The filing does not yet include details on how many shares will be offered or at what price. Those details typically emerge in amended filings as the IPO process moves forward. The company will need approval from the Securities and Exchange Commission before shares can begin trading.

Going public would give SpaceX access to public capital markets, potentially funding its ambitious projects including Starship, a fully reusable rocket system designed to carry cargo and eventually humans to Mars. The company has been conducting test flights of Starship from its facility in South Texas.

The IPO also comes at a complex moment for Musk, who owns or controls several companies including Tesla and X (formerly Twitter). His net worth fluctuates with the stock prices of his public companies, and he has previously used Tesla shares as collateral for loans.

SpaceX has disrupted the space industry by developing reusable rockets that land themselves after launch, dramatically reducing the cost of reaching orbit. The company now launches the majority of all satellites deployed globally and has become NASA's primary partner for transporting astronauts to the International Space Station.

Starlink, SpaceX's satellite internet division, has been particularly successful in rural areas and regions with limited internet infrastructure. The service has also been used to provide connectivity in disaster zones and conflict areas, including Ukraine.

The decision to go public reverses Musk's previous stance. He had long resisted taking SpaceX public, saying in past interviews that the pressures of quarterly earnings reports would conflict with the long-term investments needed for Mars colonization. The company has not explained what changed its position.

Investors will be watching closely to see how the market values a company that mixes government contracts, commercial satellite launches, and experimental technology aimed at interplanetary travel.
