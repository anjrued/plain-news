Republicans delay vote on ICE funding bill amid internal party clash

6:00 PM ET

House Republicans canceled a planned vote this week on a major funding bill for Immigration and Customs Enforcement after failing to unite their caucus behind the measure. The collapse exposes deepening divisions within the GOP over how aggressively to pursue the Trump administration's immigration enforcement agenda.

The legislation would have allocated billions of dollars to ICE for detention facilities, deportation operations, and additional enforcement personnel. Republican leadership had intended to fast-track the bill as a demonstration of party unity on border security, a top priority for President Trump and congressional Republicans.

But disagreements erupted among GOP members over specific policy provisions Trump has demanded be included in the package. Some Republicans from swing districts expressed concern about the political risks of backing measures they view as too extreme, while others aligned with Trump insisted the bill does not go far enough.

The internal dispute left Speaker and leadership without the votes needed to pass the measure in the narrowly divided House. Rather than risk a public defeat on the floor, they pulled the bill from consideration. No new vote has been scheduled.

The setback comes at a critical moment for immigration policy. The Trump administration has ramped up enforcement actions in recent months, leading to increased demand for detention space and resources. ICE officials have testified before Congress that current funding levels are insufficient to maintain operations at the pace the White House is demanding.

Democrats have largely opposed the bill, arguing it would fund what they describe as cruel and ineffective enforcement tactics. That opposition means Republicans must hold together nearly their entire caucus to pass immigration legislation without bipartisan support.

The failed vote highlights a familiar challenge for the party: translating Trump's policy demands into legislation that can win approval from a Republican conference that includes both hardline conservatives and members representing competitive districts. Similar dynamics have derailed other GOP priorities in recent months.

House leadership has not indicated whether they will attempt to revise the bill to satisfy dissenting members or pursue a different legislative strategy. The impasse could delay funding increases for ICE indefinitely, creating friction between congressional Republicans and the White House over immigration policy implementation.

For now, ICE will continue operating under existing funding levels while the political standoff plays out on Capitol Hill.
