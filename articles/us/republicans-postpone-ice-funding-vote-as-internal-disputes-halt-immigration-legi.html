Republicans postpone ICE funding vote as internal disputes halt immigration legislation

6:00 PM ET

House Republicans have shelved plans to vote on a major funding bill for Immigration and Customs Enforcement this week after the effort collapsed amid disagreements within the party over unrelated policy matters.

The bill was intended to provide significant funding increases for ICE operations, including detention facilities and enforcement activities. Republican leadership had aimed to pass the measure before the weekend as part of a broader push on immigration policy, which has been a central focus for the party.

But the vote stalled when members of the Republican caucus clashed over provisions in other legislation, according to congressional aides. The disputes were not directly related to immigration enforcement itself, but created enough friction to prevent the funding bill from advancing.

The delay represents another instance of internal division slowing the Republican agenda in the House, where the party holds a narrow majority. Over the past several months, disagreements among Republican members have repeatedly delayed or derailed legislative priorities, from spending bills to policy initiatives.

Immigration has been a top issue for Republicans since the start of the current Congress. The party has pushed for increased border security funding, expanded detention capacity, and stronger enforcement measures. The ICE funding bill was designed to support these goals by directing additional resources to the agency responsible for interior immigration enforcement.

ICE oversees the detention and removal of undocumented immigrants living in the United States, as well as investigations into human trafficking, smuggling operations, and other cross-border crimes. The agency has faced scrutiny from both sides of the political spectrum in recent years, with Republicans arguing it needs more resources and Democrats raising concerns about detention conditions and enforcement practices.

The failed vote is likely to frustrate Republicans who have made immigration enforcement a campaign priority. With the legislative calendar growing shorter as the year progresses, each delay makes it harder to advance major bills before the next election cycle begins in earnest.

House leadership has not yet announced when the ICE funding bill might return for a vote. Aides said discussions are ongoing about resolving the intraparty disagreements that blocked this week's action.

The setback comes as immigration remains a contentious national issue. Border crossing numbers have fluctuated throughout the year, and both parties have struggled to find common ground on comprehensive reform. The failure to advance even a funding bill supported by most Republicans underscores the challenge of building consensus on immigration policy, even within a single party.
