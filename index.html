5:41 PM ET

President Trump claimed on Saturday that a peace deal with Iran "has been largely negotiated," potentially marking a significant breakthrough in the ongoing conflict that began in February. Trump announced the progress after calls with Pakistani mediators, Gulf allies, and Israel, stating that final details of a "memorandum of understanding" would be announced shortly.

The agreement includes reopening the Strait of Hormuz, the critical shipping channel through which roughly 20 percent of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas passes during peacetime. The waterway has been at the center of escalating tensions between the U.S. and Iran, with both countries imposing separate blockades that have disrupted global commerce and contributed to an international economic crisis.

The closure of the strait has created severe consequences worldwide. About 20,000 seafarers and 2,000 ships have been stranded in the Gulf region, according to the International Maritime Organization. Germany halved its 2026 growth forecast to 0.5 percent, while Greece announced 500 million euros in emergency aid for households and farmers due to fuel shortages and price increases affecting essential supplies.

Pakistan has served as a mediator in the negotiations. Pakistani officials confirmed they had prepared for talks in Islamabad, though discussions have faced setbacks. Iran's parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf previously stated that reopening the strait would be impossible while what he characterized as U.S. and Israeli "flagrant breaches" of any ceasefire continue, citing a U.S. naval blockade he described as "the hostage-taking of the world's economy."

The negotiations occur against a backdrop of military tensions. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps seized two ships attempting to cross the strait, while the U.S. has seized Iranian vessels in recent weeks. Maritime security monitors reported attacks on ships in the waterway, including incidents where vessels were fired upon.

Trump's announcement suggests progress, though key details about the agreement's scope remain unclear. Nuclear weapons, a longstanding point of contention, are reportedly not part of an initial framework being discussed. Iranian officials have signaled progress on talks while maintaining that the nuclear issue requires separate negotiations.

The potential reopening would address the global economic strain caused by disrupted energy supplies and shipping delays. Oil prices and maritime insurance costs have risen significantly due to periodic threats against traffic through the narrow waterway, which spans just 21 miles at its narrowest point.

Both the U.S. and Iran have indicated movement toward a resolution, though officials have not publicly disclosed specific concessions made by either side or the exact mechanisms that would guarantee the strait's continued operation and safety for international shipping.

A gunman was fatally shot by Secret Service officers after opening fire at a White House security checkpoint on Saturday evening, according to federal authorities.

The incident occurred shortly after 6 p.m. near a checkpoint at a White House gate near 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue. A man approached the checkpoint, pulled out a pistol, and fired at officers. Secret Service personnel returned fire, striking the suspect. He was transported to a nearby hospital where he was later pronounced dead.

During the exchange of gunfire, a bystander was also shot and wounded. The Secret Service said in a statement: "It remains unclear whether the bystander was struck by the suspect's initial gunfire or during the subsequent exchange of gunfire." The bystander's condition was described as serious.

No Secret Service personnel were injured during the shooting. President Donald Trump was present at the White House at the time, and the building was briefly locked down as the gunshots rang out.

The suspect was already known to the Secret Service. Court records show the man, 21 years old, had previously attempted to enter the White House complex and had been arrested nearby in 2025 following that incident. He had been issued a "stay away order" after the previous arrest.

Journalists in the area took cover during the shooting. ABC News correspondent Selina Wang shared video on social media showing her diving for cover as gunfire erupted while she was recording a report. She said she and other press personnel were instructed to run to the briefing room. Estimates of the number of shots fired ranged from approximately 20 to 30 rounds.

FBI Director Kash Patel posted on social media that the FBI was on scene and supporting the Secret Service response. The shooting prompted an immediate lockdown of the White House complex, though the lockdown was lifted relatively quickly as the situation was brought under control.

Trump later praised the response on social media, commending the "swift and professional action" of law enforcement officials. He also referenced the incident in comments about security at the White House, noting it came roughly a month after another shooting at the White House Correspondents' Association dinner at a Washington hotel.

This shooting was the second security incident near the White House in less than three weeks. Earlier, a man said to be brandishing a gun had fired at a Secret Service officer near the route of Vice President JD Vance's motorcade outside the White House. In that incident, a civilian witness standing near the officer was wounded, and officers returned fire, striking the man in the hand, arm, and upper abdomen.

The investigation into Saturday's shooting continues as authorities work to establish additional details about the incident.

Bond market strategists warn that Treasury yields are likely to stay high even if the conflict with Iran concludes, according to analysts surveyed by Bloomberg. The projection challenges expectations that resolution of military tensions would quickly ease pressure on government debt markets.

The strategists cite structural factors beyond geopolitical risk that continue to support elevated yields. These include persistent inflation concerns, large government borrowing needs, and expectations that the Federal Reserve will maintain restrictive monetary policy longer than previously anticipated.

Recent developments show how market dynamics shift with geopolitical news. When President Trump announced a pause on airstrikes against Iranian energy infrastructure, financial markets initially reacted positively. Investors reduced expectations for interest rate increases, suggesting geopolitical relief could ease monetary pressure. However, international investors have continued betting that the UK remains vulnerable to sustained inflation following the US-Israel attack on Iran, indicating that market confidence in a quick resolution remains fragile.

The inflation outlook has already affected borrowing costs. Mortgage rates have climbed significantly as expectations for rate increases have priced into the market. In the UK, the average two-year fixed residential mortgage rate reached 5.43%, the highest level since February 2025, up from 4.83% at the start of March. This has created a challenging environment for homebuyers, with hundreds of mortgage products withdrawn from the market as lenders adjust pricing to reflect anticipated rate movements.

Nicholas Mendes, an adviser at mortgage broker John Charcol, noted that mortgage pricing moves ahead of central bank decisions. "Lenders are likely to continue repricing in advance" if markets keep pricing in higher rates, he said. This dynamic suggests that even tentative progress toward an Iran deal may not immediately reverse upward pressure on borrowing costs.

Some analysts dispute the market's inflation expectations. Derek Halpenny, head of research in global markets for Europe, the Middle East and Africa at MUFG, called expectations of four rate increases "overdone." Goldman Sachs argued that UK interest rate rises this year were unlikely, stating instead that the Bank of England would maintain its base rate at 3.75% throughout 2026.

Nevertheless, safe-haven asset demand has intensified, pushing the dollar to fresh highs this year. Gold dropped significantly during market volatility, falling 6% to $4,218 an ounce from nearly $5,600 in late January.

Higher bond yields have wide-ranging economic effects. They increase borrowing costs for mortgages, car loans, and business financing. They also make bonds more attractive relative to stocks, potentially shifting investment flows between asset classes.

The bond market outlook carries implications for policy decisions ahead. Investors and policymakers will be watching upcoming economic data closely. Inflation figures, employment reports, and consumer spending patterns will all factor into decisions about interest rates. The strategists' warning indicates that markets should prepare for a period of elevated borrowing costs regardless of how geopolitical tensions resolve.

Epic Games revealed that Rocket League will transition to Unreal Engine 6, providing the first public indication of when the company plans to release its next-generation game development platform. The announcement came as the studio addressed performance limitations in the current Unreal Engine 5.

The move targets what Epic described as UE5's multithreading wall, a technical barrier that affects how the engine processes multiple tasks simultaneously. Multithreading allows game engines to split work across multiple processor cores, improving performance and frame rates.

Rocket League currently runs on Unreal Engine 3, making the planned shift a significant technical upgrade for the popular competitive game. The transition would skip Unreal Engine 4 and 5 entirely, jumping directly to the upcoming sixth iteration of Epic's development platform.

Unreal Engine 5 launched in 2022 and introduced features like Nanite virtualized geometry and Lumen global illumination. The engine powers games including Fortnite, which Epic migrated from UE4, as well as upcoming titles from major studios.

By announcing Rocket League's UE6 migration, Epic has effectively confirmed development of Unreal Engine 6 is underway. Game engines typically require several years of development before release, and major studios often receive early access to test new versions with their projects.

The multithreading improvements suggest Epic is focusing on performance optimization for UE6. Modern games increasingly demand engines that can efficiently distribute processing loads across the multi-core processors found in current gaming hardware.

Rocket League launched in 2015 and maintains a large player base for its physics-based soccer gameplay. The game receives regular updates and seasonal content, and Epic acquired developer Psyonix in 2019.

Epic has not announced a specific release window for Unreal Engine 6. The company historically provides game developers with extended preview periods before making new engine versions widely available. Developer access typically precedes public release by months or years.

The announcement positions Epic to compete with other game engine developers working on next-generation technology. Unity, its primary competitor in the game development market, continues to update its own engine platform.

The race took place before a crowd at Indianapolis Motor Speedway on the traditional Memorial Day weekend date. Weather conditions allowed the full 200 laps to be completed without interruption.

Rosenqvist joins the list of international winners at Indianapolis, adding to Sweden's representation in the race's record books. His winning move came after carefully managing fuel strategy and track position in the final stint.

May 21, 10:12 AM ET

The Colorado Democratic Party formally censured Governor Jared Polis on Thursday for commuting the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former county clerk convicted in an election security breach case.

Polis reduced Peters' sentence from eight and a half years to approximately four and a half years, making her eligible for parole release on June 1. In his clemency letter, the Democratic governor described the original sentence as "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes."

Peters, the former clerk in Mesa County in western Colorado, was convicted in 2024 of four felonies and three misdemeanors. In 2021, she allowed unauthorized access to the county's voting equipment, enabling a former pro surfer named Conan Hayes to copy sensitive voting systems and attend a confidential software upgrade. Hayes is affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, a prominent figure who has spread false claims about the 2020 election.

The clemency decision sparked immediate backlash from fellow Democrats. Jena Griswold, Colorado's secretary of state, said the decision "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president." She emphasized that Peters should have waited for reconsideration through the courts, as directed by the Court of Appeals, rather than receiving early release.

"What this actually is special treatment," Griswold said. "This clemency will embolden the election denial movement across the country and will leave a dark, dangerous imprint on American democracy for years to come."

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, expressed the group's anger at the decision. "We're starting to hear from election officials across the country that this signals that it's open season on our elections and election officials," he said.

The sentencing judge, Matthew Barrett, had previously rejected any sympathy for Peters at her 2024 sentencing. "You are no hero," he stated. "You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again."

Polis previously signaled his intention to grant clemency by comparing Peters' case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received only probation and community service for submitting forged letters to the legislature. The governor said justice needed to be "applied evenly."

However, Griswold disputed the comparison, stating that beyond one overlapping felony count, the cases were fundamentally different. Peters' actions directly compromised election systems and continue to be used to undermine future elections, she said.

The censure represents a significant rebuke of a sitting governor by his state party organization and highlights deep divisions within Colorado Democrats over election security and accountability for those who have promoted false election claims.

In his clemency letter, the Democratic governor described the original sentence as "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes."

Peters, the former clerk in Mesa County in western Colorado, was convicted in 2024 of four felonies and three misdemeanors. In 2021, she allowed unauthorized access to the county's voting equipment, enabling a former pro surfer named Conan Hayes to copy sensitive voting systems and attend a confidential software upgrade. Hayes is affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, a prominent figure who has spread false claims about the 2020 election.

The clemency decision sparked immediate backlash from fellow Democrats. Jena Griswold, Colorado's secretary of state, said the decision "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president." She emphasized that Peters should have waited for reconsideration through the courts, as directed by the Court of Appeals, rather than receiving early release.

"What this actually is special treatment," Griswold said. "This clemency will embolden the election denial movement across the country and will leave a dark, dangerous imprint on American democracy for years to come."

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, expressed the group's anger at the decision. "We're starting to hear from election officials across the country that this signals that it's open season on our elections and election officials," he said.

The sentencing judge, Matthew Barrett, had previously rejected any sympathy for Peters at her 2024 sentencing. "You are no hero," he stated. "You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again."

Polis previously signaled his intention to grant clemency by comparing Peters' case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received only probation and community service for submitting forged letters to the legislature. The governor said justice needed to be "applied evenly."

However, Griswold disputed the comparison, stating that beyond one overlapping felony count, the cases were fundamentally different. Peters' actions directly compromised election systems and continue to be used to undermine future elections, she said.

The censure represents a significant rebuke of a sitting governor by his state party organization and highlights deep divisions within Colorado Democrats over election security and accountability for those who have promoted false election claims.
