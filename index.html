This structure can amplify factional divisions within a party as supporters consolidate behind their preferred candidate.

Multiple races were on the ballot, but the highest-profile contest drew the most attention from party officials and voters. The results will provide insight into which faction of the Republican Party holds more sway with Texas voters and may influence how candidates position themselves in future elections.

The decision reflects Boeing's inability to meet certification requirements for its crew transportation system. SpaceX has been operating crew flights to the ISS under NASA's Commercial Crew Program, which was designed to have two providers for redundancy.

The Commercial Crew Program was established to end U.S. reliance on Russian Soyuz spacecraft for transporting astronauts to the ISS. Having two certified providers was intended to provide backup capability if one system experienced problems.

SpaceX will continue serving as NASA's primary crew transportation provider under the expanded contract. The company's Crew Dragon capsules launch atop Falcon 9 rockets from Kennedy Space Center in Florida.

Boeing's certification delays have extended beyond initial projections. The company has been addressing various technical and safety requirements as part of NASA's human rating process for spacecraft systems.

The contract modification allows NASA to maintain its planned crew rotation schedule aboard the space station. Regular crew exchanges are necessary to keep the orbital laboratory fully staffed for scientific research and station maintenance operations.

Kyle Busch, a two-time NASCAR Cup Series champion, has died at age 41. NASCAR announced his death on Thursday, stating that he had been hospitalized with a severe illness.

"We are saddened and heartbroken to share the news of the passing of Kyle Busch, a two-time Cup champion and one of our sport's greatest and fiercest drivers," the organization said in a statement.

Busch's family announced his hospitalization earlier Thursday, noting he would miss the scheduled NASCAR race in Charlotte, North Carolina that weekend. According to multiple sources, pneumonia and sepsis contributed to his death.

Sepsis is a life-threatening condition that develops when the body's response to infection causes widespread inflammation and organ damage. It can result from various infections, including pneumonia, which affects the lungs. When bacteria from pneumonia enter the bloodstream, the immune system can trigger reactions that damage the body's own tissues and organs. Early symptoms include fever, rapid heart rate, rapid breathing, confusion, and severe pain. The condition requires immediate medical treatment with antibiotics and other interventions.

During a May 10 NASCAR race at Watkins Glen, Busch had radioed his crew requesting medical assistance after struggling with a sinus cold that was intensified by the intense G-forces and elevation changes at the upstate New York track. Despite his condition, he finished eighth in that race.

A Las Vegas native, Busch won championships in 2015 and 2019. He was in his fourth season at Richard Childress Racing after previously winning titles with Joe Gibbs Racing. Across NASCAR's three national series, Busch won 232 races, more than any driver in history.

Busch was known for his fierce competitive spirit and sometimes controversial behavior, earning nicknames like "Rowdy" and "Wild Thing" due to post-race incidents and feuds with other drivers. He broke onto the Cup Series scene in 2005 by winning rookie of the year.

Throughout his more than two-decade career, Busch set records in national series wins and also developed young talent as an owner in the Truck Series. He ranked 24th in Cup Series standings this season with two top-10 finishes in 12 races. He won a Truck Series race at Dover on Friday, six days before his death.

In a joint statement, Busch's family, Richard Childress Racing, and NASCAR described him as "a rare talent, one who comes along once in a generation." They noted his sharp wit and competitive spirit created a deep connection with fans through his loyal "Rowdy Nation" following.

Busch is survived by his wife, Samantha, and children Brexton and Lennix.

Sonny Rollins, widely regarded as one of the greatest jazz saxophonists in history, has died at age 95. His death was announced Monday on his official website and confirmed by his publicist Terri Hinte. He passed away at his home in Woodstock, New York. No cause of death was provided.

Rollins was one of the last surviving icons from the bebop generation, a musician who helped transform jazz from a primarily dance-oriented form into a vehicle for profound artistic expression. Born Walter Theodore Rollins in New York City in 1930 and raised in Harlem, he earned his nickname Sonny from his grandmother. He began playing saxophone at age seven, inspired by musically talented siblings and jazz legends like Louis Jordan and Fats Waller.

His early career placed him alongside future stars Jackie McLean and Kenny Drew. He quickly collaborated with major figures including Bud Powell and JJ Johnson, while beginning to compose original material. Rollins described his approach as "primitive," explaining that he followed his feelings more than formal training, a philosophy that defined his willingness to break conventions and embrace improvisation.

Miles Davis recognized his genius early, noting that Rollins became "a legend, almost a god to a lot of the younger musicians" for his aggressive, innovative style and constant stream of fresh ideas.

Rollins struggled with heroin addiction in his early years, committing armed robbery in 1950 to support his habit. He spent ten months on Rikers Island but successfully recovered through a rehabilitation program in 1955. His sobriety sparked remarkable creative output. He released his debut album as bandleader in 1953 and recorded seventeen more albums by 1959, including pivotal works like Saxophone Colossus (1956), featuring his signature tune St Thomas; Way Out West (1957), exploring a piano-free style; and Freedom Suite (1958), whose 20-minute title track became a powerful statement during the civil rights era.

Between 1959 and 1962, Rollins took a three-year sabbatical, practicing up to 15 hours daily on the Williamsburg Bridge's pedestrian walkway. This period inspired his comeback album The Bridge. Another break from 1969 to 1971 saw him study yoga, philosophy and meditation at an Indian ashram.

Throughout subsequent decades, Rollins explored Latin music, avant-garde improvisation, and fusion styles. He performed the Alfie soundtrack in 1966 and contributed uncredited saxophone to the Rolling Stones' 1981 album Tattoo You. He advocated for environmental causes through benefit concerts and his 1998 album Global Warming.

Rollins married Lucille Pearson in 1965; they remained together until her death in 2004. His influence extended far beyond performance, with Barack Obama presenting him the National Medal of the Arts in 2011, crediting Rollins with inspiring him to "take risks that I might not otherwise have taken."

May 21, 5:06 PM ET

The Trump administration has temporarily suspended deportation flights to the Democratic Republic of Congo due to concerns about an escalating Ebola outbreak in the country. Officials said they stopped the flights out of worry that they could expose Immigration and Customs Enforcement personnel to the virus and potentially introduce it to the United States.

The pause affects removal operations that have been central to the administration's immigration enforcement strategy. ICE made the decision to halt flights as a precautionary step while the outbreak continues across the African nation.

Ebola causes severe illness in humans and has proven fatal in many cases. Previous outbreaks in Central Africa have killed thousands of people. The disease spreads through direct contact with bodily fluids from infected individuals.

The DRC has experienced multiple Ebola outbreaks in recent years. Current conditions appear serious enough that American officials determined the health risks of continuing deportation flights outweighed the benefits of removing individuals from the country.

However, health experts note that the move may not actually help prevent disease spread. The administration's pause does not apply to individuals already deported to the DRC or to third countries in the region, leaving at least one woman in legal limbo after officials moved her to Kinshasa and refused to return her despite a judge's order for her removal.

The deportation suspension marks a notable shift in enforcement priorities for an administration that has emphasized strict immigration policies. ICE typically conducts removal flights to numerous countries as part of its broader deportation operations targeting individuals who entered or remained in the United States unlawfully.

The decision comes as the DRC's national soccer team cancelled its training schedule in Kinshasa due to health restrictions related to the outbreak. The team still plans to travel to the United States for pre-World Cup preparation, though organizers moved their training away from the capital city.

The administration has not specified how long the deportation flight suspension will remain in place. Officials are expected to reassess the situation as conditions in the DRC change and as public health authorities provide updated guidance on the outbreak's severity and transmission.

The pause also occurs amid broader controversy over the Trump administration's deportation flight operations. Forty House Democrats have raised concerns about what they call secretive ICE charter flights, alleging mistreatment and inhumane conditions onboard. They have called on the Federal Aviation Administration to provide transparency regarding ICE air operations, including flight data and passenger information, arguing that current practices undermine congressional oversight and public understanding of the scope and conditions of these flights.

Kyle Busch, a two-time NASCAR Cup Series champion, has died at age 41. NASCAR announced his death on Thursday, stating that he had been hospitalized with a severe illness.

"We are saddened and heartbroken to share the news of the passing of Kyle Busch, a two-time Cup champion and one of our sport's greatest and fiercest drivers," the organization said in a statement.

Busch's family announced his hospitalization earlier Thursday, noting he would miss the scheduled NASCAR race in Charlotte, North Carolina that weekend. According to multiple sources, pneumonia and sepsis contributed to his death.

Sepsis is a life-threatening condition that develops when the body's response to infection causes widespread inflammation and organ damage. It can result from various infections, including pneumonia, which affects the lungs. When bacteria from pneumonia enter the bloodstream, the immune system can trigger reactions that damage the body's own tissues and organs. Early symptoms include fever, rapid heart rate, rapid breathing, confusion, and severe pain. The condition requires immediate medical treatment with antibiotics and other interventions.

During a May 10 NASCAR race at Watkins Glen, Busch had radioed his crew requesting medical assistance after struggling with a sinus cold that was intensified by the intense G-forces and elevation changes at the upstate New York track. Despite his condition, he finished eighth in that race.

A Las Vegas native, Busch won championships in 2015 and 2019. He was in his fourth season at Richard Childress Racing after previously winning titles with Joe Gibbs Racing. Across NASCAR's three national series, Busch won 232 races, more than any driver in history.

Busch was known for his fierce competitive spirit and sometimes controversial behavior, earning nicknames like "Rowdy" and "Wild Thing" due to post-race incidents and feuds with other drivers. He broke onto the Cup Series scene in 2005 by winning rookie of the year.

Throughout his more than two-decade career, Busch set records in national series wins and also developed young talent as an owner in the Truck Series. He ranked 24th in Cup Series standings this season with two top-10 finishes in 12 races. He won a Truck Series race at Dover on Friday, six days before his death.

In a joint statement, Busch's family, Richard Childress Racing, and NASCAR described him as "a rare talent, one who comes along once in a generation." They noted his sharp wit and competitive spirit created a deep connection with fans through his loyal "Rowdy Nation" following.

Busch is survived by his wife, Samantha, and children Brexton and Lennix.

Jazz Saxophonist Sonny Rollins Dies at 95

Sonny Rollins, widely regarded as one of the greatest jazz saxophonists in history, has died at age 95. His death was announced Monday on his official website and confirmed by his publicist Terri Hinte. He passed away at...

9:00 AM ET

Sonny Rollins, widely regarded as one of the greatest jazz saxophonists in history, has died at age 95. His death was announced Monday on his official website and confirmed by his publicist Terri Hinte. He passed away at his home in Woodstock, New York. No cause of death was provided.

Rollins was one of the last surviving icons from the bebop generation, a musician who helped transform jazz from a primarily dance-oriented form into a vehicle for profound artistic expression. Born Walter Theodore Rollins in New York City in 1930 and raised in Harlem, he earned his nickname Sonny from his grandmother. He began playing saxophone at age seven, inspired by musically talented siblings and jazz legends like Louis Jordan and Fats Waller.

His early career placed him alongside future stars Jackie McLean and Kenny Drew. He quickly collaborated with major figures including Bud Powell and JJ Johnson, while beginning to compose original material. Rollins described his approach as "primitive," explaining that he followed his feelings more than formal training, a philosophy that defined his willingness to break conventions and embrace improvisation.

Miles Davis recognized his genius early, noting that Rollins became "a legend, almost a god to a lot of the younger musicians" for his aggressive, innovative style and constant stream of fresh ideas.

Rollins struggled with heroin addiction in his early years, committing armed robbery in 1950 to support his habit. He spent ten months on Rikers Island but successfully recovered through a rehabilitation program in 1955. His sobriety sparked remarkable creative output. He released his debut album as bandleader in 1953 and recorded seventeen more albums by 1959, including pivotal works like Saxophone Colossus (1956), featuring his signature tune St Thomas; Way Out West (1957), exploring a piano-free style; and Freedom Suite (1958), whose 20-minute title track became a powerful statement during the civil rights era.

Between 1959 and 1962, Rollins took a three-year sabbatical, practicing up to 15 hours daily on the Williamsburg Bridge's pedestrian walkway. This period inspired his comeback album The Bridge. Another break from 1969 to 1971 saw him study yoga, philosophy and meditation at an Indian ashram.

Throughout subsequent decades, Rollins explored Latin music, avant-garde improvisation, and fusion styles. He performed the Alfie soundtrack in 1966 and contributed uncredited saxophone to the Rolling Stones' 1981 album Tattoo You. He advocated for environmental causes through benefit concerts and his 1998 album Global Warming.

Rollins married Lucille Pearson in 1965; they remained together until her death in 2004. His influence extended far beyond performance, with Barack Obama presenting him the National Medal of the Arts in 2011, crediting Rollins with inspiring him to "take risks that I might not otherwise have taken."
