The United States carried out renewed military strikes against Iranian positions, with American Central Command citing self-defense as the justification. The strikes targeted missile sites and Iranian boats as senior Iranian negotiators remained in Qatar for talks aimed at ending the conflict.

The military action occurred even as diplomatic efforts progressed. Secretary of State Marco Rubio indicated that an agreement with Iran could take days to finalize. President Trump told reporters he is "50/50" on reaching a deal and expects a decision by Sunday.

Trump has connected any potential Iran agreement to broader Middle East peace efforts. He has pushed Saudi Arabia to recognize Israel as part of a final deal and told Muslim leaders that signing peace agreements with Israel should be mandatory as part of an Iran deal. This linkage to the Abraham Accords represents a significant condition attached to nuclear negotiations.

The strikes affected global energy markets, with Brent crude rising 2 percent as investors expressed concerns about Middle East stability. Multiple sources described negotiations as progressing, though both Iranian and American officials have downplayed expectations for an imminent breakthrough.

Iran has taken a more cautious stance regarding the talks. An Iranian foreign ministry spokesperson pointed to what he described as contradictory statements from the US as hindering negotiations. Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi stated that Tehran seeks "not a ceasefire, but a complete, comprehensive and lasting end to the war."

Regarding the strategically important Strait of Hormuz, Araghchi said Iran had not closed the waterway but had imposed restrictions on vessels from countries involved in attacking Iran. He indicated that Iran would facilitate safe passage for other nations if they coordinated with Tehran. Japan, which relies on the Middle East for more than 90 percent of its crude oil imports, was mentioned as among the countries Iran was willing to work with on the issue.

The strikes demonstrated that Iranian missiles can reach farther than previously acknowledged, with the base located approximately 4,000 kilometers from Iran.

Several NATO members and allied nations have pledged to help ensure safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz, though specifics on implementation remain unclear. Meanwhile, the US is reportedly preparing to send additional warships and troops to the region.

Google has changed the design and function of its search box for the first time since the company launched in 1998. The redesign integrates artificial intelligence directly into the core search experience.

The changes represent a fundamental shift in how Google presents search results to users. Instead of the traditional list of web links, the new search box incorporates AI-generated responses alongside conventional results.

Google CEO Sundar Pichai announced the changes in a company blog post. The update affects how billions of people access information online. The redesign follows pressure from competitors like OpenAI, which have launched AI-powered search tools in recent years.

The new interface maintains Google's signature minimalist design while adding AI capabilities that can generate direct answers to queries. Users will see AI-generated summaries and responses in addition to traditional web links.

The timing of the announcement aligns with Google's broader push to integrate AI across its products. The company has invested heavily in AI development and faced criticism that it lagged behind newer competitors in deploying consumer-facing AI tools.

Google processes billions of searches daily, making any change to its core search product significant for internet users worldwide. The search box has remained largely unchanged since the company's founding, becoming one of the most recognizable elements of the internet.

The shift to AI search changes how people interact with information online. Rather than clicking through multiple websites, users can receive synthesized answers directly from the search interface. This could reduce traffic to websites that previously received visitors through Google search results.

Sundar Pichai addressed public concerns about AI in an interview. He acknowledged that people feel anxious about artificial intelligence but defended the company's approach to deploying the technology.

The redesign marks a turning point for Google's core business. Search advertising has generated the majority of the company's revenue for decades. How the AI integration affects that business model remains to be seen.

The changes go live for all Google users. The company has not provided an option to revert to the previous search interface.

Yesterday, 11:32 PM ET

Sonny Rollins, one of the greatest jazz saxophonists of all time, has died at age 95. His death was announced on his website Monday and confirmed by his publicist Terri Hinte. He passed away at his home in Woodstock, New York.

Rollins was a defining figure in jazz history whose influence extended across generations of musicians. With more than 60 albums released from the late 1940s onward, he helped transform jazz from a form focused on dance and ballads into an expressive art form. His collaborations included work with Miles Davis, Thelonious Monk, John Coltrane, and many other jazz legends.

Born Walter Theodore Rollins in New York City in 1930, he grew up in Harlem and took up the saxophone at age seven. His nickname "Sonny" came from his grandmother. The vibrant jazz scene in his neighborhood exposed him to influential musicians from an early age, and he began performing professionally while still in high school.

Rollins became known as a genius of melody and improvisation. His bright, catchy musical lines, whether based on jazz standards or his own compositions, would be unpicked and refashioned in extended and sometimes epic solos. Saxophonist Branford Marsalis called him "the greatest improviser in the history of jazz" alongside Louis Armstrong. When presenting Rollins with the 2010 National Medal of the Arts in 2011, President Barack Obama said Rollins had inspired him to "take risks that I might not otherwise have taken."

Miles Davis wrote that Rollins quickly became "a legend, almost a god to a lot of the younger musicians" and praised him as "an aggressive, innovative player who always had fresh musical ideas." This innovative spirit shaped his prolific career.

Rollins released his debut album as bandleader in 1953 and recorded 17 more by the end of the decade. Landmark albums included Saxophone Colossus in 1956, featuring his signature tune St Thomas, and Freedom Suite in 1958, where his 20-minute title composition became an argument for freedom during the civil rights movement.

In 1959, Rollins took a three-year break from recording and performance, practicing up to 15 hours daily on the Williamsburg Bridge pedestrian walkway. He returned in 1962 with the album The Bridge. Another sabbatical between 1969 and 1971 saw him study yoga, philosophy, and meditation at an Indian ashram.

Throughout his career, Rollins demonstrated remarkable versatility, exploring Latin American music, avant-garde jazz, fusion, and funk. He also composed the soundtrack to the 1966 film Alfie and added uncredited soloing to the Rolling Stones' 1981 album Tattoo You.

Later in life, Rollins focused on larger stages and supported environmental causes, releasing the 1998 album Global Warming and performing benefit concerts addressing climate change.

Trump administration pauses deportation flights to Congo during Ebola outbreak

Immigration and Customs Enforcement stopped the flights citing concerns that personnel involved in deportations could be exposed to the virus. Officials also expressed worries...

May 21, 5:06 PM ET

The Trump administration has temporarily halted deportation flights to the Democratic Republic of Congo as an Ebola outbreak spreads across the central African nation, according to reporting by Politico.

Immigration and Customs Enforcement stopped the flights citing concerns that personnel involved in deportations could be exposed to the virus. Officials also expressed worries that the flights could potentially contribute to spreading the disease closer to the United States.

The pause represents a notable shift for an administration that has made removing undocumented immigrants a priority. The decision applies specifically to flights returning individuals to Congo, where health authorities are working to contain the outbreak.

Ebola is a severe viral disease that causes fever, severe bleeding, and organ failure. The virus spreads through direct contact with bodily fluids of infected individuals and carries a high fatality rate. Previous outbreaks in West Africa have killed thousands of people.

However, public health experts say the pause may not meaningfully prevent disease spread. The decision comes as specialists have raised broader questions about America's disease surveillance and response systems. Experts say neither Ebola nor hantavirus currently poses an immediate threat to most Americans, but warn that declining trust in public health institutions could prove dangerous during a genuine crisis.

ICE typically conducts deportation flights to countries worldwide as part of immigration enforcement operations. The agency transports individuals who have been ordered removed from the United States back to their countries of origin, coordinating with foreign governments to accept their citizens.

The deportation pause creates a complicated situation for some individuals. At least one woman has been left in limbo after officials moved her to Kinshasa, the capital of the DRC. Now, officials say they will not bring her back because of the Ebola travel ban, despite a judge's order for her return.

The Congo's national soccer team, which qualified for the upcoming World Cup, canceled training sessions in Kinshasa due to health restrictions related to the outbreak. However, the team still plans to travel to the United States for pre-tournament preparation despite the ongoing health crisis.

The administration has not announced when it might resume deportation flights to Congo. That decision will likely depend on how the outbreak develops and assessments of risk to ICE personnel and public health.

The pause also comes as Democratic lawmakers have intensified scrutiny of the Trump administration's expanded deportation flight operations. House Democrats have raised concerns about transparency and conditions on ICE charter jets, calling for greater oversight of the flights that have increased significantly during 2025.

Jazz Saxophonist Sonny Rollins Dies at 95

5:00 AM ET

Sonny Rollins, one of the greatest jazz saxophonists of all time, has died at age 95. His death was announced on his website Monday and confirmed by his publicist Terri Hinte. He passed away at his home in Woodstock, New York.

Rollins was a defining figure in jazz history whose influence extended across generations of musicians. With more than 60 albums released from the late 1940s onward, he helped transform jazz from a form focused on dance and ballads into an expressive art form. His collaborations included work with Miles Davis, Thelonious Monk, John Coltrane, and many other jazz legends.

Born Walter Theodore Rollins in New York City in 1930, he grew up in Harlem and took up the saxophone at age seven. His nickname "Sonny" came from his grandmother. The vibrant jazz scene in his neighborhood exposed him to influential musicians from an early age, and he began performing professionally while still in high school.

Rollins became known as a genius of melody and improvisation. His bright, catchy musical lines, whether based on jazz standards or his own compositions, would be unpicked and refashioned in extended and sometimes epic solos. Saxophonist Branford Marsalis called him "the greatest improviser in the history of jazz" alongside Louis Armstrong. When presenting Rollins with the 2010 National Medal of the Arts in 2011, President Barack Obama said Rollins had inspired him to "take risks that I might not otherwise have taken."

Miles Davis wrote that Rollins quickly became "a legend, almost a god to a lot of the younger musicians" and praised him as "an aggressive, innovative player who always had fresh musical ideas." This innovative spirit shaped his prolific career.

Rollins released his debut album as bandleader in 1953 and recorded 17 more by the end of the decade. Landmark albums included Saxophone Colossus in 1956, featuring his signature tune St Thomas, and Freedom Suite in 1958, where his 20-minute title composition became an argument for freedom during the civil rights movement.

In 1959, Rollins took a three-year break from recording and performance, practicing up to 15 hours daily on the Williamsburg Bridge pedestrian walkway. He returned in 1962 with the album The Bridge. Another sabbatical between 1969 and 1971 saw him study yoga, philosophy, and meditation at an Indian ashram.

Throughout his career, Rollins demonstrated remarkable versatility, exploring Latin American music, avant-garde jazz, fusion, and funk. He also composed the soundtrack to the 1966 film Alfie and added uncredited soloing to the Rolling Stones' 1981 album Tattoo You.

Later in life, Rollins focused on larger stages and supported environmental causes, releasing the 1998 album Global Warming and performing benefit concerts addressing climate change.
