8:20 AM ET

An explosion struck a passenger train in Pakistan's Balochistan province, killing at least 24 people. The train was carrying military personnel home for the Eid holiday when armed separatists attacked it.

The blast represents the latest violence in Balochistan, a southwestern province bordering Iran and Afghanistan that has experienced decades of insurgent activity. The attack came as part of a larger coordinated assault across the region.

The Balochistan Liberation Army (BLA), a separatist militant group, claimed responsibility for the train attack as well as simultaneous assaults on military installations, police stations, and government buildings across more than 10 cities. The coordinated strikes, which analysts described as unprecedented, killed at least 31 civilians and 17 security personnel overall.

According to Sarfraz Bugti, the chief minister of Balochistan, security forces killed 145 people described as militants within 40 hours of the attacks beginning. The authorities said they had custody of the bodies.

The broader assault began before dawn on Saturday, when scores of insurgents attacked security installations and destroyed railway tracks. They also carried out suicide bombings in Quetta, the provincial capital, and in the port city of Gwadar. Videos shared on social media showed insurgents moving through Quetta's streets, firing rocket launchers at a bank and torching police stations.

In Mastung, insurgents attacked a prison and freed approximately 30 inmates. In Gwadar, where China has constructed a deep-sea port, militants attempted to breach perimeter security but were stopped. In Pasni, five militants were killed when they entered Pakistan's coastguard headquarters after a suicide bomber detonated explosives at the gate.

The BLA claimed in statements to have killed 84 security personnel and captured 18 security and civilian officials, though the Guardian could not verify these claims. The group said seven of its own fighters were killed.

Pakistan's interior minister, Mohsin Naqvi, traveled to Quetta to offer condolences to families of victims. He blamed India for backing the terrorists, a claim India denied.

Pakistan has repeatedly alleged that Afghanistan provides safe havens for Baloch insurgents and other militant groups, allowing them to plan and launch attacks. Afghanistan's government has denied these accusations.

Security analyst Zahid Hussain noted that the attacks exposed previous official claims about reining in Balochistan's insurgency as false. He said the unprecedented assaults indicated the militants possessed substantial local support and highlighted broader governance problems in the province. Mobile internet services were suspended in several cities including Quetta, Gwadar, and Noshki following the violence.

10:57 AM ET

Authorities in Orange County, California, have ordered the evacuation of approximately 40,000 residents across six cities due to a critical chemical storage situation that threatens either a catastrophic spill or explosion.

The emergency began Thursday at a GKN Aerospace facility in Garden Grove, where a storage tank holding methyl methacrylate started off-gassing. Local hazmat teams responded and initially ordered evacuations, which were lifted later that day. However, damage to a valve on the tank created new complications, preventing workers from fully controlling the situation. By Friday, authorities expanded evacuation orders to include residents in Garden Grove, Cypress, Stanton, Anaheim, Buena Park, and Westminster.

Methyl methacrylate is a highly flammable chemical used to manufacture resins and plastics. According to Orange County fire authority division chief Craig Covey, the tank contains approximately 6,000 to 7,000 gallons of the substance.

"We have a tank that is actively in crisis," Covey told reporters. "There are literally two options left remaining. One: the tank fails and spills a total of about 6-7,000 gallons of very bad chemicals into the parking lot in that area. Or two: the tank goes into a thermal runaway and blows up, affecting the tanks that are around them that have fuel or the chemicals in them as well."

Covey emphasized the uncertainty surrounding the timeline. "This thing is going to fail, and we don't know when," he said. "We're doing our best to figure out when or how we can prevent it."

Health officials warned of the dangers posed by the chemical vapor. Dr. Regina Chinsio-Kwong, the county health officer, explained that if the chemical heats up, it can release harmful vapors that cause respiratory issues, eye irritation, nausea, and headaches.

Emergency crews have constructed containment barriers using sandbags to prevent any chemical spill from entering storm drains, creeks, or reaching the nearby ocean. The city established at least two shelters to house evacuees during the emergency.

Garden Grove sits approximately 30 miles southeast of downtown Los Angeles and less than a mile from Disneyland's theme parks, which were not placed under evacuation orders. However, schools and businesses in the evacuation zones were closed.

Despite the serious evacuation orders, some residents proved reluctant to leave. Garden Grove police chief Amir El-Farra reported that approximately 15 percent of those ordered to evacuate were refusing to do so.

Covey urged residents to comply with the orders. "We are setting up these evacuations in preparation for these two options: it fails or it blows up," he said. "Please follow our requests and orders for evacuations."

GKN Aerospace, a division of a British corporation specializing in airplane engines and aircraft parts, is cooperating with hazardous materials experts to develop a solution.

The US Treasury market experienced a sharp selloff Sunday, pushing borrowing costs higher and creating pressure on policymakers in Washington. The rout in Treasury bonds raised questions about how much tolerance exists for elevated government borrowing expenses.

The bond market downturn came as investors reassessed the outlook for federal debt and interest rates. Treasury yields move inversely to bond prices, meaning the selloff translated directly into higher costs for government borrowing. The movement affected bonds across different maturities.

Washington faces mounting fiscal challenges as higher borrowing costs increase the expense of financing federal debt. The Treasury relies on bond markets to fund government operations and refinance existing obligations. When yields rise substantially, debt service consumes a larger share of the federal budget.

The selloff tested the limits of what administration officials and lawmakers consider acceptable for Treasury borrowing rates. Higher yields can slow economic growth by making credit more expensive for businesses and consumers. They also complicate fiscal planning as interest payments on the national debt grow.

Market participants cited multiple factors behind the bond market pressure. Concerns about the federal deficit trajectory and inflation expectations played roles in driving yields upward. The pace of the selloff indicated investors were rapidly repricing their views on government debt.

Treasury officials monitor bond market conditions closely because stable access to credit markets is essential for government financing. Disruptions in Treasury markets can ripple through the broader financial system, affecting everything from mortgage rates to corporate borrowing costs.

The tolerance question centers on how high yields can climb before prompting policy responses. Options could include fiscal adjustments to reduce borrowing needs or interventions to stabilize bond markets. Each approach carries economic and political tradeoffs that officials must weigh.

The bond market serves as a real-time referendum on fiscal policy and economic conditions. Sharp moves in Treasury yields signal that investors are reevaluating their assumptions. The current rout indicated growing unease about the sustainability of current borrowing levels at prevailing interest rates.

Game engines like Unreal provide developers with tools to build graphics, physics, audio, and other core systems without creating those components from scratch. Epic makes Unreal Engine available to developers with a royalty structure based on game revenue.

The announcement arrives as Epic continues to expand Unreal Engine beyond gaming into film production, architecture visualization, and automotive design. Major film and television productions have adopted the technology for virtual production techniques that combine live action footage with computer-generated environments in real time.

Turkish police forcibly entered the main opposition party's headquarters in Istanbul this week, days after a court ruling removed the party's leadership. The operation marks an escalation in the government's pressure campaign against challengers to President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan.

An appeals court in Ankara had issued a ruling that annulled a 2023 leadership election within the Republican People's Party (CHP), deposing party leader Özgür Özel. The court ordered that Özel be replaced by his predecessor, Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu, who lost the 2023 presidential election to Erdoğan despite widespread opposition to the president's rule.

The police entry into party headquarters came after the CHP indicated it would challenge the court's decision. Officers forced party members to leave the building and vacated the premises in what appeared to be an effort to enforce the court's authority.

Özel, 51, had become the face of Turkey's opposition movement. His leadership of the CHP preceded local elections in 2024 where the party swept Erdoğan's Justice and Development Party from power in municipalities across the country. Özel has remained largely insulated from the legal troubles that have ensnared other opposition figures, avoiding charges that could result in detention.

The court ruling unseating Özel was widely criticized as an attempt to weaken the CHP and reinstall a leader seen as more accommodating to Erdoğan's government. The decision sparked immediate economic turbulence, with trading briefly suspended on Istanbul's stock market following a six percent drop in share prices.

The forced removal represents the latest element of an intensifying crackdown on Erdoğan's opponents. Since the 2024 elections, more than 20 CHP mayors have been detained on corruption, bribery, and terrorism-related charges. Istanbul Mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu, viewed as a probable CHP presidential candidate, has spent the past year in a maximum-security facility near Istanbul facing accusations of corruption tied to his time as mayor. He is among 400 defendants in a mass trial prosecutors allege involved a sprawling corruption scheme.

Human Rights Watch has characterized the legal campaign against CHP officials as weaponizing the criminal justice system for political purposes. Numerous municipal officials across Turkey face similar graft charges.

Earlier this week, five officials from the Beşiktaş municipality were taken into custody in a separate bribery investigation. Additionally, a court ordered Özel to pay President Erdoğan 300,000 lira in damages for calling him an "oppressor" and telling him to "leash your dogs" in criticism of the government's crackdown on opposition.

CHP officials have signaled they intend to contest a presidential election expected next year, with speculation they could run the detained İmamoğlu as their candidate.
