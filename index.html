8:35 AM ET

The World Health Organization has upgraded its risk assessment for an Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with WHO chief describing the situation as spreading rapidly. The announcement marks an escalation in global health concern over the outbreak.

The WHO's decision to upgrade the risk assessment reflects growing concern about the trajectory of the outbreak. Such upgrades typically indicate that international health authorities view the situation as requiring increased attention and resources from the global community.

The Democratic Republic of Congo has experienced multiple Ebola outbreaks in recent years, with the disease periodically emerging in different regions of the country. Ebola is a severe viral disease that can cause fatal hemorrhagic fever in humans and other primates.

The WHO's upgraded risk assessment comes as health officials work to contain the outbreak and prevent further transmission. Congo's size, limited infrastructure in affected regions, and ongoing security challenges can complicate response efforts during disease outbreaks.

Previous Ebola outbreaks in Congo have varied in scale and duration. The 2018-2020 outbreak in the eastern provinces was the second-largest Ebola outbreak in history, killing more than 2,200 people. That outbreak took nearly two years to contain.

The WHO typically uses risk assessments to communicate the potential for disease spread and to coordinate international response efforts. An upgraded risk assessment often triggers increased surveillance, resource allocation, and coordination among international health agencies.

Health organizations responding to Ebola outbreaks typically focus on case identification, contact tracing, safe burial practices, and community engagement. Vaccines developed in recent years have provided additional tools for outbreak response.

The current outbreak adds to health challenges facing the region. Congo has also dealt with ongoing malaria, measles, and cholera outbreaks in recent years, straining its health system and complicating disease response efforts.

Authorities in Orange County, California have ordered the evacuation of 40,000 people over concerns about a chemical leak that could result in a spill or explosion.

The crisis began Thursday at a facility owned by GKN Aerospace in Garden Grove, where a storage tank holding methyl methacrylate started off-gassing and threatened to fail. The highly flammable chemical is used to fabricate resins and plastics.

Local authorities initially responded with a hazmat team on Thursday and ordered residents to evacuate. Officials lifted the order later that day, but the situation deteriorated due to "damage to a valve on the tank" that "created additional operational challenges, preventing complete mitigation," according to Garden Grove authorities.

By Friday, new evacuation orders had expanded to residents in six cities as the crisis worsened.

"We have a tank that is actively in crisis," Orange County Fire Authority Division Chief Craig Covey told reporters at a news conference. "There are literally two options left remaining. One: the tank fails and spills a total of about 6-7,000 gallons of very bad chemicals into the parking lot in that area. Or two: the tank goes into a thermal runaway and blows up, affecting the tanks that are around them that have fuel or the chemicals in them as well."

Garden Grove Police Chief Amir El-Farra said approximately 15% of those facing evacuation orders were refusing to leave, according to the Orange County Register. The city has established at least two shelters for evacuees.

Covey urged residents to take the evacuation order seriously. "We are setting up these evacuations in preparation for these two options: it fails or it blows up," he said. "Please follow our requests and orders for evacuations."

Emergency responders have prepared for a third possibility in which the tank could crack rather than explode. They have laid out sand barriers to contain the chemicals in that event.

"In a weird world, that's the best-case scenario, believe it or not," Covey said, according to the New York Times.

Schools shut down Friday in Southern California as the evacuation orders remained in effect. The storage tank continued to leak the hazardous chemical throughout the day.

GKN Aerospace is a division of a British corporation that produces airplane engines and other aircraft parts. The company operates the Garden Grove facility where the storage tank containing methyl methacrylate is located.

Officials described the substance in the tank as highly volatile and maintained their warning that it could lead to an explosion. The tank holds approximately 6,000 to 7,000 gallons of the chemical.

Busch ranked 24th in the Cup Series standings this season, with two top-10 finishes in 12 races. He won a NASCAR Truck Series race at Dover International Speedway last week and was scheduled to compete in this weekend's Coca-Cola 600 in Charlotte before his family announced Thursday he had been hospitalized.

"We are saddened and heartbroken to share the news of the passing of Kyle Busch, a two-time Cup champion and one of our sport's greatest and fiercest drivers," NASCAR wrote on social media. "We extend our deepest condolences to the Busch family, Richard Childress Racing and the entire motorsports community."

Former teammate Denny Hamlin, a 22-year NASCAR veteran, said on social media: "Absolutely cannot comprehend this news. We just need to think of his family during this time. We love you KB."

Driver Ricky Stenhouse Jr said: "There aren't really words for today. I've raced against Kyle for a long time, and anyone who's lined up next to him knows exactly what made him special, he gave you everything he had, every single lap, and he made all of us better for it. But more than the wins and the records, I keep thinking about Samantha, Brexton, and Lennix, and the entire Busch family right now."

Known as "Rowdy" and "Wild Thing" for his feuds with other drivers, Busch won rookie of the year in 2005. Hall of Fame driver Jeff Gordon called him "a fierce competitor who demanded the very best from himself each time he put on the helmet."

The Carolina Hurricanes honored Busch with a video tribute and moment of silence before Thursday night's Eastern Conference finals opener. The Indianapolis Motor Speedway lit up its scoring pylon with Busch's image.

A foundation created by Busch and his wife to support couples seeking fertility treatments collected hundreds of dollars in new donations Thursday night, with dozens of messages reading "In Memory of Kyle Busch."

Base, born Robert Ginyard, rose to fame as part of the duo Rob Base and DJ E-Z Rock. Their single "It Takes Two" became a defining track of late 1980s hip-hop, built around a sample of Lyn Collins' "Think (About It)" and featuring Base's distinctive vocals over DJ E-Z Rock's production.

The song reached number 36 on the Billboard Hot 100 and became a platinum-selling single. Its infectious hook and danceable beat made it a staple at parties and sporting events, and it has been sampled and referenced in numerous songs over the decades.

"It Takes Two" appeared on the duo's debut album of the same name, released in 1988. The album also included other tracks like "Joy and Pain" and "Get on the Dance Floor," but none matched the commercial success or cultural impact of their signature hit.

Base continued performing after DJ E-Z Rock's death in 2014. He remained active on the touring circuit, performing at nostalgia shows and hip-hop festivals where audiences continued to respond enthusiastically to his classic material.

The song's influence extended beyond its initial chart success. It has been featured in films, television shows, and commercials, and has been covered and sampled by artists across multiple genres. The track's memorable refrain and upbeat energy helped establish it as one of the most recognizable songs from hip-hop's golden age.

Base's work represented a particular moment in hip-hop history when the genre was crossing over into mainstream pop culture. The success of "It Takes Two" demonstrated rap music's growing commercial viability and its ability to create dance floor anthems that appealed to audiences beyond traditional hip-hop fans.

Throughout his career, Base maintained his connection to the New York hip-hop scene where he began. He continued to perform the song that made him famous, acknowledging its role in his life and career.

Busch ranked 24th in the Cup Series standings this season, with two top-10 finishes in 12 races. Base, born Robert Ginyard, rose to fame as part of the duo Rob Base and DJ E-Z Rock. Their single "It Takes Two" became a defining track of late 1980s hip-hop, built around a sample of Lyn Collins' "Think (About It)" and featuring Base's distinctive vocals over DJ E-Z Rock's production.

The song reached number 36 on the Billboard Hot 100 and became a platinum-selling single. Its infectious hook and danceable beat made it a staple at parties and sporting events, and it has been sampled and referenced in numerous songs over the decades.

"It Takes Two" appeared on the duo's debut album of the same name, released in 1988. The album also included other tracks like "Joy and Pain" and "Get on the Dance Floor," but none matched the commercial success or cultural impact of their signature hit.

Base continued performing after DJ E-Z Rock's death in 2014. He remained active on the touring circuit, performing at nostalgia shows and hip-hop festivals where audiences continued to respond enthusiastically to his classic material.

The song's influence extended beyond its initial chart success. It has been featured in films, television shows, and commercials, and has been covered and sampled by artists across multiple genres. The track's memorable refrain and upbeat energy helped establish it as one of the most recognizable songs from hip-hop's golden age.

Base's work represented a particular moment in hip-hop history when the genre was crossing over into mainstream pop culture. The success of "It Takes Two" demonstrated rap music's growing commercial viability and its ability to create dance floor anthems that appealed to audiences beyond traditional hip-hop fans.

Throughout his career, Base maintained his connection to the New York hip-hop scene where he began. He continued to perform the song that made him famous, acknowledging its role in his life and career.
