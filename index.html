The reopening would provide both nations time to implement security arrangements and verify compliance with other agreement terms.

Trump has made clear that any Iran deal must include expansion of the Abraham Accords, the framework for normalizing relations between Arab nations and Israel. He has called such participation "mandatory" for any final agreement and stated that more countries should be brought into the diplomatic arrangement.

Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei acknowledged progress but cautioned against expectations of an imminent agreement. "It is correct to say that we have reached a conclusion on a large portion of the issues under discussion," Baghaei said. "But to say that this means the signing of an agreement is imminent, no one can make such a claim."

The sticking points center on Iran's nuclear program. Iran insists on maintaining its right to enrich uranium, though negotiators have discussed limiting enrichment to 5 percent purity, the level accepted in the 2015 nuclear deal and well below weapons grade. Iran also wants to dilute its stockpile of highly enriched uranium and opposes controls on its ballistic missile program.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio signaled resistance to Iran's missile demands, calling it a "big problem" if Iran refuses to negotiate over weapons. This disagreement represents a key obstacle to reaching a final deal.

The negotiations involve American envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner alongside UN nuclear watchdog chief Raphael Grossi and mediators from Oman. Grossi has the authority to assess whether Iran's offered access to verify its nuclear commitments meets inspection standards.

Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi stated that achieving "a fair and just agreement" was possible if diplomacy remained the priority. However, Trump's recent State of the Union speech created complications, as the president accused Iran of pursuing "sinister ambitions" and reiterated concerns about the country's ballistic missiles and nuclear intentions. These comments came just hours before Iran's diplomatic team was set to engage in talks.

Neither government has announced a timeline for completing the agreement or detailed all remaining points of disagreement. Negotiations between American and Iranian representatives are continuing, with both sides facing domestic political pressure over the potential terms.

In his decision, the judge determined that Franco had been a victim of extortion and blackmail by the minor's mother. That woman was sentenced to 10 years in prison for sexually trafficking her daughter.

Franco signed an 11-year, $182 million contract extension with the Rays in 2021 and was considered one of baseball's rising stars. He made his MLB debut in 2021 and earned an All-Star selection in 2023. The allegations against him emerged months ago, and he has since been placed on baseball's administrative leave and the Rays' restricted list, meaning he has not received payment or been allowed to participate in team activities.

The sentencing hearing is scheduled for June 16. Full details about any conditions Franco may be required to meet have not been disclosed in available reports.

Franco has not yet contacted his team regarding the court's ruling, according to reports. The Rays organization has remained largely silent throughout the legal proceedings, deferring questions to Major League Baseball's office and saying they were monitoring the situation.

Major League Baseball maintains its own separate investigation and disciplinary process independent of the criminal proceedings in the Dominican Republic. The league has not announced whether it will impose additional penalties or suspensions under its domestic violence and sexual assault policy. MLB Commissioner Rob Manfred has previously stated that the league takes such allegations seriously and conducts independent investigations.

The outcome raises questions about whether Franco will return to the roster and what precedent his case may set for how the league handles player misconduct away from the field. The Rays have not commented on whether Franco will be allowed to rejoin the team following the legal ruling.

Franco's case represents one of the most significant legal matters involving an active MLB player in recent years. His situation has drawn significant attention both within baseball and in broader discussions about athlete accountability and the league's response to serious allegations.

The judge's finding that Franco was targeted by extortion and blackmail was a notable element of the decision, distinguishing this case from other player misconduct situations. However, the judge's acknowledgment of Franco's criminal responsibility for the abuse demonstrates that the extortion claim did not erase the finding of wrongdoing.

The meeting represents an expansion of coordination between federal and state officials on combating fraud. Vance has made fraud prevention a priority during his tenure as vice president.

State attorneys general handle consumer protection and fraud enforcement at the state level, bringing civil and criminal cases against individuals and companies accused of deceptive practices. The offices also coordinate with federal agencies on cases that cross state lines or involve federal law.

The task force structure allows multiple jurisdictions to share information and resources when investigating fraud schemes. Attorneys general from different states can pool their enforcement powers to target operations that affect consumers across multiple regions.

Vance has positioned himself as a leading voice on economic issues affecting working Americans. Consumer fraud and financial scams have emerged as areas where state and federal governments share overlapping authority and interest.

The vice president has used his platform to focus on issues he says harm middle-class families. Fraud schemes, from telemarketing scams to investment fraud, cost Americans billions of dollars annually according to federal estimates.

White House officials have not disclosed which state attorneys general will attend or what specific types of fraud the task force will target. States have different approaches to fraud enforcement, with some offices taking aggressive stances on particular industries while others focus on consumer education.

The gathering follows a pattern of Vance hosting issue-specific meetings with state officials. Vice presidents traditionally play a liaison role between the federal government and state leaders, particularly on issues where both levels of government have jurisdiction.

Attorneys general bring political weight beyond their enforcement roles. Many serve as prominent figures in their state political parties and several have used the office as a stepping stone to higher office, including governorships and Senate seats.

The fraud initiative gives Vance visibility on a consumer-focused issue with bipartisan appeal. Fighting fraud generates little political controversy compared to other law enforcement priorities, as scam victims span all demographic and political groups.
