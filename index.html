US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said there has been slight progress in ongoing peace negotiations with Iran, but dismissed a proposal that would establish a tolling system for ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz. The talks come amid heightened tensions in the region following recent military conflict.

Rubio's comments indicate that diplomatic efforts to resolve the standoff with Iran continue, though significant differences remain between the two sides. The Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway through which roughly one-fifth of global oil supplies pass, has been a point of contention in the negotiations.

The rejection of the tolling system proposal suggests the United States opposes any arrangement that would give Iran effective control over commercial shipping through the strait. Such a system would allow Iran to charge fees to vessels transiting the narrow waterway between the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman.

The talks represent an effort to de-escalate tensions after recent military action in the region. Earlier reporting indicated that the United States fired more interceptor missiles to protect Israel during the latest phase of conflict with Iran than Israel itself deployed, underscoring the American military commitment to its ally in the region.

The negotiations are taking place against a backdrop of complex regional dynamics. Any agreement would need to address fundamental security concerns on both sides while maintaining freedom of navigation through one of the world's most strategically important shipping channels.

Rubio's characterization of the progress as slight indicates that substantial gaps remain between American and Iranian positions. The ongoing nature of the talks, described in live updates, suggests that negotiations are continuing even as both sides work through difficult issues.

The outcome of these diplomatic efforts could have significant implications for global energy markets and regional stability. The Strait of Hormuz remains a critical chokepoint for international commerce, and any disruption to shipping through the waterway would have far-reaching economic consequences.

Details about other specific issues under discussion in the peace talks have not been disclosed. The State Department has not provided a timeline for when substantive agreements might be reached or what additional meetings are planned.
