Yesterday, 10:56 PM ET

Donald Trump has claimed that a peace agreement with Iran "has been largely negotiated" after discussions with Pakistani mediators, Gulf allies and Israel, potentially signaling an end to the conflict that began in February between the US, Israel and Iran.

The president posted on his social media platform that "final aspects and details" of a "memorandum of understanding" were still under discussion and would be announced shortly. Trump indicated the strait of Hormuz would be reopened as part of the deal.

"An agreement has been largely negotiated, subject to finalization between the United States of America, the Islamic Republic of Iran, and the various other Countries," Trump wrote on Truth Social.

However, Trump's optimistic assessment appears tempered by other parties involved in the negotiations. Pakistan's prime minister expressed hopes of hosting more talks "very soon," suggesting negotiations may not be as advanced as Trump indicated. Iran's Fars news agency reported that the strait of Hormuz would remain under Iranian control, contradicting aspects of Trump's announcement.

The announcement comes after Trump voiced growing impatience with the pace of peace talks. He had earlier posted on social media warning Iran that "the Clock is Ticking, and they better get moving, FAST, or there won't be anything left of them. TIME IS OF THE ESSENCE!"

According to reports, Trump is scheduled to meet with his national security team to discuss next steps in the negotiations. The president also spoke with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu before an Israeli security cabinet meeting focused on Iran, Lebanon and Gaza. There is growing speculation in Israel that fighting with Iran could resume if no signs of compromise emerge.

The Trump administration has oscillated between strike threats and diplomatic overtures over the three months since the conflict began. Neither approach has yet yielded a clear resolution to the crisis.

Regional tensions remain high beyond the direct US-Iran conflict. The United Arab Emirates blamed a fire near its nuclear power plant on a drone launched by Iran or one of its proxies, describing the incident as a "dangerous escalation."

Trump and mediators in Pakistan appear to have different assessments of how close an agreement actually is. While the president suggested a deal was largely complete, Iran and Pakistan indicated more substantial negotiations were still needed.

NPR reported that any initial framework being discussed does not include the key issue of Iran's nuclear weapons program. However, Trump's social media post mentioned that a two-month negotiation period on Iran's nuclear program would be part of the agreement.

The situation remains fluid as parties continue discussions on what Trump called the final details of a potential deal to end the conflict.

The suspect approached the security checkpoint and began shooting at posted Secret Service officers, who returned fire and struck the shooter, according to official statements. The White House was briefly placed under lockdown after reporters from multiple outlets heard a sustained volley of gunshots in the area.

The condition of the wounded bystander remains unclear, as does the question of who fired the shot that struck them. Law enforcement officials have not yet determined whether the bystander was hit by gunfire from the suspect or from Secret Service agents responding to the threat.

Secret Service personnel acted quickly to neutralize the threat after the suspect opened fire on the checkpoint. The agency has not released detailed information about the number of shots fired or the specific sequence of events during the confrontation.

The brief lockdown of the White House ended after authorities secured the scene and determined there was no ongoing threat to the building or its occupants. The incident occurred on a street near the White House complex where security checkpoints are maintained to screen individuals approaching the premises.

Federal officials have not yet released information about the suspect's identity or potential motives for the attack. An investigation into the shooting is ongoing as authorities work to piece together what led to the violent confrontation.

The Secret Service is responsible for protecting the White House and its surrounding perimeter, maintaining multiple layers of security around the executive mansion. Officers posted at checkpoints are trained to respond immediately to potential threats and are authorized to use deadly force when necessary to protect the facility and those inside.

This incident marks another security challenge for the Secret Service, which maintains constant vigilance around one of the most heavily guarded buildings in the United States. The agency regularly faces attempts by individuals to breach security perimeters, though violent confrontations involving gunfire are relatively rare.

The wounded bystander's condition has not been disclosed by authorities. It remains unclear whether the individual was in the immediate vicinity of the checkpoint or was farther away when struck by gunfire during the exchange between the suspect and Secret Service officers.

Authorities have not indicated whether the suspect made any statements or demands before opening fire, or whether there were any warning signs that might have alerted security personnel to the impending attack. The investigation will likely examine these questions as officials work to understand the full circumstances surrounding the deadly confrontation.

Uber has made a takeover offer for Delivery Hero, the German food delivery company confirmed on Saturday. The move would consolidate two of the world's largest food delivery and ride-hailing platforms.

Delivery Hero operates in more than 70 countries across Asia, Europe, Latin America, and the Middle East. The company has built a substantial presence in markets where Uber Eats has a smaller footprint, making the potential acquisition strategically significant for Uber's global expansion.

The confirmation comes from Delivery Hero itself, though financial terms of the offer were not disclosed in the announcement. Uber, which operates both ride-hailing and food delivery services through Uber Eats, has been working to strengthen its position in the competitive global delivery market.

Delivery Hero has faced financial pressure in recent years as the food delivery industry matured following rapid pandemic-era growth. The sector has seen widespread consolidation as companies seek to achieve profitability through scale and market dominance. Several major delivery platforms have merged or been acquired as the industry adjusts to post-pandemic demand levels.

For Uber, acquiring Delivery Hero would provide immediate access to established operations in key international markets. Delivery Hero's strongest presence is in regions where Uber Eats has limited operations, potentially reducing direct overlap and regulatory scrutiny. The acquisition would also eliminate a significant competitor in markets where both companies currently operate.

The food delivery industry has undergone significant restructuring since 2024, with companies seeking to reduce costs and achieve sustainable profit margins. Uber has been among the more financially stable players in the space, reporting positive adjusted earnings in recent quarters. Delivery Hero, by contrast, has worked to narrow losses while maintaining its market positions.

Any deal would likely face regulatory review in multiple jurisdictions, particularly in European markets where both companies maintain operations. Antitrust authorities have scrutinized delivery platform consolidation due to concerns about market concentration and its effects on restaurants, delivery workers, and consumers.

Neither Uber nor Delivery Hero provided a timeline for negotiations or indicated whether the offer had been accepted. The companies did not comment on whether other potential bidders might emerge.

Caltech could lose its management contract for NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory for the first time in the facility's history. The California Institute of Technology has operated JPL since the laboratory's founding, maintaining one of the longest-running partnerships between a university and a federal research facility.

The potential change comes as NASA evaluates management structures for its field centers. JPL has operated under Caltech's management through a contract with NASA, making it unique among NASA's major facilities. The laboratory conducts robotic space missions and planetary exploration research.

Caltech has managed JPL since the facility was established. The arrangement has allowed the laboratory to maintain academic connections while conducting government-funded space research. JPL has been responsible for numerous NASA missions, including Mars rovers and deep space probes.

The relationship between Caltech and JPL has shaped how the laboratory recruits staff and conducts research. University management has provided JPL with access to academic talent and research networks. The laboratory employs thousands of scientists and engineers in Pasadena, California.

NASA has not announced a final decision on JPL's future management. Any change would represent a shift in how one of the space agency's premier research facilities operates. The laboratory's work includes developing spacecraft, conducting planetary science, and managing deep space communications.

The evaluation of JPL's management structure reflects broader questions about how federal research facilities partner with academic institutions. Other NASA centers operate under different management models, including direct federal operation and contracts with other organizations.

JPL's current portfolio includes active missions across the solar system and development of future spacecraft. The laboratory also operates the Deep Space Network, which provides communications for NASA missions beyond Earth orbit. How management changes might affect ongoing missions and future projects remains to be determined.

Two-time NASCAR champion Kyle Busch died at age 41 after severe pneumonia progressed into sepsis, resulting in rapid and overwhelming complications, according to a statement from his family.

Dakota Hunter, vice president of Kyle Busch Companies, released the news on Saturday after the family received the medical evaluation. Busch died Thursday at a hospital in Charlotte, North Carolina, just one day after collapsing in a Chevrolet simulator while preparing for Sunday's Coca-Cola 600 race at Charlotte Motor Speedway.

According to a 911 call obtained by the Associated Press, Busch experienced shortness of breath, felt overheated, and was coughing up blood the day before his death. In the emergency call, an unidentified caller calmly told dispatch that an individual had shortness of breath, felt very hot, thought he was going to pass out, and was producing some blood. The caller reported that Busch was lying on the bathroom floor inside the complex but was awake, according to audio from the Cabarrus County Sheriff's Office.

The driver's sudden death has caused widespread grief among motor racing fans. Busch won 234 races across NASCAR's top three series during his two-decade career, more than any driver in history.

Prominent figures paid tribute to the racing legend. Donald Trump described Busch as a true talent who loved NASCAR and its fans, calling him a winner. JD Vance called Busch one of NASCAR's greatest racers. NASCAR CEO Steve O'Donnell described him as an American badass, saying they certainly had their battles but he would give a lot of money to have a few more.

Busch was believed to have had a sinus cold while racing at Watkins Glen on May 10 and radioed his team saying he needed a shot from a doctor after the race. However, he recovered to win the Trucks Series race at Dover last weekend and finished 17th in the All-Star race on Sunday.

NASCAR driver Brad Keselowski confirmed he knew Busch was not feeling well recently but declined to provide specifics. He said those concerns were honestly erased from his mind when Busch ran the Truck race last week.

All 39 drivers in Sunday's race field will race with a black Number 8 decal on their cars to honor Busch.

Sepsis is a life-threatening medical emergency that occurs when the body has an extreme, overactive response to an infection, causing the immune system to damage its own tissues and organs, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. The condition can cause widespread inflammation, form microscopic blood clots, and make blood vessels leak.

Yesterday, 6:10 PM ET

Cristian Mungiu's English-language debut "Fjord" has won the Palme d'Or at the 79th Cannes Film Festival, marking the Romanian director's second win of the prestigious award. Mungiu previously took home the top prize nineteen years ago for his abortion drama "4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days."

The winning film stars Renate Reinsve and Sebastian Stan as Romanian religious parents who move to Norway, where they face accusations of child abuse. With this victory, the 58-year-old Mungiu becomes only the tenth director to receive two Palmes d'Or, joining an elite group that includes Francis Ford Coppola, Michael Haneke, Ken Loach, and Ruben Östlund.

US distributor Neon has already acquired "Fjord," continuing their remarkable streak of obtaining Cannes winners for seven consecutive years. This follows the success of Sean Baker's "Anora," which won the Palme two years ago before sweeping the 2025 Oscars.

The Grand Prix, the festival's runner-up prize, went to Andrey Zvyagintsev's "Minotaur," described as a dark satire examining corruption and infidelity in contemporary Russia. Valeska Grisebach's "The Dreamed Adventure," a Bulgaria-set drama about an archaeologist, claimed third place with the Jury prize.

The best director award was split between Pawel Pawlikowski for "Fatherland" and the duo of Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi for "The Black Ball." Both acting awards were also shared between pairs of performers. Virginie Efira and Tao Okamoto won best actress for Ryusuke Hamaguchi's care drama "All of a Sudden," while Valentin Campagne and Emmanuel Macchia took best actor honors for the queer World War I drama "Coward."

This year's festival was notably subdued, characterized by disappointing returns from various auteurs and a distinct lack of Hollywood glamour. Both American films in competition were overlooked by judges, including James Gray's "Paper Tiger" starring Adam Driver and Scarlett Johansson, and Ira Sachs' AIDS musical "The Man I Love" featuring Rami Malek.

Park Chan-wook led this year's jury, which included Demi Moore, Stellan Skarsgård, Chloé Zhao, and Paul Laverty. During the closing ceremony, Isabelle Huppert paid special tribute to Barbra Streisand, who received an honorary Palme d'Or alongside Peter Jackson and John Travolta. Unable to attend due to a knee injury, Streisand sent a video message praising cinema's ability to unite people. "In this crazy, volatile world that seems more fractured every day, it's reassuring to see the compelling movies at this festival, by artists from many countries," she said.

The festival's sidebar competitions saw Sandra Wollner's "Everytime" win Un Certain Regard, while Marine Atlan's "La Gradiva" took Critics Week and Clio Barnard's "I See Buildings Fall Like Lightning" won the Audience award.

Yesterday, 7:42 PM ET

A gunman who opened fire at a White House security checkpoint on Saturday evening was shot and killed by Secret Service officers, federal officials confirmed. The incident occurred shortly after 6pm local time near 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue NW.

According to the Secret Service, a man approached a checkpoint at a White House gate, drew a pistol, and began firing at officers. Secret Service personnel returned fire, striking the suspect. He was transported to a hospital where he later died from his injuries.

During the exchange of gunfire, a bystander was also wounded. The Secret Service stated that it remains unclear whether the bystander was struck by the suspect's initial shots or during the subsequent firefight between the gunman and officers. No Secret Service personnel were injured in the incident.

President Donald Trump was present in the Oval Office at the time but was not affected by the shooting. The White House was briefly placed on lockdown as the situation unfolded.

Journalists in the area reported hearing a sustained volley of gunshots, with estimates ranging from 20 to 30 shots fired. White House correspondent Selina Wang of ABC News was recording a report on US negotiations with Iran when the gunfire erupted, forcing her to dive for cover. In video she shared on social media, Wang described the sound of dozens of gunshots ringing out before being told to sprint to the press briefing room.

Chris Flanagan, an anchor for DC News Now who also took cover in the briefing room, estimated that approximately 30 shots were fired. The security lockdown was lifted after the immediate threat was resolved.

Multiple US media outlets reported that the suspect had a history of mental health issues. He had allegedly been arrested last year for attempting to enter through a different White House checkpoint without authorization and had been issued a stay away order. However, the Secret Service has not officially named the suspect.

FBI Director Kash Patel confirmed that FBI agents were on the scene supporting the Secret Service response, stating they would provide updates to the public as information became available.

This shooting marks the second such incident near the White House in recent weeks. Less than three weeks earlier, a man brandishing a gun fired at a Secret Service officer near the route of Vice President JD Vance's motorcade. In that incident, a civilian witness was wounded in the leg, and officers returned fire, striking the suspect.

The Saturday night gunfire also came nearly a month after what authorities described as an attempted assassination of President Trump on April 25 during the annual White House Correspondents' Association dinner at a Washington hotel.

Yesterday, 6:29 PM ET

The World Health Organization has declared an Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Uganda a "public health emergency of international concern" after 88 deaths and more than 300 suspected cases were reported.

The outbreak involves the Bundibugyo virus, one of three strains that can cause Ebola virus disease and the least common. No approved vaccines, treatments, or specific tests exist for this particular strain, making the situation especially challenging for health officials.

Dr. Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, the WHO director general, announced the declaration on Sunday before convening a formal emergency committee. The speed of the announcement likely reflects the gravity of the situation, according to experts.

Dr. Jean Kaseya, director general of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention, expressed his deep concern about the outbreak. "Currently, I'm on panic mode because people are dying. I don't have medicines. I don't have a vaccine to support countries," he told Sky News.

The outbreak has centered in the DRC's Ituri province in the eastern part of the country, bordering Uganda and South Sudan, approximately five days' travel from the capital. There have been 80 suspected deaths, eight laboratory-confirmed cases, and 246 suspected cases in that region.

Two cases have also been reported in Uganda's capital, Kampala, including one death, apparently in people who had traveled from the DRC. A laboratory confirmed an additional case in the city of Goma in a separate part of the DRC.

"A positive case in Goma has been confirmed by tests carried out by the laboratory. It involves the wife of a man who died of Ebola in Bunia, who travelled to Goma after her husband's death whilst already infected," said Prof. Jean-Jacques Muyembe, director of the Congolese National Institute for Biomedical Research.

The WHO stated that signs "all point towards a potentially much larger outbreak than what is currently being detected and reported, with significant local and regional risk of spread." Officials acknowledged there are "significant uncertainties as to the true number of infected persons and geographic spread associated with this event at the present time."

Conflict continuing in the Ituri region has complicated response efforts, with many people moving around and living in close quarters in urban and suburban areas, factors that can make spread more likely.

In Bunia, the capital of Ituri, residents reported unusually high numbers of burials. "Every day, people are dying, and this has been going on for about a week. In a single day, we bury two, three or even more people," said Jean Marc Asimwe, a local resident. "At this point, we don't really know what kind of disease it is."

Kaseya stressed the importance of basic infection control measures such as gloves and handwashing, though officials noted that many informal health facilities in the affected regions may have limited supplies of that equipment.
