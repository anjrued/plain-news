8:35 AM ET

A crack discovered in an overheated chemical tank in Garden Grove, California has reduced the immediate risk of explosion, offering hope to roughly 50,000 residents who have been forced to evacuate their homes.

The crisis began Thursday afternoon when a leak was reported at GKN Aerospace, an aerospace parts manufacturer located about 5 miles from Disneyland. The facility's storage tank contained between 6,000 and 7,000 gallons of methyl methacrylate, a highly flammable chemical used in plastic and resin production.

Environmental Protection Agency Administrator Lee Zeldin warned on Sunday that officials expect the tank "will fail," though the exact outcome remains uncertain. He described the most catastrophic scenario as an explosion that could trigger additional tanks to rupture, explaining why such a large evacuation was necessary. However, Zeldin noted that officials consider the most likely outcome to be a "low-volume release" of chemicals.

Firefighters have been working continuously to prevent disaster by spraying water on the tank to keep its temperature below 85 degrees Fahrenheit. On Friday, officials added a neutralizing agent to a nearby tank to further reduce explosion risk.

The breakthrough came late Saturday night when firefighters discovered a potential crack in the tank. Orange County Fire Chief TJ McGovern indicated that the crack could be relieving pressure inside the tank, making an explosion less likely. Purdue University engineering professor Andrew Whelton compared the situation to a pressurized soda can left in a hot car. "If you leave it in a hot car it can explode," Whelton explained. "But if you put a hole in the can, the product is released and the can itself doesn't explode."

However, experts cautioned that risks remain. Faisal Khan, head of the chemical engineering department at Texas A&M University, warned that a smaller explosion could still occur despite cooling efforts, since chemical reactions may continue deep within the tank.

Air quality officials reported detecting no pollutants in the surrounding area. Garden Grove Mayor Stephanie Klopfenstein described the situation as dangerous but acknowledged receiving "somewhat positive" updates from fire authority teams.

Methyl methacrylate exposure can cause serious respiratory problems, unconsciousness, neurological damage, and skin and eye irritation. However, officials noted the chemical is easily detectable by smell, allowing residents to recognize potential exposure.

At least two Garden Grove residents have filed lawsuits against GKN Aerospace, citing evacuation orders, shelter-in-place directives, exposure concerns, and property damage. Disneyland theme parks, located nearby in Anaheim, were not placed under evacuation orders but said they were monitoring the incident and supporting affected employees.

Oil prices fell significantly after reports emerged that the United States and Iran are moving closer to an agreement that could end their ongoing conflict. The potential diplomatic breakthrough sent markets into motion, with investors reassessing energy supply outlooks.

The price decline reflects expectations that a deal could ease tensions in the Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway for global oil shipments. The strait has been a focal point of concern as the conflict escalated, with disruptions threatening roughly one-fifth of the world's petroleum supply.

Stock markets rose on the news, while the dollar weakened against other currencies. The simultaneous movements across multiple asset classes underscore how deeply the Middle East conflict has affected global financial markets in recent months.

Investors remain cautious despite the positive signals. Some analysts note that while the prospect of peace has triggered immediate market reactions, uncertainties persist about whether negotiations will produce a lasting agreement. Oil prices have been volatile throughout the conflict, and traders are weighing the likelihood of sustained de-escalation.

The potential deal comes after months of heightened military and economic tensions between Washington and Tehran. The conflict disrupted shipping routes and raised concerns about broader regional stability, contributing to elevated energy costs worldwide.

Market observers are watching closely for concrete details of any agreement. The initial price movements suggest traders believe progress is genuine, but the extent of the oil price drop also indicates markets had priced in significant risk premium during the conflict.

Energy sector stocks declined as oil prices fell, while other market sectors gained ground. The shift reflects investor expectations that reduced energy costs could ease inflationary pressures and benefit companies dependent on fuel and petroleum-based materials.

The negotiations represent a potential turning point for global energy markets that have been roiled by geopolitical instability. Any formal agreement would likely take time to implement fully, but the direction of talks has already begun reshaping market expectations and investment strategies.

Google has redesigned its search box for the first time in a quarter century, integrating artificial intelligence directly into the core interface. The change marks a shift in how users interact with the search engine that has defined internet navigation since the late 1990s.

The redesign was announced at Google's I/O 2026 conference and represents the company's response to growing competition from AI-powered search alternatives. Google CEO Sundar Pichai addressed public concerns about artificial intelligence during the announcement, acknowledging widespread anxiety about the technology's rapid advancement.

The new interface incorporates Google's Gemini AI system, which the company is positioning as entering an "agentic era" where AI can take more active roles in completing tasks. The integration allows users to interact with search results through conversational AI prompts directly from the search box.

The timing of the redesign reflects pressure on Google to evolve its core product before competitors can replace traditional search with AI alternatives. Industry observers note that Google is attempting to reinvent search proactively rather than risk losing market dominance to newer AI platforms.

The search box has remained largely unchanged since Google's founding, with the company historically resisting alterations to its minimalist homepage design. The decision to modify this iconic interface signals how seriously Google views the threat from AI-powered search competitors.

Google has been gradually introducing AI features into search results over the past year through AI Overviews, which provide generated summaries above traditional search results. The new search box design takes this integration further by making AI interaction the primary interface rather than a supplementary feature.

The redesign arrives as Google faces criticism about AI-generated content quality. Some users have reported issues with AI Overviews providing inaccurate information, and workarounds have emerged allowing users to disable AI features in search results entirely.

Google's move reflects broader industry trends as major technology companies race to incorporate AI capabilities into their core products. The redesign represents a bet that users will embrace conversational AI interfaces over traditional keyword-based searching, fundamentally changing how billions of people access information online.

Two-time NASCAR Cup Series champion Kyle Busch has died at age 41, NASCAR announced Thursday. The racing organization said in a statement that Busch had been hospitalized with a severe illness.

"We are saddened and heartbroken to share the news of the passing of Kyle Busch, a two-time Cup champion and one of our sport's greatest and fiercest drivers," NASCAR said on social media.

His family later confirmed that severe pneumonia and sepsis caused his death. Busch had informed the racing community earlier Thursday that he would miss the upcoming NASCAR race in Charlotte, North Carolina due to his hospitalization.

Busch's health struggles began during a Cup Series race on May 10 at Watkins Glen. During the competition, he radioed his crew requesting medical assistance from the track doctor and a "shot" after the race. According to broadcast reports, Busch had been dealing with a sinus cold that worsened due to the intense gravitational forces and elevation changes at the New York road course. Despite his condition, he continued racing and finished eighth.

The Las Vegas native won his championships in 2015 and 2019, competing most recently in his fourth season with Richard Childress Racing. Across NASCAR's three national series, Busch recorded 232 wins, more than any driver in history. He won a truck series race at Dover on Friday, just six days before his death.

In a joint statement, the Busch family, Richard Childress Racing, and NASCAR described him as "a rare talent, one who comes along once in a generation." The statement noted that throughout his career spanning more than two decades, Busch "set records in national series wins, won championships at NASCAR's highest level and fostered the next generation of drivers as an owner in the Truck Series."

Known as "Rowdy" for his competitive and sometimes controversial behavior, Busch became a polarizing figure in racing. He burst onto the Cup Series scene in 2005 by winning Rookie of the Year. His passionate and fierce driving style created a dedicated fan base known as "Rowdy Nation."

Daniel Suarez honored his friend and former team owner in the most significant way possible following Busch's death. Suarez won the Coca-Cola 600 at Charlotte Motor Speedway on Sunday night, breaking an 82-race winless streak. Suarez, who had previously driven for Kyle Busch Motorsports in the truck series, held off Christopher Bell and Denny Hamlin in an emotional race that served as a tribute to the late driver. The victory marked Suarez's third career Cup Series win.

Busch is survived by his wife, Samantha, and children Brexton and Lennix.

Governor Jared Polis commuted the sentence of Tina Peters, a former Colorado county clerk whose case has become a focal point for those spreading false claims about the 2020 presidential election. The decision prompted the Colorado Democratic Party to formally censure the governor, marking an unusual public disagreement within the state party.

Peters was serving an eight and a half year sentence after being convicted of four felonies and three misdemeanors in 2024. Polis reduced her sentence to approximately four and a half years, making her eligible for parole release on June 1. In his clemency letter, Polis wrote that the sentence was "an extremely unusual and lengthy sentence for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes."

Peters' original conviction stemmed from allowing unauthorized people to access Mesa County's voting equipment in 2021. A former professional surfer named Conan Hayes, who is connected to MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, accessed and copied voting equipment and attended a sensitive software upgrade. Sensitive passwords and other information from the county's Dominion voting machines were later published online by right-wing figures.

Colorado's Secretary of State Jena Griswold, a Democrat, strongly criticized the decision. "This is an extremely unusual and lengthy sentence for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes," she said, characterizing the clemency as "special treatment" rather than justice. Griswold warned that the decision "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president" and will "embolden the election denial movement across the country."

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, said the group was "furious, disgusted, and deeply disappointed by the governor's decision." He noted that election officials across the country now worry that Polis' action signals "it's open season on our elections and election officials."

The judge who originally sentenced Peters in 2024 offered sharp criticism at her sentencing. "You are no hero," Judge Matthew Barrett said. "You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again."

Polis had signaled his leanings toward clemency in March when he compared Peters' case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for submitting forged letters to the legislature. Polis stated that "justice in Colorado and America needs to be applied evenly." However, Griswold and other officials disputed this comparison, noting that Peters' actions involved organizing a breach of election equipment and breaking public trust in fundamental democratic processes.

The censure vote carries no legal consequences but represents a formal statement of disapproval from party leadership. The rift between Polis and the Colorado Democratic Party highlights ongoing tensions within the party about responding to election denial and the use of clemency powers.
