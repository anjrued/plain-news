President Trump approved a federal emergency declaration for Orange County, California, following a chemical leak in Garden Grove that forced evacuations. Governor Gavin Newsom announced the federal approval.

The emergency declaration authorizes the Federal Emergency Management Agency to coordinate disaster relief efforts and provide assistance to protect public health and safety. According to FEMA, the action allows the agency to "alleviate the hardship and suffering caused by the emergency on the local population" and "lessen the threat of catastrophe."

Officials announced that the threat of a catastrophic explosion from a chemical tank had been eliminated, though residents remained evacuated from the affected area. The leak occurred in Garden Grove, a city in Orange County located in the Los Angeles metropolitan area.

The emergency declaration enables federal resources to support state and local response efforts. Such declarations typically provide funding and coordination assistance for evacuation operations, cleanup, and recovery work. The declaration came after Governor Newsom made a formal request to the federal government, which is the standard procedure before presidential action on such matters.

Authorities maintained evacuation orders even after declaring the explosion threat eliminated. The timeline for when residents might return to their homes was not immediately provided as officials continued monitoring the situation and assessing the full scope of the incident.

Local response efforts included volunteers from San Diego who helped with evacuation support for affected residents. The exact number of people evacuated and the scale of the evacuation was not detailed in available reports.

Multiple news outlets provided live updates and evacuation maps as authorities managed the crisis. The Los Angeles Times and NBC Los Angeles offered ongoing coverage and information for affected residents in the area.

The chemical leak adds to California's ongoing emergency management challenges. While the state frequently deals with natural disasters including wildfires, earthquakes, and drought conditions, industrial incidents like chemical leaks require different response protocols and coordination efforts.

Officials worked to secure the chemical tank and assess environmental impacts following the initial leak. The nature of the specific chemical involved in the incident was not specified in initial reports.

The incident represents a significant public safety event in Southern California. Orange County is one of the most populous counties in the United States and part of the larger Los Angeles metropolitan area. The emergency declaration underscores the seriousness of the situation and the need for coordinated state and federal response to protect public health and facilitate recovery efforts for the impacted community.

Oil prices dropped below $100 per barrel on Monday as markets responded positively to signs that the U.S. and Iran are moving closer to a peace agreement. Brent crude futures, the global oil benchmark, fell 6% to $97.43 a barrel, marking the lowest level in two weeks.

The price decline reflects investor optimism that a deal could end the nearly three-month U.S.-Israeli military conflict with Iran and potentially reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global energy supplies. However, significant obstacles remain. While a framework has been negotiated, the U.S. and Iran continue to disagree on key issues, including Iran's blockade of the strait. An Iranian government spokesperson cautioned that an agreement was "not imminent."

Stock markets rose alongside falling oil prices, with Japan's Nikkei index climbing nearly 3% and Europe's Stoxx 600 index gaining 1%. Several major markets, including the U.S. and UK, were closed for public holidays. The dollar weakened slightly, dropping 0.3% against a basket of major currencies, while gold climbed 1.46% to $4,574 per ounce.

Analysts urge caution about reading too much into the price movement. Warren Patterson, head of commodities strategy at ING, told Reuters: "We've been at this stage before, only for talks to break down. Therefore, the market will likely be more cautious about overreacting." Even if negotiations succeed, experts say restoring normal oil flows through the strait will take months while damaged energy infrastructure in Qatar and other regions undergoes repair.

Shipping data indicates some movement has resumed. Two liquefied natural gas tankers exited the strait on Monday heading to Pakistan and China, while a supertanker carrying Iraqi crude departed for China on Saturday after being stranded for nearly three months.

Giovanni Staunovo, a UBS analyst, emphasized that physical oil flows remain the key factor to monitor. "Flows through the strait remain restricted," he said, suggesting caution about the recent price decline.

Despite Monday's drop, oil prices remain substantially higher than before the war began. Brent crude was trading around $70 per barrel in the weeks before the conflict escalated on February 28. Barclays recently maintained its average forecast of $100 per barrel for the year, though it warned that prices could move higher.

The decline in oil prices has implications for broader economic concerns. Higher energy costs have fueled inflation fears worldwide and shifted central bank expectations away from interest rate cuts toward potential increases. Markets now anticipate the Bank of England will raise rates twice this year as policymakers contend with inflation pressures from elevated oil, gas, and commodity prices.

When users attempt to sign in, the phishing kit intercepts their credentials and OAuth token...

8:45 AM ET

The FBI has issued a warning about a phishing service called Kali365 that targets Microsoft 365 user accounts. The service allows attackers to gain access to accounts even when multi-factor authentication is enabled.

Kali365 uses real Microsoft login pages as part of its attack method. When users attempt to sign in, the phishing kit intercepts their credentials and OAuth tokens, which are authentication credentials that grant access to accounts. This technique bypasses traditional security measures including multi-factor authentication.

The FBI's warning highlights how the phishing service operates as a commercial offering, making sophisticated account takeover attacks available to a broader range of threat actors. The use of legitimate Microsoft infrastructure in the attack makes it harder for users to identify the threat.

Microsoft 365 is widely used by businesses and organizations for email, document storage, and collaboration. Successful account compromises through Kali365 could give attackers access to sensitive business communications, files, and other resources stored within these accounts.

Security researchers have documented how the phishing kit specifically targets OAuth tokens rather than just passwords. OAuth tokens allow applications to access user accounts without requiring the password each time. By stealing these tokens, attackers can maintain access to accounts even if users change their passwords after the initial compromise.

The FBI's public warning indicates the threat has reached a level of concern warranting broad notification to Microsoft 365 users. Organizations and individuals using Microsoft 365 should be aware of the threat and exercise caution when signing into their accounts, particularly by verifying the authenticity of login pages and being alert to unexpected authentication requests.

The Trump administration has temporarily halted deportation flights to the Democratic Republic of Congo as the country faces a widening Ebola outbreak, according to reporting by Politico. The pause reflects concerns that the operations could expose Immigration and Customs Enforcement personnel to the virus.

However, public health experts question whether the measure will meaningfully prevent disease spread. The decision comes as Congo struggles with an active Ebola epidemic that has prompted health authorities to restrict travel and movement in affected regions.

The highly infectious disease causes severe hemorrhagic fever and spreads through direct contact with bodily fluids of infected individuals. The virus has a high fatality rate when left untreated. ICE deportation flights require close contact between officers and deportees during processing, transportation, and handoff to receiving country authorities. Such proximity in enclosed aircraft cabins could theoretically create transmission opportunities.

The situation has created complications for some deportees. At least one woman has been left in limbo after officials moved her to Kinshasa, the capital of the DRC. The administration now says it will not bring her back because of the Ebola travel ban, despite a judge's order for her return.

The pause comes as the Trump administration faces increased scrutiny over its deportation flight operations. A group of 40 House Democrats recently sent a letter to the Federal Aviation Administration expressing "grave concerns" about what they describe as a secretive program involving mistreatment and inhumane conditions on ICE charter jets.

The lawmakers demand transparency over ICE's expanded use of commercial airliners to transfer detained immigrants. They point to "credible reports" that individuals have been placed on flights without notice to counsel or family members, effectively disappearing from public view when flights are shielded from tracking systems.

"Families are left searching for their loved ones, and attorneys are denied meaningful opportunities to intervene, raising serious due process concerns," the letter states. The letter references a Guardian investigation based on leaked flight data that revealed the Trump administration transported detained immigrants in ways that routinely violated constitutional rights.

The letter also raises questions about conditions on ICE flights, including the controversial use of full body restraints during deportation flights. The lawmakers ask the FAA to provide detailed information on how the agency assesses humanitarian conditions on these flights and how restraints affect evacuation and emergency procedures.

The Trump administration has previously defended its detention practices, describing them as "long-standing, standard ICE protocol" designed to ensure safety. The administration has also rejected claims of hidden or weaponized flights as "categorically false."

Human rights groups tracking ICE operations documented an 84 percent increase in deportation flights during 2025 compared to 2024.
