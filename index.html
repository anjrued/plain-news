2:44 PM ET

Firefighters in Orange County, California say they have eliminated the threat of a massive explosion from a damaged chemical storage tank, though tens of thousands of residents remain under evacuation orders. The overnight operation involved cooling and monitoring procedures to stabilize the overheating tank after it cracked over the weekend.

The federal government has approved an emergency declaration for Orange County, allowing federal resources to support the local response effort. Authorities worked through the night to secure the tank and prevent a potential explosion that initially triggered widespread evacuations across the region.

The cracked tank had raised serious concerns about public safety in the area. Residents were forced to leave their homes as officials assessed the risk and implemented containment procedures. Volunteers from San Diego mobilized to assist families displaced by the mandatory evacuation orders.

While the immediate threat of explosion has been addressed through the cooling and monitoring operation, officials have not yet announced when residents will be permitted to return to their homes. The state of emergency remains in effect as authorities continue managing the chemical leak and ensure the area is safe for reoccupation.

The nature of the chemical stored in the tank and the cause of the crack have not been disclosed in available reports. Orange County officials continue to monitor the situation closely as cleanup and remediation efforts proceed.

The response effort has involved coordination between local, state, and federal agencies. This comprehensive approach has enabled authorities to address the immediate danger while working toward a full resolution of the chemical leak situation.

Families displaced by the evacuation orders face ongoing uncertainty about when they can safely return to their homes and resume normal life. Community organizations and volunteers have stepped up to provide assistance to those affected, offering support during the displacement period.

The incident adds to California's emergency management challenges as the state continues to address various disaster scenarios. The successful elimination of the explosion threat represents a significant development in the emergency response, though lingering concerns about public safety justify the continued evacuation precautions.

Authorities continue to work on fully resolving the chemical leak while protecting the health and safety of residents in the affected area. The federal emergency declaration ensures that additional resources and support remain available to local officials managing the crisis.

According to reports, the proposed agreement would provide Iran with sanctions relief and access to approximately $20 billion in frozen assets in exchange for reopening the Strait of Hormuz and committing to negotiate nuclear issues over 60 days beginning in early June in Pakistan. At least $6 billion of the frozen assets are held in Qatar. A key sticking point involves conditioning the release of those assets on Iran's agreement to hand over enriched uranium.

The deal would also require Iran and the United States, along with their allies, to cease military operations. Israel would need to end its offensive in Lebanon as part of the agreement.

Iranian officials said the proposed deal still requires clarification on one or two clauses before it can be sent to Iran's supreme leader for ratification. Foreign ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei stated: "It is correct to say that we have reached a conclusion on a large portion of the issues under discussion. But to say that this means the signing of an agreement is imminent, no one can make such a claim."

Trump linked any potential agreement to expanding the Abraham Accords, urging countries in the region to normalize relations with Israel. On Saturday, he spoke with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to address ceasefire terms. Netanyahu responded that Trump reaffirmed Israel's right to defend itself "on every front, including Lebanon," though Iran has insisted the ceasefire must apply across all theaters.

The Iranian government appeared optimistic about the negotiations. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said: "What has guaranteed the preservation and stability of the country is the solidarity and empathy of the people."

Brent crude futures, the global oil benchmark, fell 6% to $97.43 a barrel, reaching their lowest level in two weeks. The decline reflects investor optimism that a potential agreement could reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a cri...

12:30 PM ET

Oil prices dropped sharply on Monday as markets reacted to progress in diplomatic talks between the US and Iran aimed at ending their three-month conflict. Brent crude futures, the global oil benchmark, fell 6% to $97.43 a barrel, reaching their lowest level in two weeks.

The decline reflects investor optimism that a potential agreement could reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global energy supplies that has been effectively closed since the US and Israel launched missile strikes on Tehran on February 28. The strait's de facto closure sent energy prices soaring, and its reopening would ease the geopolitical tensions constraining oil supply.

However, cautionary notes tempered market enthusiasm. An Iranian government spokesperson said an agreement was "not imminent," despite progress on many issues. The two sides remain at odds over several key matters, including management of the strait and the terms of any sanctions relief.

Warren Patterson, head of commodities strategy at ING, told Reuters the market should remain cautious. "We've been at this stage before, only for talks to break down," he said. Even if negotiations succeed and the strait reopens, analysts expect a return to normal oil flows will take months while damaged energy infrastructure in Qatar and other locations is repaired.

Despite Monday's decline, oil prices remain substantially higher than before the conflict began, when Brent crude traded around $70 per barrel. Investment bank Barclays maintained its forecast for average Brent crude prices of $100 this year, though it acknowledged upside risks.

Stock markets responded positively to the diplomatic developments. Japan's Nikkei index rose nearly 3%, while Europe's Stoxx 600 climbed 1%. Many major markets were closed on Monday for public holidays. The dollar dipped 0.3% against a basket of major currencies, while gold climbed 1.46% to $4,574 per ounce. Treasury bond futures also rallied.

The market reaction reflects relief that the world's most important energy chokepoint may soon return to normal operations. Stephen Innes, an independent analyst, noted that investors had priced substantial inflation fears into markets during the energy shock. The prospect of renewed oil flows offers hope that some of those price pressures could ease.

The geopolitical situation has weighed heavily on inflation expectations worldwide. Higher oil and gas costs, along with increased prices for materials like fertilizer, have driven up expectations for interest rate increases from central banks. Before the war, markets had anticipated rate cuts, but that sentiment has reversed.

Physical flows through the strait remain restricted. Data showed two liquefied natural gas tankers exiting Monday toward Pakistan and China, while a supertanker carrying Iraqi crude departed the Gulf for China after being stranded for nearly three months, according to shipping information cited by Reuters.

Suarez's win marked his third career Cup Series victory and ended an 82-race winless streak. The driver, who previously competed part-time in NASCAR's truck series for Busch's team in 2015 and 2016, paid tribute to his close friend and mentor following the race. The victory came courtesy of strategic pit work and challenging weather conditions. The race proceeded despite Busch's passing, with drivers and teams choosing to honor him through competition. NASCAR organized a tribute to the late champion before Sunday's event, ensuring the sport acknowledged his significant contributions as both a driver and team owner.

Kyle Larson finished fifth in the rain-affected competition, while William Byron placed ninth. The results reflected the difficult conditions that shaped the final outcome.

Suarez's connection to Busch added particular significance to the outcome. Having been mentored by Busch earlier in his career, Suarez represented a direct link to the late owner's influence on developing NASCAR talent. The victory thus carried meaning beyond typical competitive achievement, representing a tribute from someone Busch had helped cultivate as a driver.

The Coca-Cola 600 traditionally ranks among NASCAR's longest and most prestigious races, typically held over Memorial Day weekend. This year's running transformed into both a sporting event and a moment of collective remembrance for the racing community.

Spire Motorsports, which fielded Suarez's entry in partnership with Kyle Busch Motorsports, celebrated the emotional victory. The team had prepared for the race before Busch's death fundamentally altered the nature of the weekend's significance.

An estimated 95,000 spectators attended the event at Charlotte Motor Speedway, witnessing what became a historic moment in NASCAR honoring one of the sport's most influential figures.

The first theatrical film based on the Mandalorian streaming series earned $102 million at the domestic box office and $165 million globally during its Memorial Day weekend opening, capturing the top spot at the box office despite marking Disney's weakest Star Wars film debut since acquiring Lucasfilm.

The film's opening represents the lowest performance for a Star Wars theatrical release under Disney's ownership. It fell short of even Solo: A Star Wars Story, which opened on Memorial Day weekend in 2018 with $103 million domestically. That earlier Star Wars spin-off earned $171 million globally in its opening weekend, outpacing The Mandalorian and Grogu's international performance despite similar domestic figures.

The film continues the story of the armored bounty hunter and his young companion Grogu, introduced in the Disney+ streaming series that debuted in 2019. The Mandalorian has become the most-watched original series on Disney+. Grogu, initially referred to as "Baby Yoda" before his name was revealed, has generated significant cultural impact and commercial success, with more than 13 million Grogu toys sold since the show launched.

Industry analyst David Gross told Agence France-Presse that while the opening numbers are substantial, they reflect typical spin-off performance rather than primary Star Wars film levels. "The movie is a piece of the story, and it's earning a piece of the business," Gross explained.

The modest opening may signal continued Star Wars fatigue among audiences. This represents the first Star Wars theatrical release since The Rise of Skywalker in 2019, following Disney's decision to halt new Star Wars films after concluding they had released too many too quickly. The company subsequently shifted focus toward streaming content for the franchise.

The film's $165 million budget appears more manageable than previous Star Wars productions, potentially positioning it for profitability despite the underwhelming opening. Solo, by contrast, required a $275 million budget and became widely regarded as the franchise's first theatrical flop despite its higher global opening weekend earnings.

Despite mixed critical reception, Disney reportedly expects the film to sustain its box office performance through coming weekends based on positive audience scores. The studio views the theatrical release as part of a broader strategy encompassing merchandise sales, theme park attractions, and its streaming platform.

The Mandalorian streaming series has succeeded where other recent Star Wars+ shows have struggled. While Andor achieved both critical and commercial success, series including The Book of Boba Fett, Obi-Wan Kenobi, and The Acolyte failed to generate significant audience engagement.

Disney has Star Wars: Starfighter scheduled for theatrical release in May 2027, which will star Ryan Gosling. The franchise, which debuted in 1977, remains one of cinema's highest-grossing properties with over $10 billion in global ticket sales.

Governor Jared Polis reduced the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former county clerk convicted of crimes related to election security breaches, prompting a censure from the Colorado Democratic Party and intense criticism from state election officials.

Peters, who served as clerk in Mesa County in western Colorado, was convicted of four felonies and three misdemeanors in 2024. She allowed unauthorized people, including a former pro surfer named Conan Hayes, to access the county's voting equipment and copy it. Hayes is affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, a prominent figure in spreading false claims about the 2020 election.

A judge originally sentenced Peters to eight and a half years in prison plus six months in jail. An appeals court overturned that sentence in April and ordered reconsideration. Polis then commuted her sentence to about four and a half years, making her eligible for parole release on June 1.

In his clemency letter, Polis wrote: "This is an extremely unusual and lengthy sentence for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes." He later compared Peters' case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for submitting forged letters to the legislature. Polis said both cases had overlapping felony counts and that justice should be applied evenly.

Election officials rejected this comparison. Jena Griswold, Colorado's secretary of state and a Democrat, stated: "Peters organized the breach of the election equipment, broke the public trust and attacked the very foundations of our democratic process." She said the clemency "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president" and warned it would "embolden the election denial movement across the country."

Griswold learned of the decision only 45 minutes before it became public. She emphasized that Peters should have waited for resentencing through the courts rather than receiving "special treatment" through clemency.

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, said the group was "furious, disgusted, and deeply disappointed." He added that election officials across the country were receiving signals that "it's open season on our elections and election officials."

When Peters was originally sentenced in 2024, Judge Matthew Barrett told her: "You are no hero. You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again."

Peters' case has received significant attention from Trump, who has repeatedly called for her release and previously issued a federal pardon, though it had limited effect since her convictions are state crimes. Sensitive passwords and information from Mesa County's voting equipment later appeared online through rightwing figures.

The censure represents a significant public rebuke from Polis' own party during an election year.
