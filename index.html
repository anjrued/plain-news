Yesterday, 11:14 PM ET

Crews in Orange County, California are working to relieve pressure in an overheated chemical storage tank that officials warn could fail, forcing 50,000 residents to remain evacuated. The EPA chief stated the tank will fail if the pressure cannot be managed.

Authorities discovered a crack in the tank and are investigating whether pressure has been relieved. Officials described the response efforts as taking a new trajectory as teams work to prevent a larger release of chemicals into surrounding areas.

The evacuation has displaced tens of thousands of residents, with volunteers from San Diego traveling to Orange County to assist affected families. Earlier reports that FEMA was offering hotel stays to evacuees were incorrect, according to a correction issued by the Orange County Register.

The chemical tank became overheated, creating pressure that threatens the integrity of the storage facility. Officials have not provided a timeline for when residents might be able to return to their homes, as crews continue to monitor the situation and work to stabilize the tank.

The incident represents one of the largest chemical emergency evacuations in recent California history, affecting multiple communities across Orange County. Emergency response teams from multiple agencies are coordinating the containment effort as authorities work to prevent environmental contamination and protect public health in the affected areas.

Yesterday, 10:37 PM ET

Russia launched a devastating assault on Kyiv and surrounding areas early Monday, deploying hundreds of drones and dozens of missiles in what Ukrainian officials characterized as among the war's most intense bombardments of the capital.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy reported that the attack involved approximately 600 drones and 90 missiles of various types, including 36 ballistic missiles. He noted that not all ballistic missiles were intercepted, with Kyiv sustaining the highest concentration of strikes. "Kyiv was the primary target of this Russian attack," Zelenskyy wrote on social media.

The assault marked the third use of Russia's Oreshnik hypersonic ballistic missile in the conflict. Russia's defense ministry confirmed deploying the Oreshnik alongside Iskander, Kinzhal and Zircon missiles, claiming the strikes targeted Ukrainian military command facilities and airbases. The ministry characterized the attack as retaliation for Ukrainian strikes on Russian territory.

The human toll was significant. At least four people died across the Kyiv region, with about 100 injured overall. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko reported two deaths and 56 wounded in the capital itself, while officials in the surrounding region confirmed two additional deaths and nine wounded based on preliminary figures.

The damage extended across the city's infrastructure and cultural landmarks. The strike destroyed a market where vendors had worked for decades, damaged a water supply facility, and affected dozens of residential buildings and several schools. A fire broke out at one school, and another strike trapped people in a business center shelter.

Ukraine's National Art Museum sustained damage to its structure, including broken windows and shattered glass, though the culture ministry reported the collection itself remained intact. The foreign ministry building, which survived World War II, was lightly damaged from nearby explosions for the first time since the second world war. Government headquarters also sustained damage, with windows blown out, though no injuries occurred.

Residents described harrowing experiences. One woman who had worked at the destroyed market for 22 years told reporters she was leaving Kyiv. A 74-year-old man recounted how explosions threw him and his dog backward, destroying his apartment.

International leaders condemned the strikes. French President Emmanuel Macron said the use of the Oreshnik missile signaled Russia had reached "the dead end of its war of aggression." German Chancellor Friedrich Merz called it a "reckless escalation." EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas described the assault as "deliberate strikes on city centres" and characterized the Oreshnik use as "political scare tactic and reckless nuclear brinkmanship."

The attack followed Putin's vow to retaliate for a drone strike on a student dormitory in Russian-controlled Luhansk. Ukraine denied responsibility, claiming it had struck a drone command unit instead.

President Donald Trump indicated that talks could resume in Islamabad within two days, prompting optimism among market participants about a potential resolution.

The price decline reflects investor expectations that a peace deal could lead to reopening the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping channel that has been nearly closed since Iran responded to US-Israeli military operations by restricting access. The waterway is essential for global energy transport, with roughly one-fifth of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas passing through it.

Previous rounds of negotiations in Islamabad lasted 21 hours before ending over disagreements on Iran's nuclear program. US Vice President JD Vance departed the talks, stating that Iran had not made a firm commitment to abandon nuclear weapons development. The key sticking point involved the duration of uranium enrichment suspension, with the US delegation demanding a 20-year moratorium while Iran offered less than 10 years.

Crude prices dipped to around $95 per barrel following reports of potential renewed negotiations. Earlier in the conflict, oil had spiked well above $100 per barrel due to Strait of Hormuz disruptions. The US declared a naval blockade on Iranian ports and has suspended temporary sanctions relief that was previously intended to ease wartime energy supply shocks.

Asian stock markets largely gained on the news, with Japan's Nikkei 225 reaching a record high above 65,000 points. The rally reflected investor relief that lower oil prices could ease inflation concerns and reduce energy costs.

The US dollar weakened as traders adjusted positions based on expectations that reduced geopolitical tensions could alter the broader economic outlook. Trump praised Pakistan's army chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir, for his mediation efforts, describing him as doing a "great job" in arranging talks.

However, uncertainties remain. A Pakistani official cautioned that negotiations might require longer than Trump suggested before resuming. Iran's negotiating team has insisted that Vance lead future US discussions, expressing distrust in Trump's special envoy Steve Witkoff and his son-in-law Jared Kushner as reliable negotiating partners.

Analysts cautioned that even a quick agreement may not immediately resolve economic consequences of the conflict. Energy markets have experienced sustained volatility throughout the Iran war due to ongoing uncertainty about oil supply routes and the conflict's broader impact on regional stability. The actual reopening of the Strait of Hormuz depends on the outcome of continuing negotiations, and no formal agreement has yet been reached despite recent diplomatic movement.

Bungie announced it will end development on Destiny 2 and conduct layoffs, according to Bloomberg. The studio posted a statement titled "Every End is a New Beginning" on its website confirming the decision.

More than 120,000 fans have signed a petition calling on Sony to greenlight Destiny 3, but the studio has not announced plans for a sequel. Bungie operates under Sony ownership following its acquisition.

The layoffs follow the conclusion of active development on Destiny 2, which launched in 2017 and received regular expansions and updates over nearly nine years. The game attracted millions of players across consoles and PC.

Fans reacted to the news on social media, with BBC reporting players expressed being "at a loss for words." The petition requesting Destiny 3 development gained significant support within hours of the announcement.

Bungie has not disclosed how many employees will be affected by the layoffs or what percentage of its workforce the cuts represent. The studio also has not commented on whether any staff will transition to other projects.

The decision ends one of the longest-running live service games in the industry. Destiny 2 built on the foundation of the original Destiny, which launched in 2014. Together, the franchise spanned more than a decade of continuous content releases.

Sony acquired Bungie in 2022 for $3.6 billion. The deal allowed Bungie to remain an independent subsidiary and continue self-publishing its games. Sony has not issued a statement about the studio's restructuring or future plans.

Bungie previously developed the Halo series for Microsoft before becoming independent in 2007. The studio announced it was working on a new intellectual property called Marathon, a revival of its 1990s franchise, though no release date has been set for that project.

The late overtake capped a dramatic finish at Indianapolis Motor Speedway, where Rosenqvist managed to edge past Malukas in the race's closing moments. The margin of victory set a new record for the tightest finish in the storied event's 110-year history.

Rosenqvist's victory marks a significant achievement at one of motorsport's most prestigious races. The Swedish driver executed his winning pass after holding close to Malukas throughout the final laps of the 500-mile race.

Malukas, who had been in position to win his first Indianapolis 500, was passed just before the checkered flag. The tight finish provided one of the most dramatic conclusions to the race in recent memory.

The previous closest finish in Indianapolis 500 history had stood for years before Sunday's race. The exact margin between Rosenqvist and Malukas was measured in fractions of a second, though the precise distance was not immediately specified.

The race featured multiple drivers competing for the lead throughout the day at the 2.5-mile oval track. Rosenqvist's timing on the final lap proved decisive in securing his first victory at the Brickyard.

The Indianapolis 500 remains one of the most watched single-day sporting events in the United States. Sunday's race drew attention for its historic finish, with Rosenqvist managing to complete the pass when it mattered most.

For Malukas, the second-place finish represents both a strong performance and a near miss at what would have been his first Indianapolis 500 victory. The driver came within one position of winning the race after running at the front during the final laps.

Rosenqvist's victory adds his name to the list of Indianapolis 500 champions and gives him one of motorsport's most coveted titles. The win came in his latest attempt at the race after previous starts at the speedway.

The Mandalorian and Grogu claimed the top spot at the box office but achieved the lowest opening weekend for a Star Wars film since Disney acquired the franchise. The film earned $102 million domestically during the four-day Memorial Day weekend in North America and Canada, bringing its global total to $165 million.

Despite being adapted from Disney's popular streaming series, the film underperformed compared to previous Star Wars theatrical releases under Disney ownership. It failed to match even Solo: A Star Wars Story, which Disney released in 2018 and widely considered a box office disappointment. Solo opened with $103 million domestically and earned $171 million globally during its opening weekend, surpassing The Mandalorian and Grogu's performance.

The film stars Pedro Pascal as Din Djarin, the helmeted bounty hunter, alongside the character known as "Baby Yoda" or Grogu, who became a cultural phenomenon during the streaming series. The Mandalorian premiered on Disney+ in 2019 and became one of the platform's most-watched original programs, with merchandise sales exceeding 13 million Grogu toys since the show's debut.

Industry analyst David Gross noted that while the numbers represent significant box office earnings, "these are big numbers and this is how spin-offs perform." He explained that the film is earning "a piece of the business" as part of a larger Star Wars story.

The underperformance may signal continued Star Wars fatigue among audiences. Disney halted new Star Wars theatrical releases after The Rise of Skywalker in 2019, concluding the studio had released films too frequently. The Mandalorian and Grogu marks the first Star Wars film to reach cinemas since that decision.

Disney executives believe the film will hold steady in coming weeks based on positive audience reception, despite receiving mixed critical responses. The studio aims to leverage the film's popularity to drive viewers toward other Disney products, including theme park experiences, Disney+ subscriptions, and merchandise.

The Star Wars franchise remains among cinema's highest-grossing series, with over $10 billion in global ticket sales since its 1977 debut. Disney purchased Lucasfilm from creator George Lucas in 2012 and initially focused on theatrical releases before shifting emphasis toward Disney+ streaming content. While streaming shows like Andor and The Mandalorian have achieved critical acclaim and strong viewership, others including The Book of Boba Fett, Obi-Wan Kenobi, and The Acolyte failed to generate significant momentum.

Looking forward, Disney plans to release Star Wars: Starfighter, starring Ryan Gosling, in May 2027.

May 21, 10:12 AM ET

The Colorado Democratic Party voted to censure Governor Jared Polis over his decision to commute the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former county election official whose case has become central to Republican efforts to overturn the 2020 presidential election.

Polis reduced Peters' nearly nine-year sentence to approximately four and a half years, allowing her release on parole starting June 1. Peters, the former clerk in Mesa County in western Colorado, was convicted of four felonies and three misdemeanors in 2024. She had allowed unauthorized access to county voting equipment in 2021, permitting a former pro surfer named Conan Hayes to copy voting systems and attend a sensitive software upgrade.

In his clemency letter, Polis wrote that Peters' sentence was "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes." He later compared her case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for submitting forged letters to the legislature, arguing that Colorado's justice system should apply sentences evenly.

Colorado's top election official Jena Griswold, a Democrat, strongly rejected this comparison. She stated that Peters "organized the breach of the election equipment, broke the public trust and attacked the very foundations of our democratic process." Griswold warned that the clemency would embolden election denial movements across the country and signal that "it's open season on our elections and election officials."

The judge who originally sentenced Peters in 2024 was equally critical. Judge Matthew Barrett told Peters: "You are no hero. You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again." An appeals court overturned that sentence in April, requiring reconsideration.

Hayes is affiliated with Mike Lindell, the MyPillow CEO and prominent figure in spreading misinformation about the 2020 election. Sensitive passwords and voting system information from Mesa County's Dominion equipment were later published online by rightwing personalities.

Donald Trump repeatedly called for Peters' release, posting on Truth Social that it was "hard to wish her a Happy New Year" while she served time for "trying to stop the massive voter fraud." Trump issued a federal pardon to Peters, though it was largely symbolic since her convictions are state crimes.

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, said the group was "furious, disgusted, and deeply disappointed by the governor's decision." He emphasized concerns that the clemency signals danger to election officials nationwide.

The censure represents a formal rebuke from Colorado Democrats against their own governor, highlighting deep party disagreement over the clemency decision.

2:00 AM ET

The Colorado Democratic Party voted to censure Governor Jared Polis over his decision to commute the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former county election official whose case has become central to Republican efforts to overturn the 2020 presidential election.

Polis reduced Peters' nearly nine-year sentence to approximately four and a half years, allowing her release on parole starting June 1. Peters, the former clerk in Mesa County in western Colorado, was convicted of four felonies and three misdemeanors in 2024. She had allowed unauthorized access to county voting equipment in 2021, permitting a former pro surfer named Conan Hayes to copy voting systems and attend a sensitive software upgrade.

In his clemency letter, Polis wrote that Peters' sentence was "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes." He later compared her case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for submitting forged letters to the legislature, arguing that Colorado's justice system should apply sentences evenly.

Colorado's top election official Jena Griswold, a Democrat, strongly rejected this comparison. She stated that Peters "organized the breach of the election equipment, broke the public trust and attacked the very foundations of our democratic process." Griswold warned that the clemency would embolden election denial movements across the country and signal that "it's open season on our elections and election officials."

The judge who originally sentenced Peters in 2024 was equally critical. Judge Matthew Barrett told Peters: "You are no hero. You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again." An appeals court overturned that sentence in April, requiring reconsideration.

Hayes is affiliated with Mike Lindell, the MyPillow CEO and prominent figure in spreading misinformation about the 2020 election. Sensitive passwords and voting system information from Mesa County's Dominion equipment were later published online by rightwing personalities.

Donald Trump repeatedly called for Peters' release, posting on Truth Social that it was "hard to wish her a Happy New Year" while she served time for "trying to stop the massive voter fraud." Trump issued a federal pardon to Peters, though it was largely symbolic since her convictions are state crimes.

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, said the group was "furious, disgusted, and deeply disappointed by the governor's decision." He emphasized concerns that the clemency signals danger to election officials nationwide.

The censure represents a formal rebuke from Colorado Democrats against their own governor, highlighting deep party disagreement over the clemency decision.

Malukas, who had been in position to win his first Indianapolis 500, was passed just before the checkered flag. The tight finish provided one of the most dramatic conclusions to the race in recent memory.

The previous closest finish in Indianapolis 500 history had stood for years before Sunday's race. The exact margin between Rosenqvist and Malukas was measured in fractions of a second, though the precise distance was not immediately specified.

The race featured multiple drivers competing for the lead throughout the day at the 2.5-mile oval track. Rosenqvist's timing on the final lap proved decisive in securing his first victory at the Brickyard.

The Indianapolis 500 remains one of the most watched single-day sporting events in the United States. Sunday's race drew attention for its historic finish, with Rosenqvist managing to complete the pass when it mattered most.

For Malukas, the second-place finish represents both a strong performance and a near miss at what would have been his first Indianapolis 500 victory. The driver came within one position of winning the race after running at the front during the final laps.

Rosenqvist's victory adds his name to the list of Indianapolis 500 champions and gives him one of motorsport's most coveted titles. The win came in his latest attempt at the race after previous starts at the speedway.

It failed to match even Solo: A Star Wars Story, which Disney released in 2018 and widely considered a box office disappointment. Solo opened with $103 million domestically and earned $171 million globally during its opening weekend, surpassing The Mandalorian and Grogu's performance.

The film stars Pedro Pascal as Din Djarin, the helmeted bounty hunter, alongside the character known as "Baby Yoda" or Grogu, who became a cultural phenomenon during the streaming series. The Mandalorian premiered on Disney+ in 2019 and became one of the platform's most-watched original programs, with merchandise sales exceeding 13 million Grogu toys since the show's debut.

Industry analyst David Gross noted that while the numbers represent significant box office earnings, "these are big numbers and this is how spin-offs perform." He explained that the film is earning "a piece of the business" as part of a larger Star Wars story.

The underperformance may signal continued Star Wars fatigue among audiences. Disney halted new Star Wars theatrical releases after The Rise of Skywalker in 2019, concluding the studio had released films too frequently. The Mandalorian and Grogu marks the first Star Wars film to reach cinemas since that decision.

Disney executives believe the film will hold steady in coming weeks based on positive audience reception, despite receiving mixed critical responses. The studio aims to leverage the film's popularity to drive viewers toward other Disney products, including theme park experiences, Disney+ subscriptions, and merchandise.

The Star Wars franchise remains among cinema's highest-grossing series, with over $10 billion in global ticket sales since its 1977 debut. Disney purchased Lucasfilm from creator George Lucas in 2012 and initially focused on theatrical releases before shifting emphasis toward Disney+ streaming content. While streaming shows like Andor and The Mandalorian have achieved critical acclaim and strong viewership, others including The Book of Boba Fett, Obi-Wan Kenobi, and The Acolyte failed to generate significant momentum.

Looking forward, Disney plans to release Star Wars: Starfighter, starring Ryan Gosling, in May 2027.
