A few minutes ago

A crack discovered in an overheated chemical storage tank in Garden Grove, California, represents a potential turning point in a developing crisis that has displaced approximately 50,000 residents. Firefighters and emergency officials view the crack as a positive development that may relieve dangerous pressure buildup inside the vessel.

The incident began Thursday afternoon at GKN Aerospace, an aircraft parts manufacturer located about 5 miles from Disneyland. The facility's chemical storage tank contained between 6,000 and 7,000 gallons of methyl methacrylate, a highly flammable chemical used in plastic and resin production. When the tank began overheating, authorities implemented a massive evacuation of surrounding areas due to explosion concerns.

Lee Zeldin, the EPA administrator, stated on Sunday that the tank "will fail," but emphasized that different failure scenarios exist. He explained that the most catastrophic possibility would be an explosion powerful enough to trigger explosions in nearby tanks. However, Zeldin noted that officials believe a "low-volume release" of chemicals is the most likely outcome.

Crews have worked continuously to cool the tank by spraying it with water and keeping temperatures below 85 degrees Fahrenheit. On Friday, officials added a neutralizing agent to a nearby tank to further reduce explosion risks.

The discovery of the crack late Saturday night offered some encouragement. According to Orange County Fire Chief TJ McGovern, the crack could be relieving pressure within the tank, making an explosion less probable. An engineering professor from Purdue University compared the situation to a heated soda can, noting that a hole allows contents to release gradually rather than explode violently.

However, experts urge caution. A chemical engineering department head from Texas A&M University warned that while cooling efforts are underway, chemical reactions may still be occurring deep inside the tank. He cautioned that risks of an explosive release remain, even if reactions have slowed.

Authorities have been monitoring air quality in the region and reported no detected pollutants as of Sunday. Garden Grove Mayor Stephanie Klopfenstein emphasized the seriousness of the situation and urged residents to follow evacuation orders.

Evacuation centers throughout Orange County are operating near maximum capacity, straining local resources. The prolonged displacement has left families uncertain about when they can return home. Officials tied the timeline for residents' return to whether the crack successfully relieves pressure and whether the tank can be stabilized without releasing hazardous chemicals.

At least two lawsuits have been filed against GKN Aerospace by residents facing evacuation orders, shelter-in-place directives, and concerns about chemical exposure. Methyl methacrylate exposure can cause serious respiratory problems, neurological damage, and irritate skin, eyes, and throat.

Emergency management officials continue coordinating with state and federal agencies as the crisis develops.

Russia carried out a large-scale assault on Kyiv and surrounding areas, deploying hundreds of drones and dozens of missiles including the advanced Oreshnik hypersonic system. The attack killed at least four people and injured approximately 100 across the country, with Kyiv bearing the brunt of the bombardment.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed that Russia used about 600 drones and 90 missiles of various types during the strike, including 36 ballistic missiles. He noted that not all ballistic missiles were intercepted, with Kyiv sustaining the greatest number of direct hits. "It is important that this does not pass without consequences for Russia," Zelenskyy wrote on social media.

The Oreshnik missile, capable of traveling over 10 times the speed of sound and carrying nuclear or conventional warheads, was used for the third time in the conflict. Russia's defense ministry confirmed the weapon's deployment and claimed it targeted Ukrainian military command facilities, airbases and military enterprises. The ministry framed the attack as retaliation for Ukrainian strikes on Russian territory.

The assault caused widespread damage across Kyiv. According to Mayor Vitali Klitschko, two people died and 56 were wounded in the capital, with damage recorded in every district. A school fire and people trapped in a business center shelter were among the immediate consequences reported.

Critical infrastructure sustained significant damage. Russian strikes hit a water supply facility and destroyed a market where workers had been employed for decades. One market employee told the Associated Press that everything had burned down and she was leaving Kyiv permanently. The National Art Museum, which houses one of Ukraine's largest collections, was damaged, though initial reports indicated the collection itself was not harmed. Ukraine's foreign ministry building, damaged for the first time since World War II, and government headquarters with blown-out windows also sustained hits.

Witnesses described the attack as unprecedented in scale. A 74-year-old Kyiv resident said he rushed to save his dog when explosions began, only to be thrown back by shock waves. His apartment was destroyed despite surviving the blast.

Ukraine's culture minister said Russia was "systematically attacking civilian infrastructure and cultural institutions" in an attempt to intimidate and destroy Ukrainian identity. The foreign minister characterized the targeting of historic areas as evidence of barbarism rather than civilized conduct.

International leaders condemned the assault. French President Emmanuel Macron said the use of the Oreshnik signaled Russia hitting a dead end militarily. The EU's foreign policy chief called it a "political scare tactic and reckless nuclear brinkmanship," announcing that EU foreign ministers would discuss increasing pressure on Russia.

The attack followed Putin's vow to retaliate after a drone strike on a student dormitory in Russian-controlled Luhansk, which Ukraine denied conducting.

Oil prices fell sharply on Monday as signals emerged that the United States and Iran are moving closer to a peace agreement that could reopen the Strait of Hormuz to shipping traffic.

Prices dropped roughly 6% in Asian trading, according to Reuters, while multiple outlets reported ships beginning to move toward the strategic waterway. President Trump said peace talks on the Iran conflict are proceeding, though the actual reopening of the strait remains uncertain.

The Strait of Hormuz is a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments, and its closure during the conflict has disrupted supply chains and elevated energy prices worldwide. Any agreement to reopen the passage would likely ease those pressures.

The price decline reflects investor expectations that increased oil flow through Hormuz could soon resume. Asian markets responded positively to the diplomatic progress, with Tokyo's Nikkei 225 hitting a record high above 65,000 for the first time.

The New York Times reported Sunday that the reopening of the strait still carries uncertainty despite the optimistic signals. No specific timeline for resuming shipping traffic has been announced.

The oil price movement represents one of the sharpest single-day drops in recent months. Brent crude and West Texas Intermediate both saw significant declines as traders repositioned based on the peace talk developments.

The US dollar also weakened on the news, while stock futures reached record levels. The combination of falling oil prices and rising equities suggests investors believe a resolution to the conflict could support broader economic growth.

Multiple news services characterized the signals as mixed, indicating that while progress appears real, significant obstacles may remain before a final agreement takes effect. The pace of ships moving toward Hormuz and statements from US officials have driven market optimism despite the lack of a formal announcement.

The reasons Bungie never started work on Destiny 3 remain tied to its live service model for Destiny 2, which focused on ongoing content rather than a full sequel. That approach now appears to be ending as the studio restructures.

The layoffs add to broader challenges in the gaming industry, where multiple studios have reduced staff in recent months. Bungie's decision to stop developing new content for one of its major franchises represents a notable retreat from its previous strategy of sustained support for the title.

Yesterday, 11:45 PM ET

The Mandalorian and Grogu claimed the top spot at the box office but achieved the weakest opening weekend for a Star Wars film since Disney acquired the franchise. The movie earned $102 million domestically over the Memorial Day weekend and $165 million globally, falling short of even Solo: A Star Wars Story, which Disney previously considered its lowest-performing theatrical Star Wars release.

Solo opened on the same Memorial Day weekend in 2018 with $103 million domestically and $171 million globally. Even adjusting for inflation, Solo's opening significantly exceeded The Mandalorian and Grogu's performance. Solo ultimately grossed $392.9 million worldwide but was branded a failure due to its enormous $275 million budget. By contrast, The Mandalorian and Grogu carries a more modest $165 million budget, which may help it achieve profitability despite the weak opening.

The film marks the first Star Wars theatrical release since The Rise of Skywalker in 2019. Disney paused Star Wars film production after concluding it had released too many movies too quickly, choosing instead to focus on streaming content through Disney Plus.

Industry analyst David Gross told Agence France-Presse that while the numbers are substantial, they reflect typical performance for Star Wars spin-offs rather than primary franchise entries. "The movie is a piece of the story, and it's earning a piece of the business," he said.

The opening suggests potential Star Wars fatigue among theatrical audiences. The franchise has shifted significantly toward streaming, with successful shows like The Mandalorian and Andor gaining both critical acclaim and viewership on Disney Plus. However, other Star Wars streaming series including The Book of Boba Fett, Obi-Wan Kenobi, and The Acolyte have underperformed.

The Mandalorian series became one of Disney Plus's most-watched original programs since debuting in 2019. Grogu, the small alien character nicknamed "Baby Yoda," became a cultural phenomenon and merchandising success, with more than 13 million toys sold since the show's premiere.

Despite muted critical reviews, Disney expects the film to perform better in coming weeks based on positive audience scores. The studio also hopes the film will drive viewers toward other Disney products including merchandise, theme parks, and Disney Plus content.

Star Wars remains one of the highest-grossing movie franchises globally, with more than $10 billion in ticket sales since its 1977 debut. Disney acquired the franchise from creator George Lucas in 2012 for roughly $4 billion.

Disney has Star Wars: Starfighter in development, scheduled for May 2027, which will star Ryan Gosling.

May 21, 10:12 AM ET

The Colorado Democratic Party voted to censure Governor Jared Polis after he commuted the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former county clerk convicted of allowing unauthorized access to voting equipment.

Peters, the former clerk in Mesa County in western Colorado, had been serving a sentence of eight and a half years in prison. Polis reduced her sentence to approximately four and a half years, making her eligible for parole release on June 1. In his clemency letter, Polis wrote that the sentence was "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes."

Peters was found guilty in 2024 of four felonies and three misdemeanors. In 2021, she allowed a former pro surfer named Conan Hayes to access the county's voting equipment, copy it, and attend a sensitive software upgrade. Hayes is affiliated with Mike Lindell, the MyPillow CEO and prominent figure in spreading false claims about the 2020 election. Sensitive passwords and other information from the voting equipment later appeared online through right-wing personalities.

The decision sparked strong criticism from Colorado election officials. Jena Griswold, the state's top election official, said the clemency "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president." She warned that the decision would "embolden the election denial movement across the country and will leave a dark, dangerous imprint on American democracy for years to come."

Griswold emphasized that this represented special treatment rather than proportional sentencing. She noted that an appeals court had overturned Peters's original sentence in April and ordered a judge to reconsider it, but Peters will now be released through clemency instead of waiting for that judicial process.

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, said the group was "furious, disgusted, and deeply disappointed." He warned that election officials across the country are concerned the decision signals that "it's open season on our elections and election officials."

When sentencing Peters in 2024, Judge Matthew Barrett said she was "no hero" but rather "a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again."

Polis had been largely silent about potential clemency for months until March, when he compared Peters's case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for submitting forged letters to the legislature. Polis said justice should be applied evenly.

However, Griswold disputed the comparison, noting that while both faced similar felony counts, Peters's actions involved organizing the breach of election equipment and attacking democratic processes in ways that continue to be used to undermine elections.
