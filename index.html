# NASA Opens JPL Management Contract to Competition After 80 Years Under Caltech

NASA announced Thursday it will open the management contract for the Jet Propulsion Laboratory to competitive bidding, ending an 80-year exclusive partnership with the California Institute of Technology. The decision represents one of the most significant administrative changes in the storied laboratory's history.

JPL Director Laurie Leshin revealed the news in an email to employees, stating that NASA leadership informed her of the decision earlier that day. The move comes as the agency seeks to streamline operations and reduce costs while pursuing ambitious space exploration goals.

"Change like this is never easy," Leshin wrote to staff. "But I want to assure you that JPL's important work will continue."

The laboratory, located in Pasadena, California, has been managed by Caltech since its founding in the 1930s. JPL has developed and operated some of NASA's most celebrated missions, including the Mars rovers Curiosity and Perseverance, the Voyager probes now in interstellar space, and numerous other planetary exploration spacecraft.

NASA emphasized that the competition does not reflect dissatisfaction with Caltech's management. The agency said the decision aligns with broader efforts to evaluate and optimize how its field centers operate. JPL serves as NASA's lead center for robotic exploration of the solar system and employs approximately 5,500 people.

The announcement follows a difficult period for JPL. The laboratory recently completed a significant workforce reduction, laying off roughly 325 employees. Budget constraints and shifting priorities within NASA's planetary science program have created uncertainty about future mission funding.

Universities, research institutions, and private companies with experience managing large scientific and technical organizations are expected to submit proposals. The competition could bring new management approaches while maintaining JPL's core mission of planetary science and technology development.

NASA has not released details about the timeline for accepting proposals or when a new management arrangement might take effect. The agency also has not specified what criteria will be used to evaluate competing bids.

The change could affect how JPL collaborates with other NASA centers and how it plans and executes future missions. However, NASA stressed that JPL's fundamental role in space exploration will continue regardless of who manages the facility.

Caltech has managed JPL under a series of contracts renewed over the decades. The relationship has been mutually beneficial, with JPL providing research opportunities for Caltech faculty and students while drawing on the university's scientific expertise.

The decision may set a precedent for how NASA manages its other field centers. The agency operates multiple specialized facilities across the country, each with different management structures and partnership arrangements.
