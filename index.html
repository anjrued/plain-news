Yesterday, 10:56 PM ET

Donald Trump has claimed that a peace deal with Iran "has been largely negotiated" and could pave the way for ending the conflict that began in February between the US, Israel and Iran.

The US president announced on his social media platform that "final aspects and details" of a "memorandum of understanding" were still being discussed and "will be announced shortly." Trump said the Strait of Hormuz would be opened as part of the deal.

The announcement came after Trump held calls with a Pakistani mediator, Gulf allies and Israel. However, the optimism was tempered by Pakistan's prime minister expressing hopes of hosting more talks "very soon", and Iran's Fars news agency reporting that the Strait of Hormuz will remain under Iranian control.

According to Axios, the deal would include a two-month period for negotiations on Iran's nuclear program. Tehran also signaled progress on talks but indicated that the key issue of nuclear weapons is not part of an initial framework being discussed.

The development follows a period of heightened tensions in recent days. Trump posted on social media earlier warning Iran that "the Clock is Ticking, and they better get moving, FAST, or there won't be anything left of them. TIME IS OF THE ESSENCE!"

Axios reported that Trump is scheduled to meet with his national security team to discuss the next steps. The president also spoke with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu before an Israeli security cabinet meeting focused on Iran, Lebanon and Gaza. There has been growing speculation in Israel that fighting with Iran could resume if there are no signs of compromise.

The conflict, now entering its fourth month, has created significant international humanitarian, economic and geopolitical consequences. Reports suggest the Trump administration has been oscillating between strike threats and diplomatic overtures, with neither approach yielding a clear resolution thus far.

Regional tensions have escalated further, with the United Arab Emirates blaming a fire near its nuclear power plant on a drone launched by Iran or one of its proxies. The UAE described the incident as a "dangerous escalation."

The war has dragged millions into dire conditions while creating economic ripple effects felt globally. Observers have warned that the negative international fallout from this conflict could prove more permanently damaging than previous US military engagements in Iraq, Afghanistan and Vietnam.

As the situation remains fluid, questions persist about whether diplomatic efforts will succeed or if military action will resume. The coming days appear critical for determining whether the claimed progress on negotiations will translate into a lasting agreement or if the deadlock will continue.

Yesterday, 11:37 PM ET

A gunman was shot and killed after approaching a White House security checkpoint and opening fire on officers Saturday evening, according to federal officials. The incident prompted a brief lockdown of the White House while President Donald Trump was inside.

The shooting occurred shortly after 6pm local time when a man approached a checkpoint at a White House gate near 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue NW. The suspect took out a pistol and began firing at Secret Service officers stationed at the checkpoint.

Officers returned fire, striking the suspect, who was then transported to an area hospital where he later died. A bystander was also shot during the incident, though authorities said it remains unclear whether the person was hit by the suspect's initial gunfire or during the subsequent exchange between the gunman and officers.

No Secret Service personnel were injured in the shooting, and officials confirmed the president was not affected by the incident. Trump was in the Oval Office at the time of the shooting.

The sustained volley of gunshots sent journalists in the area running for cover. Selina Wang, a White House correspondent for ABC News, shared video on social media showing the moment gunfire interrupted her as she recorded a report. In the clip, Wang could be seen diving to take cover as what sounded like dozens of shots rang out. She later reported that journalists were told to sprint to the press briefing room.

Chris Flanagan, an anchor for local news channel DC News Now who took cover in the White House briefing room, reported that about 30 shots were fired. A CBS News producer estimated that at least 20 shots had been fired during the incident.

Hours after the shooting, Trump praised the swift and professional action of law enforcement officials in a social media post. He referenced the incident coming a month after a shooting at the White House Correspondents' Association dinner at a Washington hotel, adding that it demonstrates the importance of creating what he described as the most safe and secure space of its kind ever built in Washington, in an apparent reference to his ballroom plans.

The suspect has not been officially named, but multiple US media outlets reported that he had a history of mental health issues. According to reports, he had been arrested last year attempting to enter via a different White House checkpoint without authorization and had been issued with a Stay Away Order.

FBI Director Kash Patel confirmed the bureau was on the scene supporting the Secret Service response. The shooting comes less than three weeks after a separate incident in which a man brandishing a gun fired at a Secret Service officer near the route of Vice President JD Vance's motorcade outside the White House.

Yesterday, 4:00 AM ET

SpaceX has filed for an initial public offering, with documents revealing the company's focus on its Starlink satellite internet business as the primary driver of future growth. The IPO filing indicates that SpaceX views its communications network, rather than its rocket manufacturing operations, as its central business going forward.

The disclosure provides insight into the company's strategic direction under Elon Musk's leadership. While SpaceX gained prominence through its rocket development and launch services, including contracts with NASA and commercial satellite deployments, the filing positions Starlink as the company's main value proposition for potential investors.

Starlink operates a constellation of satellites in low Earth orbit to provide broadband internet service globally. The service has grown rapidly since its commercial launch, targeting both consumer markets and enterprise customers in areas with limited traditional internet infrastructure. The satellite network also serves military and government clients.

The timing of the IPO filing comes as SpaceX continues to expand its operations across multiple business lines. The company conducts regular launches from facilities in Florida and California, maintains crew and cargo transportation contracts with NASA for International Space Station missions, and pursues development of its Starship vehicle for deep space missions.

The filing documents show SpaceX emphasizing recurring revenue from Starlink subscriptions over one-time launch contracts. This business model shift reflects the company's assessment of long-term profitability prospects. Satellite internet services generate predictable monthly income, while launch contracts depend on securing individual deals with government and commercial customers.

SpaceX has not disclosed the number of shares it plans to offer or the expected price range for the IPO. The company also has not announced which stock exchange it will list on or provided a timeline for when shares will begin trading. The filing represents an initial step in the public offering process, which typically involves months of regulatory review and investor presentations before shares become available.

The company's valuation in private markets has reached levels making it one of the most valuable privately held companies. An IPO would allow early investors and employees to sell shares while providing SpaceX with access to public capital markets for future expansion.

New Glenn represents Blue Origin's entry into the commercial heavy-lift launch market. The rocket is designed to compete with SpaceX's Falcon Heavy and other large payload vehicles. The failure investigation marks a significant step in the company's path toward resuming launch operations.

Blue Origin has not publicly released details about the cause of the failure or the specific findings of the investigation. The company also has not announced a timeline for the next launch attempt.

The New Glenn rocket is named after astronaut John Glenn and is intended to serve both commercial and government customers. The vehicle features a reusable first stage, similar to SpaceX's Falcon 9 design approach. Blue Origin has been developing New Glenn for several years as part of its broader space business strategy.

The completion of the failure investigation is a procedural requirement before the company can proceed with future launches. Regulatory approvals and technical reviews typically follow such investigations in the commercial space industry.

Blue Origin, founded by Jeff Bezos, operates separately from his other business ventures. The company has previously conducted successful suborbital flights with its New Shepard vehicle, which carries passengers and research payloads to the edge of space. New Glenn represents a significant expansion of the company's launch capabilities into the orbital market.

The investigation's completion allows Blue Origin to move forward with implementing any necessary design changes or operational procedures identified during the review process.

Yesterday, 3:28 PM ET

Jared Isaacman, the billionaire private astronaut recently confirmed as NASA's 15th administrator, has issued a comprehensive memo outlining his vision for rebooting the space agency. The confirmation by the US Senate passed with a 67-30 vote, installing an advocate of Mars missions and ally of SpaceX CEO Elon Musk in the leadership role.

The appointment follows an unusual path to confirmation. President Donald Trump initially nominated Isaacman, then removed him, before nominating him again for the administrator position. The earlier withdrawal in May occurred during a public dispute between Musk and Trump, with Trump posting on Truth Social that Isaacman's donations to Democratic candidates motivated the decision. According to the Wall Street Journal, Isaacman tapped allies within the administration to promote his cause and re-emerged as a candidate in recent months.

During his second confirmation hearing, Isaacman told senators that NASA must accelerate its efforts to beat China back to the moon this decade. Acting NASA chief Sean Duffy, who also leads the US transportation department, congratulated Isaacman on social media and expressed hopes for success "as he begins his tenure and leads NASA as we go back to the Moon in 2028 and beat China."

The confirmation resolved a power struggle over NASA's organizational structure. Duffy had been lobbying to move the space agency under permanent control of his transportation bureau, an effort that failed with Isaacman's confirmation. This consolidation push sparked conflict with Musk around the same time Duffy stated that SpaceX had fallen behind on its NASA contracts. Duffy had looked to competitors, particularly Jeff Bezos's Blue Origin, as potential alternatives.

Isaacman brings unique credentials to the role. He made his fortune founding Shift4, a payment processing startup that now handles billions of transactions annually. Unlike typical government appointees, he has personal spaceflight experience, having flown on two SpaceX missions that he funded himself. Both missions featured all-civilian crews. His most recent flight in 2024 included a historic milestone as the first spacewalk conducted by a private company.

The new administrator now faces the challenge of implementing his vision for NASA's reboot while navigating political pressures, international competition with China's space program, and managing relationships with commercial space partners. His background as both a successful entrepreneur and experienced private astronaut positions him uniquely to bridge government space operations with the growing commercial space industry.

Two-time NASCAR Cup Series champion Kyle Busch has died at age 41 after battling a severe illness that rapidly progressed, NASCAR announced Thursday.

The racing organization expressed its grief in a statement posted to social media. "We are saddened and heartbroken to share the news of the passing of Kyle Busch, a two-time Cup champion and one of our sport's greatest and fiercest drivers," NASCAR wrote. The organization extended condolences to the Busch family, Richard Childress Racing, and the motorsports community.

According to a statement released Saturday by Dakota Hunter, vice president of Kyle Busch Companies, the family received a medical evaluation confirming that Busch died after severe pneumonia progressed into sepsis, resulting in rapid and overwhelming complications.

Busch's family had announced his hospitalization earlier on Thursday, stating he would not compete as planned at the weekend's NASCAR race in Charlotte, North Carolina. The announcement came just days after Busch had shown signs of illness during competition.

During a NASCAR Cup Series race on May 10 at Watkins Glen, Busch radioed his crew requesting medical assistance from Dr. Bill Heisel and a shot after the race. According to the television broadcast, Busch had been struggling with a sinus cold that worsened due to the intense G-forces and elevation changes at the upstate New York road course. Despite his condition, Busch continued racing and finished eighth.

The Las Vegas native won championships in 2015 and 2019. He was in his fourth season with Richard Childress Racing after previously winning titles with Joe Gibbs Racing. This season, Busch ranked 24th in the Cup Series standings with two top-10 finishes in 12 races.

Busch's career achievements were remarkable. He won 232 races across NASCAR's three national series, more than any driver in history. In a testament to his enduring skill, he won the trucks race at Dover on Friday, just six days before his death.

A joint statement from the Busch family, Richard Childress Racing, and NASCAR praised the driver as a future Hall of Famer and a rare generational talent. "He was fierce, he was passionate, he was immensely skilled and he cared deeply about the sport and fans," the statement read.

Known by nicknames "Rowdy" and "Wild Thing," Busch was a polarizing figure famous for post-race fights, regular feuds with other drivers, and sometimes outlandish behavior. The multi-talented driver burst onto the Cup Series scene in 2005, winning rookie of the year. His sharp wit and competitive spirit created a devoted fan base known as "Rowdy Nation."

Busch is survived by his wife, Samantha, and children Brexton and Lennix.

Yesterday, 8:13 PM ET

Romanian director Cristian Mungiu has won his second Palme d'Or at the Cannes Film Festival, taking home the prestigious award for his English-language debut "Fjord." The victory comes 19 years after his abortion drama "4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days" won the same honor at Cannes.

The win makes the 58-year-old Mungiu the 10th director to receive two Palmes d'Or. He joins an elite group that includes Francis Ford Coppola, Michael Haneke, Ken Loach, Ruben Östlund, the Dardenne brothers, and several other acclaimed filmmakers.

"Fjord" stars Renate Reinsve and Sebastian Stan as Romanian religious parents who relocate to Norway, where they face accusations of child abuse. The film has already been acquired by US distributor Neon, marking the seventh consecutive year the company has secured distribution rights to the Palme d'Or winner.

The 79th Cannes Film Festival saw Andrey Zvyagintsev's "Minotaur" take the runner-up Grand Prix award. The dark satire explores corruption and infidelity in contemporary Russia. Valeska Grisebach's "The Dreamed Adventure," a Bulgaria-set drama about an archaeologist, finished in third place with the Jury prize.

The director award was split between Pawel Pawlikowski for "Fatherland" and the duo of Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi for "The Black Ball." Both the acting awards were also shared between pairs of performers. Virginie Efira and Tao Okamoto won best actress for Ryusuke Hamaguchi's care drama "All of a Sudden," while Valentin Campagne and Emmanuel Macchia took the best actor prize for the queer First World War drama "Coward."

This year's festival was notably subdued, marked by disappointing returns from several established directors and a lack of Hollywood glamour. Both American films in competition were shut out by the jury. James Gray's "Paper Tiger," starring Adam Driver and Scarlett Johansson, and Ira Sachs' AIDS musical "The Man I Love," featuring Rami Malek, went home empty-handed.

Park Chan-wook presided over this year's jury, which included Demi Moore, Stellan Skarsgård, Chloé Zhao, and Paul Laverty. During the closing ceremony, Isabelle Huppert paid special tribute to Barbra Streisand, who was among three recipients of honorary Palmes d'Or this year alongside Peter Jackson and John Travolta.

Streisand was unable to attend due to a knee injury but sent a video message to the audience. "In this crazy, volatile world that seems more fractured every day, it's reassuring to see the compelling movies at this festival, by artists from many countries," she said. "Film has that magical ability to unite us, opening our hearts and minds."

Yesterday, 6:29 PM ET

The World Health Organization has declared an Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Uganda a "public health emergency of international concern" after 88 deaths and more than 300 suspected cases linked to the Bundibugyo virus strain.

Dr. Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, WHO director general, announced the decision on Sunday before convening a formal emergency committee. The rapid declaration likely reflects the severity of the situation, according to experts.

The Bundibugyo virus is one of three strains that can cause Ebola virus disease and is the least common. There are currently no approved vaccines, treatments, or specific tests for this strain, making the outbreak particularly challenging to control.

Dr. Jean Kaseya, director general of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention, expressed urgency about the crisis. "Currently, I'm on panic mode because people are dying. I don't have medicines. I don't have [a] vaccine to support countries," he told Sky News.

The outbreak has hit the DRC's Ituri province particularly hard, with 80 suspected deaths, eight laboratory-confirmed cases, and 246 suspected cases reported in the eastern region bordering Uganda and South Sudan. The area is about five days' travel from the capital.

Two cases, including one death, have been reported in Uganda's capital, Kampala, apparently in people who had traveled from the DRC. A suspected case in the DRC's capital, Kinshasa, later tested negative for the Bundibugyo virus.

The WHO warned that all signs "point towards a potentially much larger outbreak than what is currently being detected and reported, with significant local and regional risk of spread." Officials acknowledged significant uncertainties about the true number of infected people and the geographic scope of the outbreak.

Conflict in the Ituri region is complicating response efforts, with large numbers of people moving around and living in close quarters in urban and suburban areas. These conditions can accelerate disease transmission.

On Sunday, laboratory tests confirmed another Ebola case in Goma, a separate part of the DRC also affected by conflict. Professor Jean-Jacques Muyembe, director of the Congolese National Institute for Biomedical Research, said the case involves the wife of a man who died of Ebola in Bunia. She traveled to Goma after her husband's death while already infected.

In Bunia, the capital of Ituri province, residents described unusually high numbers of burials. "Every day, people are dying, and this has been going on for about a week. In a single day, we bury two, three or even more people," said Jean Marc Asimwe, a local resident. "At this point, we don't really know what kind of disease it is."

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention activated its emergency response center and planned to send additional personnel to its offices in the DRC and Uganda.

The World Health Organization has declared an Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Uganda a "public health emergency of international concern" after 88 deaths and more than 300 suspected cases linked to the Bundibugyo virus strain.

Dr. Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, WHO director general, announced the decision on Sunday before convening a formal emergency committee. The rapid declaration likely reflects the severity of the situation, according to experts.

The Bundibugyo virus is one of three strains that can cause Ebola virus disease and is the least common. There are currently no approved vaccines, treatments, or specific tests for this strain, making the outbreak particularly challenging to control.

Dr. Jean Kaseya, director general of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention, expressed urgency about the crisis. "Currently, I'm on panic mode because people are dying. I don't have medicines. I don't have [a] vaccine to support countries," he told Sky News.

The outbreak has hit the DRC's Ituri province particularly hard, with 80 suspected deaths, eight laboratory-confirmed cases, and 246 suspected cases reported in the eastern region bordering Uganda and South Sudan. The area is about five days' travel from the capital.

Two cases, including one death, have been reported in Uganda's capital, Kampala, apparently in people who had traveled from the DRC. A suspected case in the DRC's capital, Kinshasa, later tested negative for the Bundibugyo virus.

The WHO warned that all signs "point towards a potentially much larger outbreak than what is currently being detected and reported, with significant local and regional risk of spread." Officials acknowledged significant uncertainties about the true number of infected people and the geographic scope of the outbreak.

Conflict in the Ituri region is complicating response efforts, with large numbers of people moving around and living in close quarters in urban and suburban areas. These conditions can accelerate disease transmission.

On Sunday, laboratory tests confirmed another Ebola case in Goma, a separate part of the DRC also affected by conflict. Professor Jean-Jacques Muyembe, director of the Congolese National Institute for Biomedical Research, said the case involves the wife of a man who died of Ebola in Bunia. She traveled to Goma after her husband's death while already infected.

In Bunia, the capital of Ituri province, residents described unusually high numbers of burials. "Every day, people are dying, and this has been going on for about a week. In a single day, we bury two, three or even more people," said Jean Marc Asimwe, a local resident. "At this point, we don't really know what kind of disease it is."

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention activated its emergency response center and planned to send additional personnel to its offices in the DRC and Uganda.

The film has already been acquired by US distributor Neon, marking the seventh consecutive year the company has secured distribution rights to the Palme d'Or winner.

The 79th Cannes Film Festival saw Andrey Zvyagintsev's "Minotaur" take the runner-up Grand Prix award. The dark satire explores corruption and infidelity in contemporary Russia. Valeska Grisebach's "The Dreamed Adventure," a Bulgaria-set drama about an archaeologist, finished in third place with the Jury prize.

The director award was split between Pawel Pawlikowski for "Fatherland" and the duo of Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi for "The Black Ball." Both the acting awards were also shared between pairs of performers. Virginie Efira and Tao Okamoto won best actress for Ryusuke Hamaguchi's care drama "All of a Sudden," while Valentin Campagne and Emmanuel Macchia took the best actor prize for the queer First World War drama "Coward."

This year's festival was notably subdued, marked by disappointing returns from several established directors and a lack of Hollywood glamour. Both American films in competition were shut out by the jury. James Gray's "Paper Tiger," starring Adam Driver and Scarlett Johansson, and Ira Sachs' AIDS musical "The Man I Love," featuring Rami Malek, went home empty-handed.

Park Chan-wook presided over this year's jury, which included Demi Moore, Stellan Skarsgård, Chloé Zhao, and Paul Laverty. During the closing ceremony, Isabelle Huppert paid special tribute to Barbra Streisand, who was among three recipients of honorary Palmes d'Or this year alongside Peter Jackson and John Travolta.

Streisand was unable to attend due to a knee injury but sent a video message to the audience. "In this crazy, volatile world that seems more fractured every day, it's reassuring to see the compelling movies at this festival, by artists from many countries," she said. "Film has that magical ability to unite us, opening our hearts and minds."
