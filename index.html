Today, 12:00 AM ET

An Ebola outbreak in Central Africa has escalated beyond containment, according to reports from international health officials. The outbreak, which began several weeks ago, has now spread to multiple regions, overwhelming local healthcare systems and prompting emergency responses from global health organizations.

Health workers are struggling to trace contacts and isolate cases as the virus spreads faster than containment efforts can manage. The World Health Organization has deployed additional teams to the affected areas, but logistics challenges and limited infrastructure are hampering response efforts.

Scientists are investigating how the current outbreak may have started, examining whether it originated from animal-to-human transmission or from a survivor of a previous outbreak. Ebola can persist in bodily fluids of survivors for months or even years, creating potential reservoirs for new outbreaks.

The virus causes severe hemorrhagic fever with fatality rates that can exceed 50 percent in some outbreaks. Early symptoms include fever, fatigue, and muscle pain, progressing to vomiting, diarrhea, and internal bleeding. Transmission occurs through direct contact with blood or bodily fluids of infected individuals.

Local health systems in the affected region were already strained before the outbreak began. Shortages of protective equipment, isolation facilities, and trained personnel have allowed the virus to spread within healthcare settings, infecting medical workers who are critical to the response effort.

International aid organizations are mobilizing resources, including mobile laboratories for rapid testing, isolation units, and medical supplies. However, the remote location of many affected communities and ongoing security concerns in the region are complicating delivery of assistance.

Previous Ebola outbreaks have shown that rapid response and community engagement are essential for containment. The 2014-2016 West African epidemic killed more than 11,000 people before being brought under control through sustained international intervention.

Health officials stress that early detection and isolation of cases remain the most effective tools for stopping transmission. Communities are being educated about symptoms and the importance of seeking treatment immediately while avoiding traditional burial practices that can spread the virus.

Tennessee called off the execution of a death row inmate Thursday night after prison staff failed to establish the necessary intravenous lines to administer lethal injection drugs. The execution was stopped before any drugs were administered.

The prisoner was scheduled to die at Riverbend Maximum Security Institution in Nashville. According to the Tennessee Department of Correction, medical personnel were unable to place both primary and backup IV lines within the protocol timeframe. After approximately an hour of attempts, prison officials determined the execution could not proceed.

The incident marks another operational failure in Tennessee's execution process. In 2022, the state paused executions for several months to review lethal injection procedures after independent testing revealed that drugs had been improperly prepared, potentially causing inmates to be conscious during execution.

Tennessee law requires executioners to establish two separate IV access points before proceeding with a lethal injection. The protocol includes time limits for these procedures, though exact timeframes are not publicly disclosed. Prison officials cited these procedural requirements in their decision to halt Thursday's execution.

The state has not announced whether it will seek a new execution date. Any rescheduled execution would require coordination with courts and the governor's office. Tennessee uses a three-drug protocol: midazolam as a sedative, followed by vecuronium bromide to stop breathing, and potassium chloride to stop the heart.

Difficulty establishing IV lines has complicated multiple executions nationwide in recent years. Medical professionals are generally prohibited by ethical codes from participating in executions, leaving the task to corrections staff with varying levels of medical training. Factors including age, health conditions, and prior drug use can make venous access challenging.

The failed execution comes amid renewed national attention to capital punishment methods. Several states have faced drug shortages and legal challenges to their lethal injection protocols. Some states have considered or adopted alternative methods, including nitrogen gas, which Alabama used for the first time earlier this year.

Tennessee has executed seven inmates since resuming capital punishment in 2018 after a nine-year pause. The state has 44 prisoners on death row. Governor Bill Lee has not yet commented on Thursday's failed execution.

The funding represents one of the largest government investments in quantum technology to date. Quantum computers use the principles of quantum mechanics to process information in ways that could eventually solve problems beyond the reach of traditional computers, from drug discovery to cryptography.

IBM is among the primary recipients, though the government has not disclosed the full list of companies or the breakdown of funding. The investment comes as China and other nations pour resources into quantum research, creating a global race for technological dominance in the field.

Quantum computing remains largely experimental. Current quantum computers are error-prone and require extreme cooling to near absolute zero temperatures. Most experts believe practical, large-scale quantum computers are still years or decades away. However, the technology could eventually break current encryption methods, transform artificial intelligence, and revolutionize materials science.

The $2 billion will support research facilities, manufacturing capabilities, and workforce development. The Biden administration has identified quantum computing as a priority technology for maintaining American competitiveness, alongside semiconductors and artificial intelligence.

Several quantum computing companies have gone public in recent years, attracting significant private investment despite the uncertain timeline for commercial applications. The sector has faced scrutiny over whether current technology can deliver on ambitious promises.

The federal investment follows the 2022 CHIPS and Science Act, which authorized billions for technology research and manufacturing. That law included provisions for quantum information science but did not specify funding levels. This $2 billion commitment provides concrete support for an industry that has largely relied on venture capital and corporate research budgets.

Government officials say the investment aims to ensure the US remains ahead in quantum research while building domestic manufacturing capacity. China has made quantum technology a centerpiece of its national technology strategy, investing heavily in research institutes and launching the world's first quantum communication satellite.

The funding will be distributed over multiple years, with details on specific projects and timelines to be announced. Industry groups have welcomed the commitment, saying sustained government support is essential for a technology that requires long-term research before producing commercial returns.

Sea Level Rise Accelerating Since 1960, New Analysis Confirms

Global sea levels have been rising at an accelerating rate since 1960, according to new research that improves the accuracy of historical measurements. The findings confirm what climate scientists have long suspected but couldn't prove with earlier, less precise data. Researchers compiled and refined decades of sea level observations from tide gauges and satellite measurements...

Today, 12:00 AM ET

Global sea levels have been rising at an accelerating rate since 1960, according to new research that improves the accuracy of historical measurements. The findings confirm what climate scientists have long suspected but couldn't prove with earlier, less precise data.

Researchers compiled and refined decades of sea level observations from tide gauges and satellite measurements to create the most comprehensive record yet. Their analysis shows that the rate of sea level rise has increased steadily over the past six decades, with the acceleration becoming more pronounced in recent years.

The study resolves uncertainties that plagued earlier attempts to measure long-term trends. Tide gauge records, which extend back more than a century, are unevenly distributed around the globe and can be affected by local land movements. Satellite measurements, which began in the early 1990s, provide more complete global coverage but represent a shorter time span. By carefully integrating both data sources and accounting for various sources of error, the research team produced a clearer picture of how ocean heights have changed.

The acceleration matters because it affects planning for coastal infrastructure, flood defenses, and population displacement. Communities need accurate projections to make decisions about building codes, insurance, and whether to protect, accommodate, or retreat from rising waters. Current rates of sea level rise are already forcing some coastal areas to invest in expensive adaptation measures.

The research attributes the acceleration primarily to increased melting of ice sheets in Greenland and Antarctica, along with the expansion of seawater as it warms. These factors have intensified as global temperatures have climbed. While natural climate variability causes short-term fluctuations in sea level, the long-term upward trend and its acceleration are consistent with the effects of greenhouse gas emissions.

The improved accuracy of the historical record also helps validate climate models, which predicted this acceleration based on physics and observed warming. When models can successfully reproduce past changes, scientists gain more confidence in their projections of future sea level rise. Most projections indicate continued acceleration through the end of this century unless global emissions drop sharply.

The findings underscore the ongoing consequences of accumulated greenhouse gases in the atmosphere. Even if emissions were to stop today, sea levels would continue rising for decades or centuries due to the long response time of ice sheets and deep ocean circulation.

Today, 12:00 AM ET

Kyle Busch, one of NASCAR's most successful drivers, has died unexpectedly. The motorsports community is mourning the loss of the two-time Cup Series champion, though details surrounding his death have not been released.

Busch won 63 Cup Series races over his career, ranking him ninth on the all-time wins list. He claimed championship titles in 2015 and 2019 driving for Joe Gibbs Racing. His aggressive driving style and fierce competitiveness made him one of the sport's most polarizing figures, earning him both devoted fans and critics.

Born in Las Vegas in 1985, Busch began racing at age six and turned professional as a teenager. He competed in NASCAR's top series for over two decades, also winning championships in the Xfinity Series and Truck Series. His 235 combined wins across NASCAR's three national series stands as a record unlikely to be matched.

Busch recently drove for Richard Childress Racing after spending 15 seasons with Joe Gibbs Racing. He remained competitive into his late thirties, consistently qualifying for NASCAR's playoff system and winning races as recently as last season.

His younger brother Kurt Busch, also a Cup Series champion, raced alongside him for years before retiring in 2023. The brothers became the first pair of siblings to each win Cup Series championships.

Busch is survived by his wife Samantha and their two children. The family has requested privacy while they process the loss. NASCAR has not yet announced how it will honor Busch's memory at upcoming races.

Richard Childress Racing released a brief statement expressing grief and noting that Busch was preparing for the upcoming season. No indication had been given publicly that Busch was ill or planning to step away from racing.

The sudden loss leaves a significant void in NASCAR. Busch was not only an active competitor but also a mentor to younger drivers and a prominent voice in discussions about the sport's future. His death marks one of the most significant losses in racing since Dale Earnhardt died in 2001.
