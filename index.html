A few minutes ago

The United States and Iran are moving toward a peace agreement, though finalizing the deal could take several more days, according to officials involved in the talks.

President Trump claimed on social media that a peace deal "has been largely negotiated" but said final aspects and details still need discussion. He instructed US negotiators not to rush the process, writing that "both sides must take their time to get it right. There can be no mistakes."

The proposed agreement would reportedly offer Iran sanctions relief and access to as much as $20 billion in frozen assets. In exchange, Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz and agree to negotiate on its nuclear program over a 60-day period beginning June 5 in Pakistan. The deal would also require Iran and the US, along with their allies, to cease fighting, and would call for Israel to end its offensive in Lebanon.

A major point of disagreement centers on unfreezing Iranian assets held in Qatar. The US wants this conditional on Iran's progress in handing over enriched uranium. Other disputed clauses remain unresolved, with Iranian officials saying one or two points need clarification before the agreement can be sent to Iran's supreme leader for approval.

Trump defended the emerging deal against criticism from conservative members of Congress who oppose the negotiations. He dismissed skeptics on social media, saying "I don't make bad deals" and insisting that "nobody has seen" the agreement "or knows what it is." He emphasized that the US blockade of Iran's ports would remain in effect until an agreement is certified and signed.

The negotiations represent a significant shift in US-Iran relations, which have been strained for years. Trump has emphasized that his approach differs from the nuclear deal reached under President Barack Obama, which Trump withdrew from in 2018.

Some Republican lawmakers have questioned whether the administration is making too many concessions and whether Iran can be trusted to uphold any agreement. These critics have expressed skepticism about the diplomatic effort, though the administration appears committed to pursuing a resolution.

The potential peace deal comes after years of tension between the two nations. Previous diplomatic efforts have faced significant obstacles and frequently stalled over contentious issues.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio has defended the negotiations against conservative criticism and worked to build support for the diplomatic effort. Despite the opposition from some within his party, the administration continues working with Iranian counterparts to resolve remaining differences.

Both governments have maintained discretion about specific details while negotiations are ongoing. If completed, the agreement would mark a major diplomatic achievement and potentially reshape relations between the two countries.

A few minutes ago

Officials in Orange County, California have changed their strategy for containing a dangerous chemical leak that has forced 40,000 residents to evacuate from six cities. Authorities announced the new approach on Sunday as emergency crews work to address an overheated storage tank at a GKN Aerospace facility in Garden Grove.

The crisis began on Thursday when a tank holding methyl methacrylate, a highly flammable chemical used to make resins and plastics, began releasing gas and threatened to fail. Local authorities initially ordered evacuations but lifted the order later that day. However, damage to a valve on the tank created additional problems, prompting officials to issue expanded evacuation orders by Friday affecting residents in Garden Grove, Cypress, Stanton, Anaheim, Buena Park and Westminster.

"We have a tank that is actively in crisis," Orange County fire authority division chief Craig Covey told reporters. "There are literally two options left remaining. One: the tank fails and spills a total of about 6-7,000 gallons of very bad chemicals into the parking lot in that area. Or two: the tank goes into a thermal runaway and blows up, affecting the tanks that are around them that have fuel or the chemicals in them as well."

Environmental Protection Agency administrator Lee Zeldin said on Sunday that the tank "will fail" but acknowledged uncertainty about what that failure would look like. He indicated that a low-volume chemical release was considered the most likely scenario, though explosion remained a serious concern.

Emergency response teams have created containment barriers using sandbags to prevent any chemical spill from reaching storm drains, creeks or the nearby ocean. County health officer Regina Chinsio-Kwong warned that if the chemical heats up, it can release vapor harmful to human health. Exposure can cause respiratory issues, eye irritation, nausea and headaches.

Garden Grove, located about 30 miles southeast of downtown Los Angeles and less than a mile from Disneyland's theme parks, has established at least two shelters for evacuees. Police chief Amir El-Farra reported that some 15 percent of people facing evacuation orders were refusing to leave.

Covey urged residents to take evacuation orders seriously. "We are setting up these evacuations in preparation for these two options: it fails or it blows up," he said. "Please follow our requests and orders for evacuations."

GKN Aerospace is a division of a British corporation that produces airplane engines and aircraft parts. Officials have not announced a timeline for when evacuees might return to their homes as containment efforts continue.

A sharp decline in U.S. Treasury bond prices has pushed borrowing costs higher, challenging policymakers in Washington as they assess the economic fallout. The Treasury market turbulence has driven yields up across multiple maturities, forcing officials to confront difficult questions about how much the government can sustain as it finances ongoing deficits.

The sell-off reflects investor concerns about inflation, fiscal policy, and the Federal Reserve's monetary direction. When Treasury prices fall and yields rise, it signals unease about economic conditions or policy choices. The current market pressure comes as the government continues issuing substantial amounts of debt.

Higher yields create immediate consequences. The government must pay more to borrow money, which could strain the federal budget and increase the cost of servicing the national debt. These elevated borrowing costs ripple through the broader economy, affecting mortgages, business loans, and consumer credit rates.

Washington faces a difficult tradeoff. Rising interest rates can help control inflation by slowing economic activity. Yet they also increase debt servicing expenses and risk dampening growth. Both the Treasury Department and the Federal Reserve must coordinate their responses to navigate this tension.

The Treasury market underpins much of the global financial system. Sharp price declines and yield spikes command attention from policymakers worldwide. The current turbulence follows a period of relative calm and coincides with the government's substantial ongoing debt issuance.

Federal Reserve leadership must decide whether rising market rates align with its economic objectives or warrant intervention. Treasury officials are weighing whether to adjust debt issuance plans in response to shifting market conditions. For now, Washington has adopted a wait-and-see approach, observing whether the turmoil represents a temporary correction or signals a more lasting change in market dynamics.

The broader financial system faces secondary effects. Higher Treasury yields make bonds more attractive than stocks, potentially redirecting investment away from equities. Banks and other financial institutions holding large Treasury portfolios may see asset values decline as prices fall.

The coming weeks will prove instructive. Market participants and policymakers are gauging how much tolerance exists for higher borrowing costs and whether policy interventions become necessary to stabilize markets. The outcome will depend partly on whether investors view current conditions as reflecting genuine economic concerns or temporary volatility, and whether policymakers judge intervention essential to prevent broader financial disruption or prefer to allow market forces to operate freely.

Felix Rosenqvist won the 110th running of the Indianapolis 500 on Sunday in the closest finish the race has ever produced. Rosenqvist crossed the finish line 0.02 seconds ahead of David Malukas, setting a new record for the tightest margin of victory in the event's history.

The Swedish driver held off Malukas in a sprint to the checkered flag at Indianapolis Motor Speedway. The finish broke the previous record for the closest Indy 500 result, though the exact previous margin was not specified in race reports.

The race marked the 110th edition of the Indianapolis 500, one of motorsport's most prominent events. Rosenqvist's victory came after he and Malukas battled through the final laps of the 200-lap race on the 2.5-mile oval.

The photo finish required official timing equipment to determine the winner. The margin of 0.02 seconds translates to roughly three feet at the speeds the cars were traveling as they crossed the finish line.

Rosenqvist's win represents his first Indianapolis 500 victory. The 33-year-old competed in the IndyCar Series, which hosts the Memorial Day weekend race at Indianapolis Motor Speedway. Malukas, the runner-up, came within inches of claiming his first Indy 500 title.

The Indianapolis 500 draws one of the largest single-day sporting event crowds in the world. This year's race sold out for the fifth consecutive year, according to separate reports about the Coca-Cola 600 NASCAR race that also took place Sunday.

The dramatic finish provided spectators with one of the most memorable conclusions in the race's 110-year history. Previous close finishes at Indianapolis have become legendary moments in American motorsport, and Sunday's result now joins that list.

Neither driver's team affiliation nor their full season standings were detailed in available race reports. The focus remained on the historic margin that separated first and second place at the finish line.

Peters, the former clerk in Mesa County in western Colorado, was convicted of four felonies and three misdemeanor...

May 21, 10:12 AM ET

The Colorado Democratic Party voted to censure Governor Jared Polis over his decision to commute the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former county clerk. The formal rebuke represents a rare public split between the state party and its sitting Democratic governor.

Peters, the former clerk in Mesa County in western Colorado, was convicted of four felonies and three misdemeanors in 2024. She allowed unauthorized access to her county's voting equipment, including to a former pro surfer named Conan Hayes, who was affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell. Hayes copied voting equipment and attended a sensitive software upgrade. Sensitive passwords and other information from the county's Dominion voting equipment were later published online by rightwing personalities.

A judge sentenced Peters to eight and a half years in prison and six months in jail. An appeals court overturned that sentence in April and ordered reconsideration. Polis reduced Peters' sentence from eight and a half years to about four and a half years, meaning she will be released on parole on June 1.

In his clemency letter, Polis wrote: "This is an extremely unusual and lengthy sentence for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes."

Colorado's Secretary of State Jena Griswold, a Democrat, strongly opposed the decision. She said Polis' action "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president" and will "embolden the election denial movement across the country." Griswold noted that Peters organized the election equipment breach and broke public trust, saying she should receive no special treatment.

The Colorado Clerks Association expressed similar concerns. Executive director Matt Crane said the group was "furious, disgusted, and deeply disappointed," adding that election officials across the country are hearing that "it's open season on our elections and election officials."

Polis had signaled his leniency in March when he compared Peters' case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who was convicted of submitting forged letters to the legislature. Lewis received probation and community service. Polis said justice needs to be applied evenly and that he was considering sentencing disparities. However, Griswold disputed the comparison, noting that while both had overlapping felony counts, Peters' actions were fundamentally different because she organized the election equipment breach.

Peters' case has attracted national attention from Republicans who continue to make false claims about the 2020 presidential election being stolen. Trump, who has repeatedly spread misinformation about elections and Dominion voting systems, previously called for Peters to be freed and issued her a federal pardon, though that was largely symbolic since her crimes are state-level offenses.

The censure carries no legal weight and does not affect Polis' ability to govern. However, it signals deep dissatisfaction among party activists and officials with the clemency decision.
