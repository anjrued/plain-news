The timing of the announcement has drawn attention to broader questions about AI's impact on the internet. Some observers note that Google is using web content to train its AI systems while simultaneously changing how users access that same content.

The updates mark a departure from Google's traditional approach of providing links to websites. The new AI-integrated search aims to provide more direct answers and assistance.

The search box redesign coincides with growing competition in the AI space. Other technology companies have launched their own AI-powered search and information tools.

Google's dominance in search has made the company a primary gateway to the internet for most users. Any change to this gateway affects how information flows across the web.

The announcement comes as regulators in multiple countries examine Google's market position and business practices. The search box update could draw additional scrutiny regarding how the company uses web content and serves results to users.

Google has not disclosed specific technical details about how the new AI features work or what data they use. The company emphasized that the changes represent a major evolution in search technology.

The controversy emerged after Colbert's final episode as host of "The Late Show" aired on May 21. During his 11-year tenure at CBS, Colbert had maintained the show's position as the top-rated late-night program on network television for nine consecutive years. Despite these strong ratings, CBS announced the show's cancellation last July, citing financial reasons. Many observers interpreted the decision as a political gesture related to a pending merger between Skydance and Paramount, CBS's parent company.

Colbert's surprise appearance on the local cable access program became widely circulated online, showcasing the comedian performing a parody of late-night talk shows in a stripped-down, low-budget format. The segment generated significant viral interest as viewers shared clips across social media and other platforms.

In response to the spreading footage, CBS and Paramount initially pursued copyright enforcement actions designed to prevent further distribution of the content. This strategy drew substantial criticism from the public and media outlets that had covered the story, including NPR, NBC News, and USA Today. The backlash proved decisive, prompting the networks to reverse course and abandon their copyright claims.

The retreat from intellectual property enforcement represents an unusual public reversal for a major media corporation. Neither CBS nor Paramount has publicly explained the reasoning behind their initial copyright action or their subsequent decision to withdraw it.

The timing of Colbert's public access appearance coincided with the end of an era for "The Late Show," which originated as a vehicle for David Letterman in 1993 following his unsuccessful pursuit of "The Tonight Show" at NBC. Letterman hosted the program for 22 years before retiring and handing the reins to Colbert, who had gained prominence as a correspondent and member of Comedy Central's "The Daily Show."

The 11.35 p.m. time slot vacated by Colbert's departure will be occupied by "Comics Unleashed," hosted by Byron Allen. Under an unusual arrangement with CBS, Allen will lease the time slot from the network for 16 months while independently selling advertising for the program, rather than receiving direct payment from the broadcaster.

The finale episode drew notable celebrity guests including Paul McCartney, Elvis Costello, Bryan Cranston, and Tig Notaro for an 80-minute conclusion to the series. The episode marked a significant moment in late-night television, as the cancellation ended the entire show rather than simply transitioning to a new host, a practice that distinguishes "The Late Show" from franchises like "The Tonight Show" and "Late Night," which typically continue with new hosts.

The pause reflects concerns about exposing Immigration and Customs Enforcement personnel to the virus and preventing potential disease transmission to the United States. However, public heal...

May 21, 5:06 PM ET

The Trump administration has temporarily halted deportation flights to the Democratic Republic of Congo as an Ebola outbreak spreads across the country, according to reporting by Politico. The pause reflects concerns about exposing Immigration and Customs Enforcement personnel to the virus and preventing potential disease transmission to the United States.

However, public health experts have questioned whether the measure will actually help contain the outbreak. The decision comes as the health crisis in Congo creates complications for immigration enforcement operations that have accelerated significantly during Trump's presidency.

The pause has created difficult circumstances for some detainees. At least one woman has been left in limbo after officials transferred her to Kinshasa, the capital of the DRC, only to refuse her return due to the Ebola-related travel restrictions, despite a judge's order requiring her repatriation.

The timing of the pause is notable given the administration's aggressive immigration enforcement agenda. Human rights groups monitoring ICE flights documented an 84 percent increase in deportation flights during 2025 compared to the previous year. These flights have faced mounting scrutiny from lawmakers concerned about transparency and conditions aboard charter aircraft.

A group of 40 House Democrats recently sent a letter to the Federal Aviation Administration expressing what they described as "grave concerns" over the Trump administration's deportation flight program. The lawmakers called attention to allegations of mistreatment and inhumane conditions on ICE charter jets, including reports that individuals have been placed on flights without notice to their attorneys or family members.

The letter referenced an investigation that revealed the Trump administration transported detained immigrants in ways that violated their constitutional rights. It also cited allegations of abuse at a private detention center in Louisiana, described as a central hub in the administration's deportation operations.

The Democrats requested detailed FAA reports on all ICE air operations since Trump took office, including flight origins, destinations, and passenger numbers. They also demanded information about how the agency assesses humanitarian conditions on these flights, particularly regarding the use of full body restraints during deportation flights.

The Trump administration has rejected characterizations of its deportation flights as secretive or inhumane. Officials described restraint practices as "long-standing, standard ICE protocol" designed to ensure safety and claimed detention centers maintain "higher standards than most US prisons."

New Jersey congressman Rob Menendez, who led the letter effort, emphasized the importance of public understanding of these operations. "This transparency is important for the American people to understand what is happening every single day because there are so many violations of due process and legal rights happening that if people knew about them they would find it deeply problematic," he said.

The duration of the Congo flight pause remains uncertain, with no clear timeline for when operations might resume.
