Sonny Rollins, one of the greatest jazz saxophonists of all time, has died at age 95. His death was announced Monday on his website "with deep sorrow and profound love," and confirmed by his publicist Terri Hinte. He died at his home in Woodstock, New York. No cause of death was given.

Born Walter Theodore Rollins in New York City in 1930, Rollins grew up in Harlem and earned the nickname Sonny from his grandmother. He began playing saxophone at age seven, inspired by his siblings and jazz legends like Louis Jordan and Fats Waller. His high school band included future stars Jackie McLean, Kenny Drew, and Art Taylor, and he soon began performing with established musicians including Bud Powell and JJ Johnson.

Rollins became a defining force in jazz during the bebop era alongside Miles Davis and Charlie Parker. He was known for his willingness to break convention and embrace improvisation. Miles Davis wrote that Rollins quickly became "a legend, almost a god to a lot of the younger musicians" and "an aggressive, innovative player who always had fresh musical ideas." Saxophonist Branford Marsalis called him "the greatest improviser in the history of jazz" alongside Louis Armstrong. When presenting Rollins with the 2010 National Medal of the Arts, President Barack Obama said the saxophonist had inspired him to "take risks that I might not otherwise have taken."

Rollins struggled with heroin addiction in his early life. In 1950, he committed armed robbery to fund his habit and spent 10 months on Rikers Island. He kicked the addiction through a rehabilitation program in 1955, after which his creativity flourished. He released his debut album as bandleader in 1953 and recorded 17 more albums by decade's end, including landmarks like Saxophone Colossus (1956), which featured his signature tune "St Thomas," and Freedom Suite (1958), whose 20-minute title track became an elegant statement for freedom during the civil rights movement.

In 1959, Rollins took a three-year break from recording and performing, practicing up to 15 hours daily on the Williamsburg Bridge's pedestrian walkway. This period inspired his 1962 comeback album The Bridge. Another sabbatical between 1969 and 1971 saw him study yoga, philosophy, and meditation at an Indian ashram.

Throughout his career, Rollins released more than 60 albums and constantly reinvented his style, exploring Latin American music, avant-garde improvisation, and funk-influenced approaches. He also composed the soundtrack to the 1966 Michael Caine film Alfie and campaigned around climate change through benefit concerts and his 1998 album Global Warming.

Rollins married Lucille Pearson in 1965, and they remained together until her death in 2004. He is remembered as an artist dedicated to pushing the boundaries of what was possible on the saxophone.

The Trump administration has temporarily halted the removal of detainees to the Democratic Republic of Congo due to a widening Ebola outbreak in the region. The pause was announced as the disease spreads across the country, though public health experts question whether the measure will actually prevent disease transmission.

The suspension reflects concerns about exposing Immigration and Customs Enforcement officials to the virus during deportation flights. Officials accompanying detainees could potentially come into contact with the disease, prompting the administration to implement what it described as a temporary pause on these operations.

However, the administration's approach appears incomplete. According to reporting, at least one woman has been moved to Kinshasa, the capital of the DRC, and officials now say they will not bring her back because of the travel ban. This decision contradicts a judge's order for her return, leaving her in a precarious situation amid the outbreak.

The pause stands in contrast to the administration's otherwise aggressive deportation operations. ICE has significantly expanded its use of charter flights to transfer detained immigrants, with human rights groups documenting an 84 percent increase in ICE flights during 2025 compared to 2024.

The deportation operations have faced mounting scrutiny from lawmakers. A group of 40 House Democrats recently sent a letter to the Federal Aviation Administration expressing "grave concerns" about the administration's secretive deportation flight program. The lawmakers described the operations as involving "inappropriate and dangerous" efforts to shield flights from public tracking systems.

The letter references an investigation revealing that the Trump administration transported detained immigrants in ways that routinely violated their constitutional rights. Reports also identified allegations of abuse at a private detention center in Alexandria, Louisiana, which serves as a central hub in the deportation program.

Congressman Rob Menendez, the letter's lead author, emphasized the importance of transparency. "This transparency is important for the American people to understand what is happening every single day because there are so many violations of due process and legal rights happening that if people knew about them they would find it deeply problematic," he stated.

The lawmakers asked the FAA to provide detailed reports on all ICE air operations since Trump took office, including flight data and passenger numbers. They also requested information on how the agency assesses humanitarian conditions on these flights, particularly regarding the controversial use of full body restraints during deportation operations.

The Trump administration has previously defended its detention and deportation practices as meeting established legal standards and prioritizing safety.

Despite the Ebola outbreak, the Democratic Republic of Congo's World Cup team has continued planning its trip to the United States for pre-tournament training, though it cancelled activities in Kinshasa due to health restrictions in the capital.

This period inspired his 1962 comeback album The Bridge. Another sabbatical between 1969 and 1971 saw him study yoga, philosophy, and meditation at an Indian ashram.

Throughout his career, Rollins released more than 60 albums and constantly reinvented his style, exploring Latin American music, avant-garde improvisation, and funk-influenced approaches. He also composed the soundtrack to the 1966 Michael Caine film Alfie and campaigned around climate change through benefit concerts and his 1998 album Global Warming.

Rollins married Lucille Pearson in 1965, and they remained together until her death in 2004. He is remembered as an artist dedicated to pushing the boundaries of what was possible on the saxophone.
