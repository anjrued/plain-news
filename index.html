Today, 11:00 PM ET

An Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has escalated sharply as angry residents burned down a treatment facility, signaling a dangerous breakdown in the public health response. The attack came amid growing frustration over the handling of the outbreak, which authorities now describe as spiraling out of control.

Health officials are working to trace the origins of the current outbreak while attempting to contain its spread. The disease has already infected dozens of people in the affected region, though exact case numbers remain difficult to confirm due to the security situation and limited access to remote areas.

The destruction of medical infrastructure represents a critical setback for containment efforts. Ebola treatment centers provide isolation facilities essential to preventing transmission, along with supportive care that significantly improves survival rates. Without these facilities, infected individuals are more likely to spread the virus to family members and communities.

Local anger appears rooted in multiple factors, including distrust of health authorities, fear of the disease itself, and frustration with previous outbreak responses. Similar patterns emerged during the major 2018-2020 Ebola epidemic in eastern Congo, when treatment centers and health workers faced repeated attacks.

The World Health Organization and international health partners have deployed teams to the region, but the volatile security environment complicates their work. Health workers must now balance the urgent need to treat patients and trace contacts with legitimate concerns for their own safety.

Ebola is a severe viral disease that spreads through direct contact with bodily fluids of infected individuals. Without treatment, fatality rates can exceed 50 percent, though modern medical care and newer vaccines have improved outcomes when patients receive early intervention.

The current outbreak adds to Congo's ongoing health challenges. The country has experienced more than a dozen Ebola outbreaks since the virus was first identified near the Ebola River in 1976, making it one of the most experienced nations in fighting the disease. However, poverty, weak infrastructure, armed conflict, and public mistrust continue to undermine response efforts.

Authorities face an urgent need to rebuild trust with affected communities while quickly reestablishing treatment capacity. The longer the outbreak continues without effective containment, the greater the risk of wider regional spread.

Today, 11:00 PM ET

Tennessee called off the execution of Tony Carruthers on Thursday night after medical personnel could not establish intravenous access. The state will not attempt another execution for at least a year.

Carruthers, 49, was convicted of killing a fellow inmate at a maximum security prison in 1994. He was scheduled to die by lethal injection at Riverbend Maximum Security Institution in Nashville.

Prison officials spent over an hour trying to insert IV lines into Carruthers before abandoning the procedure. The state did not provide specific details about the difficulties encountered. Carruthers was returned to death row.

Tennessee Governor Bill Lee issued a temporary reprieve, citing the failed execution attempt. State law requires a one-year waiting period before the state can reschedule an execution after a failed attempt.

The botched execution adds to a growing list of problematic lethal injections across the United States. Several states have faced similar IV access issues in recent years, leading to prolonged and disputed execution procedures.

Carruthers' legal team had filed multiple appeals challenging the execution, arguing that Tennessee's lethal injection protocol created a risk of unconstitutional pain and suffering. Courts rejected those appeals before the scheduled execution time.

Tennessee uses a three-drug protocol for executions: midazolam as a sedative, followed by vecuronium bromide to stop breathing, and potassium chloride to stop the heart. Critics argue the first drug may not adequately sedate inmates before the painful final drugs are administered.

The state has carried out six executions since 2018, when it resumed capital punishment after a nine-year pause. Tennessee had temporarily halted executions to review its protocols after concerns were raised about execution procedures.

This is not the first time Tennessee has encountered technical difficulties during an execution. In 2022, the state paused executions to review procedures after problems arose during at least one execution.

The failure highlights ongoing debates about lethal injection as an execution method. Some states have turned to alternative methods, including firing squads and nitrogen gas, while others have abandoned capital punishment entirely.

Carruthers will remain on death row while the state determines next steps. Under Tennessee law, officials must wait until at least May 2026 before setting a new execution date.

Today, 11:00 PM ET

The United States government plans to award $2 billion to quantum computing companies while taking equity stakes in the firms, marking a significant shift in how the federal government supports emerging technology.

The initiative represents the government's most substantial financial commitment to quantum computing to date. Unlike traditional grants or contracts, the equity stake structure means taxpayers could see returns if these companies succeed commercially. The approach mirrors venture capital investing but with public funds.

Quantum computing represents a potential breakthrough in processing power. These machines use quantum mechanics to perform calculations that would take traditional computers thousands of years to complete. Applications range from drug discovery and materials science to cryptography and financial modeling.

Several nations view quantum computing as strategically critical. China has invested heavily in quantum research, while the European Union has launched its own quantum initiative. The technology could provide significant advantages in national security, particularly in code breaking and secure communications.

The $2 billion investment comes as quantum computing companies face a challenging funding environment. Many firms have struggled to attract private capital while working on technology that remains years from widespread commercial use. The sector requires enormous upfront investment in specialized equipment and talent before generating revenue.

The equity stake component distinguishes this program from previous government technology investments. Typically, federal agencies fund research through grants or purchase technology through contracts. Taking ownership positions creates different incentives and could influence how companies operate and make strategic decisions.

Details about which companies will receive funding and the specific equity terms remain unclear. The government will likely target firms at various stages of development, from early research to those closer to commercial applications.

The program raises questions about government involvement in private markets. Supporters argue the technology is too important and capital intensive to leave entirely to private investors. Critics worry about the government picking winners and losers in an uncertain field.

Quantum computing faces significant technical hurdles. Current quantum computers require extreme cooling and remain prone to errors. Companies are pursuing different technical approaches, and it remains unclear which method will prove most viable.

The investment reflects growing concern about maintaining American leadership in critical technologies. Similar programs have targeted semiconductor manufacturing and artificial intelligence. The government appears increasingly willing to use direct investment rather than relying solely on market forces to develop strategic capabilities.

Google has accidentally published details of a security vulnerability in Chromium before a fix was available, leaving millions of Chrome users potentially exposed to exploitation. The premature disclosure occurred through Google's public issue tracking system, where engineers typically coordinate security patches before making information public.

The vulnerability affects Chromium, the open-source browser engine that powers Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Brave, Opera, and numerous other browsers. When security researchers or Google's own teams discover flaws, standard practice involves keeping details private until developers can create and distribute a patch. This coordinated disclosure process prevents attackers from exploiting known weaknesses before users can protect themselves.

The exposed flaw's technical details were visible to anyone monitoring Google's public bug tracker. Security researchers outside Google noticed the premature publication and flagged the issue. Google has since restricted access to the vulnerability report, but the information remained publicly available for an unknown period.

Chromium security vulnerabilities are particularly significant because of the browser engine's widespread adoption. Chrome alone commands roughly 65 percent of the global browser market. When combined with other Chromium-based browsers, the affected user base extends to several billion people worldwide.

Google has not disclosed whether the company has observed active exploitation of the vulnerability. The nature and severity of the specific flaw also remain unclear, as Google typically withholds such details until the majority of users have updated their browsers.

This incident highlights the delicate balance technology companies must maintain between transparency and security. While public bug trackers enable collaboration among developers and foster accountability, they can also inadvertently arm malicious actors with roadmaps for attacks.

Browser security has become increasingly critical as web applications handle sensitive financial transactions, personal communications, and confidential business operations. A single browser vulnerability can potentially compromise passwords, banking information, and private data across countless websites.

Google routinely patches security flaws in Chrome through automatic updates, which most users receive within days of a fix becoming available. However, the window between disclosure and widespread patch adoption represents a period of elevated risk. Users who have disabled automatic updates or use Chromium-based browsers with slower update cycles may remain vulnerable for longer periods.

The company has not commented on how the premature disclosure occurred or what procedural changes might prevent similar incidents.

Sea Level Rise Has Been Accelerating Since 1960, Scientists Confirm

Scientists have confirmed that global sea level rise has been accelerating continuously since 1960, refining our understanding of one of climate change's most consequential impacts. The research improves measurement accuracy and removes previous uncertainties about whether the acceleration was consistent or sporadic. The study analyzed tide gauge records and satellite data spa...

Today, 11:00 PM ET

Scientists have confirmed that global sea level rise has been accelerating continuously since 1960, refining our understanding of one of climate change's most consequential impacts. The research improves measurement accuracy and removes previous uncertainties about whether the acceleration was consistent or sporadic.

The study analyzed tide gauge records and satellite data spanning over six decades. Researchers found that the rate of sea level rise has increased steadily rather than in fits and starts as some earlier analyses suggested. This matters because accurate projections determine infrastructure decisions, coastal planning, and climate adaptation strategies affecting hundreds of millions of people in low-lying areas worldwide.

Before 1990, sea levels rose at approximately 1.5 millimeters per year. That rate has since more than doubled. Satellite measurements beginning in 1993 provided more comprehensive global coverage than tide gauges alone, but reconciling the two data sources has been challenging. This research developed methods to better integrate both sources, producing a clearer picture of the trend.

The acceleration stems primarily from two factors: thermal expansion of warming ocean water and accelerating ice loss from Greenland and Antarctic ice sheets. As global temperatures continue rising, both processes intensify. The Greenland ice sheet alone now loses roughly 280 billion tons of ice annually, compared to relatively stable conditions in the early 1990s.

The findings eliminate previous statistical ambiguities that left room for doubt about whether acceleration was real or an artifact of measurement uncertainties. Multiple independent analysis methods now converge on the same conclusion. This consensus strengthens the scientific basis for coastal planning decisions.

Coastal cities and island nations face mounting costs from the confirmed acceleration. Even wealthy nations struggle with the expense of protecting infrastructure. In the United States, chronic flooding already disrupts communities along the Gulf and Atlantic coasts during high tides. The acceleration means these disruptions will intensify faster than previously projected under some scenarios.

The research does not change the long-term projections dramatically but narrows the uncertainty range. By 2100, seas could rise between 0.6 and 2.0 meters depending on emissions trajectories and ice sheet dynamics. The confirmed acceleration means we are currently tracking toward the higher end of previous estimates unless emissions decrease substantially. That difference translates to vastly different outcomes for coastal populations and ecosystems worldwide.

Kyle Busch, one of NASCAR's most accomplished drivers, has died unexpectedly, Motorsport.com reported Thursday. The motorsports world is mourning the loss of a driver widely considered among the greatest in the sport's history.

Details surrounding the circumstances of Busch's death have not been disclosed. The news sent shockwaves through the racing community as tributes poured in from drivers, teams, and fans.

Busch's career spanned more than two decades and included 63 Cup Series wins, placing him ninth on NASCAR's all-time wins list. He won Cup Series championships in 2015 and 2019, cementing his status as one of the sport's elite competitors. His aggressive driving style and competitive intensity made him both a polarizing and respected figure in racing.

Beyond his Cup Series success, Busch dominated NASCAR's lower divisions with historic results. He won 102 races in the Xfinity Series and 66 in the Truck Series, records that may never be broken. His ability to win across all three of NASCAR's national series showcased his versatility and raw talent.

Busch drove for Joe Gibbs Racing for 15 seasons before joining Richard Childress Racing in 2023. He most recently competed for Richard Childress Racing, continuing to add to his win total and mentor younger drivers. His presence in the garage was a constant throughout an era of significant change in NASCAR.

The driver's impact extended beyond statistics. Busch was known for his willingness to speak candidly about the sport, often becoming a lightning rod for controversy. His outspoken nature and refusal to conform to expectations made him a complex figure who commanded attention.

Busch is survived by his wife Samantha and their two children. The family has requested privacy during this time. NASCAR has not yet announced plans for memorial services or tributes.

The racing community has lost one of its most talented and accomplished competitors. Busch's legacy includes not only his impressive win total but also his influence on a generation of drivers who watched him compete at the highest level for more than 20 years.
