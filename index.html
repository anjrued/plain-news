Donald Trump has claimed that a peace agreement with Iran "has been largely negotiated," announcing on social media that the deal could potentially end the conflict launched by the US and Israel in February.

The president wrote on his social media platform that "final aspects and details" of a "memorandum of understanding" were still being discussed and "will be announced shortly." Trump said the Strait of Hormuz would be opened as part of the deal.

The announcement came after Trump held calls with a Pakistani mediator, Gulf allies and Israel. However, the claim was tempered by statements from Pakistan's prime minister expressing hopes of hosting more talks "very soon," and Iran's Fars news agency reporting that the strait would remain under Iranian control.

Tehran also signaled progress on negotiations but indicated that the key issue of nuclear weapons is not part of an initial framework being developed. According to reports, any agreement would include a two-month period for negotiations on Iran's nuclear program.

The announcement represents a shift from Trump's recent public stance. Just days earlier, he had voiced impatience at the deadlock over peace talks, posting on social media: "For Iran, the Clock is Ticking, and they better get moving, FAST, or there won't be anything left of them. TIME IS OF THE ESSENCE!"

According to Axios, Trump was scheduled to meet with his national security team to discuss next steps. He also spoke with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu before an Israeli security cabinet meeting focused on Iran, Lebanon and Gaza. There has been growing speculation in Israel that fighting with Iran could resume if there are no signs of compromise.

Three months into its conflict with Iran, the Trump administration has been oscillating between strike threats and diplomatic overtures, with neither path yielding a clear resolution so far.

Regional tensions remain high. The United Arab Emirates blamed a fire near its nuclear power plant on a drone launched by Iran or one of its proxies, describing the incident as a "dangerous escalation."

The unfinalized deal aims to end the current war between the US, Israel and Iran. The opening of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping channel for global oil supplies, would be a significant component of any agreement.

While Trump has announced progress, significant details remain to be worked out before any final agreement can be reached. The involvement of Pakistani mediators and the ongoing discussions about nuclear weapons programs suggest complex negotiations are still underway.

A few minutes ago

A gunman was shot and killed Saturday evening after approaching a White House security checkpoint and opening fire on Secret Service officers, federal officials said. The incident prompted a brief lockdown of the White House while President Donald Trump was inside.

The shooting occurred shortly after 6pm local time when a man approached a checkpoint at a White House gate near 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue NW. According to the Secret Service, the suspect took out a pistol and began firing at posted officers, who returned fire and struck the gunman. The suspect was transported to an area hospital, where he later died.

A bystander was also shot during the incident. The Secret Service said it remains unclear whether the bystander was struck by the suspect's initial gunfire or during the subsequent exchange between the gunman and officers. No Secret Service personnel were injured in the shooting, and officials said the president was not affected by the incident.

Journalists in the area reported hearing a sustained volley of gunshots that sent them running for cover. Selina Wang, a White House correspondent for ABC News, shared video on social media showing the moment gunfire interrupted her as she recorded a report. In the clip, Wang could be seen diving for cover as what sounded like dozens of shots rang out. Other witnesses estimated that between 20 and 30 shots were fired during the exchange.

The suspect has not been officially named, but multiple US media outlets reported that he had a history of mental health issues. According to these reports, the man had been arrested last year while trying to enter via a different White House checkpoint without authorization and had been issued with a stay away order.

Hours after the shooting, Trump praised the swift and professional action of law enforcement officials in a social media post. He noted that the incident came a month after a shooting at the White House Correspondents' Association dinner at a Washington hotel.

FBI Director Kash Patel said the FBI was on the scene supporting the Secret Service response and would provide updates to the public as information became available.

The shooting comes less than three weeks after a previous incident in which a man reportedly brandishing a gun fired at a Secret Service officer near the route of JD Vance's motorcade outside the White House. In that incident, a civilian witness standing behind the officer was wounded in his leg. Officers returned fire, striking that suspect in the hand, left arm and upper abdomen.

Yesterday, 2:16 PM ET

President Trump said the next Federal Reserve chair should be totally independent, according to a BBC report published Friday. Trump addressed the matter as the Federal Reserve prepares for a leadership transition.

Trump told the incoming Fed chair to avoid looking to the White House or any outside party for guidance on monetary policy. According to reports, he instructed the nominee not to look at him or anybody else when making decisions.

The comments come as the Federal Reserve maintains its role as the central bank of the United States, responsible for setting interest rates and managing monetary policy. The Fed operates as an independent agency within the government, with the chair serving a four-year term.

Trump's statement addresses the relationship between the executive branch and the Federal Reserve, an institution designed to operate free from political pressure. The Fed chair position carries significant influence over the economy through decisions on interest rates, inflation targets, and financial regulation.

The Federal Reserve's independence has been a foundational principle since the modern central banking system was established. This separation allows the Fed to make decisions based on economic data and long-term stability rather than short-term political considerations.

Trump's remarks about independence appear to signal his position on how the Fed should function during the upcoming term. The president does appoint the Fed chair, subject to Senate confirmation, but the chair operates independently once confirmed.

The timing of Trump's statement comes as markets watch closely for signals about future monetary policy direction. Interest rate decisions by the Fed affect borrowing costs for consumers and businesses, influence inflation, and impact employment levels across the economy.

The Fed chair nominee referenced in reports has not been publicly identified. The confirmation process for a Fed chair includes hearings before the Senate Banking Committee, where nominees face questions about their views on monetary policy, inflation control, and the Fed's dual mandate of maximum employment and stable prices.

Yesterday, 10:32 PM ET

Blue Origin has completed its investigation into the failure of its New Glenn rocket during its first launch attempt. The investigation focused on determining the cause of the launch failure and developing corrective measures.

New Glenn, Blue Origin's heavy-lift orbital rocket, represents the company's entry into the commercial launch market. The vehicle is designed to compete with SpaceX's Falcon 9 and other commercial launch providers for satellite deployment and cargo missions.

The completion of the investigation marks a significant step for Blue Origin as it works to establish itself in the orbital launch market. The company founded by Jeff Bezos has been developing New Glenn for several years as part of its effort to expand beyond suborbital tourism flights.

Blue Origin has not provided specific details about the root cause of the failure or the timeline for the next launch attempt. The investigation findings will inform modifications to the rocket and launch procedures before the company attempts another orbital flight.

The New Glenn rocket is named after John Glenn, the first American to orbit Earth. The vehicle stands over 300 feet tall and is designed to be partially reusable, with the first stage intended to land on a recovery vessel after launch.

The failure represents a setback for Blue Origin's commercial launch ambitions. The company has been working to secure contracts for satellite launches and compete in the growing commercial space market. Several customers have payload manifests scheduled for future New Glenn flights.

The completion of the investigation allows Blue Origin to move forward with implementing corrective actions and preparing for subsequent launch attempts. The company will need to demonstrate reliable performance to establish New Glenn as a viable option for commercial and government customers seeking launch services.

Usyk Stops Verhoeven in 11th Round to Retain Heavyweight Title

Oleksandr Usyk retained his heavyweight world titles with a controversial 11th-round stoppage of kickboxing champion Rico Verhoeven in Egypt, though the Ukrainian boxer faced a tougher challenge than many expected against the boxing novice. The fight, dubbed "Glory in Giza" and held at the Pyramids of Giza on 23 May, marked the first heavyweight title fight ever held in Egypt...

2:30 AM ET

Oleksandr Usyk retained his heavyweight world titles with a controversial 11th-round stoppage of kickboxing champion Rico Verhoeven in Egypt, though the Ukrainian boxer faced a tougher challenge than many expected against the boxing novice.

The fight, dubbed "Glory in Giza" and held at the Pyramids of Giza on 23 May, marked the first heavyweight title fight ever held in Egypt according to The Ring magazine. The bout was streamed live on Dazn to a global audience.

Verhoeven, a 36-year-old Dutch fighter with a 66-10 record and 21 knockouts in kickboxing, had only one previous professional boxing match in 2014, which he won by knockout. Despite his limited boxing experience, the longtime kickboxing champion proved to be a difficult opponent for Usyk, who had not fought since a fifth-round knockout of Daniel Dubois at Wembley in July.

The stoppage itself drew criticism, with reports describing it as highly controversial. Usyk, the twice undisputed heavyweight champion who holds the WBA, WBC, and IBF belts, avoided what some observers suggested could have been a shock defeat by securing the late stoppage.

Before the fight, both champions showed mutual respect. "I truly respect people who reach the very top in their sport," Usyk said. "Rico is one of them, a powerful athlete and a great champion. He's truly the king of kickboxing. Being a champion isn't just about belts. I'm ready and looking forward to meeting him in the ring."

Verhoeven had expressed confidence about the challenge. "I spent 12 years as the undisputed heavyweight kickboxing champion and accomplished everything I set out to accomplish," he said. "But staying at the top for that long didn't take away from the hunger, it strengthened it. Usyk is the undisputed in boxing. That's the kind of challenge that motivated me. Undisputed versus undisputed. The best versus the best."

The fight drew comparisons to recent spectacle bouts, including Anthony Joshua's match against YouTuber Jake Paul in Miami, which was streamed to Netflix's roughly 300 million subscribers. British heavyweight Fabio Wardley, who will defend the WBO title against Dubois on 9 May in Manchester, had criticized Usyk's choice of opponent before the fight.

"I don't feel like it's a real, genuine challenge nor someone that deserves the shot," Wardley told Sky Sports. "But I do understand or do concede Usyk is at a point in his career where he's able to do what he wants for a bit in that sense."

Usyk has now won six fights in four years, with two wins each over Joshua, Tyson Fury, and Dubois. He vacated his WBO title in November instead of facing Wardley.
