A few minutes ago

The United States military conducted strikes against Iranian missile launch sites and mine-laying vessels in southern Iran while top Iranian negotiators arrived in Qatar for peace talks. US Central Command characterized the operations as self-defense measures in response to threats against American troops.

The timing underscores the complicated dynamics between military action and diplomatic engagement. Iranian officials, including the country's top negotiator and foreign minister, traveled to Doha to meet with Qatar's prime minister. The discussions focused on several key issues including the Strait of Hormuz, Iran's enriched uranium stockpile, and the potential release of frozen Iranian funds as part of a final agreement.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio emphasized the strategic importance of the Strait of Hormuz, telling reporters that "the straits have to be open" and "they're going to be open one way or the other." He made these comments during an official visit to India. Rubio also suggested that negotiating the specific language in a deal could "take a few days," though he noted that President Trump has expressed his desire to reach an agreement or accept no deal at all.

Iranian foreign ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei stated that while substantial progress had been made on many issues, claiming an imminent agreement would be premature. He indicated that nuclear issues would only be negotiated after the framework accord was finalized.

President Trump has connected any Iran deal to broader regional arrangements, insisting that additional countries join the Abraham Accords, which normalize relations between Israel and Arab nations. Trump specifically mentioned Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, Turkey, Egypt, and Jordan as countries that should "immediately" sign on to the agreements. Bahrain and the United Arab Emirates previously joined the accords in 2020.

The proposal has drawn skepticism from some quarters. Masood Khan, Pakistan's former ambassador to the United States, questioned how workable the proposal would be, noting that the Abraham Accords requirement represented a new dimension to negotiations that had not previously been on the agenda.

Oil markets reflected investor sentiment about potential progress. While West Texas Intermediate crude fell more than 5 percent, reaching 91.33 dollars per barrel, North Sea Brent crude rose 1.6 percent to 97.68 dollars. Oil prices had dropped below 100 dollars per barrel earlier amid optimism about a potential reopening of the crucial shipping route.

The negotiations occur against the backdrop of intensifying regional tensions. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu ordered military escalation against Hezbollah in Lebanon, while the militant group responded by attacking Israeli positions in the north. Iran has pointed to Israeli actions and confusion in American positions as obstacles to reaching a complete agreement.

Both the United States and Iran have publicly downplayed expectations for an imminent breakthrough, even as senior officials continue diplomatic discussions in Qatar.

A few minutes ago

Officials in Orange County, California said the risk of a catastrophic explosion at a chemical storage tank has diminished, allowing thousands of evacuated residents to begin returning home. The discovery of a crack in the tank over the weekend appears to have relieved pressure inside the vessel, reducing the likelihood of a major blast.

The incident started Thursday afternoon at a GKN Aerospace facility in Garden Grove, about 5 miles from Disneyland, when a leak was reported at a tank containing 6,000 to 7,000 gallons of methyl methacrylate, a highly flammable chemical used in plastic and resin production. The situation prompted emergency officials to evacuate approximately 50,000 people from surrounding areas as a precaution.

"We're being told that the tank will fail, but there are different scenarios as to what that means," EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin said on Sunday. The most catastrophic scenario would have been an explosion that triggered additional tank explosions in the surrounding area, he explained. However, officials indicated the most likely scenario was a "low-volume release" of chemicals.

Firefighters spent days spraying water on the tank to keep its temperature below 85 degrees Fahrenheit to prevent a runaway reaction. On Friday, officials added a neutralizing agent to a nearby tank to further limit explosion risks. Late Saturday night, firefighters discovered a potential crack in the tank, which officials viewed as a positive development.

"Think of a soda can. If you leave it in a hot car it can explode. But if you put a hole in the can, the product is released and the can itself doesn't explode," said Andrew Whelton, an engineering professor at Purdue University. The crack could eventually allow authorities to gradually drain the chemicals from the tank.

However, some experts urged continued caution. Faisal Khan, head of the chemical engineering department at Texas A&M University, warned that "cooling is happening on the surface of the tank while runaway reaction may be occurring deep inside the tank." He noted that while the reaction had slowed, the risk of an explosive release had not been entirely eliminated.

A new and reduced evacuation zone has been established, with approximately 16,000 people still unable to return home. Officials monitoring air quality in the area reported detecting no pollutants. Garden Grove Mayor Stephanie Klopfenstein acknowledged the progress while noting the situation remained serious.

At least two lawsuits have been filed against GKN Aerospace by residents seeking damages for evacuation orders, shelter-in-place directives, and exposure concerns. Methyl methacrylate exposure can cause serious respiratory problems, neurological damage, and irritation to skin, eyes, and throat.

Google has fundamentally redesigned its search interface for the first time in nearly three decades, embedding artificial intelligence capabilities directly into the platform that billions of people use daily. The company unveiled the changes at its I/O developer conference, signaling what executives call the start of an "agentic Gemini era."

The updated search box now accepts longer, more conversational queries than the keyword-focused syntax that has defined Google Search since its inception. Rather than returning lists of website links, the redesigned interface directs users toward AI-generated summaries and visual elements, with options to interact directly with Google's Gemini chatbot. Users can still access traditional link-based results by selecting a "Web" tab.

"Google Search is AI search," said Elizabeth Reid, who leads the product, describing the overhaul as the most significant change in the platform's nearly 30-year history. The updates rolled out globally across desktop and mobile versions on Tuesday.

The changes are powered by Gemini 3.5, a new AI model announced simultaneously. According to Josh Woodward, who oversees Gemini's development, the system aims to create "a universal assistant that's personal, proactive, persistent." Monthly Gemini app users have reached 900 million, though this trails ChatGPT's 900 million weekly users.

Google Search queries hit record levels last month, with the company noting that its AI Mode chatbot interface has doubled its query volume every quarter since launching a year ago. Searches that include photos, videos, or documents automatically trigger the AI mode.

For paid subscribers, Google is introducing "information agents" that can conduct autonomous research and deliver summaries or action plans. A "generative user interface" feature will create customized visuals and interactive dashboards based on user requests. Gemini Spark, another new agent, can access information from Gmail, Google Calendar, and other Google products to assist with research, shopping, and planning trips or appointments.

The expansion of AI agents from niche business applications to mainstream consumer search represents a significant shift in how technology companies deploy artificial intelligence.

Google also announced new intelligent eyewear developed with Samsung, Warby Parker, and Gentle Monster. The voice-activated glasses with embedded cameras will respond to Gemini commands and launch this fall. The move revives Google's efforts in wearable technology following the commercial failure of Google Glass in 2014 and 2015.

Industry analysts have raised concerns that AI-generated answers in search results could reduce traffic to websites that traditionally depended on Google referrals, potentially affecting content creators whose work trains these systems. Google executives, including CEO Sundar Pichai, have acknowledged public anxiety about AI technology but maintain that integration will enhance search functionality.

The Knicks' path to the championship series has been remarkably smooth. They swept the Philadelphia 76ers in the second round and defeated the Atlanta Hawks 4-1 in the first round. Jalen Brunson earned Eastern Conference Finals MVP honors after the series concluded. The guard averaged 25.5 points and 7.8 assists across the four games. Game 4 was decided early when the Knicks scored 20 consecutive points late in the first quarter and into the second period. Karl-Anthony Towns contributed 19 points and 14 rebounds for New York. Cleveland's Donovan Mitchell scored 31 points but could not spark his team to competitive play.

Cavaliers coach Kenny Atkinson acknowledged the gap between the teams: "They're playing better basketball. You got to give them credit. They're on a heater. I don't want to detract from what we've done, but sometimes you've got to give the other team credit."

The Knicks now await their Finals opponent, which will come from the Western Conference. The Oklahoma City Thunder and San Antonio Spurs are currently tied 2-2 in their series, with Game 5 scheduled for Tuesday.

New York is chasing its first championship since 1973. The last time the franchise reached the Finals in 1999, they lost to the San Antonio Spurs.

The Knicks' return to prominence has energized their fanbase. Celebrity supporters including director Spike Lee and actor Timothée Chalamet traveled to Cleveland for the clinching game. Guard Landry Shamet remarked that "Knicks fans are a specific species of human that should be studied, they're crazy. They fly out to Cleveland on a Monday, they're everywhere."

The excitement extends to ticket prices for the Finals. Courtside seats for New York's first home game on June 8 were already listed at $105,000 on resale websites.

9:41 PM ET

Sonny Rollins, one of the greatest jazz saxophonists of all time, has died at age 95. His death was announced on his website on Monday, with his publicist Terri Hinte confirming the news. He passed away at his home in Woodstock, New York.

Born Walter Theodore Rollins in New York City in 1930, he earned the nickname Sonny from his grandmother. He grew up in Harlem, inspired by his siblings' musical talents and jazz legends like Louis Jordan and Fats Waller. He began learning saxophone at age seven and quickly became a fixture in the vibrant local jazz scene. While still in high school, he played alongside future stars Jackie McLean, Kenny Drew, and Art Taylor.

Rollins became instrumental in reshaping jazz during the bebop and hard bop eras. Miles Davis himself noted how Rollins quickly became "a legend, almost a god to a lot of the younger musicians" and was "an aggressive, innovative player who always had fresh musical ideas." Saxophonist Branford Marsalis has called him "the greatest improviser in the history of jazz" alongside Louis Armstrong.

His career faced a significant setback when heroin addiction led him to commit armed robbery in 1950. He served 10 months in jail on Rikers Island but successfully overcame his addiction through a rehabilitation program in 1955. This turning point sparked tremendous creative output. He released his debut album as bandleader in 1953 and recorded 17 more albums by the end of the decade.

His landmark 1956 album "Saxophone Colossus" featured the signature tune "St. Thomas," which drew from calypso and was named after his mother's Caribbean birthplace. He continued exploring new directions with albums like "Way Out West" in 1957 and "Freedom Suite" in 1958, where his 20-minute title composition became an elegant statement for freedom during the civil rights movement.

Rollins was famous for his rigorous approach to his craft. In 1959, he took a three-year break from recording and performing, practicing up to 15 hours daily on the Williamsburg Bridge pedestrian walkway. He later took another sabbatical between 1969 and 1971 to study yoga, philosophy, and meditation at an Indian ashram.

Throughout the 1960s and beyond, Rollins remained innovative, incorporating avant-garde, fusion, Latin American, funk, and calypso influences into his work. He also composed music for the 1966 film "Alfie" and later campaigned on environmental issues with benefit concerts and his 1998 album "Global Warming."

Rollins married Lucille Pearson in 1965, and they remained together until her death in 2004. He continued performing into his eighties with remarkable dedication and stamina.

May 21, 5:06 PM ET

The Trump administration has temporarily halted deportation flights to the Democratic Republic of Congo as the country faces a widening Ebola outbreak, according to reporting by Politico. The pause reflects concerns about exposing Immigration and Customs Enforcement officials to the virus and preventing potential disease spread to the United States.

However, experts say the measure will not effectively prevent the spread of Ebola. The decision also leaves at least one woman in legal limbo after officials relocated her to Kinshasa, the capital of the DRC. Despite a judge's order for her return, officials have stated they will not bring her back because of the travel restrictions related to the outbreak.

ICE regularly conducts deportation flights to countries worldwide as part of immigration enforcement operations. The flights transport individuals ordered removed from the United States back to their countries of origin. The Congo flights had been part of this broader deportation effort before the temporary pause.

The Democratic Republic of Congo has experienced multiple Ebola outbreaks in recent years. The current outbreak has grown significantly, drawing international attention and concerns from public health experts. Ebola is a highly infectious virus that causes severe illness and carries a high fatality rate. Previous outbreaks in the region have killed thousands and required extensive international public health responses.

Kate White, a nurse working on Ebola response efforts, expressed deep concern about the situation. "I am extremely concerned about the inability to get resources" to the Democratic Republic of Congo, she stated to BBC News.

The administration has not announced broader travel restrictions related to the outbreak beyond pausing the deportation flights. The temporary nature of the pause means flights could resume once the outbreak situation changes, though the administration has not specified when that might occur. The decision will likely depend on how the outbreak develops and risk assessments involving ICE personnel and the American public.

The move comes as the Trump administration balances its hardline immigration enforcement agenda with disease control concerns. The administration has previously defended its immigration detention and deportation practices, describing claims of mistreatment as categorically false and arguing its detention centers maintain high standards.

The pause also occurs amid broader scrutiny of the Trump administration's deportation programs. House Democrats have raised concerns about the transparency and conditions of ICE charter flights used for deportations, requesting detailed information from the Federal Aviation Administration about air operations and demanding greater oversight of detention practices.

The administration faces pressure from lawmakers and human rights groups monitoring its immigration enforcement operations as it continues implementing its expanded deportation agenda.

Sonny Rollins, one of the greatest jazz saxophonists of all time, has died at age 95. His death was announced on his website on Monday, with his publicist Terri Hinte confirming the news. He passed away at his home in Woodstock, New York.

Born Walter Theodore Rollins in New York City in 1930, he earned the nickname Sonny from his grandmother. He grew up in Harlem, inspired by his siblings' musical talents and jazz legends like Louis Jordan and Fats Waller. He began learning saxophone at age seven and quickly became a fixture in the vibrant local jazz scene. While still in high school, he played alongside future stars Jackie McLean, Kenny Drew, and Art Taylor.

Rollins became instrumental in reshaping jazz during the bebop and hard bop eras. Miles Davis himself noted how Rollins quickly became "a legend, almost a god to a lot of the younger musicians" and was "an aggressive, innovative player who always had fresh musical ideas." Saxophonist Branford Marsalis has called him "the greatest improviser in the history of jazz" alongside Louis Armstrong.

His career faced a significant setback when heroin addiction led him to commit armed robbery in 1950. He served 10 months in jail on Rikers Island but successfully overcame his addiction through a rehabilitation program in 1955. This turning point sparked tremendous creative output. He released his debut album as bandleader in 1953 and recorded 17 more albums by the end of the decade.

His landmark 1956 album "Saxophone Colossus" featured the signature tune "St. Thomas," which drew from calypso and was named after his mother's Caribbean birthplace. He continued exploring new directions with albums like "Way Out West" in 1957 and "Freedom Suite" in 1958, where his 20-minute title composition became an elegant statement for freedom during the civil rights movement.

Rollins was famous for his rigorous approach to his craft. In 1959, he took a three-year break from recording and performing, practicing up to 15 hours daily on the Williamsburg Bridge pedestrian walkway. He later took another sabbatical between 1969 and 1971 to study yoga, philosophy, and meditation at an Indian ashram.

Throughout the 1960s and beyond, Rollins remained innovative, incorporating avant-garde, fusion, Latin American, funk, and calypso influences into his work. He also composed music for the 1966 film "Alfie" and later campaigned on environmental issues with benefit concerts and his 1998 album "Global Warming."

Rollins married Lucille Pearson in 1965, and they remained together until her death in 2004. He continued performing into his eighties with remarkable dedication and stamina.
