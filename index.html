The World Health Organization has escalated its assessment of the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with WHO chief Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus warning that the disease is spreading rapidly in the central African nation.

The upgraded risk assessment reflects growing international concern about the speed and scope of the outbreak. Ebola is a severe, often fatal illness in humans, transmitted through direct contact with infected blood, bodily fluids, or contaminated materials.

The Democratic Republic of Congo has experienced multiple Ebola outbreaks over the past decade, but the current situation has prompted heightened alarm from global health authorities. The WHO decision to upgrade the risk assessment typically signals that additional resources and international coordination may be needed to contain the outbreak.

Ebola outbreaks can overwhelm local health systems and require significant international support to control. The virus has a high fatality rate and no widely available cure, making rapid containment essential. Previous outbreaks in the region have demonstrated how quickly the disease can spread without aggressive intervention measures.

The WHO has not specified the number of confirmed cases or deaths in the current outbreak, but the decision to upgrade the risk assessment and the language used by the organization's chief suggest the situation is deteriorating. Congo's remote geography and limited healthcare infrastructure have historically complicated outbreak response efforts.

The international health community has developed improved protocols for responding to Ebola outbreaks following the devastating West African epidemic of 2014-2016, which killed more than 11,000 people. Those protocols include rapid deployment of medical personnel, isolation facilities, and experimental vaccines that have shown promise in containing spread.

The WHO typically coordinates international response efforts during major disease outbreaks, working with national governments and international partners to deploy resources and expertise. The organization's risk assessment upgrade often triggers mobilization of these resources and increased surveillance in neighboring countries to prevent cross-border transmission.

Public health experts emphasize that early detection and rapid response are critical to preventing Ebola outbreaks from spiraling into major epidemics. The current situation in Congo will test whether lessons learned from previous outbreaks can be effectively applied to contain the virus before it spreads more widely.

11:06 AM ET

NASA announced it will open the management contract for the Jet Propulsion Laboratory to competitive bidding, ending decades of exclusive operation by the California Institute of Technology. The decision marks a significant shift in the oversight of one of NASA's premier research centers.

The Jet Propulsion Laboratory in Pasadena, California, has been managed by Caltech since its founding in the 1930s. The facility conducts robotic space missions and develops advanced technologies for planetary exploration. JPL has been responsible for landmark missions including the Mars rovers, the Voyager spacecraft, and the recently launched Europa Clipper.

The competitive procurement process will allow other organizations to bid for the right to manage the laboratory's operations. NASA stated that the move aims to ensure the most effective management structure for JPL's future missions and research programs.

The announcement comes amid broader discussions about NASA's organizational structure and contractor relationships. Administrator Jared Isaacman, who recently issued a comprehensive memo on agency reforms, has emphasized the need to evaluate all aspects of NASA's operations.

JPL employs thousands of scientists, engineers, and support staff who work on missions across the solar system. The laboratory's work spans planetary science, Earth observation, astrophysics, and technology development. Its Deep Space Network provides critical communications infrastructure for missions throughout the solar system.

Caltech will have the opportunity to compete for the new contract alongside other potential bidders. The timeline for the competition and transition has not been announced. NASA indicated it will provide additional details on the procurement process in the coming months.

The decision reflects NASA's interest in exploring different management models for its field centers. JPL is one of ten NASA centers nationwide, though it is unique in being operated by a university rather than through direct federal management or a corporate contractor.

Industry observers note that the competitive process could bring new approaches to JPL's operations while potentially creating uncertainty during the transition period. The laboratory is currently managing multiple active missions including the Mars Perseverance rover and preparing for future launches.

Rob Base, the rapper best known for the 1988 hip-hop anthem "It Takes Two," has died at age 59.

The artist, whose real name was Robert Ginyard, achieved mainstream success with the hit single that became one of the most recognizable tracks of the late 1980s. "It Takes Two," featuring DJ E-Z Rock, reached number 36 on the Billboard Hot 100 and became a cultural touchstone, sampled and referenced countless times in subsequent decades.

Base formed his musical partnership with DJ E-Z Rock in the mid-1980s in Harlem, New York. Their collaboration produced the album "It Takes Two" in 1988, which went platinum. The title track's infectious beat and memorable hook made it a staple at parties, sporting events, and in popular culture, earning it a lasting place in hip-hop history.

The song's influence extended far beyond its initial chart performance. It became a go-to track for DJs and was featured in numerous films, television shows, and commercials over the years. Its distinctive sample from Lyn Collins's "Think (About It)" helped define the sound of late-1980s hip-hop and influenced subsequent generations of producers and artists.

Following the success of "It Takes Two," Base continued to perform and record music, though he never matched the commercial heights of his signature hit. DJ E-Z Rock, his longtime collaborator, died in 2014.

Base's impact on hip-hop culture remained significant throughout his career. He continued to tour and perform "It Takes Two" for audiences who grew up with the song and new generations discovering it through samples and cultural references.

The rapper's death marks the loss of an artist who created one of hip-hop's most enduring party anthems. "It Takes Two" remains a fixture in popular music, regularly appearing on greatest hip-hop songs lists and continuing to be played at celebrations and events worldwide.
