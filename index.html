Yesterday, 8:58 PM ET

A 21-year-old gunman opened fire outside the White House on Saturday before being fatally shot by federal agents. The suspect was already known to the Secret Service due to previous attempts to enter the complex, according to court records.

The incident occurred around 6pm when the suspect approached a checkpoint near 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue. The gunman removed a pistol from a bag and fired at the checkpoint. Secret Service officers returned fire, striking the suspect. A bystander was also wounded in the exchange, though authorities have not determined whether the injury came from the gunman's shots or return fire from officers. No Secret Service agents were injured.

The suspect, identified in media reports as Nasire Best, was taken to a nearby hospital and later pronounced dead. The wounded bystander remains in serious condition.

Court documents filed in DC superior court show that Best had been known to the Secret Service for "walking around the White House complex inquiring how to gain access at various entry points" on multiple occasions during summer 2025. On June 26, 2025, he was involuntarily committed for "obstructing vehicle entry" to part of the White House complex. On July 10, 2025, he was arrested for unlawful entry after ignoring warning signs and walking into a restricted area outside the building.

According to the court filing, Best claimed to be Jesus and stated that he wanted to get arrested. A judge issued a "stay away order" barring him from the White House area. A bench warrant was later issued after Best failed to appear at a subsequent hearing.

Law enforcement officials described Best as an emotionally disturbed person known to law enforcement. An unnamed official familiar with the incident provided this characterization to The Washington Post.

President Donald Trump was inside the White House at the time of the shooting. On social media, Trump described the gunman as having "a violent history" and a "possible obsession" with the White House. Trump praised the "swift and professional action" taken by law enforcement officers.

The White House was briefly locked down following the shooting. The Secret Service said officers engaged the armed individual according to standard protocols for responding to threats near protected facilities.

The FBI is assisting the Secret Service and local police in investigating the incident. The shooting occurred at one of several checkpoints that screen visitors and vehicles approaching the White House complex.

This incident came approximately one month after a separate shooting at the White House Correspondents' Association dinner at a Washington hotel.

The United States and Iran are working toward a peace agreement that would extend an existing ceasefire and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, according to multiple reports. President Trump stated that a "memorandum of understanding" in the talks has been "largely negotiated," marking potential progress in ending the war between the two nations.

Under the proposed agreement, a 60-day ceasefire extension would be implemented while the Strait of Hormuz is reopened, allowing Iran to freely sell oil. Negotiations on limiting Iran's nuclear program would also proceed during this period. As part of the deal, Iran would clear mines it has deployed in the strait and refrain from imposing tolls on ships passing through the waterway.

In return, the United States would lift its blockade on Iranian ports that has been in effect since April 13. The agreement would also reportedly unfreeze some Iranian assets being held in banks outside Iran. Hostilities would be halted on all fronts, including Israeli operations and the conflict in Lebanon.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the deal could achieve "a completely open" Strait of Hormuz "without tolls" if successful. However, Iranian media reported that the strait would remain under Iranian control.

The Strait of Hormuz is one of the world's most important maritime chokepoints, with roughly a fifth of global oil supply passing through the narrow waterway. Its closure during the conflict has had significant economic implications for international energy markets. The potential reopening has already influenced oil prices.

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei acknowledged progress but cautioned against assumptions about an imminent agreement. "It is correct to say that we have reached a conclusion on a large portion of the issues under discussion. But to say that this means the signing of an agreement is imminent—no one can make such a claim," he stated.

A senior Iranian source told Reuters that Tehran had not agreed to hand over its highly enriched uranium stockpile, contradicting earlier reporting. The source indicated that Iran's nuclear issue was not part of the preliminary agreement.

President Trump has directed U.S. negotiators not to rush into finalizing terms, stating he is in no hurry to complete the deal. He also responded to critics, saying they lack knowledge of what the agreement contains.

While both countries face pressure to reach an agreement, substantial obstacles remain before any final deal can be reached. Details about enforcement mechanisms, timelines, and other key provisions have not been publicly disclosed. U.S. and Iranian negotiators continue discussions as both sides work to bridge remaining differences.

Oil prices dropped below $100 per barrel as signs emerged of progress in diplomatic negotiations between the United States and Iran. The decline followed statements from President Trump describing the talks as proceeding in a constructive manner.

Multiple oil benchmarks fell approximately 5 percent during trading. The price movements reflected market expectations that a diplomatic resolution could ease tensions in the Middle East and potentially reopen key shipping routes through the Strait of Hormuz.

The negotiations between Washington and Tehran have drawn sustained market attention due to their potential impact on global energy supplies. The strait serves as a critical passage for international oil shipments, and its disruption has contributed to elevated energy prices in recent months.

Traders responded to the diplomatic signals by reducing risk premiums that had been built into oil futures. The sharp price movement demonstrated the market's sensitivity to developments in the standoff between the two nations.

Energy analysts noted that oil markets had priced in significant geopolitical risk related to the Iran situation. Any move toward de-escalation would naturally prompt traders to reassess those risk calculations and adjust positions accordingly.

The White House has not provided detailed information about the substance of the negotiations or specific areas of progress. Trump's characterization of the talks as constructive marked the most positive public assessment from a senior US official since negotiations began.

Iranian officials have not issued parallel statements about the talks. The lack of confirmation from Tehran left some market participants cautious about the durability of the price decline.

The oil price drop coincided with broader market movements, including gains in global stock indexes. Investors appeared to be repositioning portfolios based on expectations of reduced geopolitical tension in the region.

Energy sector stocks showed mixed performance as investors weighed the implications of lower oil prices against the potential benefits of regional stability. Some analysts cautioned that diplomatic progress could prove fragile and that markets might be moving too quickly to price in a resolution.

"We are saddened and heartbroken to share the news of the passing of Kyle Busch, a two-time Cup champion and one of our sport's greatest and fiercest drivers," NASCAR said in a social media post.

Busch's family had announced his hospitalization earlier that day, stating he would not compete in the scheduled NASCAR race in Charlotte, North Carolina. A joint statement from his family, Richard Childress Racing and NASCAR described him as "a rare talent, one who comes along once in a generation."

The Las Vegas native won NASCAR championships in 2015 and 2019 during his career spanning more than two decades. He competed in his fourth season at Richard Childress Racing after winning titles with Joe Gibbs Racing. Busch set records throughout his racing career, winning 232 races across NASCAR's three national series, more than any driver in history.

In recent weeks, Busch had been dealing with health struggles. During a NASCAR Cup Series race on May 10 at Watkins Glen, he radioed his crew requesting medical assistance and a "shot" after the competition. According to the broadcast, Busch had been fighting a sinus cold that worsened due to the intense G-forces and elevation changes at the upstate New York road course. Despite his condition, he finished the race in eighth place.

The following Friday, just six days before his death, Busch won the trucks race at Dover.

At the time of his death, Busch ranked 24th in the Cup Series standings with two top-10 finishes in 12 races this season.

Known by the nicknames "Rowdy" and "Wild Thing," Busch became a polarizing but beloved figure in racing. He burst onto the Cup Series scene in 2005 by winning rookie of the year. His sharp wit and competitive spirit created what fans called "Rowdy Nation," attracting supporters of all ages.

Throughout his career, Busch was known for his fierce competitive nature, post-race confrontations with other drivers and bold behavior. Beyond his accomplishments as a driver, he also fostered the next generation of racing talent as an owner in the Truck Series.

A future Hall of Famer, Busch is survived by his wife, Samantha, and children Brexton and Lennix.

The NASCAR community gathered to honor his memory and legacy. The racing organization called his death "far too soon," acknowledging the significant loss to the sport.

Governor Jared Polis has faced a public rebuke from his own state party after commuting the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former Colorado county clerk whose case has become central to Republican claims about election security and the 2020 presidential election.

Peters, who served as Mesa County Clerk and Recorder, allowed unauthorized people to access her county's voting equipment in 2021. A former pro surfer named Conan Hayes, who is affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, gained access to the county's voting systems and copied sensitive passwords and information from Dominion voting equipment. This data was later published online by right-wing figures.

A judge sentenced Peters to eight and a half years in prison plus six months in jail in 2024 after she was convicted of four felonies and three misdemeanors. However, an appeals court overturned that sentence in April and ordered a judge to reconsider it. Polis then reduced her sentence to approximately four and a half years, meaning she will be released on parole on June 1.

In his clemency letter, Polis wrote that the original sentence was "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes." He pointed to sentencing disparities in Colorado, comparing Peters's case to former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for similar charges.

Election officials in Colorado strongly opposed the decision. Jena Griswold, Colorado's secretary of state, said the clemency "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president." She emphasized that Peters organized the breach of election equipment and broke public trust. Griswold warned that the decision signals to election officials across the country that "it's open season on our elections."

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, said the group was "furious, disgusted, and deeply disappointed." Election officials have stressed that Peters should receive no special treatment.

The case has attracted significant attention from Republicans. Donald Trump issued a federal pardon to Peters last year, though this was largely symbolic since her crimes are state charges. Trump also instructed the Justice Department to seek her release and has repeatedly called for her freedom on social media, continuing to spread false claims about election fraud.

Polis, a Democrat in his second term as governor, had remained quiet about potential clemency for months before signaling his intention in March. The Colorado Democratic Party's censure represents a rare formal rebuke of a sitting governor by his own party. While censures carry no legal penalties, they serve as official statements of party disapproval and highlight deep divisions within Colorado's Democratic establishment over this divisive issue.
