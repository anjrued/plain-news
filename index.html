A few minutes ago

Firefighters in Orange County, California have eliminated the threat of a catastrophic explosion from an overheating chemical tank in Garden Grove after an overnight operation, according to fire officials.

The crisis began Thursday afternoon when a leak was discovered at GKN Aerospace, a facility that manufactures parts for commercial and military aircraft. The tank contained between 6,000 and 7,000 gallons of methyl methacrylate, a highly flammable chemical used to produce plastic and resin. Authorities warned that an overheated tank "will fail" and could trigger an explosion that might ignite nearby tanks, prompting evacuations affecting approximately 50,000 people.

Garden Grove Mayor Stephanie Klopfenstein urged residents to take evacuation orders seriously, calling the situation "very dangerous." The EPA administrator noted that the "most catastrophic scenario" would involve an explosion that spreads to other tanks, explaining the scale of the evacuation in surrounding areas.

Firefighters worked continuously to cool the chemical tank by spraying it with water and keeping the temperature below 85 degrees Fahrenheit. On Friday, officials added a neutralizing agent to a nearby tank to limit explosion risk. Late Saturday night, firefighters discovered a potential crack in the tank, which became a turning point in the response.

The crack offered hope because it could relieve pressure building inside the tank. An engineering professor at Purdue University explained the principle using a simple analogy: a pressurized soda can left in a hot car may explode, but punching a hole in the can releases the contents without exploding the container itself. Officials said the crack could eventually allow them to gradually drain the chemicals from the tank.

However, some experts urged caution. A chemical engineering professor at Texas A&M University warned that cooling occurs on the tank's surface while a runaway reaction may continue deep inside. He noted that while the reaction has slowed, the risk of an explosive release has not been completely eliminated.

Air quality monitoring detected no pollutants in the area, with officials reporting that measurements remained completely normal. LA County agencies confirmed there was no current threat to LA County communities.

Despite the improved situation, tens of thousands of residents remained under evacuation orders. At least two lawsuits have been filed against GKN Aerospace by Garden Grove residents over the chemical leak. The suits cite evacuation orders, shelter-in-place directives, exposure concerns, chemical odors, and contamination fears.

Exposure to methyl methacrylate can cause serious respiratory problems, unconsciousness, neurological damage, and irritation to skin, eyes, and throat. However, Orange County health officials noted that the chemical is easy to smell and residents may detect it over large areas without being harmed.

Disneyland theme parks, located in nearby Anaheim, were not under evacuation orders but said they were monitoring the incident and supporting affected employees.

The deal would also provide Iran with sanctions relief and access to approximately $20 billion in frozen assets. In exchange, Iran would agree to negotiate on nuclear-related issues over a 60-day period beginning June 5 in Pakistan.

On social media, Trump stated that critics of the agreement "know nothing about" its potential benefits and that "time is on our side" in the talks. The progress in negotiations has already affected global markets. Oil prices have dropped as tankers began moving toward the Strait of Hormuz in anticipation of the waterway reopening.

Several unresolved issues remain between the negotiators. At the center of the delay is a U.S. demand that the unfreezing of frozen assets held in Qatar be made conditional on progress regarding Iran's enriched uranium. The deal also reportedly requires Iran and the U.S. and their allies to cease fighting, and for Israel to end its offensive in Lebanon.

The proposed agreement has sparked criticism from Republican hawks who question the deal's terms. Trump defended himself against this backlash, claiming his deal would be "THE EXACT OPPOSITE" of the agreement negotiated under President Barack Obama, which Trump withdrew from in 2018.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu spoke with Trump to seek reassurances about the ceasefire, particularly regarding Israel's ability to continue operations against Hezbollah in Lebanon. A critical security vulnerability in Ghost CMS has been exploited to compromise more than 700 websites, including those belonging to Harvard University and DuckDuckGo. The flaw, designated CVE-2026-26980, is an SQL injection vulnerability that attackers have used to launch a widespread ClickFix campaign.

The SQL injection flaw allowed attackers to gain unauthorized access to Ghost-powered websites and manipulate their databases. Once exploited, the compromised sites began serving fake Cloudflare security alerts to visitors, attempting to trick them into downloading malware.

Harvard University and DuckDuckGo are among the high-profile victims of the campaign. The breadth of the attack suggests automated exploitation targeting vulnerable Ghost installations across the internet. Security researchers detected the campaign after noticing patterns of similar fake security prompts appearing across multiple compromised sites.

ClickFix attacks rely on social engineering to convince users that their systems face immediate security threats. The fake alerts displayed on compromised sites mimicked legitimate Cloudflare warnings, instructing visitors to take actions that would install malicious software on their devices.

Ghost CMS is an open-source publishing platform used by numerous websites and blogs. The platform's popularity made it an attractive target for attackers seeking to compromise multiple sites through a single vulnerability. The scale of the exploitation indicates that many Ghost installations had not applied available security patches.

The vulnerability represents a significant supply chain risk, where a single flaw in widely-used software can cascade across hundreds of websites. Organizations running Ghost CMS installations should immediately verify they have applied the latest security updates to protect against CVE-2026-26980.

The attack demonstrates how content management system vulnerabilities can be weaponized for large-scale campaigns. By compromising trusted websites, attackers gained a platform to distribute malware to visitors who would normally trust those domains. This technique increases the likelihood that users will follow malicious instructions, believing they come from legitimate sources.

Website administrators using Ghost CMS should audit their installations for signs of compromise and ensure all security patches are current. Visitors to affected sites may have been exposed to malware installation attempts through the fake Cloudflare alerts.

Peters served as the elected clerk in Mesa County in western Colorado. In 2020, she allowed an unauthorized person to access the county's voting equipment using a security badge. Passwords and sensitive information related to the election system were subsequently made public and used by election deniers to question the validity of the 2020 election results. A jury convicted Peters in 2024 of attempting to influence a public servant, conspiracy to commit criminal impersonation, first-degree official misconduct, violation of duty, and failure to comply with the secretary of state. She received a nine-year prison sentence.

Polis signaled his openness to commuting Peters's sentence by comparing her case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis. Lewis was convicted of four felonies, including attempting to influence a public official, but received probation and community service rather than prison time. "It is not lost on me that she was convicted of the exact same felony charge as Tina Peters," Polis wrote on social media, "and yet Tina Peters, as a non-violent first time offender got a nine year sentence."

The governor's suggestion drew swift criticism from Democrats across Colorado. Secretary of State Jena Griswold, also a Democrat, disputed the comparison. She noted that Lewis was convicted of one count of attempting to influence a public servant and three counts of forgery, while Peters "organized the breach of the election equipment, broke the public trust and attacked the very foundations of our democratic process."

When asked whether Polis should reduce Peters's sentence, no Democrat in the state legislature supported the idea. U.S. Senator Michael Bennet, a Democrat running for Colorado governor, stated that Peters "knowingly broke the law, undermined our elections, and was rightfully convicted by a jury of her peers."

The district attorney who prosecuted Peters called any sentence modification a "gross injustice." The prosecutor noted that sentencing ranges exist because the same offense can be committed in different ways with varying consequences.

The censure represents a rare public rebuke from a state party against its own sitting governor. It reflects internal Democratic divisions over how to respond to cases involving election-related claims and signals discontent with Polis's handling of the Peters matter. The case has drawn national attention, particularly from Republicans and former President Donald Trump, who has urged Polis to pardon Peters as part of broader efforts to spread misinformation about the 2020 election.
