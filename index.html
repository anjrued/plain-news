Growing speculation in Israel suggests that fighting with Iran could resume if there are no signs of compromise in the near future. The war, which began in February, has shown no clear signs of resolution despite oscillating between military threats and diplomatic overtures from the Trump administration.

Regional Tensions Escalate

Domestic Political Battles

2:38 AM ET

A gunman died after approaching a White House security checkpoint and opening fire on officers Saturday evening, federal officials confirmed. The incident prompted a brief lockdown of the White House while President Donald Trump was present.

The shooting occurred shortly after 6pm local time when a man approached a checkpoint at a White House gate near 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue NW. The suspect drew a pistol and fired at Secret Service officers, who returned fire and struck him. He was transported to a hospital where he later died.

A bystander was also wounded during the incident. The Secret Service stated it remains unclear whether the bystander was hit by the suspect's initial gunfire or during the subsequent exchange between the gunman and officers. No Secret Service personnel were injured, and the president was not affected by the incident.

The sound of sustained gunfire sent journalists in the area running for cover. Selina Wang, a White House correspondent for ABC News, shared video on social media showing the moment gunshots interrupted her report on US negotiations with Iran. In the footage, Wang dove for cover as what sounded like dozens of shots rang out. Local news anchor Chris Flanagan estimated about 30 shots were fired, while a CBS News producer estimated at least 20.

Journalists and staff were instructed to sprint to the press briefing room during the incident. The security lockdown was lifted after the situation was contained.

Hours after the shooting, Trump praised the "swift and professional action" of law enforcement officials in a social media post. He noted the incident came a month after a shooting at the White House Correspondents' Association dinner at a Washington hotel, adding it demonstrates the importance of creating "the most safe and secure space of its kind ever built in Washington, D.C" for future presidents, in an apparent reference to his ballroom plans.

The suspect has not been officially named, but multiple US media outlets reported he had a history of mental health issues. He had been arrested last year while attempting to enter via a different White House checkpoint without authorization and had been issued a stay away order.

FBI Director Kash Patel stated the FBI was on scene supporting the Secret Service response and would update the public as information became available.

This shooting comes less than three weeks after another incident in which a man brandishing a gun fired at a Secret Service officer near JD Vance's motorcade route outside the White House. In that case, a civilian witness was wounded in the leg, and officers returned fire, striking the suspect.

SpaceX announced plans Wednesday to go public on the US stock market, revealing financial details about Elon Musk's rocket and satellite company for the first time. The company will list on the Nasdaq exchange under the ticker symbol SPCX, seeking up to $80 billion in investment at a valuation of approximately $1.75 trillion. The IPO is expected to occur on June 12.

The company confidentially filed for its initial public offering last month, which allowed for a regulatory review period before the information became public. Founded in 2002, SpaceX has grown into the most valuable component of Musk's technology empire and serves as a major rocket maker with extensive US government contracts.

The newly disclosed financial information shows SpaceX had revenue of $18.7 billion in 2025 against capital expenditures exceeding $20 billion last year. The company reported losses of over $4.2 billion in the first three months of 2026, while investing billions into artificial intelligence initiatives.

The investor prospectus revealed that SpaceX's connectivity segment, which includes its Starlink satellite internet service, has become the strongest part of its business. Starlink generated $11.4 billion in revenue during 2025 and brought in over $3.2 billion between January and March 2026 alone.

While SpaceX has long promoted its mission of expanding human presence to Mars, that goal has taken a backseat to other projects. The company is now focusing on creating orbital data centers to support the AI boom and expanding Starlink services. SpaceX also acquired xAI, Musk's artificial intelligence company, in February.

"Our mission is to build the systems and technologies necessary to make life multiplanetary, to understand the true nature of the universe, and to extend the light of consciousness to the stars," SpaceX stated in its filing.

The IPO timing comes shortly after Musk lost a court battle with OpenAI and its CEO Sam Altman. A federal jury concluded after a three-week trial that Altman and OpenAI were not liable for Musk's claims regarding breach of a founding agreement and unjust enrichment. SpaceX's prospectus lists OpenAI and other major AI firms like Anthropic as key competitors.

The forthcoming public offering is expected to increase Musk's wealth substantially. According to Forbes, Musk currently has a net worth of $807 billion, making him the world's richest person. The IPO could move him closer to becoming a trillionaire.

SpaceX is part of what analysts describe as one of the most significant periods for public offerings in market history. All three major AI companies mentioned are set to go public this year, each at valuations reaching hundreds of billions or exceeding a trillion dollars.

Yesterday, 7:55 PM ET

The California Institute of Technology could lose management control of NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory for the first time in the facility's history, according to the Los Angeles Times. JPL has been managed by Caltech since its founding.

The potential change in management would mark a significant shift for one of NASA's premier research centers. JPL is responsible for robotic space missions and has played a key role in projects including Mars rovers, deep space probes, and Earth observation satellites.

Caltech has operated JPL under a contract with NASA since the laboratory's establishment. The relationship has defined JPL's organizational structure and research culture for decades. The facility is located in Pasadena, California, near Caltech's main campus.

The Los Angeles Times report indicates that NASA is considering alternative management arrangements for the laboratory. No specific timeline for a potential management transition has been announced.

JPL employs thousands of scientists, engineers, and support staff who work on planetary exploration and space technology development. The laboratory's missions have included the Voyager probes, the Curiosity and Perseverance Mars rovers, and the Cassini mission to Saturn.

The management structure at JPL has allowed Caltech to provide academic oversight while NASA provides funding and mission direction. This arrangement has been viewed as a model for how universities can partner with government agencies on large-scale scientific projects.

Any change in JPL's management would require new contractual arrangements between NASA and whatever entity assumes control. The decision would affect not only day-to-day operations but also long-term planning for future space missions and research initiatives.

Neither NASA nor Caltech has issued official statements confirming the potential management change or outlining reasons for considering such a shift.

Newly confirmed NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman has issued a comprehensive memo outlining his vision for reorganizing the space agency. The billionaire private astronaut, who was confirmed by the Senate in a 67-30 vote, is moving quickly to implement changes he believes will help NASA accelerate its mission delivery and maintain American leadership in space.

Isaacman becomes NASA's 15th administrator after a turbulent nomination process. President Trump initially nominated him earlier this year, then withdrew the nomination in May during a public dispute with Elon Musk, citing Isaacman's donations to Democratic candidates as the reason. Isaacman later re-emerged as a candidate and, according to the Wall Street Journal, worked with allies within the administration to secure his position.

The new administrator is an advocate of Mars missions and a close ally of SpaceX CEO Elon Musk. During his second confirmation hearing two weeks before the vote, Isaacman told senators that NASA must accelerate its efforts to beat China back to the moon this decade. Acting NASA chief Sean Duffy, who also leads the Transportation Department, congratulated Isaacman on social media and expressed hope for success "as we go back to the Moon in 2028 and beat China."

Isaacman's confirmation resolved an internal administration conflict. Duffy had been pushing to move NASA under the permanent control of the Transportation Department, an effort that failed with Isaacman's appointment. This consolidation attempt had sparked tension with Musk around the same time Duffy criticized SpaceX for falling behind on its NASA contracts and suggested looking to competitors like Jeff Bezos's Blue Origin for alternatives.

The new administrator brings a unique background to the role. Isaacman made his fortune founding Shift4, a payment processing startup that now handles billions of transactions annually. Unlike previous NASA leaders who came through traditional aerospace or political channels, Isaacman has personal spaceflight experience, having flown on two SpaceX missions that he funded himself with all-civilian crews. His most recent flight in 2024 included the first spacewalk conducted by a private company.

His close ties to SpaceX and commercial space ventures signal a potential shift in how NASA approaches partnerships with private industry. The agency faces significant pressure to maintain America's position in space exploration as China advances its own lunar program. Isaacman's emphasis on faster mission delivery and his experience with commercial spaceflight suggest he intends to leverage private sector capabilities to meet these challenges.

The fight, dubbed "Glory in Giza" and held at the Pyramids of Giza on May 23, marked the first heavyweight title bout ever staged in Egypt. The event was streamed live on DAZN and pitted the unified boxing champion against a man with minimal professional boxing experience.

Usyk, who had not fought since his fifth-round knockout of Daniel Dubois at Wembley in July, put his WBA, WBC and IBF heavyweight titles on the line against the 36-year-old Dutchman. Verhoeven, despite his decorated kickboxing career with a 66-10 record and 21 knockouts, had only one professional boxing bout to his name, a knockout victory in 2014.

The Ukrainian champion, who has won six fights over the past four years including two victories each over Anthony Joshua, Tyson Fury and Dubois, vacated his WBO title in November rather than face British mandatory challenger Fabio Wardley.

Verhoeven, who spent 12 years as undisputed heavyweight kickboxing champion, expressed motivation about the challenge. "I accomplished everything I set out to accomplish," he said before the fight. "But staying at the top for that long didn't take away from the hunger, it strengthened it. Usyk is the undisputed in boxing. That's the kind of challenge that motivated me. Undisputed versus undisputed. The best versus the best."

However, Wardley criticized the matchup as unworthy. "I don't feel like it's a real, genuine challenge nor someone that deserves the shot," the 31-year-old told Sky Sports. "But I do understand Usyk is at a point in his career where he's able to do what he wants for a bit in that sense. He's earned the position to take an easy fight."

Wardley defended the WBO title against Dubois on May 9 in Manchester before Usyk's Egypt spectacle.

The fight drew comparisons to other recent boxing curiosities, including Anthony Joshua's bout against YouTuber Jake Paul in Miami, which was streamed to Netflix's approximately 300 million subscribers worldwide.

Usyk acknowledged his opponent's achievements before the fight. "I truly respect people who reach the very top in their sport," he said. "Rico is one of them, a powerful athlete and a great champion. He's truly the king of kickboxing."

Despite the controversial nature of the stoppage, Usyk emerged with his heavyweight titles intact, though questions remain about the competitive value of the matchup.

Yesterday, 8:13 PM ET

Romanian director Cristian Mungiu has won the Palme d'Or at the Cannes Film Festival for his English-language debut "Fjord," marking his second win of the prestigious award. The 58-year-old filmmaker previously took home the top prize 19 years ago for his abortion drama "4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days."

"Fjord" stars Renate Reinsve and Sebastian Stan as Romanian religious parents who relocate to Norway, where they become accused of child abuse. The victory makes Mungiu the 10th director in history to receive two Palmes d'Or, joining an elite group that includes Francis Ford Coppola, the Dardenne brothers, Michael Haneke, Ken Loach, and Ruben Östlund.

US distributor Neon has already acquired the film, marking their seventh consecutive year taking home the festival's top prize. This continues a successful track record for the distributor, as Sean Baker's "Anora" previously won the Palme two years ago before sweeping the 2025 Oscars.

The 79th Cannes Film Festival was described as slightly muted, with disappointing returns from various auteurs and a notable absence of Hollywood glamour. Both American films in competition were ignored by the judges, including James Gray's "Paper Tiger" starring Adam Driver and Scarlett Johansson, and Ira Sachs' AIDS musical "The Man I Love" starring Rami Malek.

Andrey Zvyagintsev's "Minotaur," a dark satire about corruption and infidelity in contemporary Russia, won the runner-up Grand Prix. Valeska Grisebach's Bulgaria-set drama "The Dreamed Adventure" took third place with the Jury prize.

The director award was shared between Pawel Pawlikowski for "Fatherland" and Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi for "The Black Ball." Both acting awards were also split between pairs of performers. Virginie Efira and Tao Okamoto shared the best actress prize for Ryusuke Hamaguchi's care drama "All of a Sudden," while Valentin Campagne and Emmanuel Macchia won best actor for the queer World War I drama "Coward."

Park Chan-wook presided over this year's jury, which included Demi Moore, Stellan Skarsgård, Chloé Zhao, and Paul Laverty. During the closing ceremony, Isabelle Huppert paid special tribute to Barbra Streisand, one of three honorary Palme d'Or recipients this year alongside Peter Jackson and John Travolta.

Streisand was unable to attend the festival due to a knee injury but sent a video message to the audience. "In this crazy, volatile world that seems more fractured every day, it's reassuring to see the compelling movies at this festival, by artists from many countries," she said. "Film has that magical ability to unite us, opening our hearts and minds."

Communities near the epicenter of an ongoing Ebola outbreak are expressing fear and concerns about the spread of misinformation, according to reports from the affected region. Local residents have raised alarms about inaccurate information circulating within their communities as the public health crisis continues.

The concerns come as health authorities work to contain the outbreak and provide accurate information to affected populations. Misinformation during disease outbreaks can complicate response efforts by undermining public trust in health measures and leading to behaviors that increase transmission risk.

Ebola is a severe viral disease that spreads through direct contact with bodily fluids of infected individuals. Previous outbreaks have demonstrated the importance of accurate public health communication in controlling the disease's spread. Communities near outbreak epicenters face heightened risk and often require intensive education and support from health workers.

The current situation reflects challenges that have emerged in past Ebola outbreaks, where false information about transmission, prevention, and treatment has hindered response efforts. Health officials typically work to counter misinformation through community engagement, trusted local leaders, and clear communication about the disease and protective measures.

In response to the outbreak, U.S. health authorities have expanded screening measures. The CDC announced the addition of an Atlanta area airport for Ebola screening, expanding the network of entry points where travelers from affected regions will undergo health checks. This measure aims to detect potential cases before they can spread within the United States.

The screening expansion follows established protocols used during previous Ebola outbreaks, when U.S. airports implemented temperature checks and health questionnaires for passengers arriving from affected countries. The Atlanta airport joins other U.S. facilities designated for enhanced screening as part of the federal response to international health threats.

Public health experts emphasize that containing Ebola requires coordinated efforts including rapid case identification, contact tracing, safe burial practices, and community cooperation. The virus has a high fatality rate but does not spread through casual contact or airborne transmission, making it containable with proper public health measures.

Local fears about the current outbreak underscore the need for ongoing communication between health authorities and affected communities to ensure accurate information reaches those at risk.
