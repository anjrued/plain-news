Yesterday, 9:30 PM ET

A 21-year-old man who fired a pistol at a security checkpoint near the White House on Saturday was shot and killed by Secret Service officers. A bystander was also struck during the exchange of gunfire, though it remains unclear whether the bystander was hit by the suspect's shots or return fire from officers. No Secret Service agents were injured.

The incident occurred shortly after 6 p.m. when the suspect approached a checkpoint near 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue. He removed a pistol from a bag and opened fire. Secret Service officers returned fire, striking the suspect. The White House was briefly locked down following the shooting.

The suspect, identified in media reports as Nasire Best, was already known to the Secret Service. According to court filings, Best had been observed multiple times during the previous summer "walking around the White House complex inquiring how to gain access at various entry points."

In June 2025, Best was involuntarily committed after obstructing vehicle entry to part of the White House complex. The following month, on July 10, he was arrested for unlawful entry after ignoring warning signs and walking into a restricted area outside the White House. During that arrest, Best claimed to be Jesus and stated that he wanted to get arrested, according to court documents.

A judge issued a stay-away order at an initial hearing, prohibiting Best from entering the White House area. A bench warrant was later issued after he failed to attend a subsequent court hearing.

An unnamed law enforcement official described Best to the Washington Post as an emotionally disturbed person who was known to law enforcement.

President Donald Trump was inside the White House when the shooting occurred. He said on social media that the gunman had "a violent history" and a "possible obsession" with the White House. Trump praised the "swift and professional action" of law enforcement officials in response to the incident.

Trump also used the incident to reference his contentious plans for a new ballroom at the White House, stating that it "goes to show how important it is, for all future presidents, to get, what will be, the most safe and secure space of its kind ever built in Washington, DC."

The FBI is assisting the Secret Service and local police in investigating the incident. The Secret Service has not released specific details about the weapons used or the number of shots fired during the confrontation.

This shooting came one month after a separate shooting incident at the White House Correspondents' Association dinner held at a Washington hotel.

Yesterday, 9:23 PM ET

Russia launched a massive assault on Kyiv and surrounding areas using hundreds of drones and dozens of missiles, including its advanced Oreshnik hypersonic weapon system. The overnight attack killed at least four people and wounded approximately 100 across the country, with Kyiv bearing the brunt of the bombardment.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the assault involved 600 drones and 90 missiles of various types, including 36 ballistic missiles. He noted that not all ballistic missiles were successfully intercepted and that Kyiv was the primary target. "They are genuinely deranged," Zelenskyy said on Telegram, describing damage to water supply facilities, a market that burned down, dozens of residential buildings, and several schools. Russia also hit the city of Bila Tserkva in the Kyiv region with the Oreshnik missile.

According to preliminary reports, two people were killed in Kyiv and 56 wounded, while two more died in the surrounding Kyiv region with nine injured. Kyiv's mayor, Vitali Klitschko, reported damage across every district of the capital. A school fire resulted from one strike, while another attack on a business center left people trapped in a shelter.

Russia's defense ministry confirmed using the Oreshnik, along with Iskander, Kinzhal, and Zircon missiles, claiming they targeted Ukrainian military command facilities, airbases, and military enterprises. The ministry characterized the strikes as retaliation for Ukrainian attacks on Russian civilian areas. Ukraine denied responsibility for a deadly drone attack on a student dormitory in the Russian-controlled Luhansk region that sparked Moscow's threat of revenge.

The assault damaged several significant buildings. Ukraine's National Art Museum sustained damage to ceilings, windows, and interior spaces, though staff confirmed the collection itself remained undamaged. Ukraine's foreign ministry building was damaged for the first time since World War II, with the historic structure suffering light damage from nearby explosions. Government headquarters also sustained damage, with windows blown out but no injuries reported.

A longtime market worker described the devastation: "It was a terrible night and there has never been anything like it in the entire war. My job is gone, everything is gone, everything has burned down."

International condemnation followed quickly. French President Emmanuel Macron condemned the use of the Oreshnik missile as a sign of "the dead end of Russia's war of aggression." German Chancellor Friedrich Merz called it a "reckless escalation." EU Foreign Policy Chief Kaja Kallas described the strikes as "abhorrent acts of terror meant to kill as many civilians as possible," characterizing the Oreshnik deployment as "political scare tactic and reckless nuclear brinkmanship."

This marked the third use of the Oreshnik missile system since Russia began deploying the weapon in the conflict. The scale and sophistication of weapons deployed in this attack represented a significant intensification of Russia's assault on the Ukrainian capital.

Oil Prices Drop on Signs of US-Iran Deal

Oil prices fell sharply on Monday after reports suggested the United States and Iran were moving closer to a peace agreement, sending benchmark crude below $100 a barrel for the first time in two weeks. The decline reflected market expectations that a deal could resolve the three-month conflict and restore normal shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for...

7:08 AM ET

Oil prices fell sharply on Monday after reports suggested the United States and Iran were moving closer to a peace agreement, sending benchmark crude below $100 a barrel for the first time in two weeks. The decline reflected market expectations that a deal could resolve the three-month conflict and restore normal shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global energy supplies.

Brent crude futures, the global oil benchmark, dropped 6 percent to $97.28 a barrel as traders responded to signs of diplomatic progress. The prospect of reduced Middle East tensions prompted investors to sell off oil holdings, while uncertainty remained about the timeline and specific terms of any agreement.

However, officials urged caution about the prospects for a quick resolution. An Iranian government spokesperson said an agreement was "not imminent," and the two sides remained at odds over key issues, including Iran's blockade of the strait. Warren Patterson, head of commodities strategy at ING, told Reuters that previous negotiation attempts had collapsed, so "the market will likely be more cautious about overreacting."

Even if negotiations succeed, analysts cautioned that normalizing oil flows would take months. Damaged energy infrastructure in Qatar and elsewhere requires repair, and shipping operations need time to resume regular patterns. Some tankers carrying liquefied natural gas and crude oil began moving through the strait on Monday, suggesting cautious optimism about reopening, though flows remained restricted.

Stock markets responded positively to the diplomatic developments. Japan's Nikkei 225 index rose nearly 3 percent and surpassed 65,000 for the first time, driven partly by the falling oil prices. The pan-European Stoxx 600 index gained 0.9 percent. Several major markets, including the United States and United Kingdom, were closed on Monday for public holidays.

The dollar weakened against other currencies, declining 0.3 percent against a basket of major currencies. The British pound gained nearly 0.6 percent to $1.3506, while gold climbed 1.46 percent to $4,574 an ounce.

The market response reflected how dramatically geopolitical developments can shift investor sentiment and commodity prices. Prior to the conflict, markets had anticipated interest rate cuts from central banks. But the surge in oil and energy prices triggered inflation concerns that quickly reversed expectations toward rate increases. The Bank of England now faces market expectations of two rate increases this year.

Energy analysts said traders were reacting to early signals of progress rather than confirmed policy changes. The key factor remains whether physical oil flows through the strait actually resume, which would determine whether price declines prove sustainable or temporary. Traders continue to monitor diplomatic channels for official announcements and details about any agreement's impact on global energy supplies.

Google has changed the appearance and function of its search box, marking the first design overhaul in 25 years. The company announced the change as part of its broader push to integrate artificial intelligence directly into search.

The updated search box incorporates AI-powered features that allow users to ask more complex questions rather than entering traditional keyword searches. Google says the redesign represents its biggest search upgrade in a quarter century.

The changes come as Google faces growing competition from AI-powered alternatives that threaten its dominance in web search. The company has accelerated its AI integration efforts to maintain its position in the market.

Sundar Pichai, Google's chief executive, discussed the changes and acknowledged public concerns about artificial intelligence. The company described the shift as entering an "agentic Gemini era," referring to its Gemini AI system.

Users can still access traditional Google search results through a modifier that disables AI features. Some users have reported issues with AI Overviews, the feature that generates AI summaries at the top of search results.

Google has faced criticism over AI Overview errors, including struggles to accurately define common words like "disregard," "stop," and "ignore." The company's AI system has produced incorrect or confusing results for some basic queries.

The search box redesign represents Google's attempt to reinvent its core product before competitors capture market share. The company is betting that AI-enhanced search will appeal to users even as it fundamentally changes how people interact with the service.

Google has positioned the changes as improvements that make search more conversational and intuitive. The company has not said whether the traditional search interface will remain available long-term or if users will eventually be required to use the AI-enhanced version.

The redesign affects how billions of people access information online. Google processes billions of searches each day, making any change to its interface significant for internet users worldwide.

Yesterday, 8:49 PM ET

NASCAR champion Kyle Busch died last Thursday at age 41 from severe pneumonia that progressed into sepsis, according to a statement from his family. Dakota Hunter, vice president of Kyle Busch Companies, confirmed the medical findings on Saturday after the family received an evaluation of Busch's condition.

Busch collapsed in a Chevrolet simulator on Wednesday while preparing for Sunday's Coca-Cola 600 at Charlotte Motor Speedway. He was rushed to a hospital in Charlotte, North Carolina, where he died the following day.

According to a 911 call obtained by the Associated Press, Busch experienced shortness of breath, felt overheated, and was coughing up blood before his collapse. A caller told emergency dispatch that the driver was "producing a little bit of blood" and feared he might lose consciousness. Busch was found lying on the bathroom floor but remained conscious when paramedics arrived.

"The medical evaluation provided to the Busch Family concluded that severe pneumonia progressed into sepsis, resulting in rapid and overwhelming associated complications," Hunter said in a statement.

Sepsis is a life-threatening condition that develops when the body's immune response to infection becomes extreme and damages its own tissues and organs. The immune system typically releases chemicals to fight pathogens, but with sepsis, this response becomes overactive, causing widespread inflammation, blood clots, and leaking blood vessels.

Busch had shown signs of illness earlier in May while racing at Watkins Glen, where he radioed his team requesting medical attention after the race. Despite these symptoms, he won the Trucks Series race at Dover last weekend before finishing 17th in the All-Star race on Sunday.

NASCAR has organized tributes to honor Busch's legacy. All 39 drivers competing in Sunday's Coca-Cola 600 will display a black number 8 decal on their cars in his honor. Daniel Suárez won the race and dedicated his victory to his close friend, ending an 82-race winless streak.

Busch was a two-time NASCAR champion who won 234 races across NASCAR's top three series during his two-decade career, more than any other driver in history. Brad Keselowski acknowledged that Busch had not been feeling well recently, though he did not provide specifics.

The NASCAR community has expressed widespread grief over Busch's unexpected death. NASCAR CEO Steve O'Donnell called him "an American badass," while figures including Donald Trump and JD Vance paid tribute to his talent and competitive spirit.

The family has not yet announced additional memorial services beyond the pre-race tribute held at Charlotte Motor Speedway.

Colorado Governor Jared Polis has drawn criticism from within his own party after commuting the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former county clerk whose case has become central to election denial efforts following the 2020 presidential election.

Peters, who was serving nearly nine years in prison, will be released on parole June 1 after Polis reduced her sentence to approximately four and a half years. Peters was convicted in 2024 of four felonies and three misdemeanors stemming from her role in allowing unauthorized access to Mesa County's voting equipment in 2021.

In his clemency letter, Polis wrote that Peters' sentence was "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes." He also compared her case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for similar charges.

Colorado's top election official, Secretary of State Jena Griswold, strongly opposed the decision. "This validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president," Griswold said. She warned that the clemency would encourage election denial movements nationwide and damage American democracy.

Peters allowed Conan Hayes, a former professional surfer affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, to access and copy the county's voting systems and attend a sensitive software upgrade. Sensitive passwords and information from the voting equipment were later published online by right-wing figures. A judge who sentenced Peters called her "a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again."

An appeals court overturned Peters' original sentence in April and ordered reconsideration. Trump, who has repeatedly spread false claims about the 2020 election, previously issued a federal pardon for Peters and instructed the Justice Department to seek her release. Trump has publicly called Peters a victim of unfair treatment.

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, expressed strong disapproval. "We're starting to hear from election officials across the country that this signals that it's open season on our elections and election officials," he said.

Griswold disputed Polis' comparison between Peters and Lewis, noting that their cases differ significantly beyond one shared charge. "Peters organized the breach of the election equipment, broke the public trust and attacked the very foundations of our democratic process," Griswold stated. She emphasized that Peters should receive no special treatment and that her actions continue being used to undermine future elections.

The clemency decision creates unusual tension within Colorado's Democratic Party, as Polis, serving his second term as governor, has historically kept election integrity a priority for state Democrats.
