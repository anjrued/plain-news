Yesterday, 8:58 PM ET

A 21-year-old gunman was fatally shot by Secret Service officers after opening fire outside the White House on Saturday evening. The incident left a bystander in serious condition and prompted a brief lockdown of the complex.

The suspect approached a checkpoint near 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue NW shortly after 6 p.m., removed a pistol from a bag, and fired at officers, according to the Secret Service. Officers returned fire, striking the gunman. A bystander was also struck during the exchange, though it remains unclear whether they were hit by the suspect's shots or by return fire from agents. No Secret Service agents were injured in the incident.

The gunman, identified in media reports as Nasire Best, was already known to the Secret Service before Saturday's shooting. Court records show Best had previously attempted to access the White House complex. According to a July 2025 court filing, Best was observed multiple times during the summer of 2025 "walking around the White House complex inquiring how to gain access at various entry points."

Best was involuntarily committed on June 26, 2025, for obstructing vehicle entry to part of the White House complex. Two weeks later, on July 10, 2025, he was arrested for unlawful entry after officers found him ignoring warning signs in a restricted area outside the White House. During that arrest, Best claimed to be Jesus and stated that he wanted to get arrested, according to court documents. A judge issued a stay-away order barring Best from the White House area. The New York Post reported that a bench warrant was later issued after Best failed to appear at a subsequent hearing.

An unnamed law enforcement official described Best to The Washington Post as an emotionally disturbed person known to law enforcement. President Donald Trump, who was inside the White House during the incident, commented on the shooting in social media posts. Trump characterized the gunman as having "a violent history" and a "possible obsession" with the White House. He praised the "swift and professional action" of law enforcement and used the incident to discuss future security improvements for the White House.

The FBI is assisting the Secret Service and local police in the ongoing investigation. Officials are examining the sequence of events that led to the shooting and reviewing available security camera footage from the area.

The injured bystander was not immediately identified in initial reports. Medical personnel treated the individual for injuries sustained during the gunfire exchange at the checkpoint, which serves as a screening point for individuals attempting to access the White House grounds.

The announcement comes as Google faces increasing competition in the AI search space and criticism over how artificial intelligence changes user access to information. The redesign also affects how users choose between traditional search and AI-assisted queries. The interface now prompts users to decide whether to "Ask AI" or use conventional search methods, introducing a decision point that did not previously exist.

Industry analysts described the change as marking the end of the internet's "golden age," suggesting the modification represents a fundamental shift in how information is accessed online. Alternative search engines have seen renewed interest as users evaluate options beyond Google's AI-integrated approach.

The timing of the announcement, weeks before Apple's WWDC conference where the company is expected to reveal its own AI initiatives, highlights intensifying competition in the artificial intelligence sector. Google's move signals that major technology companies view AI integration as necessary for remaining competitive in search and information retrieval.

The changes roll out as part of Google's broader effort to incorporate Gemini AI across its product lineup, reshaping services that have remained largely unchanged for decades.

Two-time NASCAR Cup Series champion Kyle Busch has died at age 41, the racing organization announced Thursday. NASCAR released a statement expressing grief over the loss of one of the sport's most accomplished competitors.

"We are saddened and heartbroken to share the news of the passing of Kyle Busch, a two-time Cup champion and one of our sport's greatest and fiercest drivers," NASCAR said on social media. The organization extended condolences to Busch's family, Richard Childress Racing, and the motorsports community.

Busch's family announced his hospitalization earlier Thursday, noting he would miss the scheduled NASCAR race in Charlotte, North Carolina that weekend. The specific cause of death was not initially disclosed by NASCAR, though his family later stated he died from complications related to sepsis and severe pneumonia.

The Las Vegas native had a career spanning more than two decades. He won championships in 2015 and 2019 and holds the record for most wins across NASCAR's three national series with 232 victories, more than any driver in history. He was in his fourth season with Richard Childress Racing after previously winning titles with Joe Gibbs Racing.

Earlier in May, during a NASCAR Cup Series race at Watkins Glen, Busch radioed his crew requesting medical assistance after struggling with a sinus cold that worsened due to the intense G-forces at the upstate New York road course. Despite the illness, he completed the race and finished eighth. He won a truck series race at Dover on Friday, six days before his death.

At the time of his death, Busch ranked 24th in the Cup Series standings with two top-10 finishes in 12 races that season.

In a joint statement, Busch's family, Richard Childress Racing, and NASCAR described him as a generational talent. "A future Hall of Famer, Kyle was a rare talent, one who comes along once in a generation. He was fierce, he was passionate, he was immensely skilled and he cared deeply about the sport and fans," the statement read.

Known by nicknames including "Rowdy" and "Wild Thing," Busch became a polarizing figure in the sport due to post-race confrontations, feuds with other drivers, and unconventional behavior. He entered the Cup Series in 2005 and won rookie of the year. Beyond driving, Busch also fostered the next generation of drivers as an owner in the Truck Series and built a dedicated fanbase known as "Rowdy Nation."

Busch is survived by his wife Samantha and children Brexton and Lennix.

The NASCAR community mourned his passing with tributes preceding the Coca-Cola 600 race held three days after his death.

The performance falls short of even Solo: A Star Wars Story, which many considered a box office disappointment when it launched in 2018. Solo earned $103 million domestically and $171 million globally during its opening weekend, figures that exceed The Mandalorian and Grogu's totals even before adjusting for inflation.

The film brings the popular Disney Plus series to the big screen, featuring Pedro Pascal as the titular character and the beloved character known as Grogu, widely recognized as "Baby Yoda." Despite the modest opening weekend, industry analysts suggest the results may not indicate failure. David Gross, an analyst quoted by Agence France-Presse, noted that while the numbers are "not on the level of the primary Star Wars films," they remain substantial for a spin-off. He explained that spin-offs typically earn "a piece of the business" rather than matching main franchise entries.

The weaker performance could signal ongoing Star Wars fatigue. Disney had paused theatrical Star Wars releases after The Rise of Skywalker in 2019, concluding the studio had released too many films too quickly. The Mandalorian and Grogu marks the franchise's return to cinemas after a five-year gap.

Disney expects the film to maintain box office strength in coming weeks, banking on positive audience reception despite mixed critical reviews. The studio hopes the release will drive viewers toward related products including merchandise, theme park experiences, and Disney Plus content.

The franchise's history provides perspective on the current situation. Disney's 2012 acquisition of Lucasfilm for roughly $4 billion initially appeared to be a sound investment, with early releases like The Force Awakens, Rogue One, and The Last Jedi each surpassing the $1 billion mark.

Governor Jared Polis commuted the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former Colorado county clerk convicted of allowing unauthorized access to voting equipment, prompting significant backlash from within his own party.

Peters, the former Mesa County clerk, had been sentenced to eight and a half years in prison for four felonies and three misdemeanors. In 2021, she allowed Conan Hayes, a former pro surfer affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, to access the county's voting systems and copy sensitive equipment during a software upgrade. Polis reduced her sentence to approximately four and a half years, making her eligible for parole on June 1.

In his clemency letter, Polis wrote: "This is an extremely unusual and lengthy sentence for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes." However, state election officials strongly disagreed with the decision.

Jena Griswold, Colorado's secretary of state, said the clemency "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president." She warned that "clemency also will embolden the election denial movement across the country and will leave a dark, dangerous imprint on American democracy for years to come."

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, called the decision deeply troubling. "We're starting to hear from election officials across the country that this signals that it's open season on our elections and election officials," Crane said.

Peters has become a prominent figure among those spreading false claims about the 2020 election. Trump repeatedly called for her release and issued a federal pardon, which had limited effect since her convictions are state-level crimes. Sensitive passwords and other information from Mesa County's voting equipment were later published online by right-wing figures.

Polis had previously suggested comparing Peters' case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for submitting forged letters to the legislature. Both had overlapping felony counts of attempting to influence a public official. Polis stated that "justice needs to be applied evenly" and cited sentencing disparities.

Election officials rejected this comparison. Griswold emphasized that Peters "organized the breach of the election equipment, broke the public trust and attacked the very foundations of our democratic process."

The judge who originally sentenced Peters in 2024 had been critical of her defense claims. Judge Matthew Barrett told Peters: "You are no hero. You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again."

An appeals court had overturned the original sentence in April and ordered reconsideration, which preceded Polis' commutation decision. The censure from Colorado's Democratic Party represents an unusual public rebuke of a sitting Democratic governor by his own party organization.
