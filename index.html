Russia strikes Kyiv with hypersonic ballistic missile

The strike on Bila Tserkva in the Kyiv region was part of a much larger operation involving 600 drones and 90 missiles of various typ...

Yesterday, 8:59 AM ET

Russia launched a devastating attack on Kyiv and surrounding areas using its advanced Oreshnik hypersonic ballistic missile for the third time in the conflict. The assault killed at least four people and injured approximately 100 across Ukraine.

The strike on Bila Tserkva in the Kyiv region was part of a much larger operation involving 600 drones and 90 missiles of various types, according to President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. Of those missiles, 36 were ballistic weapons. Ukrainian air defenses did not intercept all the ballistic missiles, with Kyiv bearing the heaviest impact.

The attack damaged critical infrastructure across the capital. A water supply facility was struck, a market burned to the ground, and dozens of residential buildings sustained damage. Several schools were also hit. In Kyiv's government headquarters, windows were blown out, though no one was injured. The historic foreign ministry building, which had stood intact since World War Two, suffered light damage from nearby explosions.

Ukraine's National Art Museum, which houses one of the country's most significant collections, was damaged in the assault. The culture ministry said the building sustained damaged ceilings, broken windows, and scattered debris, though the collection itself reportedly remained unharmed. Culture Minister Tetyana Berezhna stated that Russia was "systematically attacking civilian infrastructure and cultural institutions" in an attempt to intimidate Ukraine and destroy its identity.

According to Kyiv's Mayor Vitali Klitschko, two people were killed in the capital with 56 wounded, while the surrounding Kyiv region reported two additional deaths and nine injuries based on preliminary estimates. The damage was recorded in every district of Kyiv.

Russian officials confirmed the use of the Oreshnik, which can carry nuclear or conventional warheads, alongside Iskander, Kinzhal, and Zircon missiles. Moscow claimed the attacks targeted Ukrainian military command facilities, airbases, and military enterprises, characterizing the operation as retaliation for Ukrainian strikes on Russian territory.

Zelenskyy described the assault as a "heavy attack" and called for international consequences for Russia. Residents described terrifying scenes. One 22-year-old market worker told the Associated Press she was leaving Kyiv because everything she had worked for had burned down. A 74-year-old witness said explosions threw him and his dog backward like "a pin by the shock wave."

International leaders condemned the attack. French President Emmanuel Macron said the use of the Oreshnik signaled "the dead end of Russia's war of aggression." EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas called the strikes "abhorrent acts of terror" and described the use of the hypersonic missile as "political scare tactic and reckless nuclear brinkmanship."

The assault followed Russian President Vladimir Putin's threats of retaliation over a drone strike on a student dormitory in Russian-controlled Luhansk that Moscow said killed 21 people.

Oil prices fell to their lowest levels in two weeks as ships began moving through the Strait of Hormuz, easing concerns about global energy supplies. The decline followed optimism over a potential diplomatic agreement between the United States and Iran.

West Texas Intermediate and Brent crude futures both traded lower in early Monday trading. The price drop reflected market relief as tanker traffic resumed through the strategic waterway, which carries roughly one-fifth of global oil supply.

The dollar also weakened as traders reduced expectations for extended Federal Reserve monetary tightening. Currency markets responded to the reduced geopolitical risk premium that had supported the dollar in recent sessions.

Japan's Nikkei 225 stock index climbed above 65,000 for the first time, powered in part by the falling oil prices. Lower energy costs benefit Japan's economy, which imports nearly all of its petroleum. The milestone came during overnight Asian trading hours.

The oil market had spiked in recent weeks amid tensions in the Persian Gulf region. Traders had priced in supply disruption risks as diplomatic negotiations appeared uncertain. The Strait of Hormuz connects the Persian Gulf with the Gulf of Oman and serves as a critical chokepoint for oil tankers heading to Asia, Europe, and other markets.

Market analysts noted that while shipping has resumed, prices remain sensitive to any new developments. The energy market typically reacts quickly to geopolitical signals affecting major production or transit zones.

Investors also adjusted expectations for Federal Reserve policy. Bond markets began pricing in potential interest rate cuts later this year as inflation pressures show signs of moderating. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has not publicly commented on the recent market moves.

The oil price decline offered relief to consumers and businesses facing elevated energy costs. Gasoline prices in the United States had risen steadily over the past month. Transportation and manufacturing sectors that depend heavily on fuel costs stand to benefit from the decrease.

Crude oil storage levels and production data will provide further direction for prices in coming weeks. OPEC member nations have not announced any immediate changes to production quotas in response to the price movement.

Yesterday, 12:53 PM ET

Star Citizen has become the first video game to raise $1 billion through crowdfunding, developer Cloud Imperium Games announced. The space simulation game crossed the funding milestone after 13 years of development that began with a Kickstarter campaign in 2012.

The project now holds the record for the most crowdfunded video game in history. Cloud Imperium Games also provided an update on Squadron 42, the single-player campaign component of Star Citizen that has been in development alongside the multiplayer game.

Star Citizen remains in active development with no official release date announced. The game allows players to explore a persistent online universe with space combat, trading, and exploration elements. Players who contribute funding gain access to the game's alpha builds and can purchase virtual spacecraft and other in-game items.

The billion-dollar figure represents contributions from hundreds of thousands of backers worldwide who have supported the project over more than a decade. Cloud Imperium Games has used the funds to expand its development studios and staff to work on both Star Citizen and Squadron 42.

Squadron 42 was originally scheduled for release years ago but has faced multiple delays. The developer indicated progress continues on the single-player campaign but did not announce a specific launch window. The game features a cast of Hollywood actors and aims to deliver a cinematic space combat experience separate from the multiplayer Star Citizen universe.

The crowdfunding model has allowed Cloud Imperium Games to develop Star Citizen without traditional publisher backing, giving the studio creative control over the project's scope and timeline. The approach has also drawn criticism from some who question the lengthy development period and whether the finished games will justify the unprecedented level of crowdfunding support.

Despite the concerns, funding for Star Citizen continues to grow each year as the developer adds new features and content to the playable alpha version available to backers.

Felix Rosenqvist passed David Malukas in the final moments of the Indianapolis 500 on Monday to secure victory by the smallest margin in the race's 110-year history. The late pass gave Rosenqvist his first Indy 500 win in what became an instant classic finish at Indianapolis Motor Speedway.

The Swedish driver made his decisive move late in the 200-lap race, overtaking Malukas just before the checkered flag. The finish broke the previous record for the closest winning margin at the Indianapolis 500, though the exact measurement was not immediately available.

Rosenqvist, who drives in the IndyCar Series, had previously come close to victory at the Speedway but never captured the sport's most prestigious race. His breakthrough came after staying within striking distance of the lead throughout the final laps.

Malukas, the American driver who led going into the final stretch, held off the field for most of the closing laps before Rosenqvist's late charge. The finish capped a tense battle between the two drivers as they navigated traffic and made split-second decisions in pursuit of the $3.6 million winner's prize.

The race, held on Memorial Day weekend as tradition dictates, drew hundreds of thousands of spectators to the 2.5-mile oval in Indiana. The Indianapolis 500 remains one of the highest-profile events in American motorsports, dating back to its first running in 1911.

Rosenqvist's victory adds his name to the Borg-Warner Trophy alongside racing legends who have won the event. The 34-year-old driver had shown speed throughout the month of May at Indianapolis, qualifying well and running competitively in practice sessions before the main event.

The photo finish provided a dramatic conclusion to a race that saw multiple lead changes and strategic decisions play out over more than three hours of competition. IndyCar officials confirmed the record-setting margin shortly after the race concluded, cementing the finish's place in Indianapolis 500 history.

The Mandalorian and Grogu claimed the top spot at the box office during its opening weekend, but the numbers tell a less triumphant story. The film achieved the lowest opening weekend of any Star Wars movie released since Disney acquired the franchise in 2012.

The movie earned $102 million domestically over the US four-day Memorial Day weekend, bringing its global total to $165 million. Despite being based on one of Disney's most successful streaming series, the film failed to match the opening performance of Solo: A Star Wars Story, which previously held the record for the weakest Star Wars debut under Disney ownership.

Solo, which also opened over Memorial Day in 2018, earned $103 million domestically and $171 million globally. Even accounting for inflation, Solo's opening significantly outperformed The Mandalorian and Grogu. Industry analysts attribute this difference partly to Star Wars fatigue, as The Mandalorian and Grogu marks the first theatrical Star Wars release since The Rise of Skywalker in 2019.

Disney paused its theatrical Star Wars releases after concluding it had released films too rapidly. The company subsequently shifted focus to developing Star Wars content for its Disney Plus streaming platform, where The Mandalorian became the service's most-watched original series.

The film's performance comes as a notable contrast to the opening weekends of other Disney-era Star Wars films, particularly the sequel trilogy entries. However, industry analyst David Gross noted that the numbers should be understood in context. "These are big numbers and this is how spin-offs perform," Gross told Agence France-Presse. "The movie is a piece of the story, and it's earning a piece of the business."

Disney reportedly expects the film will maintain strong box office performance in coming weeks, buoyed by positive audience reception despite receiving mixed critical reviews. The studio is counting on the film to drive traffic across its entertainment ecosystem, including merchandise sales and theme park attractions. Since the character Grogu debuted in the original series in 2019, more than 13 million Grogu toys have sold.

The Mandalorian and Grogu had a significantly smaller budget of $165 million compared to Solo's $275 million production cost, potentially positioning it for profitability despite the softer opening. Disney has several Star Wars projects in development for both theatrical and streaming releases. Ryan Gosling's Star Wars: Starfighter is scheduled for theatrical release in May 2027.

The Star Wars franchise has generated over $10 billion in global ticket sales since its 1977 debut. While theatrical performance has declined, Disney Plus series like Andor have found critical acclaim, though other streaming entries have generated less enthusiasm among audiences.

May 21, 10:12 AM ET

The Colorado Democratic Party voted to censure Governor Jared Polis following his decision to commute the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former county clerk whose case has become central to Republican efforts to challenge the 2020 election results.

Peters, who was incarcerated for allowing unauthorized access to her county's voting systems, will be released on parole on June 1 after Polis reduced her sentence from eight and a half years to approximately four and a half years. In his clemency letter, Polis wrote that the sentence was "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes."

The former Mesa County clerk was convicted in 2024 of four felonies and three misdemeanors. In 2021, she permitted Conan Hayes, a former pro surfer affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, to access the county's voting equipment and copy sensitive materials, including passwords and other information related to Dominion voting systems. This information was subsequently published online by right-wing personalities promoting false election fraud claims.

The decision provoked fierce criticism from Democratic officials. Jena Griswold, Colorado's secretary of state, said the clemency "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president." She warned that releasing Peters "will embolden the election denial movement across the country and will leave a dark, dangerous imprint on American democracy for years to come."

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, expressed that election officials nationwide were concerned the decision signals "open season on our elections and election officials."

Griswold disputed Polis's comparison of Peters's case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for submitting forged letters to the legislature. While both faced similar charges related to attempting to influence public officials, Griswold stated that "Peters organized the breach of the election equipment, broke the public trust and attacked the very foundations of our democratic process."

A judge had originally sentenced Peters to eight and a half years in prison, stating during sentencing that "You are no hero. You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again." An appeals court overturned that sentence in April, ordering reconsideration.

Peters has received strong support from former President Donald Trump, who issued a federal pardon for her last year despite her conviction being for state crimes. Trump has repeatedly called for her release and instructed the Justice Department to pursue her freedom.

The censure represents a significant divide between Polis and his party over the decision, highlighting tensions within Democratic ranks regarding election security and the treatment of election officials who have promoted false fraud claims.
