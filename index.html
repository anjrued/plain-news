The United States conducted military strikes against Iranian targets on Tuesday, marking the first American military action since a ceasefire began on April 8. The attacks targeted missile launchers and efforts to lay mines in the Strait of Hormuz, killing four Iranian soldiers.

Iran's Foreign Ministry condemned the strikes as "an act of bad faith" and "a definitive violation of the ceasefire," warning it would not leave the aggression unanswered. However, Iran has not withdrawn from ongoing peace talks mediated by Pakistan and Qatar, signaling that negotiators want to preserve diplomatic momentum despite the military escalation.

The Iranian military has not announced specific reprisals, suggesting a deliberate choice to avoid disrupting delicate final negotiations toward an agreement both sides view as historically significant. Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, Iran's parliamentary speaker and chief negotiator, remained in Doha on Tuesday, focusing on how to transfer more than $12 billion in frozen Iranian assets to an Iranian account. He is also seeking sanctions relief for Iran's oil and petrochemical exports during a planned 60-day period for nuclear negotiations.

The proposed agreement includes a separate 30-day timeline for the United States to lift its blockade on Iranian oil ports and for Iran to allow commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, restoring maritime traffic to pre-war levels. The brief agreement would end the conflict but leave many issues unresolved, creating political sensitivity for all parties involved.

Hardliners in Washington, Tehran, and Jerusalem are pressuring negotiators against further concessions. Some Iranian lawmakers have insisted that any agreement should maintain Iranian control of the Strait of Hormuz. However, Ghalibaf, recently reelected as speaker by a large margin, has managed to marginalize this opposition for now.

Reports indicate that access to frozen Iranian assets represents the last serious point of disagreement between Tehran and Washington. According to Iranian allies, no further discussions about the Strait of Hormuz or nuclear constraints can proceed without transferring these frozen funds first. One Iranian MP claimed a plan to transfer $12 billion through Qatar to a Russian account before reaching Iran had been blocked by the United States at the final moment.

Oil markets responded to the renewed fighting, with Brent crude futures rising 4 percent. The developments prompted President Trump to convene a rare cabinet meeting at Camp David, marking only his second visit to the presidential retreat during his second term.

Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei, who took power after his father was killed in the war's opening strikes, claimed the tide of history favors Iran. He predicted the elimination of Israel by 2040 and called for unity among Muslim countries as the hajj pilgrimage began.

An industrial accident at a packaging plant in Longview, Washington resulted in at least one fatality and multiple injuries on Tuesday morning. The incident occurred at the Nippon Dynawave Packaging facility when a tank containing white liquor, a chemical solution used in the paper industry, ruptured.

The Longview fire department responded to what officials described as a hazardous materials incident at the kraft pulp and paper mill and liquid packaging facility. Workers at the scene sustained chemical burns from the ruptured tank. Emergency responders transported multiple people to hospitals for treatment.

The facility employs approximately 1,000 workers. Authorities have not yet disclosed the identities of those killed or injured in the accident. Details about the exact circumstances that led to the tank rupture remain under investigation by state and local authorities.

White liquor is a chemical solution commonly used in the paper production process. The substance can pose significant hazards when handled improperly or when containment systems fail. The ruptured tank released the chemical, exposing workers in the vicinity to the hazardous material.

The incident marks a serious workplace safety event at an industrial facility in southwestern Washington. The Nippon Dynawave facility operates as a major employer in the Longview area, which sits along the Columbia River approximately 50 miles north of Portland, Oregon.

Authorities are working to determine what caused the tank to rupture and whether safety protocols were properly followed. Occupational Safety and Health Administration typically investigates workplace fatalities at industrial sites to determine if violations contributed to the incident. The investigation will likely examine maintenance records, safety procedures, and employee training at the facility.

Emergency response teams secured the scene following the implosion. The fire department worked to contain any environmental impact from the released chemical. The status of the facility's operations and whether it will remain closed during the investigation has not been announced.

The accident comes as industrial communities across the Pacific Northwest grapple with workplace safety concerns. Facilities handling hazardous chemicals are subject to federal and state safety regulations designed to protect workers and surrounding communities.

The Nippon Dynawave company has not issued a public statement regarding the incident. Authorities have not provided information about when normal operations might resume or what additional safety measures may be implemented.

Investigators will focus on determining whether the implosion resulted from equipment failure, human error, or other factors. The findings could have implications for safety protocols at similar industrial facilities throughout the region.

Drivers for Uber and Lyft in Massachusetts have achieved a historic milestone by forming the first statewide union for ride-share workers in the United States. The App Drivers Union has now been officially certified, representing a significant breakthrough in labor organizing within the gig economy sector.

This unionization effort comes at a critical time when ride-share drivers face mounting financial pressures. Many drivers across the country have been struggling with sharply rising fuel costs, which have cut deeply into their earnings. Drivers in the Boston area report spending hundreds of dollars more each month on gas, with some experiencing fuel cost increases of $300 or more monthly without corresponding raises from the platforms.

The financial strain has forced many drivers to make difficult choices. Some work longer hours, sometimes 10 to 12 hours daily, just to maintain their previous income levels. Others have reduced their driving to conserve fuel costs, effectively earning less overall. Ride-sharing companies have responded with discount programs and rewards through branded debit cards, but drivers say these measures fall far short of addressing their concerns.

"Whatever passengers are paying, the drivers are getting probably about 25 to 30 percent of that," explained one long-time driver, criticizing the gap between passenger fares and driver compensation. Drivers note that the support offered by Uber and Lyft feels inadequate compared to what they actually need: direct pay increases.

Beyond immediate fuel cost concerns, the unionization movement also reflects broader anxieties about automation and job security. As ride-sharing companies continue investing in autonomous vehicle technology, drivers worry about the long-term viability of their livelihoods. The certification of the union gives drivers collective representation to address these concerns with company leadership.

The Massachusetts achievement addresses a longstanding challenge in gig economy organizing. Since ride-share drivers are classified as independent contractors rather than employees, they have historically faced obstacles in traditional union organizing. This certification establishes a new framework that could influence labor organizing efforts in other states.

The union recognition enables formal discussions between driver representatives and Uber and Lyft management regarding compensation, working conditions, and the impact of technological changes on their work. Industry observers expect this Massachusetts precedent to shape how other jurisdictions approach labor organizing for gig economy workers.

The formation of this first statewide ride-share union comes after years of debate about worker classification in the gig economy. Various states have wrestled with legislation defining employment status and determining what protections app-based workers should receive. The Massachusetts outcome may serve as a model for similar organizing efforts across the country.

Nvidia has officially ended support for its GeForce Control Panel, removing the application from driver version 610.47 WHQL. The retirement of the software marks the conclusion of a two-decade presence in the PC gaming community.

The GeForce Control Panel served as the primary interface for Nvidia graphics card owners to adjust graphics settings, manage display configurations, and fine-tune performance options. Users relied on the application to customize how games and applications used their Nvidia GPUs since its introduction in the mid-2000s.

Nvidia is replacing the Control Panel with its newer Nvidia App. The company will now distribute all future driver updates exclusively through this application. The Nvidia App merges features from both the Control Panel and GeForce Experience, Nvidia's software suite designed for graphics card owners.

This transition represents a broader shift in how Nvidia delivers software tools to users. The company developed the Nvidia App as a modern alternative that consolidates various functions into a single interface. According to Nvidia, this approach aligns with current operating system standards and user expectations.

Graphics card owners who update to the latest drivers will no longer have access to the Control Panel. Nvidia has not provided an option to continue using the legacy software alongside newer driver versions. The company is directing all users to adopt the Nvidia App for managing graphics settings and installing drivers.

The change affects anyone using Nvidia GeForce graphics cards who wants to receive the latest driver updates. Those who choose not to update their drivers can technically continue using the Control Panel, but they will miss out on performance improvements, bug fixes, and support for newly released games that typically come with driver updates.

The timing of the retirement reflects the technology industry's ongoing movement away from older software architectures. Many companies are transitioning legacy applications toward solutions designed for modern operating systems and contemporary user interfaces.

Nvidia's decision to consolidate its software offerings demonstrates the company's effort to streamline its product ecosystem. By combining the Control Panel and GeForce Experience into a single application, Nvidia aims to simplify the user experience and reduce fragmentation across its software portfolio.

Users transitioning to the Nvidia App will encounter a redesigned interface, though the core functionality for graphics management remains available. The application provides access to driver installation, graphics settings customization, and performance monitoring features previously scattered across multiple programs.

For long-time Nvidia users accustomed to the Control Panel's familiar layout and features, this transition will require adjustment. However, Nvidia positions the change as necessary progress toward more efficient and unified software delivery.

The Knicks dominated their path through the Eastern Conference, also sweeping the Philadelphia 76ers in the second round before beating the Atlanta Hawks 4-2 in the first round. They now sit on an 11-game winning streak, the third longest in a single postseason in NBA history.

Monday's Game 4 ended decisively with a 130-93 victory. The Knicks went on a devastating 20-0 run at the end of the first quarter and into the second quarter that essentially decided the contest. After Monday's game, Brunson acknowledged his teammates' contributions to the achievement. "It means a lot to get to the finals, but I wouldn't be here without my teammates," he said on the ESPN broadcast. "My coach, this staff, the fanbase, without them none of this is possible."

Josh Hart praised Brunson's steady presence during the run. "His leadership is vital for us. He's an even keel, he doesn't let us get too high or too low. Even games like this when we're up 15 or 20, he wants to make sure we're doubling down on our habits," Hart said.

The Finals represent New York's pursuit of their first NBA championship since 1973. The team's first home game is scheduled for June 8, with courtside seats already commanding prices of $105,000 on resale sites.

Sonny Rollins, one of the greatest jazz saxophonists of all time, has died at age 95. His death was announced on his official website on Monday and confirmed by his publicist Terri Hinte. He died at his home in Woodstock, New York, on Monday afternoon. No cause of death was provided.

Rollins rose to prominence in the 1950s as a genius of melody and improvisation. Over more than six decades, he released more than 60 albums and collaborated with many of jazz's most important figures, including Miles Davis, Thelonious Monk, John Coltrane, Dizzy Gillespie, and Max Roach. He was among the last living stars of the bebop generation, helping transform jazz from a dance and ballad form into a startlingly expressive art form.

Born Walter Theodore Rollins in New York City in 1930, he grew up in Harlem and earned the nickname Sonny from his grandmother. He began learning saxophone at age seven, inspired by his siblings and jazz legends like Louis Jordan and Fats Waller. By high school, he was already playing with future stars including Jackie McLean and Kenny Drew.

Rollins described himself as "primitive," saying he went "with my feelings more than my brain." This approach to breaking with convention and embracing improvisation helped chart a new course for jazz. Miles Davis noted how Rollins quickly became "a legend, almost a god to a lot of the younger musicians" due to his aggressive, innovative style and fresh musical ideas.

His career was interrupted by struggles with heroin addiction. In 1950, he committed armed robbery to support his habit. After serving ten months on Rikers Island, he entered a rehabilitation program in 1955 that helped him recover. His sobriety sparked a creative explosion, beginning with his debut album as bandleader in 1953. His landmark 1956 album "Saxophone Colossus" featured the signature tune "St Thomas," inspired by his mother's Caribbean birthplace. Other notable works included "Way Out West" in 1957 and "Freedom Suite" in 1958, where he created an elegant argument for freedom during the civil rights movement.

In 1959, Rollins withdrew from public performance for three years to practice intensively on the Williamsburg Bridge pedestrian walkway, partly to avoid disturbing neighbors. He returned with the 1962 album "The Bridge." He took another sabbatical between 1969 and 1971 to study yoga, philosophy, and meditation at an Indian ashram.

Throughout his career, Rollins continued evolving his sound, embracing avant-garde directions, fusion, funk, and calypso. He performed the soundtrack to the 1966 film "Alfie" and contributed uncredited saxophone to the Rolling Stones' 1981 album "Tattoo You."

Rollins received numerous honors, including a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award and the National Medal of the Arts, which Barack Obama presented to him in 2011. Rollins married Lucille Pearson in 1965, and they remained together until her death in 2004.

May 21, 5:06 PM ET

The Trump administration has temporarily halted the removal of detainees to the Democratic Republic of Congo due to a widening Ebola outbreak, according to reporting by Politico. Officials stopped the flights out of concern that they could expose Immigration and Customs Enforcement personnel to the virus.

However, the pause creates complications that experts say may not actually prevent disease spread. At least one woman has been left in limbo after officials moved her to Kinshasa, the capital of the DRC, only to refuse her return based on the travel restrictions, despite a judge's order for her return to the United States.

ICE typically assigns officers to accompany deportees on international flights, which can last many hours. Those officers then spend time on the ground in destination countries before returning to the United States. The agency has cited the need to protect its personnel and prevent potential disease transmission as the reason for the pause.

The Ebola outbreak in Congo has raised alarm among public health officials. The virus causes severe illness with a high fatality rate. The country's healthcare infrastructure has struggled to contain the virus, particularly in remote areas where access to medical care is limited.

The decision represents a rare exception in the Trump administration's aggressive deportation agenda. The administration has prioritized removing individuals from the country, with ICE conducting regular deportation flights to numerous nations as part of its enforcement operations. Human rights groups tracking ICE flights recorded an 84 percent increase in deportation flights during 2025 compared to 2024.

The pause appears to be temporary rather than a permanent halt. The administration has not specified when flights might resume or what conditions would need to be met for operations to restart.

The Trump administration's handling of deportation flights has faced scrutiny from House Democrats. A group of 40 lawmakers wrote to the Federal Aviation Administration, expressing grave concerns about what they describe as secretive deportation programs and demanding transparency. The letter references investigations revealing that the administration transported detained immigrants in ways that routinely violated constitutional rights, including allegations of abuse at detention centers.

The letter also calls for information about controversial practices on ICE flights, including the use of full body restraints during deportations. The Trump administration has previously defended these restraints as standard protocol designed to ensure safety.

The DRC team for the upcoming World Cup has also been affected by the health restrictions, canceling the Kinshasa leg of its pre-World Cup training despite still planning its US trip for competition.

Sonny Rollins, one of the greatest jazz saxophonists of all time, has died at age 95. His death was announced on his official website on Monday and confirmed by his publicist Terri Hinte. He died at his home in Woodstock, New York, on Monday afternoon. No cause of death was provided.

Rollins rose to prominence in the 1950s as a genius of melody and improvisation. Over more than six decades, he released more than 60 albums and collaborated with many of jazz's most important figures, including Miles Davis, Thelonious Monk, John Coltrane, Dizzy Gillespie, and Max Roach. He was among the last living stars of the bebop generation, helping transform jazz from a dance and ballad form into a startlingly expressive art form.

Born Walter Theodore Rollins in New York City in 1930, he grew up in Harlem and earned the nickname Sonny from his grandmother. He began learning saxophone at age seven, inspired by his siblings and jazz legends like Louis Jordan and Fats Waller. By high school, he was already playing with future stars including Jackie McLean and Kenny Drew.

Rollins described himself as "primitive," saying he went "with my feelings more than my brain." This approach to breaking with convention and embracing improvisation helped chart a new course for jazz. Miles Davis noted how Rollins quickly became "a legend, almost a god to a lot of the younger musicians" due to his aggressive, innovative style and fresh musical ideas.

His career was interrupted by struggles with heroin addiction. In 1950, he committed armed robbery to support his habit. After serving ten months on Rikers Island, he entered a rehabilitation program in 1955 that helped him recover. His sobriety sparked a creative explosion, beginning with his debut album as bandleader in 1953. His landmark 1956 album "Saxophone Colossus" featured the signature tune "St Thomas," inspired by his mother's Caribbean birthplace. Other notable works included "Way Out West" in 1957 and "Freedom Suite" in 1958, where he created an elegant argument for freedom during the civil rights movement.

In 1959, Rollins withdrew from public performance for three years to practice intensively on the Williamsburg Bridge pedestrian walkway, partly to avoid disturbing neighbors. He returned with the 1962 album "The Bridge." He took another sabbatical between 1969 and 1971 to study yoga, philosophy, and meditation at an Indian ashram.

Throughout his career, Rollins continued evolving his sound, embracing avant-garde directions, fusion, funk, and calypso. He performed the soundtrack to the 1966 film "Alfie" and contributed uncredited saxophone to the Rolling Stones' 1981 album "Tattoo You."

Rollins received numerous honors, including a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award and the National Medal of the Arts, which Barack Obama presented to him in 2011. Rollins married Lucille Pearson in 1965, and they remained together until her death in 2004.
