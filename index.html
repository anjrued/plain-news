Today, 10:00 PM ET

A new Ebola outbreak is spreading rapidly across central Africa, with health officials working to contain transmission while investigating how the virus emerged. The outbreak has prompted international concern as case numbers climb and response teams struggle to reach affected communities.

The World Health Organization confirmed the outbreak spans multiple countries in the region, with the Democratic Republic of Congo reporting the highest concentration of cases. Local health systems face severe strain as treatment centers reach capacity and medical supplies run low.

Researchers are investigating whether the outbreak originated from a spillover event from wildlife or whether the virus persisted undetected in a survivor population from previous outbreaks. Ebola can remain dormant in certain tissues of survivors for years, occasionally causing flare-ups. Scientists are sequencing viral samples to determine the genetic lineage and trace transmission chains.

Contact tracing teams have identified hundreds of potential exposures, but insecurity and difficult terrain in affected areas hamper tracking efforts. Some communities remain accessible only by foot or helicopter, delaying the deployment of vaccines and medical personnel. Misinformation about the disease has also led to resistance to health interventions in certain villages.

The outbreak comes as global health emergency funds remain depleted from recent disease responses. International aid organizations have appealed for immediate funding to scale up the response, including vaccine deployment, treatment capacity, and community engagement programs.

Ebola causes severe fever and bleeding and kills roughly half of those infected, though survival rates improve significantly with early treatment. The virus spreads through direct contact with bodily fluids of infected individuals, making healthcare workers and family caregivers particularly vulnerable.

Health officials stress that rapid containment is possible with sufficient resources and community cooperation. Previous outbreaks have been controlled through intensive surveillance, safe burial practices, and ring vaccination strategies that immunize contacts of confirmed cases. The current outbreak's trajectory will largely depend on how quickly response teams can establish control measures in remote affected areas.

U.S. to Invest $2 Billion in Quantum Computing Firms, Taking Equity Stakes

Today, 10:00 PM ET

The United States government will award $2 billion to quantum computing companies while taking equity stakes in the firms, marking a significant shift in federal technology investment strategy. The move represents the largest direct government investment in quantum computing to date and positions the U.S. to maintain a competitive edge in what many consider the next frontier of computing technology.

Quantum computers use the properties of quantum mechanics to process information in fundamentally different ways than traditional computers. They promise to solve complex problems in drug discovery, cryptography, materials science, and artificial intelligence that are currently impossible or would take conventional computers thousands of years to complete.

The equity stake component breaks new ground for federal technology funding. Rather than providing grants or loans that companies repay, the government will become a partial owner in these firms. This approach allows taxpayers to benefit financially if the companies succeed while giving the government influence over strategic technology development.

The decision comes amid intensifying global competition in quantum technology. China has invested heavily in quantum research, launching a quantum satellite and building a $10 billion National Laboratory for Quantum Information Sciences. The European Union has also committed billions to quantum initiatives through its Quantum Flagship program.

Several American companies are likely recipients of the funding, though specific allocations have not been announced. IBM, Google, and Microsoft have all made significant quantum computing advances in recent years. Smaller specialized firms like IonQ, Rigetti Computing, and Atom Computing may also receive support.

The program will require companies receiving funds to maintain certain operations and research activities within the United States. Recipients must also share research findings with federal agencies and collaborate on national security applications.

Industry experts view the investment as necessary to prevent the U.S. from falling behind in a technology with profound economic and security implications. Quantum computers could eventually break current encryption systems that protect everything from bank transactions to military communications. They could also accelerate the development of new materials, optimize complex logistics networks, and advance artificial intelligence capabilities.

The funding will be distributed over multiple years and administered through the Department of Commerce and the National Institute of Standards and Technology. Companies will need to demonstrate technical progress and meet development milestones to receive continued funding.

Google inadvertently disclosed details of an unpatched security vulnerability in Chromium, the open-source browser that forms the basis of Chrome, Edge, and numerous other web browsers. The premature disclosure occurred before developers could issue a fix, potentially putting billions of users at risk.

The vulnerability details were published in Google's bug tracking system before a patch became available to the public. Security researchers and potential attackers now have access to information about the flaw's technical workings. This type of premature disclosure, known as a zero-day vulnerability when exploited, gives malicious actors a window of opportunity to develop attacks before users can protect themselves.

Chromium powers not only Google Chrome but also Microsoft Edge, Brave, Opera, and many other browsers. A security flaw in the underlying codebase affects a substantial portion of internet users worldwide. The exact nature of the vulnerability and its potential impact remain unclear, but the accidental disclosure represents a significant lapse in Google's vulnerability management process.

Google typically follows responsible disclosure practices, coordinating with other browser makers and allowing time for patches to reach users before publishing technical details. This incident breaks that protocol. The company has not yet issued a public statement about the exposure or provided a timeline for when a fix will be available.

Browser makers now face pressure to develop and distribute patches quickly. The challenge intensifies because Chromium's widespread adoption means multiple organizations must coordinate their response. Microsoft, Brave, and other companies that build browsers on Chromium must incorporate Google's eventual fix into their own products and push updates to users.

Users cannot take immediate protective action beyond staying alert for browser updates and installing them promptly when available. Browser updates typically install automatically, but users should verify they are running the latest version once a patch becomes available. Organizations that manage large numbers of devices may need to accelerate their update schedules.

The incident highlights the complexity of managing security in widely-used open-source projects. While open-source software benefits from broad scrutiny, coordination failures can expose millions of users simultaneously. Google's security team will likely review internal processes to prevent similar premature disclosures in the future.

Sea Level Rise Accelerating Since 1960, New Analysis Confirms

Sea levels have been rising at an accelerating rate since 1960, according to new research that refines decades of coastal measurements. The findings confirm what climate scientists have long suspected but now demonstrate with greater precision. Researchers compiled and analyzed tide gauge data from coastal stations worldwide, improving the accuracy of historical sea level reco...

Today, 10:00 PM ET

Sea levels have been rising at an accelerating rate since 1960, according to new research that refines decades of coastal measurements. The findings confirm what climate scientists have long suspected but now demonstrate with greater precision.

Researchers compiled and analyzed tide gauge data from coastal stations worldwide, improving the accuracy of historical sea level records. The study addresses previous uncertainties in measurements caused by factors like land subsidence and equipment variations at different monitoring sites.

The analysis shows that global mean sea level rise has increased from approximately 1.5 millimeters per year in the early 20th century to more than 3 millimeters per year currently. This acceleration has become more pronounced since 1960, coinciding with increased greenhouse gas emissions and global temperature rise.

The improved dataset allows scientists to better separate natural variations from the underlying trend driven by climate change. Factors like ocean temperature expansion and ice sheet melting in Greenland and Antarctica contribute to the rising seas. Understanding the precise rate of acceleration helps coastal planners prepare infrastructure and communities for future impacts.

The research matters because hundreds of millions of people live in low-lying coastal areas vulnerable to flooding and erosion. Small islands and delta regions face particular risk. Even seemingly modest increases in sea level can dramatically increase the frequency and severity of coastal flooding during storms.

The findings provide policymakers with more reliable projections for the coming decades. Current models suggest sea levels could rise by 30 to 60 centimeters by 2100 under moderate emissions scenarios, with significantly higher increases possible if emissions continue unabated.

The improved historical record also helps scientists validate computer models that simulate ocean behavior and ice sheet dynamics. Better models lead to more accurate predictions about regional sea level changes, which vary considerably depending on ocean currents, land movement, and ice melt patterns.

Coastal cities from Miami to Mumbai are already investing billions in sea walls, improved drainage systems, and other adaptation measures. The new data helps justify these investments by providing clearer evidence of the long-term threat.

Kyle Busch, one of NASCAR's most successful drivers, has died unexpectedly, the motorsports community confirmed Thursday. He was 39 years old. No cause of death has been announced.

Busch won two NASCAR Cup Series championships in 2015 and 2019 and accumulated 63 Cup Series victories over his career, placing him ninth on the all-time wins list. He also holds the record for most combined wins across NASCAR's top three series with 223 victories.

Known for his aggressive driving style and fierce competitiveness, Busch was both celebrated and controversial throughout his two-decade career. He began racing full-time in the Cup Series in 2005 with Hendrick Motorsports before moving to Joe Gibbs Racing in 2008, where he spent 15 seasons. In 2023, he joined Richard Childress Racing.

Busch was racing actively this season and had competed as recently as last weekend. He is survived by his wife Samantha and their two children.

Tributes poured in from across the racing world following news of his death. Fellow drivers, team owners, and NASCAR officials expressed shock and grief at the loss of one of the sport's defining figures of the modern era.

Busch's career included victories in the Daytona 500, Coca-Cola 600, and Southern 500, three of NASCAR's most prestigious races. His 63 Cup Series wins place him among an elite group of drivers, and his dominance across multiple series showcased his versatility as a racer.

Beyond his on-track achievements, Busch was known for his foundation work supporting children with life-threatening illnesses. The Kyle Busch Foundation has granted hundreds of wishes and provided support to families facing medical challenges.

NASCAR has not yet announced how it will honor Busch at upcoming races. The racing community is expected to hold tributes in the coming days as the sport mourns one of its biggest stars.
