8:59 AM ET

Oil prices dropped sharply on Monday, falling below the $100 per barrel mark as diplomatic progress between the United States and Iran raised hopes for ending their nearly three-month conflict. Brent crude futures, the global benchmark, declined 6% to $97.28 a barrel, marking the lowest level in two weeks.

The price movement reflected investor optimism about a potential agreement that could reopen the Strait of Hormuz, one of the world's most critical energy chokepoints. Approximately one-fifth of global petroleum passes through the waterway, and its near-closure since late February had driven energy prices sharply higher and fueled worldwide inflation concerns.

Stock markets responded positively to the diplomatic developments. Japan's Nikkei rose nearly 3%, while the pan-European Stoxx 600 index gained 0.9%. Several major markets remained closed for public holidays on Monday. The dollar weakened by 0.3% against a basket of major currencies, while the pound climbed nearly 0.6% to $1.3506. Gold prices rose 1.46% to $4,574 an ounce.

Despite the optimistic market reaction, caution persists among investors and analysts. While negotiators have established a framework for an agreement, the U.S. and Iran remain divided on key issues, including Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz. An Iranian government spokesperson emphasized that an agreement was "not imminent."

Warren Patterson, head of commodities strategy at ING, cautioned Reuters: "We've been at this stage before, only for talks to break down. Therefore, the market will likely be more cautious about overreacting."

The Trump administration has characterized the talks as advancing but has not released specific details about potential terms or timelines for reaching a final agreement. Trump stated on Saturday that any deal would include reopening the Strait of Hormuz.

Analysts note that even if the waterway reopens, restoring normal oil flows could take months. Damaged energy infrastructure in Qatar and other regions requires repair before full recovery is possible. Giovanni Staunovo, a UBS analyst, stressed: "We continue to believe that the key factors for the oil market to watch should be the physical oil flows and so far, flows through the strait remain restricted."

The recent geopolitical developments have already shifted market expectations. Prior to the escalation in late February, markets anticipated interest rate cuts from central banks. The energy shock and resulting inflation concerns reversed those expectations, with central banks now expected to raise rates instead. The Bank of England is anticipated to increase rates twice during the year.

Investors are weighing the potential for increased Iranian oil exports if sanctions are modified as part of any agreement. Such a development could add significant supply to global markets and further ease prices beyond the immediate diplomatic optimism.

The announcement comes as Google faces increased competition in AI-powered search. The company has been working to integrate AI capabilities across its products while maintaining its position as the dominant search engine.

Google CEO Sundar Pichai addressed public concerns about artificial intelligence during the conference. He acknowledged the anxiety many people feel about AI technology and its rapid deployment across consumer products.

The search box redesign is part of a broader strategy to incorporate AI features into Google's core products. The company framed the changes as improvements to how users interact with information online.

The timing of the announcement is notable as competitors have been releasing their own AI-powered search tools. Google has been adding AI-generated overviews to search results in recent months, though the feature has drawn mixed reactions from users.

Some users have expressed frustration with AI-generated content in search results. Reports indicate that Google has been struggling with certain search queries, particularly those involving words like "disregard," "stop," and "ignore." Users discovered these terms were producing unexpected results.

A workaround has emerged that allows users to disable AI features in search results by using a specific search modifier. The method provides an option for those who prefer traditional search results without AI-generated summaries.

The search box redesign represents Google's most significant change to its core product interface in decades. The company built its business on the simplicity of a white page with a search box, and this marks a departure from that minimalist approach.

Google has not provided specific details about all the new features or how they will roll out to users. The changes are expected to appear gradually as the company refines the AI integration.

The decision drew swift criticism from Colorado election officials. She warned that the decision would "embolden the election denial movement across the country and will leave a dark, dangerous imprint on American democracy for years to come."

Griswold emphasized that Peters had not waited for court reconsideration as directed by the Court of Appeals. "What it actually is is special treatment," she stated.

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, said the group was "furious, disgusted, and deeply disappointed by the governor's decision." He noted that election officials across the country interpreted the clemency as "open season on our elections and election officials."

Sensitive passwords and other information from Mesa County's voting equipment, made by Dominion, were later published online by right-wing personalities. During sentencing in 2024, Judge Matthew Barrett told Peters: "You are no hero. You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again."

Peters's case has been a rallying point for former President Donald Trump and other Republicans who continue to falsely claim the 2020 election was stolen. Trump, who issued a symbolic federal pardon to Peters last year, had repeatedly called for her release.

Before announcing his clemency decision, Polis compared Peters's case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who was convicted of submitting forged letters to the legislature and received probation and community service. Polis argued the cases had sentencing disparities. However, Griswold disputed the comparison, noting that Peters "organized the breach of the election equipment, broke the public trust and attacked the very foundations of our democratic process."
