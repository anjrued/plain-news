8:20 AM ET

An explosion on a passenger train in Pakistan's Balochistan province killed at least 24 people. The train was transporting military personnel home for the Eid holiday when armed separatists carried out the attack.

Balochistan, Pakistan's largest province by area, sits along the border with Afghanistan and Iran. The region has long faced serious security challenges, with separatist groups and militants targeting government facilities and transportation networks. Trains and railway infrastructure have been frequent targets of attacks in the province.

The latest incident reflects an intensifying wave of violence across Pakistan in recent months. Security experts have attributed much of this surge to Baloch separatist groups and the Pakistani Taliban, known as Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan, which maintains ties to Afghanistan's Taliban.

This attack comes just days after the outlawed Balochistan Liberation Army carried out coordinated attacks in the southwestern province that killed approximately 50 people. In response, Pakistani security forces reported killing over 200 militants.

Pakistan's government has deployed military forces across Balochistan to counter militant activity and protect critical infrastructure. Despite these security measures, attacks on trains, gas pipelines, and other facilities continue to occur regularly. The railway system serves as a vital transportation link for both passengers and freight across the province.

The violence is not limited to Balochistan. A suicide bombing struck a mosque in Islamabad, the nation's capital, during Friday prayers just days earlier, killing 31 people and injuring at least 169 others. The Islamic State claimed responsibility for that attack, which targeted Shia worshippers at the Khadija al-Kubra mosque. The blast sparked widespread concern about the security situation in the capital, where attacks are typically less frequent than in other regions.

Pakistan's leadership condemned the various attacks. President Asif Ali Zardari stated that "targeting innocent civilians is a crime against humanity" and pledged that the nation stood with affected families. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif ordered a full investigation, saying "those who are responsible must be identified and punished."

The province of Balochistan contains significant natural resources but remains one of Pakistan's poorest regions. Local separatist groups have carried out attacks while protesting what they describe as the exploitation of the area's resources without adequate benefit to residents.

Authorities have not yet confirmed specific details about the train explosion's exact location along the route or released complete casualty figures. The death toll may rise as rescue operations continue and officials assess the full extent of casualties. Pakistani authorities typically conduct investigations following such attacks to determine responsibility and work to prevent future incidents.

Authorities in Orange County, California have ordered the evacuation of approximately 40,000 residents due to concerns about a dangerous chemical leak at an aerospace facility. The incident began Thursday at a GKN Aerospace storage tank in Garden Grove containing methyl methacrylate, a highly flammable chemical used in resin and plastic manufacturing.

Initial hazmat response Thursday led officials to lift evacuation orders the same day. However, the situation deteriorated when damage to a tank valve created additional problems that prevented complete mitigation of the threat. By Friday, expanded evacuation orders covered residents across six cities including Cypress, Stanton, Anaheim, Buena Park, and Westminster.

Fire officials warned of two possible outcomes. Orange County Fire Authority Division Chief Craig Covey told reporters: "There are literally two options left remaining. One: the tank fails and spills a total of about 6-7,000 gallons of very bad chemicals into the parking lot in that area. Or two: the tank goes into a thermal runaway and blows up, affecting the tanks that are around them that have fuel or the chemicals in them as well."

Covey emphasized the urgency of the situation, stating "This thing is going to fail, and we don't know when." He urged residents to take evacuation orders seriously, saying officials were preparing "in preparation for these two options: it fails or it blows up."

County Health Officer Dr Regina Chinsio-Kwong explained that if the chemical heats up, it releases harmful vapor that can cause respiratory problems, eye irritation, nausea, and headaches. Crews have created containment barriers using sandbags to prevent any chemical spill from entering storm drains or nearby waterways and creeks.

Garden Grove Police Chief Amir El-Farra reported that approximately 15 percent of residents under evacuation orders were refusing to leave. The city established at least two emergency shelters for displaced residents.

Garden Grove is situated approximately 30 miles southeast of downtown Los Angeles and less than a mile from Disneyland's theme parks, which were not placed under evacuation orders.

EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin acknowledged the severity of the crisis on Sunday, telling CNN that officials had been warned the tank would fail, though "there are different scenarios as to what that means."

GKN Aerospace, a division of a British corporation that produces airplane engines and aircraft components, owns the facility where the crisis originated. As containment efforts continue, officials have provided no timeline for when evacuated residents might safely return to their homes.

6:32 PM ET

Oil prices tumbled on Wednesday as diplomatic progress between the United States and Iran raised hopes of an agreement that could reopen the Strait of Hormuz to international shipping.

Brent crude fell sharply, dropping 11 percent to reach $97 a barrel after news broke that the White House believed it was moving close to finalizing a framework agreement with Tehran. The decline marked the first time oil has fallen below $100 since April 22, a significant retreat from last week's high of $126 a barrel.

President Donald Trump signaled optimism about the negotiations in a social media post, stating that if Iran agrees to the terms, the "highly effective Blockade will allow the Hormuz Strait to be OPEN TO ALL, including Iran." He added that he would briefly pause his "Project Freedom" operation, which has been escorting ships through the strait, to allow time for finalizing a deal. However, Trump also warned that if negotiations fail, "the bombing starts."

The Strait of Hormuz carries roughly one-fifth of global oil supplies and has been blocked by Iran since late February, creating a significant energy crisis. Trump's naval blockade of Iranian ports has remained in place throughout the tensions, contributing to the spike in energy prices in recent weeks.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy responded to the pause in operations by saying that safe passage through the strait would be ensured once US threats cease and new procedures are implemented. The Guards' statement did not specify what these new procedures would entail.

However, oil prices recovered somewhat later in the day after Iran dismissed reports of an imminent deal as an "American wishlist and not a reality." Oil ended the day down 7.3 percent at $101.83 a barrel, suggesting market caution about the negotiations' ultimate success.

The prospect of easing Middle East tensions sparked broader market gains. European stock markets rallied significantly, with the UK's FTSE 100 rising 2 percent, France's CAC 40 jumping 3 percent, and Germany's DAX climbing 2.1 percent. The S&P 500 in the United States rose nearly 1.5 percent.

Bond markets also showed signs of relief, with UK gilt yields declining slightly after hitting their highest levels since 1998. The yield on 30-year gilts fell about 9 basis points to 5.63 percent.

Susannah Streeter, chief investment strategist at Wealth Club, observed that "a dam of tension has eased with relief flooding into financial markets, amid hopes that hostilities will cease in the Middle East."

The developments came as shipping companies continued reporting incidents in the region. French shipping group CMA CGM said one of its vessels, the San Antonio, had been attacked while crossing the strait on Tuesday, resulting in crew injuries and vessel damage.

Star Citizen has become the first video game to reach $1 billion in crowdfunding, developer Cloud Imperium Games announced. The space simulation game reached the milestone after more than a decade of development that began with a Kickstarter campaign in 2012.

The game remains in active development with no confirmed release date for its persistent universe mode. Cloud Imperium also provided an update on Squadron 42, the single-player campaign component of the project. The studio said Squadron 42 has entered its final development phase.

Chris Roberts founded Cloud Imperium Games to develop Star Citizen as an ambitious space trading and combat simulator. The initial Kickstarter campaign raised $2.1 million, but the project continued accepting direct pledges from backers through its own website. Supporters can purchase virtual spacecraft and other digital items, with some packages selling for thousands of dollars.

The $1 billion total makes Star Citizen the most expensive video game ever developed based on publicly available budget information. The project has drawn both dedicated supporters and critics who question whether the game will ever reach a complete release state.

Cloud Imperium operates studios in Los Angeles, Austin, Manchester, and Frankfurt with more than 1,000 employees working on the project. The company releases periodic updates to the playable alpha version of Star Citizen, which allows backers to test various gameplay systems.

Squadron 42 features a cast including Mark Hamill, Gary Oldman, and Gillian Anderson. Cloud Imperium previously announced release windows for the single-player game but delayed them multiple times. The studio now says Squadron 42 is in its final polish phase but has not committed to a specific launch date.

The persistent universe portion of Star Citizen lets players explore space, trade goods, engage in combat, and interact with other players. The alpha version currently includes multiple star systems and gameplay loops, though many planned features remain under development.

The Colorado Democratic Party voted to censure Governor Jared Polis over his decision to commute the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former county clerk convicted of election-related crimes. The formal rebuke from the governor's own party reflects deep divisions within Democratic ranks over the clemency decision.

Peters, the former clerk in Mesa County in western Colorado, was convicted in 2024 of four felonies and three misdemeanors. In 2021, she allowed a former pro surfer named Conan Hayes to access the county's voting equipment, copy it, and attend a sensitive upgrade of the county's voting software. Hayes is affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, a prominent spreader of false claims about the 2020 election.

Polis reduced Peters' sentence from eight and a half years to approximately four and a half years, making her eligible for parole release on June 1. In his clemency letter, the governor wrote that the sentence was "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes."

The decision prompted swift and sharp criticism from Democratic officials. Jena Griswold, Colorado's top election official, said the clemency "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president." She warned that the decision would "embolden the election denial movement across the country and will leave a dark, dangerous imprint on American democracy for years to come."

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, expressed that the group was "furious, disgusted, and deeply disappointed by the governor's decision." Election officials nationwide have expressed concern that the clemency signals that tampering with elections carries minimal consequences.

Griswold disputed Polis' reasoning for the commutation. The governor had compared Peters' case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for submitting forged letters to the legislature. Polis cited sentencing disparities between the two cases.

However, Griswold countered that the comparison was inaccurate. "Peters organized the breach of the election equipment, broke the public trust and attacked the very foundations of our democratic process," she stated. "Her actions are still being used to try to undermine the 2026 election."

The sensitive passwords and other information from Mesa County's voting equipment were later published online by right-wing personalities. At sentencing in 2024, Judge Matthew Barrett told Peters: "You are no hero. You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again."

An appeals court had overturned Peters' original sentence in April and ordered reconsideration. Rather than wait for resentencing through the courts, Polis granted the commutation. Griswold characterized this as "special treatment" for someone who remains central to election denial efforts and false claims about the 2020 election.
