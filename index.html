Authorities confirmed the death toll following the derailment caused by the explosive device. The bombing represents the latest security incident affecting civilian transportation in Pakistan.

A gunman opened fire near a White House security checkpoint on Saturday, prompting a brief lockdown of the complex while President Donald Trump was present. The suspect was fatally shot by Secret Service officers who returned fire, according to federal officials.

The incident marked the third time in roughly a month that gunfire has occurred in the vicinity of Trump. A bystander was wounded during the exchange, though the severity of their injuries was not detailed in available reports.

The shooting unfolded as the suspect approached a checkpoint near the building and began firing at officers. Journalists in the area scrambled for cover as sustained gunshots rang out. The Secret Service confirmed that officers responded to the threat and successfully stopped the attacker, preventing further harm.

This latest incident has intensified scrutiny over security protocols protecting the president and federal facilities. The shooting occurred less than two years after Trump survived two separate assassination attempts, one in Butler, Pennsylvania and another at a golf course in Florida.

Sean Curran, the Secret Service director, defended the agency's response, stating that security measures in place proved effective in stopping the suspect before additional damage could occur. "It shows that our multi-layered protection works," Curran said Saturday evening.

However, some lawmakers have raised concerns about security gaps. Republican congressman Mike Lawler, who was present at a White House Correspondents' Association dinner earlier that day, expressed alarm about potential vulnerabilities. "There needs to be wholesale change," Lawler told reporters. He warned that the suspect could have accessed other events and caused mass casualties.

Federal and local officials indicated that the Trump administration had provided lower security levels for the correspondents' dinner than typical for events attended by the president and cabinet members. The event was not formally designated as a "national special security event," a designation that would have placed the Secret Service in full operational control, according to the Washington Post.

Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche confirmed on NBC's "Meet the Press" that law enforcement officials believed the suspect was targeting administration officials, "likely including the president," based on a preliminary assessment.

The shooting has prompted requests for briefings from multiple congressional committees, including the House Oversight and Homeland Security Committees and the Senate Judiciary Committee. Some Republican lawmakers have floated the possibility of creating a dedicated House committee to investigate the incident and examine security protocols at protected facilities.

The Secret Service maintained that officers successfully contained the threat without allowing the suspect to breach the president's immediate protective perimeter. The incident has reignited debates about security at the nation's capital and the adequacy of current protective measures for high-ranking government officials.

A sharp sell-off in US Treasury bonds is driving borrowing costs higher and testing the federal government's tolerance for elevated interest rates. The sell-off comes as investors reassess the outlook for government debt amid concerns about fiscal policy and inflation.

Treasury yields have climbed as bond prices fall, reflecting investor caution about the direction of federal spending and debt levels. Higher yields increase the cost for the government to borrow money and can ripple through the economy, affecting mortgage rates, corporate borrowing, and consumer loans.

The movement in bond markets reflects broader uncertainty about economic policy and fiscal sustainability. When Treasury bonds lose value, it signals that investors are demanding higher returns to hold government debt, either because they perceive greater risk or because they expect interest rates to remain elevated.

Washington faces a decision about how to respond to these market dynamics. Higher borrowing costs could constrain federal spending options and increase the burden of servicing existing debt. The Treasury Department regularly auctions bonds to fund government operations, and rising yields make that funding more expensive.

The sell-off in Treasuries also affects financial markets more broadly. Treasury bonds are considered a benchmark for other forms of debt, and changes in their yields influence pricing across credit markets. Investors use Treasury rates as a baseline for assessing risk and return in other investments.

Economists watch Treasury markets closely because they provide signals about investor confidence in government finances and expectations for economic growth and inflation. A sustained increase in borrowing costs could affect fiscal planning and budget priorities.

The current situation reflects ongoing questions about the balance between government spending needs and debt sustainability. Treasury yields had been relatively stable in recent months, but recent market movements have brought borrowing costs back into focus as a policy consideration.

The federal government's response to higher borrowing costs could include adjustments to debt issuance strategies or broader fiscal policy changes. Market participants are watching for signals from Treasury officials about how they view current yield levels and whether any policy adjustments are under consideration.

Star Citizen has become the highest-funded crowdfunded project in history, reaching $1 billion in total funding. The space simulation game launched its crowdfunding campaign in 2012 and has been in development for over a decade.

Developer Cloud Imperium Games announced the milestone as the project continues work on both the persistent universe of Star Citizen and its single-player campaign, Squadron 42. The game has attracted funding from millions of backers worldwide who have purchased virtual spaceships and other in-game items.

The project remains in alpha testing, with no confirmed release date for the full game. Squadron 42, the single-player component featuring Hollywood actors including Mark Hamill and Gary Oldman, has been repeatedly delayed since its original 2016 target date.

Cloud Imperium provided an update on Squadron 42's development status alongside the funding announcement. The studio has released regular content updates to the playable alpha version of Star Citizen, adding new gameplay features, locations, and ships.

The $1 billion figure represents cumulative crowdfunding contributions from individual backers rather than traditional publisher investment. The project's budget has made it one of the most expensive video games ever developed, though exact development costs have not been publicly disclosed.

Star Citizen allows players to explore a vast universe, engage in space combat, trade goods, and participate in various missions. The game runs on Amazon's Lumberyard engine and supports both first-person and ship-based gameplay.

The crowdfunding model has proven unusually successful for Star Citizen despite the extended development timeline. The game continues to attract new backers and generate ongoing revenue through sales of virtual items and ships that can cost hundreds or thousands of dollars.

Cloud Imperium operates studios in the United States, United Kingdom, and Germany, employing hundreds of developers working on the project. The company has faced scrutiny over the lengthy development process and whether the final product will justify the unprecedented level of crowdfunding support.

Polis reduced Peters' nearly nine-year sentence to approximately four and a half years. She is scheduled for relea...

May 21, 10:12 AM ET

The Colorado Democratic Party has formally censured Governor Jared Polis for commuting the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former county clerk whose case has become central to efforts by former President Donald Trump and his allies to overturn the 2020 election.

Polis reduced Peters' nearly nine-year sentence to approximately four and a half years. She is scheduled for release on parole on June 1. In his clemency letter, the governor described the sentence as "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes."

Peters, the former Mesa County clerk in western Colorado, was convicted in 2024 of four felonies and three misdemeanors. In 2021, she allowed unauthorized access to county voting equipment. A pro surfer named Conan Hayes, who is affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, accessed the voting systems, copied them, and attended a sensitive software upgrade. Sensitive passwords and information from the Dominion voting equipment were later published online by right-wing figures spreading election misinformation.

A judge sentenced Peters to eight and a half years in prison and six months in jail in 2024, telling her: "You are no hero. You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again." An appeals court overturned that sentence in April and ordered reconsideration.

The clemency decision has sparked fierce criticism from Colorado election officials. Secretary of State Jena Griswold, a Democrat, said the decision "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president." She warned that the clemency "will embolden the election denial movement across the country and will leave a dark, dangerous imprint on American democracy for years to come."

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, stated the group was "furious, disgusted, and deeply disappointed by the governor's decision." Election officials across the country have expressed concern that the clemency signals "it's open season on our elections and election officials."

Polis signaled his intentions in March by comparing Peters' case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who was convicted of submitting forged letters and received probation and community service. He cited sentencing disparities in his reasoning. However, election officials rejected the comparison, noting that Peters' actions involved organizing a breach of election equipment and breaking public trust in ways that differed significantly from Lewis's offense.

Trump had repeatedly called for Peters' release, including posting on Truth Social last year. He previously issued a federal pardon for Peters, though it had limited effect since she faced state charges rather than federal ones.

The $1 billion figure represents cumulative crowdfunding contributions from individual backers rather than traditional publisher investment. The project's budget has made it one of the most expensive video games ever developed, though exact development costs have not been publicly disclosed.

Star Citizen allows players to explore a vast universe, engage in space combat, trade goods, and participate in various missions. The game runs on Amazon's Lumberyard engine and supports both first-person and ship-based gameplay.

The crowdfunding model has proven unusually successful for Star Citizen despite the extended development timeline. The game continues to attract new backers and generate ongoing revenue through sales of virtual items and ships that can cost hundreds or thousands of dollars.

Cloud Imperium operates studios in the United States, United Kingdom, and Germany, employing hundreds of developers working on the project.
