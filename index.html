Yesterday, 8:58 PM ET

A suspect was killed after opening fire on Secret Service officers near the White House on Monday, according to the agency. A juvenile bystander was also shot during the incident and is receiving hospital treatment.

The shooting took place around 15th Street and Independence Avenue, near the Washington Monument. Plainclothes Secret Service officers first identified the individual, who they believed was carrying a firearm and acting suspiciously. The officers called in support from uniformed agents to assist.

When the suspect saw the uniformed agents approaching, he fled on foot, drew his gun and fired at the officers. "They returned fire and engaged," said Matthew Quinn, the deputy director of the Secret Service. The suspect was struck during the exchange and was transported to a hospital.

The incident occurred shortly after Vice President JD Vance's motorcade passed through the area. Quinn said it remains unclear whether Vance was a target of the shooting. The White House was briefly locked down as authorities investigated. President Donald Trump, who was holding a small business event at the White House at the time, continued his event without interruption. Journalists who were outside the building were ushered into the briefing room as emergency crews responded.

The suspect was known to the Secret Service before the shooting. According to court records, the 21-year-old man had previously attempted to enter the White House complex. An affidavit filed in DC Superior Court in 2025 documented an earlier arrest near the building following that attempted entry.

A juvenile bystander was wounded during the shooting but did not sustain life-threatening injuries, according to Quinn. The young person is receiving treatment at a hospital.

The Secret Service encouraged people to avoid the area as emergency crews worked the scene near the White House. Quinn declined to comment on the suspect's condition when asked by reporters.

No details have been released about what specifically prompted the suspect to open fire on the officers or whether he had attempted to breach any checkpoint before drawing his weapon. The circumstances surrounding the shooting were still being investigated by authorities.

The incident represents a significant security breach near one of the nation's most heavily protected buildings. Multiple Secret Service checkpoints operate around the White House complex to screen visitors and secure the perimeter. The shooting occurred during daytime hours when foot traffic in the downtown Washington area is typically significant.

Ukrainian officials documented the attack as part of ongoing assessments of Russian military operations. Oil prices fell sharply, declining between 5% and 6%, after President Trump indicated that peace negotiations with Iran are moving forward constructively. The price movement reflected market expectations that diplomatic progress could lead to the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical global shipping route through which roughly a fifth of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas flows.

Trump told a reporter on Tuesday that US-Iranian peace talks could resume in Islamabad within two days and praised Pakistan's army chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir, for mediating the negotiations. "He's fantastic, and therefore it's more likely that we go back there," Trump said of Munir, describing him as doing a "great job" in arranging discussions.

The first round of ceasefire talks took place over the weekend in Islamabad, lasting 21 hours before ending on Sunday. US Vice President JD Vance walked out, stating that Iran had failed to make an "affirmative commitment that they will not seek a nuclear weapon." A key sticking point involved the duration of uranium enrichment suspension. Vance's delegation demanded a 20-year moratorium, while Iran reportedly offered less than 10 years.

The closure of the Strait of Hormuz has been a major factor driving oil prices higher. Iran closed the waterway in response to the US-Israeli attack that began on February 28. Following the weekend talks, crude prices dipped to around $95 per barrel after reports of possible second-round negotiations emerged on Tuesday.

Despite the dip, broader economic conditions remain precarious. The cost of crude on the spot market has hovered around $100 per barrel since Iran's closure of the strait. Some movement has occurred with merchant vessels, though the situation remains fluid. US Central Command reported that six merchant vessels complied with direction to turn around and re-enter an Iranian port, while one tanker, the Rich Starry, reversed course again and passed through the waterway.

The Trump administration declared a naval blockade on ships using Iranian ports in the Gulf as pressure on Iran's economy. The US Treasury Department announced it does not plan to renew temporary sanctions relief on Iranian oil that was intended to ease war-related supply shocks.

The oil price decline occurred alongside broader market movements. Tokyo's Nikkei 225 stock index reached a record high above 65,000 points, while the US dollar weakened against other currencies. Asian stock markets mostly posted gains as investors responded positively to diplomatic developments.

Market analysts noted that oil prices remain sensitive to developments in the peace process. Further updates about negotiations or changes to the Strait of Hormuz situation could trigger additional price movements. The synchronized response across energy markets, equities, and currencies suggests investors view the diplomatic progress as credible enough to warrant repositioning portfolios based on reduced geopolitical risk.

Yesterday, 11:34 AM ET

Google has redesigned its search box, marking the first major change to the feature in 25 years. The company introduced AI-powered search capabilities that alter how users interact with the platform, moving away from the traditional text-only search experience that has defined Google since its founding.

The changes were announced at Google I/O 2026, where the company positioned the updates as part of what it calls the "agentic Gemini era." The new search interface integrates artificial intelligence directly into the search experience, fundamentally changing how results are presented to users.

The overhaul has prompted immediate user response. Some users have already found ways to disable the AI features, using simple modifiers to return to traditional search results. One technical issue emerged immediately after launch when Google's system struggled to properly define certain common words, including "disregard," "stop," and "ignore." The problem became widespread enough that searches for the word "disregard" itself began trending.

The timing comes as Google faces new competitive pressure in the AI search space. OpenClaw, an open-source project, recently surpassed 300,000 GitHub stars before Google launched its competing product called Spark. Industry observers noted the rapid succession of these developments.

Multiple technology publications have described the changes as marking the end of the internet's golden age and positioning Google as "the web's new landlord." Some commentators suggested users explore alternative search engines in response to the changes.

The company's decision to integrate AI into its core search product represents a strategic shift after years of maintaining a relatively unchanged search interface. Google has not disclosed how many users are currently experiencing the new AI-powered search or whether the rollout is global.

The changes affect how information is retrieved and displayed across Google's platform, which processes billions of searches daily. The company framed the update as an upgrade to its search capabilities, though the immediate technical issues and user pushback suggest the transition may face hurdles in the coming weeks.

Malukas came within feet of winning his first Indianapolis 500 but was unable to hold off Rosenqvist's charge. The two drivers raced wheel-to-wheel through the final lap before Rosenqvist secured the position.

The race took place on Memorial Day weekend, continuing the Indianapolis 500's long-standing tradition as one of motorsport's marquee events. Hundreds of thousands of spectators typically attend the race each year at the 2.5-mile oval track.

The movie generated $102 million domestically during North America's four-day Memorial Day weekend. While this places it ahead of other films at the box office, it falls short of even Disney's previous lowest-performing Star Wars release. Solo: A Star Wars Story, which opened on the same weekend in 2018, brought in $103 million domestically and $171 million globally, according to box office records.

The film stars Pedro Pascal as Din Djarin, the armored bounty hunter known as the Mandalorian, alongside Grogu, the small green character that audiences have affectionately called "Baby Yoda." The theatrical release expands the story from Disney Plus' popular streaming series, which ran for three seasons before being greenlit as a feature film.

Despite the underwhelming opening compared to previous Star Wars releases, industry analysts note the performance may still represent a success given the film's $165 million production budget, significantly lower than Solo's $275 million. "The movie is a piece of the story, and it's earning a piece of the business," said industry analyst David Gross. He added that while the numbers are "not on the level of the primary Star Wars films," they reflect how spin-off projects typically perform.

The Mandalorian and Grogu marks the first Star Wars theatrical release since 2019's The Rise of Skywalker. Disney had paused Star Wars film production after determining the studio had released movies too frequently. The company has instead focused on developing Star Wars content for Disney Plus, with varying degrees of critical and audience success.

Disney executives remain optimistic about the film's prospects. The studio anticipates it will perform well in coming weeks based on positive audience reception, despite receiving mixed reviews from critics. The company views the release as part of a broader strategy to drive viewers toward merchandise, theme park experiences, and Disney Plus content.

The Mandalorian series has become the most-watched original program on Disney Plus, while Grogu merchandise has proven commercially successful, with more than 13 million toys sold since the character's 2019 debut.

Looking ahead, Disney has scheduled Star Wars: Starfighter, featuring Ryan Gosling, for release in May 2027. The studio's approach reflects efforts to revive the franchise's theatrical presence after the extended gap in releases.

May 21, 10:12 AM ET

Colorado's Democratic Party formally censured Governor Jared Polis for commuting the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former county clerk whose case has become central to Republican election denial efforts.

Polis reduced Peters' sentence from eight and a half years to approximately four and a half years in prison. Peters, the former clerk in Mesa County, will be released on parole June 1. She was convicted in 2024 of four felonies and three misdemeanors related to allowing unauthorized access to her county's voting equipment.

In his clemency letter, Polis described the sentence as "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes." However, state election officials and Democratic leaders strongly disagreed with the decision.

Jena Griswold, Colorado's secretary of state and a Democrat, said the clemency validates Trump's goals and empowers the election denial movement nationally. "He has learned now just how hard he needs to push and threaten the state of Colorado to get what he wants," she said. Griswold learned of Polis' decision just 45 minutes before it became public.

Griswold rejected Polis' earlier comparison between Peters' case and that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received only probation and community service for overlapping felony charges. "Beyond one count in common, it is not accurate to suggest that Peters's and Sonya Jaquez Lewis's actions or impacts are the same," Griswold said. She emphasized that Peters organized the election equipment breach, broke public trust, and undermined democratic foundations.

In 2021, Peters allowed a former pro surfer named Conan Hayes to access voting equipment and attend a sensitive software upgrade. Hayes is affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, a prominent spreader of election misinformation. Sensitive passwords and information from the county's Dominion voting equipment were later published online by right-wing figures.

When Peters was originally sentenced in 2024, Judge Matthew Barrett told her: "You are no hero. You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again."

An appeals court overturned that sentence in April and ordered reconsideration, creating the opening for Polis' clemency.

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, said election officials across the country view the decision as a dangerous signal. "We're starting to hear from election officials across the country that this signals that it's open season on our elections and election officials," he said.

Trump has repeatedly called for Peters' release and previously issued a federal pardon, though that was largely symbolic since her convictions were under state law. The clemency decision marks a rare public breach between Polis and his own party's leadership.
