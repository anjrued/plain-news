A judge had sentenced Peters to eight and a half years in prison and six months in jail. Polis reduced her sentence to approximately four and a half years, making her eligible for parole release on June 1.

In his clemency letter, Polis wrote that the sentence was "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes." He had signaled his intentions in March by comparing Peters' case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for submitting forged letters to the legislature. Polis argued that justice needed to be applied evenly.

However, Colorado election officials strongly disputed this comparison. Jena Griswold, Colorado's secretary of state, said it was inaccurate to equate the two cases. "Peters organized the breach of the election equipment, broke the public trust and attacked the very foundations of our democratic process," Griswold stated. She characterized the clemency as "special treatment" rather than a correction of sentencing disparity.

Griswold further criticized the decision as validating "Trump's basest impulses" and emboldening the election denial movement. She warned that the clemency would send dangerous signals across the country about the consequences for tampering with elections. "He has learned now just how hard he needs to push and threaten the state of Colorado to get what he wants," she said.

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, expressed similar outrage, saying the group was "furious, disgusted, and deeply disappointed." He noted that election officials nationwide were concerned the decision signaled "open season on our elections and election officials."

Peters' case has become central to election denial efforts. Conan Hayes is affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, a prominent figure in spreading misinformation about the 2020 election. Sensitive passwords and information from Mesa County's voting equipment were later published online by right-wing personalities.

Former President Donald Trump has repeatedly called for Peters' release and previously issued a federal pardon, though that was largely symbolic since her convictions were state crimes. Trump instructed the Justice Department to pursue her release from prison.

The censure reflects deep divisions within Colorado's Democratic Party over how to handle election security violations and represents a rare formal rebuke of a sitting governor by his own party organization.
