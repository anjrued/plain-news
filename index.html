A few minutes ago

A storage tank holding a highly flammable chemical has forced the evacuation of approximately 40,000 residents across six Orange County cities, with authorities warning the container will eventually fail.

The crisis began Thursday at a GKN Aerospace facility in Garden Grove when a storage tank containing methyl methacrylate began off-gassing and risked failure. The chemical, used in the production of resins and plastics, is extremely flammable. Local hazmat teams responded, and authorities initially ordered evacuations before lifting the order later that day. However, damage to a valve on the tank created additional complications, preventing complete mitigation of the problem.

By Friday, Orange County fire authority division chief Craig Covey described a dire situation. "We have a tank that is actively in crisis," he said at a news conference. "There are literally two options left remaining. One: the tank fails and spills a total of about 6-7,000 gallons of very bad chemicals into the parking lot in that area. Or two: the tank goes into a thermal runaway and blows up, affecting the tanks that are around them that have fuel or the chemicals in them as well."

Covey emphasized the uncertainty of timing: "This thing is going to fail, and we don't know when. We're doing our best to figure out when or how we can prevent it."

The expanded evacuation orders cover residents in Garden Grove, Cypress, Stanton, Anaheim, Buena Park, and Westminster. Dr. Regina Chinsio-Kwong, the county health officer, warned that if the chemical becomes overheated, it releases harmful vapors that can cause respiratory issues, eye irritation, nausea, and headaches.

Emergency crews have constructed containment barriers using sandbags to prevent any chemical spill from entering storm drains or reaching nearby creeks and the ocean. Despite the seriousness of the evacuation orders, Garden Grove police chief Amir El-Farra reported that approximately 15 percent of people facing evacuation were refusing to leave. The city established at least two shelters for evacuees.

Garden Grove is located about 30 miles southeast of downtown Los Angeles and less than a mile from Disneyland's two theme parks. However, the parks were not placed under evacuation orders as of Friday.

Environmental Protection Agency administrator Lee Zeldin told CNN that officials expect the tank will fail but face uncertainty about what that failure will entail. He indicated that a low-volume chemical release was considered the most likely scenario, though fears of an explosion remained.

GKN Aerospace is a division of a British corporation that manufactures airplane engines and other aircraft components. Officials urged residents to comply with evacuation orders as crews prepared for either a chemical spill or a potential explosion.

11:28 AM ET

Russian filmmaker Andrey Zvyagintsev is making a significant return to international cinema at the Cannes Film Festival, premiering his first feature in nine years after a prolonged absence from filmmaking.

The 62-year-old director has chosen France as his home in exile while bringing his new film "Minotaur" to the festival where he established his reputation as one of Russia's most important contemporary directors. His 2014 film "Leviathan" won multiple prizes at Cannes and cemented his status in world cinema, though he has not released a feature film since 2017's "Loveless."

Zvyagintsev's return comes after a harrowing personal struggle. When Russia invaded Ukraine in February 2022, the director was stranded at a clinic in Hanover, Germany, recovering from severe COVID-19 that had caused approximately 90 percent lung damage. He spent 11 months in various hospitals, gradually relearning basic physical functions like walking and holding a spoon. Despite this ordeal, he channeled his anguish into creating his new work.

"Minotaur" adapts French director Claude Chabrol's 1969 erotic thriller "The Unfaithful Wife" and relocates it to a medium-sized Russian provincial town. The story follows a business executive confronting both professional challenges and personal betrayal when he discovers his wife is having an affair.

Zvyagintsev is competing for the festival's top prize, the Palme d'Or, alongside acclaimed international directors including Pedro Almodóvar, László Nemes, and Asghar Farhadi. While he has previously won the best screenplay and jury prizes at Cannes, he has never claimed the festival's highest honor.

His absence from cinema during the past nine years reflects the broader impact of Russia's political trajectory on the artistic community. According to Julian Graffy, a professor of Russian literature and film at University College London, "Many artistic careers have been broken by the political turn that Russia has taken. But since Zvyagintsev was the most important of the new wave of directors that flowered in the early 21st century, the loss of his voice has been felt most keenly of them all."

Throughout his career, Zvyagintsev's films have explored themes of power, corruption, and state oppression. His works are widely regarded as damning examinations of Russian society under authoritarian rule, though he has occasionally insisted in interviews that his intentions were not overtly political. Russian authorities eventually recognized the critical nature of his work, with the former culture minister publicly criticizing "Leviathan" despite the government initially funding part of the film's budget.

Zvyagintsev's return to Cannes represents a crucial moment for international cinema and a personal triumph over adversity.

May 21, 10:12 AM ET

The Colorado Democratic Party voted to censure Governor Jared Polis over his decision to commute the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former county clerk who allowed unauthorized access to voting equipment in Mesa County.

Peters was convicted in 2024 of four felonies and three misdemeanors. In 2021, she permitted Conan Hayes, a former professional surfer affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, to access and copy the county's voting equipment and attend a sensitive upgrade of the voting software. Sensitive passwords and other information from the Dominion voting systems were later published online by right-wing figures.

Polis reduced Peters' sentence from eight and a half years to approximately four and a half years, making her eligible for parole release on June 1. In his clemency letter, Polis wrote that the sentence was "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes."

The decision drew sharp criticism from Democratic officials. Jena Griswold, Colorado's secretary of state, said the clemency "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president." She warned that the decision signals to election officials nationwide that "it's open season on our elections" and will "embolden the election denial movement across the country."

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, said the group was "furious, disgusted, and deeply disappointed by the governor's decision."

Polis had signaled his intentions in March when he posted on social media comparing Peters' case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for submitting forged letters to the legislature. However, election officials disputed the comparison, noting that Peters' actions directly compromised election security infrastructure and breached public trust.

Judge Matthew Barrett had sentenced Peters to eight and a half years in prison and six months in jail, telling her: "You are no hero. You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again." An appeals court overturned that sentence in April and ordered reconsideration.

Peters' case has become a focal point for those spreading false claims about the 2020 election. Donald Trump, who continues to promote misinformation about the election, has repeatedly called for Peters' release. He previously issued a federal pardon, though it had limited effect since Peters faced state charges. Trump also instructed the justice department to attempt securing her release.

The censure represents one of the strongest rebukes a state party can issue to an elected official from its own ranks. It reflects ongoing tensions within Democratic leadership over how to respond to election-related cases and signals party concern that the clemency undermines efforts to protect election integrity and hold officials accountable for election crimes.

Zvyagintsev is competing for the festival's top prize, the Palme d'Or, alongside acclaimed international directors including Pedro Almodóvar, László Nemes, and Asghar Farhadi.
