Tulsi Gabbard is leaving her post as US director of national intelligence following a tumultuous stint in which she was largely sidelined as Donald Trump launched attacks on Venezuela and Iran.

In a letter to the US president, she said she would resign and leave her post on 30 June. "While we have made significant progress … I recognize there is still important work to be done," she wrote.

The White House forced Gabbard to resign, the Reuters news agency reported, citing a source familiar with the issue. However, a spokesperson for Gabbard's office said rumors that Gabbard had been forcibly ousted from office were "100% false".

In her resignation letter, Gabbard said her husband, Abraham, "has recently been diagnosed with an extremely rare form of bone cancer". She added: "At this time, I must step away from public service to be by his side and fully support him through this battle."

Gabbard was an unconventional choice for the role. The former Democrat lacked an intelligence background and her foreign policy views at times diverged with Trump's, particularly on questions of overseas military intervention. Yet she also took unusual measures to ingratiate herself to the president by vowing to root out politicization across US spy agencies and embracing his agenda of election denial.

Over time, Gabbard became increasingly sidelined by the president, who excluded her from key national security conversations on Iran and Venezuela, according to people familiar with the office of the director of national intelligence.

Trump was asking cabinet members last month whether he should replace Gabbard, according to two people briefed on the discussions.

"Unfortunately, after having done a great job, Tulsi Gabbard will be leaving the Administration on June 30th," he wrote in a statement on his Truth Social platform on Friday. Gabbard "has done an incredible job, and we will miss her", the president said, adding that Aaron Lukas, principal deputy director of national intelligence, would serve as acting director of national intelligence.

The resignation comes as Trump faces mounting pressure over multiple foreign policy crises. US arms sales to Taiwan have been "paused" to ensure the US military has enough munitions for its Iran operations, according to Washington's acting navy secretary, in the latest blow to Taipei after a series of comments by Donald Trump.

Meanwhile, the US president and Secretary of State Marco Rubio on Thursday again raised the spectre of military intervention in Cuba, a renewed threat that takes on greater weight a day after the administration announced criminal charges against Raúl Castro, the island's former leader.

The laboratory has been responsible for numerous space missions, including Mars rovers and deep space probes. JPL's work encompasses robotics, planetary science, and space technology development.

Caltech's relationship with JPL has defined both institutions for generations. The university has provided academic oversight and research direction for the laboratory's activities.

JPL employs thousands of scientists, engineers, and support staff in Pasadena, California. The laboratory's operations include mission control for active spacecraft and development of future exploration programs.

The facility's current projects span multiple areas of space exploration. Teams at JPL work on missions to Mars, Jupiter's moons, and other destinations in the solar system.

No final decision has been announced regarding the management change. The evaluation process involves multiple stakeholders, including NASA, Caltech, and other potential management entities.

The outcome will determine how JPL operates in the coming years. Any transition in management would need to address ongoing missions, existing contracts, and staff arrangements.

JPL's history includes landmark achievements in space exploration. The laboratory designed and operated the Voyager spacecraft, Mars rovers, and numerous other missions that have shaped understanding of the solar system.

Caltech's academic community has maintained close ties with JPL researchers. Faculty and students have participated in laboratory projects, creating connections between university research and space exploration programs.

The management question affects not only the institutions involved but also the broader space exploration community. JPL's work influences NASA's mission planning and scientific priorities.

The rapper's work helped pave the way for the commercial explosion of hip-hop in the 1990s and beyond. His approach to making accessible, danceable hip-hop influenced countless artists who followed.

Base is survived by his family and a legacy that includes one of the most enduring party anthems in music history. The song continues to be played at events worldwide and remains a touchstone for discussions about hip-hop's evolution into popular music.
