Donald Trump claimed on Saturday that a peace deal with Iran "has been largely negotiated," potentially signaling an end to the conflict that began in February between the US, Israel, and Iran.

Writing on his Truth Social platform, Trump said the "final aspects and details" of a "memorandum of understanding" were still being discussed and would be announced shortly. The president indicated that reopening the Strait of Hormuz would be part of any agreement.

"An agreement has been largely negotiated, subject to finalization between the United States of America, the Islamic Republic of Iran, and the various other Countries," Trump posted on social media.

The announcement came after Trump held calls with Pakistani mediators, Gulf allies, and Israeli officials. Pakistan has expressed hopes of hosting further talks "very soon" to finalize the framework. However, Iran's Fars news agency reported that the Strait of Hormuz would remain under Iranian control, suggesting disagreements over key details persist.

Despite Trump's optimistic tone, Tehran and mediating countries including Pakistan appeared less certain about the immediacy of a deal. According to reports, an initial framework being discussed does not include the contentious issue of Iran's nuclear weapons program, though a two-month negotiation period on nuclear matters could follow any initial agreement.

Before making his announcement, Trump spoke with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu ahead of an Israeli security cabinet meeting focused on Iran, Lebanon, and Gaza. There is growing speculation in Israel that fighting could resume if Iran shows no signs of compromise in negotiations.

Earlier on Sunday, Trump had posted a more threatening message aimed at Tehran: "For Iran, the Clock is Ticking, and they better get moving, FAST, or there won't be anything left of them. TIME IS OF THE ESSENCE!"

According to Axios, Trump is scheduled to meet with his national security team to discuss next steps on Iran policy. The administration has oscillated between diplomatic overtures and military threats over the three months since the conflict began, with neither approach yet producing a clear resolution.

Regional tensions remain high. The United Arab Emirates blamed Iran or one of its proxies for a drone attack that caused a fire near its nuclear power plant, describing the incident as a "dangerous escalation."

The Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway through which a significant portion of global oil exports pass, has been a flashpoint throughout the conflict. Its reopening would mark a major step toward normalizing regional security and international commerce.

While Trump characterized the negotiations as largely complete, significant obstacles appear to remain before any formal agreement can be finalized and implemented.

6:53 PM ET

The White House was placed under a brief security lockdown on Saturday evening after a sustained volley of gunshots erupted near the building, prompting reporters and staff to take cover.

According to the US Secret Service, a man approached a security checkpoint at a White House gate near 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue NW shortly after 6pm local time. The individual produced a pistol and opened fire on officers stationed at the checkpoint.

Secret Service officers returned fire, striking the suspect. He was transported to a local hospital where he later died from his injuries, federal officials confirmed.

During the exchange of gunfire, a bystander on the street was also shot. The Secret Service stated that it remains unclear whether the bystander was struck by the suspect's initial gunfire or during the subsequent exchange between the gunman and officers. No Secret Service personnel were injured in the incident.

President Donald Trump was at the White House during the shooting but was not impacted by the events. He had earlier mentioned being in the Oval Office but did not immediately comment on the gunfire.

Multiple journalists reported hearing what sounded like dozens of shots. Selina Wang, a White House correspondent for ABC News, captured video of the moment gunfire interrupted her recording of a report on US negotiations with Iran. In the footage, Wang can be seen diving for cover as the shots rang out.

"We were told to sprint to the press briefing room, where we are holding now," Wang said on social media.

Chris Flanagan, an anchor for local news channel DC News Now who sheltered in the White House briefing room, reported that approximately 30 shots were fired. A CBS News producer made a similar estimate, saying at least 20 shots had been heard.

FBI Director Kash Patel confirmed the bureau's involvement, posting that the FBI was on scene supporting the Secret Service response and would provide updates as information became available.

The security lockdown was lifted after the situation was contained. The Secret Service initially released a statement around 6:45pm local time saying they were aware of reports of shots fired and were working to verify information with personnel on the ground.

This shooting marks the second such incident near the White House in recent weeks. Less than three weeks earlier, a man brandishing a gun fired at a Secret Service officer near the route of Vice President JD Vance's motorcade outside the White House. In that incident, a civilian witness was wounded in the leg, and officers returned fire, striking the gunman.

The Saturday gunfire also occurred nearly a month after what authorities described as an attempted assassination of the president on April 25 as he attended the annual White House Correspondents' Association dinner at a Washington hotel.

SpaceX has confidentially filed for an initial public offering with US regulators, setting the stage for what could become one of the most valuable stock market debuts in history. The company founded by Elon Musk is reportedly seeking a valuation of approximately $1.75 trillion.

According to reports from Bloomberg and the Wall Street Journal, the confidential filing allows regulators time to review SpaceX's financial disclosures before they become public. The IPO could potentially take place as early as June, with Bloomberg reporting it as part of what is expected to be a significant year for high-value public offerings.

Over the past decade, SpaceX has emerged as the most valuable enterprise within Musk's collection of technology companies. The firm has secured extensive and lucrative contracts with the US government covering both defense and space travel operations. The American space program has become heavily dependent on SpaceX, with NASA relying on the company's rockets for the majority of its launches.

Beyond its rocket business, SpaceX has established itself as a major player in satellite communications through its Starlink subsidiary. Starlink now ranks among the largest satellite operators globally, with the satellite internet service estimated to generate more than half of SpaceX's total revenue. The service has also expanded Musk's geopolitical influence, with entities like the Ukrainian military depending on Starlink for combat operations and strategic planning.

As the company prepared for its public offering, SpaceX acquired Musk's artificial intelligence firm xAI in February. The acquisition came with announced plans to construct solar-powered data centers in space, intended to address the computing and energy requirements associated with the AI industry's rapid growth. The AI company, which owns the social media platform X and develops the Grok chatbot, was valued at $250 billion in the transaction despite lagging behind competitors like Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's ChatGPT in capabilities.

In recent months, SpaceX has redirected its attention toward orbital data centers and supporting NASA's upcoming moon landing missions. Musk's previously prominent promises about establishing a human civilization on Mars have become less central to the company's public messaging.

The details of SpaceX's finances, which have remained closely guarded even from most investors, are expected to be disclosed closer to the IPO date. Information regarding share pricing and other listing details will become public after the Securities and Exchange Commission completes its review of the filing. Bloomberg reports that SpaceX is collaborating with several major international banks, including the UK-based Barclays, to coordinate orders for the public offering.

A successful IPO would represent a major achievement for Musk, who is already the world's wealthiest individual.

The California Institute of Technology faces the possibility of losing control of NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory for the first time in the institution's history. JPL, one of the world's premier space research facilities, has been managed by Caltech since its founding.

The potential change comes as NASA evaluates the management structure of the laboratory, which has been responsible for numerous groundbreaking space missions. JPL has played a central role in American space exploration, including the Mars rover missions and deep space probes.

Caltech has operated JPL under a contract with NASA for decades. The arrangement has allowed the academic institution to provide scientific leadership and oversight for the laboratory's research and mission operations. The facility employs thousands of scientists, engineers, and support staff who work on planetary exploration and space technology development.

The consideration of a management change reflects ongoing discussions about how NASA's field centers and contractor-operated facilities should be structured. JPL represents one of the agency's most significant partnerships with an academic institution.

No final decision has been announced regarding the future management structure of the laboratory. The review process involves evaluating various factors related to mission performance, cost efficiency, and scientific output.

JPL has been instrumental in advancing planetary science and robotic exploration. The laboratory has managed missions to every planet in the solar system and has developed technology that has enabled spacecraft to operate for years beyond their planned lifespans.

Caltech's connection to JPL has been a defining feature of both institutions. The university has used the partnership to attract top scientists and engineers, while providing JPL with access to cutting-edge academic research and talented graduates.

The outcome of NASA's evaluation will determine whether Caltech continues its decades-long stewardship of the facility or whether a new management structure will be implemented. Any change would represent a significant shift in how one of America's most important space research institutions operates.

The United States Senate has confirmed billionaire private astronaut Jared Isaacman as NASA's 15th administrator, marking a significant leadership change for the space agency. The confirmation vote passed 67-30, finalizing his appointment under President Donald Trump's administration.

Isaacman's path to confirmation was notably turbulent. Trump initially nominated him earlier this year, then withdrew the nomination during a public dispute with SpaceX CEO Elon Musk in May. At the time, Trump posted on Truth Social that Isaacman's donations to Democratic candidates motivated the decision to pull back the nomination. However, according to the Wall Street Journal, Isaacman worked with allies within the administration to revive his candidacy in recent months, ultimately securing the position.

During his second confirmation hearing two weeks before the vote, Isaacman emphasized the urgency of returning Americans to the moon before China achieves the same goal this decade. His confirmation ensures NASA remains an independent agency after acting administrator Sean Duffy, who also serves as transportation secretary, attempted to move the space agency under his department's authority.

Duffy congratulated Isaacman on Twitter, expressing hopes for success "as he begins his tenure and leads NASA as we go back to the Moon in 2028 and beat China." The consolidation effort Duffy championed sparked conflict with Musk around the same time the transport secretary criticized SpaceX for falling behind on NASA contracts and suggested looking to competitors like Jeff Bezos's Blue Origin for alternatives.

Isaacman built his fortune by founding Shift4, a payment processing startup that now handles billions of transactions annually. His space credentials include flying on two SpaceX missions, both of which he personally funded with all-civilian crews. The most recent mission took place in 2024 and featured the first spacewalk conducted by a private company, demonstrating his hands-on experience with commercial spaceflight.

As an advocate for Mars missions and a close ally of Elon Musk, Isaacman brings a perspective shaped by his private spaceflight experience to NASA's leadership. His confirmation comes at a critical juncture as the agency works to accelerate its lunar program and maintain America's competitive position in space exploration against China's rapidly advancing capabilities.

The new administrator inherits an agency facing pressure to meet ambitious timelines while managing relationships with commercial space partners and international competitors. His confirmation ends a period of uncertainty and establishes clear direction for NASA's immediate future under his leadership.

Two-time NASCAR Cup Series champion Kyle Busch has died at age 41 after severe pneumonia progressed into sepsis, his family confirmed Saturday.

The family received the medical evaluation over the weekend, according to Dakota Hunter, vice president of Kyle Busch Companies. The statement indicated that the pneumonia resulted in rapid and overwhelming associated complications that led to his death on Thursday.

NASCAR announced Busch's passing in a statement on Thursday, expressing sadness over the loss of "one of our sport's greatest and fiercest drivers." The racing organization extended condolences to the Busch family, Richard Childress Racing, and the entire motorsports community.

Earlier on Thursday, Busch's family had announced his hospitalization with a severe illness, saying he would not compete as planned in the weekend NASCAR race in Charlotte, North Carolina. The announcement came just days after Busch won a trucks race at Dover on Friday, six days before his death.

During a NASCAR Cup Series race on May 10 at Watkins Glen, Busch had radioed his crew requesting medical aid from Dr. Bill Heisel and a shot after the race. The TV broadcast reported that Busch had been struggling with a sinus cold that was worsened by the intense G-forces and elevation changes at the upstate New York road course. Despite his discomfort, Busch continued racing and finished eighth.

The Las Vegas native won NASCAR championships in 2015 and 2019. At the time of his death, Busch ranked 24th in the Cup Series standings with two top-10 finishes in 12 races this season. He was in his fourth season at Richard Childress Racing after winning titles with Joe Gibbs Racing.

Busch won 232 races across NASCAR's three national series, more than any driver in history. Known by the nicknames "Rowdy" and "Wild Thing," he was a polarizing figure famous for post-race fights, regular feuds with other drivers, and sometimes outlandish behavior. The multi-talented driver burst onto the Cup Series scene in 2005 by winning rookie of the year.

In a joint statement, the Busch family, Richard Childress Racing, and NASCAR called him "a future Hall of Famer" and "a rare talent, one who comes along once in a generation." The statement praised his fierce passion, immense skill, and deep care for the sport and fans. It noted his sharp wit and competitive spirit sparked a deep emotional connection with fans, creating the loyal "Rowdy Nation."

Busch is survived by his wife, Samantha, and children Brexton and Lennix.

5:20 PM ET

Romanian director Cristian Mungiu has secured his second Palme d'Or at the Cannes Film Festival, nearly two decades after his first win. His English-language debut, Fjord, took the top prize at the 79th edition of the festival, making the 58-year-old filmmaker the 10th director in history to receive two Palmes.

Mungiu first won the prestigious award in 2007 for 4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days, a searing abortion drama. Fjord marks a new direction for the director, being his first film in English. The movie stars Renate Reinsve and Sebastian Stan as Romanian religious parents who relocate to Norway, where they face accusations of child abuse.

By winning a second Palme d'Or, Mungiu joins an exclusive group of directors including Francis Ford Coppola, Michael Haneke, Ken Loach, the Dardenne brothers, and most recently Ruben Östlund. Other members of this distinguished club include Alf Sjöberg, Bille August, Emir Kusturica, and Shōhei Imamura.

The Grand Prix, the festival's second-place prize, went to Andrey Zvyagintsev's Minotaur, described as a dark satire examining corruption and infidelity in contemporary Russia. Valeska Grisebach took third place with the Jury prize for The Dreamed Adventure, a Bulgaria-set drama about an archaeologist.

The director award was shared between Pawel Pawlikowski for Fatherland and the duo of Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi for The Black Ball. Both acting prizes were also split between pairs of performers. Virginie Efira and Tao Okamoto shared the best actress award for their work in Ryusuke Hamaguchi's care drama All of a Sudden, while Valentin Campagne and Emmanuel Macchia won best actor for the queer World War I drama Coward.

This year's festival was notably quieter than usual, with fewer Hollywood stars and somewhat disappointing showings from established auteurs. The two American films in competition, James Gray's Paper Tiger starring Adam Driver and Scarlett Johansson, and Ira Sachs' AIDS musical The Man I Love with Rami Malek, were both overlooked by the judges.

US distributor Neon has already acquired Fjord, marking their seventh consecutive year purchasing the Palme d'Or winner. The company previously distributed Sean Baker's Anora, which won the Palme two years ago and went on to sweep the 2025 Oscars.

Park Chan-wook led this year's jury, which included Demi Moore, Stellan Skarsgård, Chloé Zhao, and Paul Laverty. During the closing ceremony, Isabelle Huppert paid tribute to Barbra Streisand, one of three honorary Palme d'Or recipients alongside Peter Jackson and John Travolta. Streisand, unable to attend due to a knee injury, sent a video message celebrating cinema's ability to unite people across cultures.

Valeska Grisebach took third place with the Jury prize for The Dreamed Adventure, a Bulgaria-set drama about an archaeologist.

The director award was shared between Pawel Pawlikowski for Fatherland and the duo of Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi for The Black Ball. Both acting prizes were also split between pairs of performers. Virginie Efira and Tao Okamoto shared the best actress award for their work in Ryusuke Hamaguchi's care drama All of a Sudden, while Valentin Campagne and Emmanuel Macchia won best actor for the queer World War I drama Coward.

This year's festival was notably quieter than usual, with fewer Hollywood stars and somewhat disappointing showings from established auteurs. The two American films in competition, James Gray's Paper Tiger starring Adam Driver and Scarlett Johansson, and Ira Sachs' AIDS musical The Man I Love with Rami Malek, were both overlooked by the judges.

US distributor Neon has already acquired Fjord, marking their seventh consecutive year purchasing the Palme d'Or winner. The company previously distributed Sean Baker's Anora, which won the Palme two years ago and went on to sweep the 2025 Oscars.

Park Chan-wook led this year's jury, which included Demi Moore, Stellan Skarsgård, Chloé Zhao, and Paul Laverty. During the closing ceremony, Isabelle Huppert paid tribute to Barbra Streisand, one of three honorary Palme d'Or recipients alongside Peter Jackson and John Travolta. Streisand, unable to attend due to a knee injury, sent a video message celebrating cinema's ability to unite people across cultures.
