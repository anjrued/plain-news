Yesterday, 11:10 PM ET

US military forces conducted strikes on targets in Iran, including missile launch sites, in what officials described as self-defense operations. The strikes took place along Iran's Gulf coast as diplomatic delegations gathered in Qatar for ongoing negotiations.

Central Command characterized the military action as self-defense strikes while maintaining that a ceasefire remained in place. The operations targeted Iranian missile infrastructure in southern Iran, coinciding with diplomatic efforts in Doha aimed at reaching a broader agreement between the two countries.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio stated that a deal with Iran could take days to finalize, speaking shortly after the strikes were launched. He said the Strait of Hormuz would reopen "one way or the other," referencing the critical shipping lane that has been central to tensions in the region.

The strikes caused mixed reactions in global markets. Asian shares and oil prices showed varied responses to the military action and ongoing diplomatic efforts. The military operations occurred even as negotiators worked to resolve several key issues between Washington and Tehran.

The negotiations in Qatar involve multiple points of contention. President Trump has linked any potential Iran deal to the Abraham Accords, stating that Muslim nations involved in an agreement must join the normalization framework. The administration seeks to expand the accords as part of its broader regional strategy.

The deal under discussion has faced criticism. Some observers question whether the diplomatic approach represents a genuine breakthrough or an unrealistic strategy. Trump has also sought to broaden the scope of negotiations to include regional partnerships beyond the immediate bilateral issues with Iran.

Despite the military strikes, both Iran and the US have downplayed expectations for an immediate breakthrough. Negotiators continue to work through disagreements over Iran's nuclear program and sanctions relief, among other matters. The simultaneous military action and diplomatic talks reflect the complexity of the current US-Iran relationship.

The strait remains a focal point of concern for global shipping and energy markets. The outcome of the Qatar negotiations could reshape regional security arrangements and international access to the Gulf waterway.

Yesterday, 11:24 PM ET

Thousands of residents in Orange County, California, have been allowed to return home after officials determined that a catastrophic explosion at a chemical storage facility is no longer a threat. The evacuation orders that had displaced approximately 40,000 people across six cities were lifted following an overnight operation that stabilized the dangerous situation.

The crisis began Thursday at a GKN Aerospace facility in Garden Grove, where a storage tank containing methyl methacrylate, a highly flammable chemical used in producing resins and plastics, began releasing gases and showing signs of potential failure. Authorities initially ordered evacuations but lifted them the same day. However, the situation worsened when damage to a valve on the tank created additional operational challenges that prevented complete mitigation, prompting new evacuation orders by Friday.

"We have a tank that is actively in crisis," Orange County Fire Authority division chief Craig Covey told reporters. "There are literally two options left remaining. One: the tank fails and spills a total of about 6-7,000 gallons of very bad chemicals into the parking lot in that area. Or two: the tank goes into a thermal runaway and blows up, affecting the tanks that are around them that have fuel or the chemicals in them as well."

The affected areas included Garden Grove, Anaheim, Cypress, Stanton, Buena Park, and Westminster. The facility is located less than a mile from Disneyland, which was not placed under evacuation orders. Garden Grove sits approximately 30 miles southeast of downtown Los Angeles.

Firefighters worked throughout the weekend as the tank cracked, which authorities hoped would relieve internal pressure and reduce explosion risk. The breakthrough came overnight Monday when officials announced they had successfully stabilized the tank through cooling and continuous monitoring, eliminating the possibility of a massive blast.

Dr. Regina Chinsio-Kwong, the county health officer, had warned that if the chemical overheated, it could release harmful vapors capable of causing respiratory issues, itching, burning eyes, nausea, and headaches. To prepare for a potential spill, crews had constructed containment barriers using sandbags to prevent the toxic chemical from entering storm drains or reaching nearby waterways and the ocean.

Despite the elimination of explosion risks, county officials stressed the crisis remains active. Emergency management personnel continue working at the facility to address remaining hazards and complete containment operations. The storage tank remains under close observation as crews work toward full resolution of the incident.

During the evacuation, some 15 percent of residents in the affected zones initially refused to leave their homes, according to authorities. The city established multiple shelters to accommodate displaced families during the emergency response.

The filing reveals financial metrics that investors will use to evaluate the company's valuation. SpaceX has grown into a dominant force in commercial spaceflight, launching satellites, cargo, and astronauts to the International Space Station while developing its Starship rocket system.

One significant detail in the filing involves Musk's associates. A firm connected to someone described as Musk's best friend stands to gain more than $100 billion from the IPO. That same firm is also owed billions of dollars by SpaceX, according to the disclosure documents.

The public offering comes as SpaceX operates like an artificial intelligence company in terms of spending levels, according to financial charts included in the filing. The company has invested heavily in infrastructure and technology development, mirroring the capital-intensive approach of major AI firms.

The filing characterizes the IPO as a bet that normal business gravity does not apply to Musk's enterprises. SpaceX has operated outside typical aerospace industry constraints, building reusable rockets and undercutting traditional launch providers on price. The company now seeks to convince public investors that its growth trajectory will continue.

SpaceX has not announced a specific date for the IPO or disclosed how many shares it plans to sell. The company also has not revealed the price range at which it expects to offer stock. Those details typically emerge in amended filings as the offering date approaches.

The IPO will give the public its first detailed look at SpaceX's revenue, profit margins, debt levels, and contract pipeline. Yesterday, 7:57 AM ET

Google has overhauled its search interface for the first time in nearly 30 years, integrating artificial intelligence directly into the search experience. The changes, announced at Google I/O, represent a fundamental shift in how the company delivers information to billions of users worldwide.

The redesign allows users to ask longer, more conversational queries instead of using search's traditional syntax. Elizabeth Reid, who oversees the product, described the update as "the biggest Google Search had seen in its nearly 30-year history." Rather than returning a simple list of links, the new interface prioritizes visual elements and AI-generated responses similar to Google's existing AI Overviews feature, which displays bulleted summaries.

The changes are powered by Gemini 3.5, Google's latest artificial intelligence model announced the same day. Users can still access the traditional web link results by clicking a "Web" tab if they prefer the original search format.

Google has introduced several new features for subscribers to its AI Pro and Ultra plans. Gemini Spark, an AI agent, can access information from Gmail, Google Calendar and other Google products to perform research, handle shopping, and plan trips or appointments. The company is also rolling out "information agents," autonomous bots that conduct in-depth research and return summaries or action plans. A "generative user interface" feature can create customized visuals and interactive elements based on user requests.

The transformation reflects Google's response to competition in the AI space. Reid noted that Google Search queries reached an all-time high last month. Since launching "AI Mode" a year ago, which directs users to chat with a Gemini-powered bot rather than view links, chatbot queries have doubled every quarter. When users add photos, videos or documents to the Chrome search bar, the interface automatically switches to AI mode.

Google executives described Gemini as working toward creating "a universal assistant that's personal, proactive, persistent." The company reported that 900 million people use the Gemini app monthly, though this remains lower than ChatGPT's 900 million weekly users.

At the same conference, Google unveiled intelligent eyewear developed with partners Samsung, Warby Parker and Gentle Monster. The voice-activated glasses with embedded cameras will reach consumers in autumn. The move follows Google Glass, which launched in 2014 for 1,500 dollars and was discontinued after widespread criticism.

The global rollout to desktop and mobile versions of Google Search began on Tuesday. The changes mark a significant shift in how people access online information, though Google has not disclosed specific impacts on website traffic or advertising revenue, which have historically depended on users clicking through to external sites.

Yesterday, 9:41 PM ET

Sonny Rollins, one of the greatest jazz saxophonists of all time, has died at age 95. His death was announced Monday on his official website and confirmed by his publicist Terri Hinte. He died at his home in Woodstock, New York on Monday afternoon. No cause of death was provided.

Rollins earned his reputation as a genius of improvisation and melody throughout a career spanning more than seven decades. Saxophonist Branford Marsalis called him "the greatest improviser in the history of jazz" alongside Louis Armstrong. When presenting Rollins with the National Medal of the Arts in 2011, President Barack Obama said the musician had inspired him to "take risks that I might not otherwise have taken."

Born Walter Theodore Rollins in Harlem in 1930, he began playing saxophone at age seven. Inspired by his siblings who played piano and violin, as well as jazz legends like Louis Jordan and Fats Waller, Rollins quickly developed his skills. His high school band included future stars Jackie McLean, Kenny Drew and Art Taylor.

Rollins helped shape jazz alongside Miles Davis and Charlie Parker during the bebop era. Davis wrote about how Rollins became "a legend, almost a god to a lot of the younger musicians" and praised him as an aggressive, innovative player with fresh musical ideas. Rollins released his debut album as a bandleader in 1953 and recorded seventeen more albums by the end of the 1950s.

His landmark recordings included "Saxophone Colossus" in 1956, which featured his signature composition "St Thomas," named after his mother's Caribbean birthplace. In 1957, he released "Way Out West," which explored a piano-free "strolling" style. The 1958 album "Freedom Suite" included a twenty-minute title track that served as an elegant statement for freedom during the civil rights movement.

From 1956 to 1959, Rollins famously practiced for up to fifteen hours daily on the pedestrian walkway of the Williamsburg Bridge, partly to avoid disturbing neighbors. This dedication led to his 1962 comeback album "The Bridge." Another three-year break from 1969 to 1971 saw him travel to an Indian ashram to study yoga, philosophy and meditation.

Throughout his career, Rollins recorded more than sixty albums and collaborated with major jazz figures including Thelonious Monk, John Coltrane, Dizzy Gillespie and Max Roach. He later explored Latin American music, funk and calypso influences. He also composed and performed the soundtrack to the 1966 Michael Caine film "Alfie."

Rollins married Lucille Pearson in 1965, and they remained together until her death in 2004. He was one of the last surviving stars of the bebop generation, representing a direct link to jazz's golden era and its development as a major American art form.

Trump pauses deportation flights to Congo over Ebola concerns

The pause reflects concerns about exposing Immigration and Customs Enforcement officials to the disease. However, experts question whether the measure will actually prevent disease spread. The decision also creates compli...

May 21, 5:06 PM ET

The Trump administration has temporarily halted the removal of detainees to the Democratic Republic of Congo due to a widening Ebola outbreak in the country. The pause reflects concerns about exposing Immigration and Customs Enforcement officials to the disease.

However, experts question whether the measure will actually prevent disease spread. The decision also creates complications for individuals already transferred to the region, with at least one woman now stuck in limbo after officials moved her to Kinshasa, the capital of the DRC, and subsequently refused to bring her back despite a judge's order for her return.

The temporary pause applies specifically to flights bound for Congo and marks one of the first instances where disease concerns have prompted modifications to the administration's deportation policies. The Ebola virus causes severe illness and has killed thousands in past Central African outbreaks, triggering international public health responses. The current outbreak has been severe enough that the DRC's World Cup team cancelled training in Kinshasa, though the team still plans to travel to the United States for competition.

The decision comes amid broader controversy over the Trump administration's deportation flight operations. A group of 40 House Democrats recently sent a letter to the Federal Aviation Administration expressing grave concerns about what they describe as a secretive program involving ICE charter jets. The lawmakers cited credible reports that individuals have been placed on flights without notice to their lawyers or family members, effectively disappearing from public view when flights are shielded from public tracking systems.

According to monitoring by human rights groups, the Trump administration increased ICE flights by 84 percent in 2025 compared to 2024. The letter references an investigation by the Guardian based on leaked flight data, which revealed the administration transported detained immigrants in ways that routinely violated their constitutional rights. The reporting also identified allegations of abuse at a private detention center in Alexandria, Louisiana, a central node in the deportation program.

The lawmakers, led by New Jersey congressman Rob Menendez, demanded the FAA provide detailed reports on all ICE air operations since Trump took office, including flight origins, destinations, and passenger numbers. They also called for information on how the agency assesses humanitarian conditions on ICE flights, particularly regarding the controversial use of full body restraints during deportation flights.

The Trump administration has previously defended its deportation operations as following standard protocols and maintaining higher standards than most U.S. prisons. Officials have described claims of hidden or weaponized flights as categorically false.

The administration has not specified how long the Congo pause will remain in effect. Such decisions typically depend on assessments of outbreak severity and transmission risk from health authorities. The flights could resume once officials determine exposure risk to personnel has sufficiently diminished.

Sonny Rollins, one of the greatest jazz saxophonists of all time, has died at age 95. His death was announced Monday on his official website and confirmed by his publicist Terri Hinte. He died at his home in Woodstock, New York on Monday afternoon. No cause of death was provided.

Rollins earned his reputation as a genius of improvisation and melody throughout a career spanning more than seven decades. Saxophonist Branford Marsalis called him "the greatest improviser in the history of jazz" alongside Louis Armstrong. When presenting Rollins with the National Medal of the Arts in 2011, President Barack Obama said the musician had inspired him to "take risks that I might not otherwise have taken."

Born Walter Theodore Rollins in Harlem in 1930, he began playing saxophone at age seven. Inspired by his siblings who played piano and violin, as well as jazz legends like Louis Jordan and Fats Waller, Rollins quickly developed his skills. His high school band included future stars Jackie McLean, Kenny Drew and Art Taylor.

Rollins helped shape jazz alongside Miles Davis and Charlie Parker during the bebop era. Davis wrote about how Rollins became "a legend, almost a god to a lot of the younger musicians" and praised him as an aggressive, innovative player with fresh musical ideas. Rollins released his debut album as a bandleader in 1953 and recorded seventeen more albums by the end of the 1950s.

His landmark recordings included "Saxophone Colossus" in 1956, which featured his signature composition "St Thomas," named after his mother's Caribbean birthplace. In 1957, he released "Way Out West," which explored a piano-free "strolling" style. The 1958 album "Freedom Suite" included a twenty-minute title track that served as an elegant statement for freedom during the civil rights movement.

From 1956 to 1959, Rollins famously practiced for up to fifteen hours daily on the pedestrian walkway of the Williamsburg Bridge, partly to avoid disturbing neighbors. This dedication led to his 1962 comeback album "The Bridge." Another three-year break from 1969 to 1971 saw him travel to an Indian ashram to study yoga, philosophy and meditation.

Throughout his career, Rollins recorded more than sixty albums and collaborated with major jazz figures including Thelonious Monk, John Coltrane, Dizzy Gillespie and Max Roach. He later explored Latin American music, funk and calypso influences. He also composed and performed the soundtrack to the 1966 Michael Caine film "Alfie."

Rollins married Lucille Pearson in 1965, and they remained together until her death in 2004. He was one of the last surviving stars of the bebop generation, representing a direct link to jazz's golden era and its development as a major American art form.
