# 40,000 Evacuated in Orange County After Chemical Tank Threatens to Explode

More than 40,000 people have been ordered to evacuate in Orange County, California, after a storage tank containing a highly flammable chemical began leaking and threatened to explode or rupture.

The crisis began Thursday at a GKN Aerospace facility in Garden Grove, where a tank holding methyl methacrylate started off-gassing and showing signs of failure. The chemical, used to manufacture resins and plastics, is highly flammable and poses a serious explosion risk.

Authorities initially ordered evacuations on Thursday and brought in a hazmat team to address the problem. They lifted the order later that day, but the situation worsened when damage to a valve on the tank created additional complications that prevented workers from fully containing the leak.

By Friday, evacuation orders had expanded to residents in six cities across Orange County. "We have a tank that is actively in crisis," Orange County Fire Authority Division Chief Craig Covey told reporters at a news conference.

Covey outlined two possible scenarios facing emergency responders. "There are literally two options left remaining. One: the tank fails and spills a total of about 6-7,000 gallons of very bad chemicals into the parking lot in that area. Or two: the tank goes into a thermal runaway and blows up, affecting the tanks that are around them that have fuel or the chemicals in them as well."

Garden Grove Police Chief Amir El-Farra said approximately 15% of residents under evacuation orders were refusing to leave, according to the Orange County Register. The city has established at least two shelters for displaced residents.

Officials stressed the urgency of the situation and urged residents to take the evacuation orders seriously. "We are setting up these evacuations in preparation for these two options: it fails or it blows up," Covey said. "Please follow our requests and orders for evacuations."

Emergency responders have laid out sand barriers around the facility to contain the chemicals if the tank cracks rather than explodes. Covey called this scenario the best possible outcome. "In a weird world, that's the best-case scenario, believe it or not," he said.

GKN Aerospace, a division of a British corporation, produces airplane engines and aircraft parts. The Garden Grove facility stores methyl methacrylate for use in manufacturing processes.

Schools in the affected areas have been shut down as authorities work to manage the crisis. Officials have not provided a timeline for when residents might be able to return to their homes, as emergency crews continue monitoring the tank's stability and preparing for potential failure.

# Eight Dead, One Missing After Avalanche Strikes Sierra Nevada Backcountry Skiers

Eight skiers have been confirmed dead and one remains missing after an avalanche swept through the Castle Peak area of California's Sierra Nevada mountains on Tuesday morning. The incident ranks as the deadliest single avalanche in the United States in 45 years.

Six other skiers who were stranded have been rescued, two with injuries requiring hospitalization. The avalanche struck around 11:30 AM on Tuesday, engulfing a group of backcountry skiers that included four guides and 11 clients on a guided ski tour about 10 miles north of Lake Tahoe.

"This is an ongoing incident and some facts do change," Nevada County Sheriff Shannan Moon said during a Wednesday press conference. She explained that while 12 people had signed up for the three-day backcountry skiing trek, one person backed out at the last minute.

The group was on the final day of their expedition when the avalanche hit. Search and rescue operations involving approximately 46 emergency first responders were launched immediately after a 911 call reported the incident.

Rescuers used a Sno-Cat vehicle to reach within 2 miles of where six survivors had sheltered in a makeshift refuge constructed partly from tarpaulin sheets. Search and rescue volunteers then skied the remaining distance to reach them. The survivors had already recovered three bodies before help arrived.

First responders located five additional bodies but could not transport them back due to severe weather conditions. Two of the rescued skiers were not mobile because of their injuries. One was stabilized and released from the hospital, while the other remained under treatment for non-life-threatening injuries.

The tragedy hit close to home for the rescue community. "One of the nine missing decedents is a spouse of one of our Tahoe Nordic search and rescue team members," said Placer County Sheriff Wayne Woo.

The Sierra Avalanche Center had posted a high avalanche danger alert before dawn on Tuesday. The area experienced extremely hazardous conditions with heavy snowfall, strong winds, and low visibility. The nearby town of Soda Springs recorded at least 30 inches of snow in a 24-hour period.

"It's particularly dangerous in the backcountry right now just because we're at the height of the storm," said Brandon Schwartz, lead avalanche forecaster at the Sierra Avalanche Center.

Nevada County Sheriff Captain Russell Greene questioned the decision to take paying customers into such conditions. "I don't think it was a wise choice, but we don't know all the details yet," he told the Associated Press.

The avalanche stretched about the length of a football field, and the terrain has since accumulated another 3 feet of snow, keeping avalanche danger high in the area.

The last deadlier avalanche in the US occurred in 1981 when 11 people died on Mount Rainier in Washington state.

# Trump Says New Fed Chair Should Be "Totally Independent" as Warsh Takes Office

President Trump said Friday that the new Federal Reserve chair should be "totally independent," as Kevin Warsh was sworn in to lead the central bank during a period of mounting economic pressure.

Warsh, a former Wall Street banker hand-picked by Trump, takes control of the powerful institution as the president faces growing backlash over the cost of living and disapproval of his economic agenda. The appointment comes at a time when Trump has been applying extraordinary pressure on the Fed to cut interest rates, even as prices continue to climb.

The Federal Reserve chair position is one of the most influential economic policymaking roles in the United States, with authority over interest rates and banking regulation. The Fed operates as an independent agency designed to make monetary policy decisions without political interference.

Trump's comments about independence stand in stark contrast to his actions regarding other federal agencies. This week, Federal Communications Commission chairman Brendan Carr declared that the FCC is not an "independent" agency, and the word was removed from its online mission statement. Carr, a vocal Trump supporter, told senators on Wednesday that the FCC "is not an independent agency, formally speaking."

Before Carr's testimony, the FCC website described the agency as "an independent US government agency overseen by Congress," according to a screenshot captured by news outlet Axios. During his appearance, however, that language disappeared.

Democratic senators challenged Carr over threats he made against TV networks broadcasting content critical of Trump. In comments to a conservative podcaster, Carr said regarding late-night host Jimmy Kimmel: "We can do this the easy way or the hard way. These companies can find ways to change conduct and take action, frankly, on Kimmel or, you know, there's going to be additional work for the FCC ahead."

Senator Amy Klobuchar of Minnesota asked Carr: "Do you think it is appropriate to use your position to threaten companies that broadcast political satire?" Carr replied by citing the vague public interest standard from a 1934 federal law.

Senator Tammy Baldwin of Wisconsin called Carr "a parrot for President Trump" who has "diminished the independence of the FCC."

Warsh previously served on the Federal Reserve Board of Governors from 2006 to 2011, giving him experience managing the central bank during the financial crisis. He now faces the challenge of pursuing the Fed's dual mandate of maximum employment and stable prices while navigating Trump's public demands for rate cuts.

The contrast between Trump's stated support for Fed independence and his administration's approach to other agencies has drawn attention from economic observers and market participants watching how Warsh will handle pressure from the White House.

# Google Blocks Searches for the Word "Disregard" Due to AI Search Problems

Google has temporarily disabled searches for the word "disregard" after its AI Overview feature began malfunctioning. Users attempting to search for the term receive no results, marking an unusual technical failure for the world's most popular search engine.

The problem was first spotted on Friday by users on social media platform X. When people searched for "disregard," Google's AI Overview section displayed responses similar to a traditional chatbot instead of providing the usual AI summary or search results. The company has since blocked the term entirely rather than show broken results.

The issue appears connected to Google's recent major overhaul of its search platform. At its annual I/O conference for software developers on Tuesday, Google announced the biggest changes to Search in its nearly 30-year history. Elizabeth Reid, who oversees Google Search, said the updates would transform how billions of people find information online.

The new AI-powered search relies on Google's Gemini 3.5 artificial intelligence model. The updated search box now accepts longer, more conversational queries instead of the traditional keyword-based searches. Results now display more visual elements and suggestions to interact with Google's AI tools.

"Google Search is AI search," Reid said at the conference, describing the fundamental shift in how the platform operates.

The changes rolled out globally to desktop and mobile versions of Google Search on Tuesday. Users can still access the traditional link-based results by clicking a "Web" tab, but the default experience now features AI-generated overviews and summaries.

Reid reported that Google Search queries reached an all-time high last month. Since the debut of "AI Mode" a year ago, which lets users chat with a Gemini-powered bot, queries to the chatbot interface have doubled every quarter.

Josh Woodward, who oversees Gemini's development, said the goal is creating "a universal assistant that's personal, proactive, persistent." Google announced that 900 million people use the Gemini app monthly, though this still trails ChatGPT's 900 million weekly users.

The "disregard" blocking appears to be a workaround for a bug in the AI Overview system rather than an intentional censorship decision. Similar technical issues have affected Google's AI features since their introduction, as the company rushes to compete with rivals like ChatGPT and Microsoft's AI-powered Bing.

Google has not released a statement explaining when the restriction will be lifted or whether other terms are similarly affected. The block prevents users from accessing the word's definition, usage examples, or any content featuring "disregard" as a search term.

The incident highlights the challenges tech companies face when replacing traditional search with AI-generated responses.
