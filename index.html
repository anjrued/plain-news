Gabbard's resignation represents a significant change in the leadership of the U.S. intelligence community. The director of national intelligence serves as the head of the intelligence community and principal intelligence advisor to the president.

The resignation was announced on Friday. Gabbard cited her husband's health as the driving factor in her decision to step down from the position.

Gabbard, a former congresswoman from Hawaii, has been a controversial figure in national politics. Her appointment to lead the intelligence community had drawn both support and criticism from different political quarters.

The departure leaves a critical national security position vacant. The director of national intelligence oversees 18 intelligence agencies and is responsible for coordinating intelligence activities across the federal government.

No information has been provided about who will serve as acting director or who might be nominated as Gabbard's permanent replacement. The timing of the resignation and the transition process remain to be determined.

The position of director of national intelligence was created in 2004 following the September 11 attacks as part of a restructuring of the intelligence community. The role carries significant responsibility for protecting national security and advising on threats facing the United States.

"It Takes Two" became one of the most recognizable songs of the late 1980s hip-hop era, sampling Lyn Collins' "Think (About It)" and featuring the distinctive vocals of Base alongside DJ E-Z Rock's production.

The song reached number 36 on the Billboard Hot 100 and became a cultural touchstone, frequently used in films, commercials, and sporting events over the decades. It was certified platinum by the Recording Industry Association of America.

Base's death was confirmed on Friday, though no cause of death has been announced. The news comes as a loss to the hip-hop community, where "It Takes Two" remains a staple of golden age rap and is widely celebrated for its infectious energy and party atmosphere.

Beyond his signature hit, Base released the album "It Takes Two" in 1988, which also included tracks like "Joy and Pain" and "Get on the Dance Floor." DJ E-Z Rock, Base's original partner, died in 2014 at age 46. The duo's contribution to hip-hop history remains significant, with "It Takes Two" frequently cited as one of the genre's most enduring party anthems and a defining track of its era.
