Google has inadvertently disclosed details of an unpatched security vulnerability in Chromium, the open-source browser that powers Chrome, Edge, and dozens of other web browsers. The premature disclosure means millions of users may be at risk before a fix becomes widely available.

The vulnerability details were published in Google's issue tracking system before a patch had been finalized and distributed. Security researchers and potentially malicious actors can now examine the flaw's technical specifics, creating a window of opportunity for exploitation. Google has since restricted access to the issue report, but the information circulated among security communities during the exposure period.

Chromium serves as the foundation for most modern browsers beyond Firefox and Safari. Google Chrome alone accounts for roughly 65 percent of global browser market share, while Microsoft Edge and other Chromium-based browsers add millions more users. The scope of potential impact extends across desktop and mobile platforms.

This type of premature disclosure, sometimes called a zero-day vulnerability when actively exploited, represents a significant security concern. Attackers can reverse-engineer the flaw from public descriptions and develop exploits faster than users can apply patches. The risk is heightened because browser vulnerabilities can enable attackers to execute malicious code, steal credentials, or compromise user data without requiring any action beyond visiting a compromised website.

Google typically follows coordinated disclosure practices, which involve privately notifying affected parties and developing patches before making vulnerability details public. This approach gives software vendors time to fix problems and users time to update their systems. The accidental exposure breaks this standard protocol.

The company has not specified when a patch will be released or provided details about the vulnerability's severity rating. Browser makers typically issue emergency updates for critical security flaws outside their regular update schedules. Users of Chrome and other Chromium-based browsers should watch for security updates in coming days and install them immediately when available.

This incident highlights ongoing challenges in coordinating security disclosures across large open-source projects. Chromium's code is used by multiple vendors who must each compile and distribute their own patched versions. The lag between patch availability and widespread deployment creates extended risk periods.

Users cannot take specific protective action until patches arrive, though standard security practices like avoiding suspicious websites and keeping browsers updated provide baseline protection.

Today, 8:00 PM ET

New research has confirmed what climate scientists have long suspected: the rate of sea level rise has been increasing steadily since 1960, with the acceleration continuing into the present day.

The findings come from an improved analysis of historical sea level measurements, providing the clearest picture yet of how quickly oceans are rising. The research addresses a critical gap in understanding how climate change is affecting coastal regions where hundreds of millions of people live.

Scientists combined data from tide gauges and satellite measurements to create a more accurate historical record. The work required accounting for various factors that affect local sea level readings, including land subsidence, ocean currents, and gravitational effects from melting ice sheets.

The acceleration itself is significant. While sea levels rose at a relatively steady pace through the early 20th century, the rate began increasing around 1960 and has continued to climb. Current rates of sea level rise are now substantially higher than they were six decades ago.

The research helps resolve uncertainties that have existed in previous analyses. Earlier studies sometimes showed conflicting results about when acceleration began or how pronounced it was. The improved methodology provides more confidence in the timeline and magnitude of changes.

The primary drivers of accelerating sea level rise are well established: thermal expansion of warming ocean water and increased melting of land-based ice from glaciers and ice sheets in Greenland and Antarctica. As global temperatures have risen, both processes have intensified.

The findings have direct implications for coastal planning and infrastructure decisions. Cities and regions that use historical sea level data to plan for future flooding need to account for acceleration, not just steady linear rise. Projections based on constant rates will significantly underestimate future sea levels.

The research also provides a clearer baseline for testing climate models. Accurate historical records of sea level acceleration allow scientists to validate whether their models correctly capture the physical processes driving ocean rise.

While the study confirms acceleration is occurring, the exact future trajectory depends on greenhouse gas emissions and resulting temperature increases. Higher emissions scenarios lead to faster acceleration, while reduced emissions could slow the rate of increase, though not reverse the overall rising trend that is already locked in from past warming.

Analysis of SpaceX's ownership structure reveals that a public offering would deliver the vast majority of financial gains to CEO Elon Musk and a tight circle of early investors and senior executives. New research suggests magnetic field direction could affect how the chemical building blocks of life form and organize themselves.

8:00 PM ET

Scientists have discovered that magnetic fields may play a previously unknown role in shaping how biological molecules form and orient themselves. The research examined how magnetic field direction affects certain chemical reactions important to the origin of life, finding that the presence and orientation of magnetic fields can influence which molecular structures emerge.

The findings suggest that Earth's magnetic field could have been a factor in the development of life's molecular asymmetry, including why biological molecules often favor one structural form over its mirror image. This adds a new dimension to theories about how life originated on Earth and what conditions might be necessary for life to develop on other planets. The research opens questions about whether planets without strong magnetic fields would produce different kinds of biochemistry.

Scientists explain why humans are predominantly right-handed

New research identifies two evolutionary factors that led to right-handedness becoming dominant in human populations.

8:00 PM ET

A new study proposes an explanation for why roughly 90 percent of humans are right-handed, identifying two key evolutionary factors that drove this population-level asymmetry. The research examines both genetic and social mechanisms that reinforced right-hand preference over generations, creating one of the most consistent behavioral traits across human cultures.

The first factor involves brain lateralization, where language and tool-use functions concentrated in the left hemisphere, which controls the right side of the body. The second factor relates to social learning and cultural transmission, where right-handed tool use became standardized because it was easier to teach and learn skills when everyone used the same hand. The research draws on studies of hand anatomy, archaeological evidence of ancient tool use, and genetic data. Understanding handedness provides insights into human brain evolution and how individual variation is maintained even when one trait becomes heavily dominant.
