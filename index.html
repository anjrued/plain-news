Oil prices dropped below $100 a barrel on Monday following reports that the United States and Iran are moving closer to reaching a peace agreement. Brent crude futures, the global oil benchmark, fell 6 percent to $97.43 a barrel, marking the lowest level in two weeks as markets responded to diplomatic progress.

The price decline reflects investor expectations that a deal could end the nearly three-month conflict between the US, Israel, and Iran. The war had prompted Iran to close the Strait of Hormuz, a critical energy chokepoint that disrupted global oil flows and sent prices soaring after missile strikes began on February 28.

However, significant obstacles remain. While a framework for an agreement has been negotiated, the US and Iran continue to disagree on key issues, including Iran's blockade of the strait. An Iranian government spokesperson cautioned that an agreement was "not imminent," suggesting negotiations remain uncertain.

Market analysts urged caution about the price decline. Warren Patterson, head of commodities strategy at ING, told Reuters: "We've been at this stage before, only for talks to break down. Therefore, the market will likely be more cautious about overreacting."

Even if negotiations succeed and the strait reopens, analysts expect a gradual return to normal oil flows. Damaged energy infrastructure in Qatar and other regions will require months to repair. Current shipping data shows some movement, with two tankers carrying liquefied natural gas exiting the strait on Monday and a supertanker carrying Iraqi crude leaving the Gulf after being stranded for nearly three months. However, Giovanni Staunovo, an analyst at UBS, noted that flows through the strait "remain restricted" despite these developments.

Oil prices remain substantially higher than before the conflict, when Brent crude traded around $70 per barrel. Barclays maintained its average Brent crude forecast of $100 for the year but acknowledged risks of higher prices.

Stock markets responded positively to the diplomatic developments. Japan's Nikkei rose nearly 3 percent while the pan-European Stoxx 600 index gained 1 percent. Several major markets, including those in the US and UK, were closed Monday for a public holiday. The dollar dipped 0.3 percent against a basket of major currencies, while the pound gained almost 0.6 percent to $1.3506. Gold climbed 1.46 percent to $4,574 an ounce.

Stephen Innes, an independent analyst, said the market response reflected optimism about the potential reopening of the critical energy chokepoint. Investors had priced considerable inflation fear into markets during the recent energy shock, and the prospect of normalizing flows encouraged a more positive outlook.

The broader economic implications remain significant. Higher oil prices have fueled inflation concerns worldwide, prompting central banks to shift from considering rate cuts toward predicting rate increases.

8:45 AM ET

The FBI issued a warning about Kali365, a phishing service targeting Microsoft 365 accounts. The service uses OAuth consent techniques to bypass multifactor authentication, allowing attackers to access accounts even when additional security measures are in place.

Kali365 represents a new category of phishing threat that exploits legitimate Microsoft login pages. Instead of creating fake login screens, attackers use real Microsoft authentication flows to trick users into granting access to malicious applications through OAuth consent prompts.

The Australian Signals Directorate also warned about device code phishing as security firm Proofpoint tracked the growth of criminal toolkits using similar techniques. These attacks work by presenting users with authentic Microsoft login pages, making them harder to detect than traditional phishing attempts.

OAuth consent phishing bypasses traditional security measures because it does not require attackers to steal passwords or break through multifactor authentication. Instead, users unknowingly grant permissions to attacker-controlled applications that appear legitimate during the authentication process.

The phishing service has become part of a growing ecosystem of criminal tools designed to exploit enterprise cloud services. Security researchers note that these attacks are particularly effective because they leverage trusted platforms and authentication systems that users are trained to recognize as legitimate.

Organizations using Microsoft 365 should review their OAuth consent policies and educate users about the risks of granting application permissions. The FBI warning highlights the evolving nature of phishing threats as attackers shift from credential theft to permission-based access methods that exploit user trust in legitimate authentication systems.

Disney paused theatrical Star Wars releases after The Rise of Skywalker in 2019, concluding the studio had released too many films too quickly. The Mandalorian and Grogu represents the franchise's return to cinemas following that seven-year gap.

Despite the lower opening compared to other Star Wars films, the movie may still prove financially successful. Disney executives expect the film to hold well in coming weeks based on positive audience reception, even as critical response has been muted.

The studio is banking on the film's popularity extending beyond theatrical releases. Disney hopes it will drive viewers toward merchandise, theme park attractions, and its streaming service. The Mandalorian remains one of Disney+'s flagship shows, though other recent Star Wars series like Obi-Wan Kenobi and The Acolyte have underperformed.

The Mandalorian and Grogu claimed the top spot at the box office during its opening weekend but set an unwelcome record as Disney's weakest-performing Star Wars film launch. The movie earned $102 million domestically over the four-day Memorial Day weekend, contributing to a global total of $165 million.

The figures represent a significant drop compared to previous Star Wars releases under Disney ownership. The film failed to match the opening weekend performance of Solo: A Star Wars Story, which Disney released in 2018. Solo opened on Memorial Day weekend that same year with $103 million domestically and $171 million globally, outperforming The Mandalorian and Grogu's opening despite being considered a box office disappointment at the time.

The film brings the popular Disney+ series to theaters, following the bounty hunter Din Djarin, played by Pedro Pascal, and his small companion known as "Baby Yoda" among audiences. The characters have proven popular on the streaming platform, with The Mandalorian becoming the most-watched original series on Disney+. More than 13 million Grogu toys have sold since the show debuted in 2019.

Industry analyst David Gross noted that while the opening numbers are substantial, they reflect typical performance for spin-off films rather than primary Star Wars releases. "The movie is a piece of the story, and it's earning a piece of the business," he told Agence France-Presse.

The modest opening may signal continued audience fatigue with Star Wars content. Disney paused theatrical Star Wars releases after The Rise of Skywalker in 2019, concluding the studio had released too many films too quickly. The Mandalorian and Grogu represents the franchise's return to cinemas following that seven-year gap.

Despite the lower opening compared to other Star Wars films, the movie may still prove financially successful. It carries a budget of $165 million, substantially less than Solo's $275 million production cost. Disney executives expect the film to hold well in coming weeks based on positive audience reception, even as critical response has been muted.

The studio is banking on the film's popularity extending beyond theatrical releases. Disney hopes it will drive viewers toward merchandise, theme park attractions, and its streaming service. The Mandalorian remains one of Disney+'s flagship shows, though other recent Star Wars series like Obi-Wan Kenobi and The Acolyte have underperformed.

Star Wars remains among the highest-grossing franchises of all time with over $10 billion in global ticket sales since its 1977 debut. Disney acquired the franchise from creator George Lucas in 2012. The next Star Wars theatrical film, Star Wars: Starfighter starring Ryan Gosling, is scheduled for May 2027.

The Spurs had led by as many as 16 points in the first half before the Thunder mounted a comeback. With the score up nine late in the second quarter, Wembanyama called for the ball and took a couple dribbles before the shot clock forced him to attempt the long-range shot. The buzzer-beater provided a crucial spark that the Spurs carried through the remainder of the game.

San Antonio dominated the rest of the matchup, ultimately winning by 21 points. The Thunder struggled from three-point range throughout the night, repeatedly missing from beyond the arc. The Spurs capitalized on Oklahoma City's shooting struggles while Wembanyama controlled play on both ends of the floor.

The half-court shot recalled Wembanyama's 32-foot three-pointer in Game 1, which forced a second overtime in a game the Spurs were losing. That earlier shot came under significant pressure when safer options were available. In Game 1, Wembanyama produced a 41-point, 24-rebound double-double that forced observers to question whether the defending champion Thunder could contain him.

Since that explosive performance, Wembanyama's scoring has varied. In Game 2 he had a milder showing, while in Game 3 his presence in the paint was notably quiet with only four rebounds. The Thunder's center Isaiah Hartenstein applied heavy physical defense designed to limit Wembanyama's ability to operate inside.

Game 4 marked a return to form. Wembanyama scored 33 of the Spurs' 103 points while maintaining his defensive impact. More significantly, he accomplished this in just 31 minutes. Late in the fourth quarter with San Antonio holding a comfortable lead, Wembanyama rested on a stationary bike in the tunnel, allowing his body to recover before future games.

The contrast in performance when Wembanyama rests is stark. In his 31 minutes, the Spurs outscored the Thunder by 29 points. During Luke Kornet's 13 minutes of backup center play, Oklahoma City outscored San Antonio by nine points. Kornet recorded respectable statistics with six points and seven rebounds, but the Thunder found it easier to score when Wembanyama was unavailable.

The Spurs face a precarious path forward in this series. They must rely on herculean performances from Wembanyama while receiving adequate support from their other players. The Thunder remain the deeper team overall, but Wembanyama's extraordinary talent has kept San Antonio competitive in this matchup against the league's best team.
