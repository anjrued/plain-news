Today, 6:00 AM ET

Oil prices rose sharply on Wednesday as negotiations to reopen the Strait of Hormuz hit an impasse, threatening global energy supplies through one of the world's most critical shipping chokepoints.

The strait, a narrow passage between Iran and Oman, carries roughly one-fifth of global oil supply. Any disruption to shipping through the waterway affects crude prices worldwide and increases costs for consumers. Recent diplomatic efforts between the United States and Iran had raised hopes for easing tensions in the region, but talks have stalled without agreement on access terms.

The price increase reflects market concern about supply security rather than an actual shortage. Traders are pricing in risk premiums as uncertainty grows about when normal shipping operations will resume. Energy analysts note that prolonged closure or restricted access could force tankers to take longer alternative routes around Africa, adding significant time and cost to deliveries.

The situation comes as global energy markets remain sensitive to geopolitical disruption. While the U.S. and Iran have been in diplomatic contact regarding broader peace negotiations, the practical details of reopening the strait have proven contentious. Iran controls the northern shore of the waterway and has historically used access as leverage in regional disputes.

Asia-Pacific markets, heavily dependent on Middle Eastern oil imports, are watching the situation closely. Japan, South Korea, China, and India all rely on steady flows through the strait to meet energy needs. Any extended disruption would require these countries to tap strategic reserves or secure alternative supplies at higher prices.

The immediate economic impact extends beyond fuel costs. Industries dependent on petroleum products, from manufacturing to aviation, face input cost increases that typically get passed to consumers. Transportation costs rise when fuel prices spike, affecting prices for goods across supply chains.

U.S. officials have not commented publicly on the status of negotiations. Iran has previously stated that shipping access depends on broader diplomatic progress and sanctions relief. The standoff highlights how infrastructure bottlenecks in global trade can quickly translate to economic consequences felt worldwide, even when physical supply remains adequate in absolute terms.

This prevents malicious actors from weaponizing the information while systems remain undefended.

Google has not specified the nature of the vulnerability or its severity level. The company removed the detailed information from its bug tracker after the error was discovered, but security researchers and potentially malicious actors may have already copied the technical details during the window of exposure.

Browser makers are now racing to develop and distribute patches. Users of Chromium-based browsers should update to the latest version as soon as fixes become available. Most modern browsers update automatically, but users can manually check for updates in their browser settings.

The incident highlights ongoing challenges in coordinating security across the interconnected software ecosystem. When a single codebase like Chromium powers multiple products from different companies, a vulnerability disclosure requires synchronized response from numerous organizations. Google's error compressed that timeline unexpectedly.

This is not the first time premature disclosure has created security risks. The practice of responsible disclosure developed specifically to prevent these situations, where the window between public knowledge and available fixes creates maximum danger for users.

Google has not commented on how the premature disclosure occurred or what changes it will implement to prevent similar incidents. The company's security team typically maintains strict protocols around vulnerability disclosure, making this breach of procedure particularly notable.

Today, 6:00 AM ET

A breakthrough in classical computing has cast doubt on recent quantum supremacy demonstrations, after researchers used a standard laptop with new mathematical techniques to solve problems previously thought to require quantum computers.

The development centers on tensor network methods, a mathematical framework that allows classical computers to simulate quantum systems more efficiently than before. Using this approach, researchers successfully tackled computational problems that quantum computers had claimed as exclusive territory, completing the calculations in reasonable timeframes on conventional hardware.

Quantum supremacy, a term describing when quantum computers solve problems impossible for classical machines, has been a major milestone claim by quantum computing companies. Google declared quantum supremacy in 2019 when its Sycamore processor completed a calculation in 200 seconds that would allegedly take classical computers 10,000 years. Similar claims followed from other quantum computing efforts.

The new classical computing method does not invalidate quantum computing research entirely, but it does reframe the competitive landscape. The tensor network approach exploits specific mathematical structures in quantum problems that allow classical computers to shortcut through calculations more efficiently. This means the gap between quantum and classical computing may be narrower than previously believed for certain problem types.

Researchers emphasize this represents progress for both fields. Classical computing benefits from mathematical innovations inspired by quantum research, while quantum computing researchers gain clearer understanding of where quantum advantages truly exist. The findings push quantum computing developers to identify problems where quantum machines offer genuine, insurmountable advantages over classical systems.

The implications extend beyond academic competition. Billions of dollars in investment have flowed into quantum computing based partly on supremacy claims. Companies and governments betting on quantum technology now face questions about timelines and applications. The research suggests practical quantum advantages may arrive later than anticipated, or in different problem domains than currently targeted.

Experts note this pattern has occurred before in computing history. When one approach claims a breakthrough, it often spurs innovation in competing methods. The laptop achievement demonstrates that classical computing has not exhausted its potential for improvement, particularly when researchers apply insights from quantum mechanics to traditional algorithms.

The quantum computing field continues advancing on multiple fronts, including error correction and qubit stability. However, this classical computing breakthrough serves as a reminder that extraordinary claims require continued scrutiny, and that the path to practical quantum advantage remains longer and more complex than early demonstrations suggested.
