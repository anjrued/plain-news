A few minutes ago

Donald Trump announced Saturday that a peace deal with Iran has been "largely negotiated" following discussions with Pakistani mediators, Gulf allies, and Israel. The agreement could potentially end the war launched by the United States and Israel against Iran in February.

Writing on his social media platform, Trump said "final aspects and details" of a "memorandum of understanding" were still being discussed and would be announced shortly. He indicated the Strait of Hormuz would reopen as part of the deal.

"An agreement has been largely negotiated, subject to finalization between the United States of America, the Islamic Republic of Iran, and the various other Countries," Trump posted.

However, Iran's Fars news agency, which maintains close ties to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, contradicted Trump's claims. The outlet reported the strait would remain under Iranian control, stating that "the management of the Strait, determining the route, time, method of passage, and issuing permits will continue to be the monopoly and discretion of the Islamic Republic of Iran." The agency said Trump's assertion that an agreement was nearly final was "inconsistent with reality."

Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif congratulated Trump on his peace efforts and expressed hope to host another round of talks between the US and Iran "very soon." Sharif described Trump's call with leaders of Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Turkey, Egypt, the UAE, Jordan, and Pakistan as "very useful and productive."

According to sources cited by multiple news outlets, the potential deal would include an official declaration ending the war, with two-month negotiations on Iran's nuclear program, the reopening of the crucial shipping lane, and an end to the US blockade of Iranian ports. Three senior Iranian officials told the New York Times the agreement could release $25 billion in frozen Iranian assets, with a nuclear agreement to be negotiated within 30 to 60 days.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio hinted during a visit to India that "news" might arrive "later today," even as Trump continued threatening to strike Iran if negotiations failed.

The potential deal triggered criticism from Republican hawks. Mike Pompeo, who served as CIA director and secretary of state during Trump's first term, denounced the rumored terms as too similar to the 2015 nuclear deal negotiated under President Barack Obama. "The deal being floated with Iran seems straight out of the Wendy Sherman-Robert Malley-Ben Rhodes playbook: Pay the IRGC to build a WMD program and terrorize the world," Pompeo wrote on social media.

Trump said he met with American negotiators, including special envoy Steve Witkoff and adviser Jared Kushner, along with Vice President JD Vance, to discuss the latest proposals. He also spoke separately with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, describing that conversation as going "very well."

A gunman was shot dead after opening fire at a Secret Service checkpoint near the White House on Saturday, federal officials confirmed. The suspect approached the checkpoint and began shooting at posted officers, who returned fire and fatally struck him. A bystander was also wounded during the exchange, though it remains unclear whether they were hit by the suspect's gunfire or shots from law enforcement.

The White House was briefly locked down as multiple gunshots rang out, with journalists in the area running for cover. President Donald Trump was present in the building during the incident.

The Secret Service said the suspect opened fire on agents stationed at the checkpoint. Officers responded by drawing their weapons and shooting back, ultimately killing the gunman. The sustained volley of gunfire sent people nearby scrambling for safety.

Federal officials have not yet released the identity of the suspect or provided details about a possible motive for the attack. The investigation into the shooting remains ongoing.

The wounded bystander's condition has not been disclosed. Authorities are working to determine whether the civilian was struck by bullets fired by the suspect or by Secret Service agents during the confrontation.

Security checkpoints around the White House are staffed by Secret Service personnel who screen visitors and monitor the perimeter of the executive mansion. The checkpoints serve as a critical layer of protection for the president and White House grounds.

Saturday's incident marks a rare instance of gunfire at a White House checkpoint. While security threats near the White House occur periodically, shootings involving exchanges of fire between suspects and Secret Service agents are uncommon.

The Secret Service is responsible for protecting the president, vice president, and other high-ranking officials, as well as securing the White House complex. Agents stationed at checkpoints are trained to respond rapidly to potential threats.

Following the lockdown, normal operations at the White House resumed. The Secret Service and other law enforcement agencies continue to investigate the circumstances surrounding the shooting, including how the suspect approached the checkpoint armed and what may have motivated the attack.

Investigators will likely review surveillance footage and interview witnesses to reconstruct the sequence of events. The Secret Service typically conducts thorough reviews of security incidents to identify any gaps in protection and implement improvements.

The identity of the deceased suspect is expected to be released once next of kin have been notified. Authorities will also work to determine whether the individual had any history of threatening behavior or previous encounters with law enforcement.

Two-time NASCAR Cup Series champion Kyle Busch has died at age 41 after severe pneumonia progressed into sepsis, his family announced. The racing legend passed away on Thursday following a rapid decline from what began as a sinus infection.

NASCAR released a statement expressing deep sorrow over the loss. "We are saddened and heartbroken to share the news of the passing of Kyle Busch, a two-time Cup champion and one of our sport's greatest and fiercest drivers," the organization wrote on social media. "We extend our deepest condolences to the Busch family, Richard Childress Racing and the entire motorsports community."

According to a statement from Dakota Hunter, vice president of Kyle Busch Companies, the family received a medical evaluation on Saturday confirming that severe pneumonia had progressed into sepsis, resulting in rapid and overwhelming associated complications.

The illness had been developing for days before Busch's hospitalization. During a NASCAR Cup Series race on May 10 at Watkins Glen in upstate New York, Busch radioed his crew requesting medical assistance from Dr. Bill Heisel and a shot after the race. The TV broadcast reported that Busch had been struggling with a sinus cold that was worsened by the intense G-forces and elevation changes at the road course. Despite his condition, Busch continued racing and finished eighth.

Earlier on Thursday, Busch's family had announced his hospitalization, stating he would not compete as planned at the weekend's NASCAR race in Charlotte, North Carolina. Just six days before his death, Busch had won the trucks race at Dover on Friday.

The Las Vegas native won NASCAR championships in 2015 and 2019. He ranked 24th in the Cup Series standings this season, with two top-10 finishes in 12 races. Busch was in his fourth season at Richard Childress Racing after winning his titles with Joe Gibbs Racing.

A joint statement from the Busch family, Richard Childress Racing and NASCAR praised the driver's legacy. "A future Hall of Famer, Kyle was a rare talent, one who comes along once in a generation. He was fierce, he was passionate, he was immensely skilled and he cared deeply about the sport and fans."

Known by nicknames "Rowdy" and "Wild Thing," Busch was a polarizing figure famous for post-race fights, regular feuds with other drivers and sometimes outlandish behavior. The multi-talented driver burst onto the Cup Series scene in 2005 by winning rookie of the year. He won 232 races across NASCAR's three national series, more than any driver in history.

Busch is survived by his wife, Samantha, and children Brexton and Lennix.

Yesterday, 8:13 PM ET

Romanian director Cristian Mungiu has won the Palme d'Or at the Cannes Film Festival for his English-language debut "Fjord," marking his second top prize at the prestigious festival. The 58-year-old filmmaker previously won the award 19 years ago for his abortion drama "4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days."

The victory makes Mungiu the 10th director to receive two Palmes d'Or, joining an elite group that includes Francis Ford Coppola, Michael Haneke, Ken Loach, and Ruben Östlund, among others.

"Fjord" stars Renate Reinsve and Sebastian Stan as Romanian religious parents who relocate to Norway, where they face accusations of child abuse. The film has already been acquired by US distributor Neon, marking the seventh consecutive year the company has taken home the festival's top prize. Neon previously distributed "Anora," which won the Palme two years ago and went on to sweep the 2025 Oscars.

The 79th Cannes Film Festival featured a competitive lineup, though observers noted a somewhat muted atmosphere marked by disappointing returns from some established filmmakers and a notable absence of Hollywood glamour. Two American films in competition, James Gray's "Paper Tiger" starring Adam Driver and Scarlett Johansson, and Ira Sachs' AIDS musical "The Man I Love" starring Rami Malek, were both overlooked by the judges.

The runner-up Grand Prix went to Andrey Zvyagintsev's "Minotaur," described as a dark satire of corruption and infidelity in contemporary Russia. The third-place Jury Prize was awarded to Valeska Grisebach's Bulgaria-set drama "The Dreamed Adventure," about an archaeologist.

The director award was shared between Pawel Pawlikowski for "Fatherland" and Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi for "The Black Ball." Both acting awards were split between pairs of performers. Virginie Efira and Tao Okamoto shared the best actress prize for Ryusuke Hamaguchi's care drama "All of a Sudden," while Valentin Campagne and Emmanuel Macchia jointly won best actor for the queer World War I drama "Coward."

Park Chan-wook presided over this year's jury, which included Demi Moore, Stellan Skarsgård, Chloé Zhao, and Paul Laverty. The festival also honored three recipients with honorary Palmes d'Or: Barbra Streisand, Peter Jackson, and John Travolta. Streisand was unable to attend due to a knee injury but sent a video message. "In this crazy, volatile world that seems more fractured every day, it's reassuring to see the compelling movies at this festival, by artists from many countries," Streisand said. "Film has that magical ability to unite us, opening our hearts and minds."

Yesterday, 7:42 PM ET

A gunman was shot and killed after opening fire at a White House security checkpoint on Saturday evening, according to federal officials. The incident prompted a brief lockdown of the White House while President Donald Trump was present.

The shooting occurred shortly after 6 p.m. local time when a man approached a checkpoint at a White House gate near 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue NW. The suspect pulled out a pistol and began firing at Secret Service officers stationed at the checkpoint.

Officers returned fire, striking the suspect, who was then transported to a local hospital where he later died. A bystander was also wounded during the incident, though officials said it remains unclear whether the person was hit by the suspect's initial gunfire or during the subsequent exchange between the gunman and Secret Service personnel.

No Secret Service officers were injured in the shooting, and President Trump was not affected by the incident, officials confirmed. Trump said earlier that he was in the Oval Office but did not immediately comment on the gunfire.

Journalists in the area were sent running for cover as a sustained volley of gunshots rang out. Selina Wang, a White House correspondent for ABC News, was recording a report when the shooting began. Video she shared on social media showed her diving to take cover as what sounded like dozens of gunshots echoed nearby.

"We were told to sprint to the press briefing room, where we are holding now," Wang said. Chris Flanagan, an anchor for local news channel DC News Now who also took cover in the White House briefing room, reported that about 30 shots were fired. A CBS News producer estimated at least 20 shots had been fired.

The Secret Service issued a statement around 6:45 p.m. saying it was aware of reports of shots fired near 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue NW and was working to verify the information with personnel on the ground. The security lockdown was later lifted.

While the suspect has not been officially named, multiple U.S. media outlets reported that he had a history of mental health issues. According to these reports, he had been arrested last year for attempting to enter through a different White House checkpoint without authorization and had been issued a stay away order.

FBI Director Kash Patel posted that the FBI was on scene supporting the Secret Service response to the shooting and would provide updates to the public as information became available.

This incident follows another shooting less than three weeks earlier in which a man brandishing a gun fired at a Secret Service officer near the route of Vice President JD Vance's motorcade outside the White House.

Yesterday, 6:29 PM ET

The World Health Organization has declared the Ebola outbreak affecting the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Uganda a "public health emergency of international concern" as the disease continues to spread across the region.

The WHO made the declaration on Sunday after the outbreak of the Bundibugyo virus strain had been linked to 88 deaths and more than 300 suspected cases. WHO Director General Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus announced the decision before convening a formal emergency committee, with experts saying the speed likely reflects the gravity of the situation.

The Bundibugyo virus is one of three strains that can cause Ebola virus disease and is the least common. There are currently no approved vaccines, treatments, or specific tests for this strain, making the outbreak particularly concerning for health officials.

Dr Jean Kaseya, director general of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention, expressed urgency about the situation. "Currently, I'm on panic mode because people are dying. I don't have medicines. I don't have [a] vaccine to support countries," he told Sky News.

The Africa CDC announced the outbreak publicly on Friday but said it remains unclear when it began. The organization is convening experts and is likely to announce a continental public health emergency in the coming days.

Most cases have been reported in the DRC's Ituri province in the east of the country, which borders Uganda and South Sudan. There have been 80 suspected deaths, eight laboratory-confirmed cases, and 246 suspected cases in this region. Two cases, including one death, have been reported in Uganda's capital, Kampala, apparently in people who had traveled from the DRC.

The WHO warned there are "significant uncertainties as to the true number of infected persons and geographic spread associated with this event at the present time." The organization said signs "all point towards a potentially much larger outbreak than what is currently being detected and reported, with significant local and regional risk of spread."

Conflict continues in the Ituri region, with many people moving around and living in close quarters in urban and suburban areas, factors that can make disease spread more likely. On Sunday, a laboratory confirmed another Ebola case in the city of Goma, in a separate part of the DRC also affected by conflict.

Kaseya emphasized the need for vaccine and medicine manufacturing capacity on the continent, calling it an "equity issue." He warned that "Western countries, they don't understand that when Africa is affected, they are also at risk because people are flying every day."

Officials said they are in talks with companies who have potential tests, vaccines, and treatments at early stages of development to see whether any could be safely used during the outbreak, expressing hope that some might become available in the coming weeks.

WHO Director General Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus announced the decision before convening a formal emergency committee, with experts saying the speed likely reflects the gravity of the situation.

The Bundibugyo virus is one of three strains that can cause Ebola virus disease and is the least common. There are currently no approved vaccines, treatments, or specific tests for this strain, making the outbreak particularly concerning for health officials.

Dr Jean Kaseya, director general of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention, expressed urgency about the situation. "Currently, I'm on panic mode because people are dying. I don't have medicines. I don't have [a] vaccine to support countries," he told Sky News.

The Africa CDC announced the outbreak publicly on Friday but said it remains unclear when it began. The organization is convening experts and is likely to announce a continental public health emergency in the coming days.

Most cases have been reported in the DRC's Ituri province in the east of the country, which borders Uganda and South Sudan. There have been 80 suspected deaths, eight laboratory-confirmed cases, and 246 suspected cases in this region. Two cases, including one death, have been reported in Uganda's capital, Kampala, apparently in people who had traveled from the DRC.

The WHO warned there are "significant uncertainties as to the true number of infected persons and geographic spread associated with this event at the present time." The organization said signs "all point towards a potentially much larger outbreak than what is currently being detected and reported, with significant local and regional risk of spread."

Conflict continues in the Ituri region, with many people moving around and living in close quarters in urban and suburban areas, factors that can make disease spread more likely. On Sunday, a laboratory confirmed another Ebola case in the city of Goma, in a separate part of the DRC also affected by conflict.

Kaseya emphasized the need for vaccine and medicine manufacturing capacity on the continent, calling it an "equity issue." He warned that "Western countries, they don't understand that when Africa is affected, they are also at risk because people are flying every day."

Officials said they are in talks with companies who have potential tests, vaccines, and treatments at early stages of development to see whether any could be safely used during the outbreak, expressing hope that some might become available in the coming weeks.
