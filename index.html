Today, 12:00 AM ET

An Ebola outbreak in Central Africa has rapidly escalated beyond containment efforts, with health officials reporting cases across multiple countries. The World Health Organization has declared the situation a public health emergency as infection rates climb faster than medical teams can respond.

The outbreak appears to have originated in a remote region near the border of Democratic Republic of Congo and Uganda. Scientists are investigating whether the virus jumped from animal populations to humans through bushmeat handling or contact with infected bats. Early cases went undetected for weeks, allowing the disease to spread through communities before isolation protocols could be established.

Medical teams face significant challenges in containing the outbreak. Many affected areas lack basic health infrastructure, and some communities remain skeptical of outside medical intervention following previous epidemics. Traditional burial practices that involve contact with deceased relatives have accelerated transmission despite education campaigns.

Ebola causes severe hemorrhagic fever with fatality rates between 25 and 90 percent depending on the strain and access to treatment. Symptoms include sudden fever, intense weakness, muscle pain, and headache, followed by vomiting, diarrhea, and in severe cases, internal and external bleeding. The virus spreads through direct contact with bodily fluids of infected people or contaminated surfaces.

International aid organizations have deployed emergency response teams to establish treatment centers and implement contact tracing. Vaccine supplies are being rushed to the region, though logistical hurdles complicate distribution in remote areas. Healthcare workers, who face the highest infection risk, are being prioritized for vaccination.

The outbreak has already disrupted trade and travel across the region. Several countries have closed border crossings and implemented screening protocols at airports and major transit points. Economic activity in affected areas has ground to a halt as people avoid public gatherings and markets.

Previous Ebola outbreaks have demonstrated both the deadly potential of the virus and the possibility of containment with rapid response. The 2014-2016 West Africa outbreak killed more than 11,000 people before being brought under control through massive international intervention. Health officials say the current situation requires similar urgency and resources to prevent comparable devastation.

The standoff highlights a rare split between Trump and GOP senators over immigration policy. Republicans support increased ICE funding but oppose directing substantial resources toward compensating families affected by the controversial 2017-2018 separation policy. The blocked legislation would have provided emergency operational funding for the agency, which Trump administration officials say is critically needed.

The $1.8 billion payout fund represents one of the largest proposed settlements related to the family separation policy, which resulted in thousands of children being removed from their parents at the southern border. However, conservative members of the Senate view the compensation as rewarding those who crossed the border illegally. They argue that any funding bill should focus exclusively on enforcement capabilities rather than payments to affected families. The disagreement reveals tension between Trump's pragmatic approach to clearing legal obstacles and his party's ideological resistance to what they perceive as amnesty-related spending.

The blocked vote leaves both ICE funding and the compensation question unresolved. Senate leadership has not announced when they will attempt another vote or whether they will consider separating the payout provisions from the operational funding. The stalemate could affect ICE's ability to expand detention capacity and enforcement operations, priorities Trump has repeatedly emphasized since returning to office.

Sea Level Rise Accelerating Since 1960, New Analysis Confirms

The findings confirm earlier projections and provide clearer data on a process that threatens coastal communities worldwide. Researchers have improved methods for measuring historical sea level changes, account...

Today, 12:00 AM ET

Sea level rise has been accelerating continuously since 1960, according to new research that refines scientists' understanding of how quickly Earth's oceans are rising. The findings confirm earlier projections and provide clearer data on a process that threatens coastal communities worldwide.

Researchers have improved methods for measuring historical sea level changes, accounting for factors that previously introduced uncertainty into the data. The study shows that the rate of rise is not constant but has been increasing steadily for more than six decades.

The acceleration corresponds with rising global temperatures and the accelerating loss of ice from Greenland and Antarctica. As ocean water warms, it expands, taking up more space. Meanwhile, melting ice sheets add additional water to the oceans. Both processes contribute to the measured rise.

The research matters because it helps scientists predict future sea level changes with greater accuracy. Coastal cities and island nations use these projections to plan infrastructure, flood defenses, and in some cases, relocations. Better data means better decisions about how to protect vulnerable populations.

Current estimates suggest sea levels are rising by roughly 3.4 millimeters per year on average, but the rate varies by location due to ocean currents, land subsidence, and other regional factors. Some coastal areas experience faster rises than the global average.

The improved methodology used in this study could help resolve ongoing debates about exactly how much seas have risen over the past century and how much they might rise by 2100. Previous estimates for end-of-century sea level rise have ranged from half a meter to over two meters, depending on greenhouse gas emission scenarios and how quickly ice sheets respond to warming.

The study adds to mounting evidence that climate change is already producing measurable, accelerating impacts. Unlike some climate effects that remain uncertain or distant, sea level rise is directly observable and affects millions of people living in coastal zones.

Scientists emphasize that the acceleration detected since 1960 will likely continue unless global temperatures stabilize. Even if warming stopped today, thermal expansion of ocean water already warmed would continue pushing sea levels higher for decades.

Kyle Busch, one of NASCAR's most accomplished drivers, has died unexpectedly, the motorsports community confirmed. The 39-year-old two-time Cup Series champion was a fixture in stock car racing for over two decades.

Busch won 63 Cup Series races during his career, placing him ninth on the all-time wins list. He captured championships in 2015 and 2019 while driving for Joe Gibbs Racing. He moved to Richard Childress Racing in 2023, where he continued competing at the sport's highest level.

Known for his aggressive driving style and competitive intensity, Busch was equally successful in NASCAR's lower divisions. He holds the all-time record for wins in the Xfinity Series with 102 victories and won 66 races in the Truck Series. His 231 combined wins across NASCAR's three national series remain unmatched.

Busch began his Cup Series career in 2004 and quickly established himself as one of the sport's elite drivers. He won at least one race in 19 consecutive seasons from 2005 to 2023, demonstrating remarkable consistency throughout his career. His younger brother Kurt Busch, also a Cup Series champion, retired from racing in 2023.

The circumstances surrounding Busch's death have not been disclosed. NASCAR, team owners, and fellow drivers expressed shock and grief at the news. Richard Childress Racing and representatives for Busch's family have not yet released official statements.

Busch is survived by his wife Samantha and their two children. The Las Vegas native was also known for his charitable work through the Kyle Busch Foundation, which supported pediatric patients and children with chronic illnesses.

NASCAR has not announced whether this weekend's scheduled race events will be affected. The Cup Series was preparing for its next event following the completion of the regular season's opening races. Busch had been competing in his second full season with Richard Childress Racing.

The racing world has lost one of its most decorated and polarizing figures. Whether admired or criticized, Busch's impact on NASCAR was undeniable, and his absence will be felt throughout the sport.
