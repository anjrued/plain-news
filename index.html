3:53 PM ET

Authorities in Orange County, California have ordered the evacuation of 40,000 people over concerns about a chemical leak that threatens to spill or explode. The problem arose on Thursday at a facility owned by GKN Aerospace in the town of Garden Grove, where a storage tank holding methyl methacrylate began off-gassing and threatened to fail.

The chemical, which is highly flammable, is used to fabricate resins and plastics. Local authorities originally responded to the incident with a hazmat team on Thursday, ordering local residents to evacuate. They lifted the order later that day, but the problem worsened due to "damage to a valve on the tank" that "created additional operational challenges, preventing complete mitigation," Garden Grove authorities wrote in an evacuation order.

By Friday, new evacuation orders had expanded to residents in six cities. "We have a tank that is actively in crisis," Orange County Fire Authority Division Chief Craig Covey told reporters at a news conference. "There are literally two options left remaining. One: the tank fails and spills a total of about 6-7,000 gallons of very bad chemicals into the parking lot in that area. Or two: the tank goes into a thermal runaway and blows up, affecting the tanks that are around them that have fuel or the chemicals in them as well."

"This thing is going to fail, and we don't know when," Covey said. "We're doing our best to figure out when or how we can prevent it."

Crews have created containment barriers with sandbags in case there is a chemical spill from the tank to prevent the toxic chemical from getting into storm drains or reaching creeks or the nearby ocean.

Dr. Regina Chinsio-Kwong, the county health officer, said if the chemical heats up, it can release a vapor that is harmful to people's health. It can cause respiratory issues, itching and burning eyes, nausea and headaches.

Garden Grove Police Chief Amir El-Farra said some 15% of those facing evacuation orders were refusing to leave, according to the Orange County Register. The city has established at least two shelters for evacuees.

Garden Grove is about 38 miles south of downtown Los Angeles and less than a mile from Disneyland's two theme parks, which were not under evacuation orders Friday. The evacuation orders cover residents of five other Orange County cities: Cypress, Stanton, Anaheim, Buena Park and Westminster.

Covey urged residents to take the order seriously. "We are setting up these evacuations in preparation for these two options: it fails or it blows up," Covey said. "Please follow our requests and orders for evacuations."

GKN Aerospace is a division of a British corporation that produces airplane engines and other aircraft parts.

Scientists have identified remnants of a galaxy that was consumed by the Milky Way billions of years ago. The discovery provides new insights into how our galaxy grew through the process of galactic cannibalism.

Researchers analyzed stellar populations and chemical compositions to identify stars that originated from the smaller galaxy. These stars, now scattered throughout the Milky Way, retain distinctive characteristics that mark their foreign origin.

The evidence suggests the merger occurred during an earlier phase of the Milky Way's development. When galaxies collide and merge, the larger galaxy typically absorbs the smaller one, incorporating its stars, gas, and dark matter. This process has shaped the structure and evolution of the Milky Way over billions of years.

The consumed galaxy would have been significantly smaller than the Milky Way at the time of the merger. As the two galaxies came together, gravitational forces pulled the smaller galaxy apart, distributing its stars throughout the larger structure.

Scientists use several methods to identify these ancient merger events. They examine the motion, age, and chemical composition of stars to determine which ones formed in the Milky Way and which came from elsewhere. Stars from the absorbed galaxy often move in different patterns than native Milky Way stars and may have different chemical signatures based on the conditions in which they formed.

The Milky Way has consumed multiple smaller galaxies throughout its history. Each merger event leaves behind traces that astronomers can detect and study. By identifying and characterizing these remnants, researchers piece together the history of how the Milky Way achieved its current size and structure.

This discovery adds to the catalog of known merger events in the Milky Way's past. Understanding these events helps scientists build models of galaxy formation and evolution throughout the universe. The findings also contribute to knowledge about the rate at which large galaxies like the Milky Way grow through mergers versus through the gradual accumulation of gas and the formation of new stars.

The research demonstrates how modern astronomical techniques allow scientists to reconstruct events that occurred billions of years ago, revealing the dynamic history of our home galaxy.
