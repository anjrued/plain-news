The U.S. strikes targeted military installations in Iran, prompting Tehran to accuse Washington of violating a ceasefire.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said negotiations to end the war were continuing despite the military action. President Trump offered what officials described as conflicting signals on peace talks, at one point saying he sought a good deal or no deal at all. The Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps issued threats of retaliation following the strikes on launch sites and boats.

Iran accused the United States of committing a grave violation of the ceasefire agreement. Trump's insistence on favorable terms or no agreement has complicated the diplomatic picture.

Diplomatic efforts continue despite the military actions. Rubio emphasized that negotiations had not stopped, though he did not provide details on the substance of the talks or their progress. The parallel tracks of military strikes and diplomatic engagement reflect the complex and volatile nature of the current standoff between the two nations.

NASA outlined plans to establish a permanent base on the Moon, revealing it will deploy hopping drones and roving vehicles as part of the effort. The space agency announced three uncrewed lunar missions set to launch this year to prepare for the base construction.

Jeff Bezos's Blue Origin was selected to conduct the first of the three uncrewed missions. The missions represent a key step in NASA's Artemis program, which aims to return humans to the lunar surface and establish a sustained presence there.

The initiative marks a shift from the Apollo era's brief surface visits to long-term habitation. NASA's strategy involves testing technology and gathering data through robotic missions before sending astronauts to live and work at the lunar base.

The hopping drones will allow exploration of terrain that wheeled rovers cannot easily navigate, while traditional rovers will survey larger areas and transport equipment. These vehicles will help identify optimal locations for base infrastructure and assess resources like water ice that could support human crews.

NASA views the Moon base as preparation for eventual missions to Mars. The lunar surface provides a testing ground for life support systems, habitat construction, and resource extraction technologies that would be critical for Mars exploration. Building a base 240,000 miles from Earth will help engineers identify and solve problems before attempting missions to Mars, which lies tens of millions of miles away.

The three uncrewed missions planned for this year will deliver scientific instruments, test landing systems, and demonstrate capabilities needed for base construction. Blue Origin's selection gives the company its first major NASA lunar contract, competing with SpaceX and other aerospace firms vying for roles in the Artemis program.

The announcement comes as NASA pushes to maintain American leadership in space exploration amid growing international competition. China has conducted multiple successful lunar missions in recent years and has stated its own intentions to establish a lunar research station.

The Mandalorian and Grogu achieved the lowest opening weekend for a Star Wars film in Disney's history, despite claiming the top spot at the box office. The film earned $102 million domestically during the four-day Memorial Day weekend, bringing its global total to $165 million.

The performance fell short of even Solo: A Star Wars Story, which opened on the same Memorial Day weekend in 2018 with $103 million domestically. When adjusted for inflation, Solo's opening was substantially stronger, earning the equivalent of $226.8 million in 2024 dollars compared to The Mandalorian and Grogu's $165 million global opening.

Pedro Pascal stars as the armored bounty hunter traveling the galaxy with Grogu, the character widely known as Baby Yoda. The film represents a theatrical expansion of the popular Disney Plus series that debuted in 2019.

Industry analyst David Gross told Agence France-Presse that the numbers reflect how spin-off films typically perform. "The movie is a piece of the story, and it's earning a piece of the business," he said. However, he noted the takings are "not on the level of the primary Star Wars films."

Despite the modest opening, Disney does not appear concerned. The studio previously paused theatrical Star Wars releases after 2019's The Rise of Skywalker, concluding that too many films had been released too quickly. Hollywood reports indicate Disney expects the film to hold steady in coming weekends due to positive audience reception, even as critics offered muted responses.

The studio is banking on the film's appeal to families rather than hardcore franchise fans. This represents a shift from traditional Star Wars theatrical releases, which historically drew core fanbase audiences. Parents bringing children to theaters expanded the demographic beyond the dedicated fan communities that typically powered opening weekends.

Disney's broader strategy extends beyond box office performance. The company is using The Mandalorian and Grogu to drive viewers toward merchandise, theme park experiences, and Disney Plus content. The Mandalorian remains the most-watched original series on Disney Plus, and over 13 million Grogu toys have sold since the show's 2019 launch.

While the film may prove financially successful relative to its $165 million budget, it signals possible Star Wars fatigue among audiences. It marks the first Star Wars theatrical release in seven years following Disney's decision to focus on streaming content.

Disney continues investing in Star Wars series for Disney Plus, though recent shows have produced mixed results. The Mandalorian and Andor achieved both critical and audience success, while The Book of Boba Fett, Obi-Wan Kenobi, and The Acolyte failed to gain significant traction.

Ryan Gosling is set to star in Star Wars: Starfighter, scheduled for May 2027 release.

A few minutes ago

The Trump administration has proposed creating a government-wide nondisclosure agreement that would apply to both new and existing federal employees. The proposal aims to prevent leaks of government information.

The nondisclosure agreement would represent a significant expansion of secrecy requirements across the federal workforce. Currently, only certain employees with access to classified information or sensitive positions are required to sign such agreements.

The administration has characterized the proposal as necessary to stop unauthorized disclosures of government information. Federal workers typically enjoy First Amendment protections when speaking on matters of public concern, though those protections can be limited by legitimate government interests.

The proposal comes as the administration has expressed concerns about leaks to the media. If implemented, the nondisclosure agreements could affect millions of federal employees across numerous agencies.

Legal experts have questioned whether blanket nondisclosure agreements for all federal employees would survive court challenges. Previous attempts to restrict federal employee speech have faced scrutiny over potential violations of whistleblower protections and constitutional rights.

The administration has not yet released details about what information would be covered by the agreements, how violations would be enforced, or what penalties employees who breach the agreements might face. The proposal would need to go through a rulemaking process before taking effect.

Federal employee unions have historically opposed broad restrictions on worker communications. The agreements could complicate oversight functions that rely on information from government employees.

The timing of the proposal is unclear, and it remains unknown when the administration plans to finalize or implement the policy. The move represents part of broader administration efforts to control information flows from within the federal government.
