An Ebola outbreak has rapidly escalated beyond containment measures, according to reports from health authorities and researchers. The outbreak's origins remain under investigation as case numbers climb and response teams struggle to track transmission chains.

The virus, which causes severe hemorrhagic fever with fatality rates that can exceed 50 percent, spreads through direct contact with bodily fluids of infected individuals. Health workers are among those at highest risk during outbreak conditions.

Researchers are working to identify the initial transmission event, which typically involves contact with infected wildlife before spreading person to person. Understanding the outbreak's origins is critical for preventing future spillover events and improving early detection systems.

The current outbreak has strained local health infrastructure and prompted international health agencies to deploy additional resources. Contact tracing efforts are underway to identify and monitor individuals who may have been exposed to confirmed cases.

Ebola outbreaks have historically been contained through rapid isolation of cases, careful burial practices, and community education about transmission risks. The disease can incubate for up to 21 days, complicating efforts to identify all potential cases before symptoms appear.

Previous major outbreaks have demonstrated both the devastating potential of the virus and the effectiveness of coordinated public health response. The 2014-2016 West Africa outbreak killed more than 11,000 people across multiple countries before being brought under control.

Vaccines developed in recent years have proven effective in protecting healthcare workers and contacts of confirmed cases. However, deployment logistics in remote areas and community acceptance of interventions remain significant challenges during active outbreaks.

Health authorities continue to assess the geographic spread and implement containment protocols while investigating how this outbreak began.

Today, 9:00 PM ET

Tennessee prison officials failed to execute Tony Carruthers on Thursday after medical staff could not establish intravenous access within the required time window. The state has announced it will not attempt another execution for at least a year.

The execution team worked for over an hour trying to insert IV lines into Carruthers, who was convicted in a 1981 triple murder. Under Tennessee protocol, execution teams have a limited window to establish IV access before the death warrant expires. When that deadline passed without successful venous access, officials halted the procedure.

This marks the second consecutive botched execution attempt in Tennessee. The state faced similar difficulties during an execution in February, raising questions about execution protocols and medical personnel training. Tennessee uses a three-drug lethal injection protocol that requires reliable intravenous access to administer the chemicals.

Carruthers, now 65, was sentenced to death for killing three people during a robbery. His attorneys had previously raised concerns about his medical conditions potentially complicating IV access, though those arguments were rejected by courts.

The failed execution adds Tennessee to a growing list of states experiencing technical difficulties with lethal injection procedures. Several states have struggled to find qualified medical personnel willing to participate in executions, and some condemned inmates have medical conditions that make venous access difficult.

Tennessee officials said they would review their execution procedures before attempting another one. The state maintains one of the more active death chambers in the country, having carried out seven executions since 2018 after a nearly decade-long pause.

The one-year delay means Tennessee will not schedule another execution until at least spring 2026. Death penalty experts note that such extended gaps between attempts often lead to renewed legal challenges and procedural reviews.

Carruthers will return to death row at Riverbend Maximum Security Institution. His legal team has not yet indicated whether they will file additional appeals based on the failed execution attempt, though such filings are common after botched procedures.

The incident highlights ongoing practical challenges facing states that maintain capital punishment. As medical professionals increasingly decline to participate in executions due to ethical concerns, states face mounting difficulties carrying out death sentences even when courts have cleared legal obstacles.

Today, 9:00 PM ET

The United States government will invest $2 billion in quantum computing technology through partnerships with IBM and other private companies. The announcement marks one of the largest federal commitments to quantum research as nations compete to develop computers capable of solving problems beyond the reach of conventional machines.

Quantum computers use the properties of quantum mechanics to process information in fundamentally different ways than traditional computers. While current quantum systems remain experimental, the technology promises breakthroughs in drug discovery, materials science, cryptography, and artificial intelligence. The investment aims to accelerate the timeline from research to practical applications.

IBM will receive a significant portion of the funding to expand its quantum computing infrastructure and research programs. The company currently operates one of the world's largest fleets of quantum computers available through cloud access. Other firms involved in the initiative include startups and established technology companies working on different quantum computing approaches.

The funding comes as China and other nations pour billions into quantum research. The Chinese government has invested an estimated $15 billion in quantum technology over the past decade. European Union members have also launched coordinated quantum initiatives. US officials frame the investment as necessary to maintain technological leadership in a field with both economic and national security implications.

The $2 billion will be distributed over several years through grants, contracts, and public-private partnerships. Recipients must meet milestones in developing quantum hardware, software, and trained workforce. The program includes funding for universities to establish quantum research centers and train the next generation of quantum scientists and engineers.

Quantum computing remains in early stages despite decades of research. Current quantum computers can only run for brief periods before errors accumulate. They require extreme cooling to near absolute zero temperatures. Most experts believe practical quantum computers capable of outperforming conventional computers on commercially useful tasks remain years away. This investment represents a bet that sustained funding will help overcome these technical barriers and establish American companies as leaders when the technology matures.

Google Exposes Unfixed Security Flaw in Chrome by Mistake

The vulnerability, tracked in Google's public issue tracker, was made visible before developers completed a security patch. This t...

Today, 9:00 PM ET

Google accidentally published technical details of an unpatched security vulnerability in Chromium, the open-source foundation of Chrome and other browsers. The premature disclosure leaves millions of users vulnerable until a fix becomes available.

The vulnerability, tracked in Google's public issue tracker, was made visible before developers completed a security patch. This type of disclosure gives attackers a roadmap to exploit the flaw before users can protect themselves. Security researchers consider premature exposure of unpatched vulnerabilities a serious lapse in responsible disclosure practices.

Chromium powers not only Chrome but also Microsoft Edge, Brave, Opera, and other browsers with a combined user base exceeding 3 billion people worldwide. When a Chromium vulnerability remains unpatched, the exposure extends across the entire browser ecosystem.

Google maintains a structured vulnerability disclosure process designed to give developers time to create and distribute fixes before making technical details public. The typical timeline allows 90 days for patching, or shorter periods for actively exploited flaws. The accidental exposure appears to have bypassed these internal controls.

The company has not disclosed the severity rating of the vulnerability or whether security researchers have detected active exploitation attempts. Google's transparency around security issues typically includes detailed technical breakdowns, but only after patches reach users through automatic browser updates.

Browser vulnerabilities represent high-value targets for attackers because they provide direct access to user data, passwords, and online activities. Even a moderate-severity flaw can enable credential theft, tracking, or access to sensitive information when exploited before patches deploy.

Chrome updates automatically in the background for most users, meaning a patch should reach the majority of users within days of release. However, enterprise deployments and users who disable automatic updates may remain vulnerable longer. Google has not provided a timeline for when the fix will ship.

The incident highlights tensions between transparency and security in open-source development. While public issue trackers enable collaborative development and accountability, they also create risks when access controls fail. Security teams must balance visibility with the need to protect users from active threats.

Google is expected to release an emergency security update addressing the exposed vulnerability. Users can manually check for updates by navigating to Chrome's settings menu and selecting "About Chrome," which triggers an immediate update check.

Sea Level Rise Has Accelerated Since 1960, New Analysis Confirms

Scientists analyzed sea level records spanning more than 30 years, com...

Today, 9:00 PM ET

Global sea levels have been rising at an accelerating rate since 1960, according to new research that refines decades of satellite and tide gauge measurements. The findings resolve previous uncertainties in the data and confirm that coastal flooding risks are growing faster than earlier estimates suggested.

Scientists analyzed sea level records spanning more than 30 years, combining measurements from tide gauges along coastlines with satellite observations. The work improves the accuracy of long-term trends by accounting for variations in measurement methods and natural ocean cycles that can obscure underlying patterns.

The acceleration means that sea levels are not just rising, they are rising faster each decade. While precise rates vary by location due to ocean currents and land movement, the global average shows a clear upward curve beginning in the mid-20th century.

The research has immediate implications for coastal planning and infrastructure. Cities and nations use sea level projections to design flood defenses, plan relocations, and assess insurance risks. More accurate historical data allows better forecasting of future conditions.

Tide gauge records, some dating back more than a century, show sea levels fluctuating naturally over years and decades. Separating these natural variations from the long-term human-caused trend requires careful statistical analysis. Satellite measurements, available since the early 1990s, provide global coverage but must be calibrated against the longer tide gauge record.

The acceleration correlates with increasing global temperatures and the melting of ice sheets in Greenland and Antarctica. Thermal expansion of warming ocean water also contributes to rising seas. Both processes have intensified as greenhouse gas concentrations have increased.

Coastal communities face compounding risks. Higher baseline sea levels mean that storm surges reach farther inland, and high tides increasingly flood streets and infrastructure even without storms. Low-lying island nations and delta regions with dense populations face particularly acute threats.

The improved measurements give policymakers a clearer picture of how quickly they need to act. Sea level rise is already affecting real estate values, insurance markets, and migration patterns in vulnerable areas. The research confirms that these pressures will continue mounting for decades regardless of near-term emissions reductions, due to the delayed response of oceans and ice sheets to past warming.

Kyle Busch, one of NASCAR's most successful drivers, has died at age 41 following hospitalization with a severe illness. The news was confirmed Thursday, sending shockwaves through the motorsports community.

Busch was a two-time NASCAR Cup Series champion, winning titles in 2015 and 2019. Over his career, he accumulated 63 Cup Series wins, ranking him ninth on the all-time wins list. He also won championships in both the NASCAR Xfinity Series and Truck Series, making him one of only a handful of drivers to win titles across all three major NASCAR divisions.

Known for his aggressive driving style and competitive nature, Busch was both celebrated and controversial throughout his career. He earned the nickname "Rowdy" early on and lived up to it on the track, drawing passionate reactions from fans and fellow drivers alike. Despite the polarizing nature of his personality, his talent was undeniable.

Busch currently drove the No. 8 Chevrolet for Richard Childress Racing, a team he joined in 2023 after spending 15 years with Joe Gibbs Racing. His move to RCR marked a significant shift in his career, as he took over the iconic number previously driven by his childhood hero, Dale Earnhardt.

The nature of Busch's illness has not been disclosed. Reports indicate he was hospitalized recently, but details about his condition were kept private. His death comes during the 2025 NASCAR season, leaving his team and the sport to grapple with the sudden loss.

Busch is survived by his wife Samantha and their two children. The NASCAR community has begun paying tribute to the driver, with fellow competitors and team owners expressing shock and sadness at the news.

NASCAR has not yet announced plans for how it will honor Busch's memory during upcoming races. The organization is expected to release an official statement and may implement tribute initiatives as the season continues. Busch's impact on stock car racing extends beyond statistics, as he helped elevate the sport's profile and inspired a generation of younger drivers with his fierce competitiveness and undeniable skill behind the wheel.
