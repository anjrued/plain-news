Turkish riot police have forcibly entered the headquarters of the country's main opposition party, the Republican People's Party (CHP), using tear gas and rubber bullets. The raid comes days after party officials vowed to defy a controversial court ruling that removed their elected leader.

The confrontation follows a Thursday decision by an appeals court in Ankara that annulled the CHP's 2023 leadership contest, deposing party leader Özgür Özel. The 51-year-old Özel has become the face of Turkey's opposition and is credited with rejuvenating the CHP. He is also one of the few remaining party figures who has avoided charges that could result in detention.

The court ordered Özel replaced by his predecessor, Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu, who lost a pivotal general election to President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan in 2023. Özel's leadership preceded the 2024 local elections, where the CHP swept Erdoğan's Justice and Development Party from power in municipalities and mayoralties across the country.

Earlier this week, another Ankara court ordered Özel to pay President Erdoğan 300,000 lira in damages for remarks including calling him an "oppressor." Özel had also called on Erdoğan to "leash your dogs" in criticism of a sweeping crackdown on opposition figures. Erdoğan responded by calling the opposition leader "delusional," saying Turkey must "protect the reputation of politics in the face of attacks."

The court case unseating Özel has been widely criticized as an effort to subdue the CHP and reinstall a leader more amenable to Erdoğan's rule. Kılıçdaroğlu, who has called for the "purification" of his own party, told the pro-government channel TGRT Haber that he hoped the ruling would prove "beneficial to Turkey and the CHP."

The ruling briefly disrupted Turkey's struggling economy, with trading suspended on the Istanbul stock market amid a 6% drop in share prices.

Since the 2024 elections, observers have denounced a fresh crackdown targeting Erdoğan's opponents, primarily opposition mayors and local officials from the CHP. More than 20 CHP mayors have been detained on corruption, bribery, and terrorism-related charges.

The arrest last year of Istanbul mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu, seen as a probable CHP presidential candidate, represented a watershed moment for the party. Thousands protested in the streets, but İmamoğlu has spent the intervening year in a maximum-security facility. He was among 400 defendants in a mass trial accused of corruption related to his time as mayor. Human Rights Watch called the trial part of a broad effort to weaponize the criminal justice system against the CHP.

CHP officials have indicated they remain keen to fight a presidential election expected next year.

10:57 AM ET

Authorities in Orange County, California, have ordered 40,000 people to evacuate their homes as a chemical storage tank threatens to explode or spill its toxic contents. The crisis began Thursday at a GKN Aerospace facility in Garden Grove, where a tank containing methyl methacrylate started off-gassing and showing signs of potential failure.

The chemical, which is highly flammable and used in manufacturing resins and plastics, initially prompted a hazmat team response and evacuation order on Thursday. Officials lifted that order later in the day, but the situation deteriorated due to damage to a valve on the tank that created additional operational challenges and prevented complete mitigation, according to Garden Grove authorities.

By Friday, new evacuation orders had expanded to residents in six cities across Orange County, including Garden Grove, Cypress, Stanton, Anaheim, Buena Park and Westminster. Garden Grove is located about 30 miles southeast of downtown Los Angeles and less than a mile from Disneyland's two theme parks, which were not under evacuation orders.

"We have a tank that is actively in crisis," Orange County Fire Authority Division Chief Craig Covey told reporters. "There are literally two options left remaining. One: the tank fails and spills a total of about 6,000 to 7,000 gallons of very bad chemicals into the parking lot in that area. Or two: the tank goes into a thermal runaway and blows up, affecting the tanks that are around them that have fuel or the chemicals in them as well."

Covey emphasized the uncertainty of the timeline, saying, "This thing is going to fail, and we don't know when. We're doing our best to figure out when or how we can prevent it."

Emergency crews have constructed containment barriers using sandbags to prevent any chemical spill from entering storm drains or reaching nearby creeks and the ocean. Dr. Regina Chinsio-Kwong, the county health officer, warned that if the chemical heats up, it can release harmful vapors that cause respiratory issues, burning and itching eyes, nausea and headaches.

Garden Grove Police Chief Amir El-Farra reported that approximately 15 percent of people under evacuation orders were refusing to leave. The city has established at least two shelters for evacuees.

California Governor Gavin Newsom declared a state of emergency Saturday, directing the governor's office of emergency services and other state agencies to provide additional support to Orange County responders. The proclamation also makes state-owned properties available to shelter evacuees.

Covey urged residents to take the evacuation orders seriously. "We are setting up these evacuations in preparation for these two options: it fails or it blows up. Please follow our requests and orders for evacuations."

GKN Aerospace is a division of a British corporation that produces airplane engines and other aircraft parts.

US Treasury prices have fallen sharply, driving up yields and increasing the government's borrowing costs. The decline in Treasury markets is testing the tolerance of policymakers in Washington as higher yields translate directly into more expensive federal debt financing.

The Treasury market rout comes at a time when the federal government faces significant borrowing needs. Rising yields mean taxpayers will pay more in interest on government debt, a development that could complicate fiscal planning and budget negotiations in Congress.

Treasury yields move inversely to prices, so the recent price decline has pushed yields higher across the curve. These higher yields affect not just government borrowing but also mortgage rates, corporate debt, and other forms of financing tied to Treasury benchmarks.

The market movement reflects investor concerns about the trajectory of federal spending and the sustainability of current debt levels. With the national debt already at historic highs relative to the size of the economy, any increase in borrowing costs has significant fiscal implications.

Washington faces a decision about how to respond to these market signals. Higher borrowing costs could pressure lawmakers to address deficit spending, or they could continue current fiscal policies and accept the increased expense of servicing the debt.

The Federal Reserve's monetary policy also plays a role in Treasury market dynamics. Interest rate decisions by the central bank influence Treasury yields, creating a complex interaction between fiscal and monetary policy.

Economists are watching closely to see whether the yield increase represents a temporary market adjustment or a more sustained shift in investor sentiment about US government debt. The answer will determine whether Washington needs to make significant changes to fiscal policy or whether current borrowing levels remain sustainable despite higher costs.

The Treasury market serves as a barometer of investor confidence in US fiscal policy. The current price decline suggests markets are demanding higher returns to hold government debt, a shift that carries implications for the entire financial system given the central role of Treasuries as a benchmark for other investments.
