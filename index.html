An explosion struck a train in Pakistan's southwestern Balochistan province, killing at least 24 people. The train had been transporting military personnel home for Eid when armed separatists attacked it, according to officials.

The blast is the latest in a series of coordinated assaults across Balochistan. Security forces have intensified operations against separatist militants following a large-scale assault that left at least 31 civilians and 17 security personnel dead. Balochistan's chief minister, Sarfraz Bugti, reported that 145 people described as militants were killed within 40 hours of the initial attacks, with their bodies in government custody.

The initial wave of violence began before dawn on Saturday, when scores of insurgents attacked military installations, police stations, and banks across more than 10 cities. The coordinated nature of these simultaneous assaults was described by analysts as unprecedented in the region's recent history. Insurgents also blocked main roads, destroyed railway tracks, and carried out suicide attacks in the provincial capital, Quetta, and the port city of Gwadar.

The Balochistan Liberation Army, a proscribed separatist group, claimed responsibility for the attacks. In a statement, the BLA claimed to have killed 84 security personnel and taken 18 security and civilian officials prisoner, though these claims could not be independently verified. The group said seven of its fighters were killed.

Balochistan, Pakistan's largest province by area, has experienced decades of separatist insurgency. The violence has intensified in recent years, with attacks targeting both security forces and civilians. The province borders Afghanistan and Iran and has long faced instability from multiple militant organizations operating in remote areas.

Videos shared on social media documented the scale of the attacks, showing insurgents moving freely through city streets, firing rocket launchers, and torching police stations. In Mastung, dozens of insurgents attacked a prison and freed nearly 30 inmates. In Gwadar, where China has developed a deep-sea port, militants attempted to breach perimeter security but were stopped by authorities.

Mobile internet services were suspended in several cities including Quetta, Gwadar, and Noshki as authorities responded to the violence. Pakistan's defence minister, Khawaja Asif, stated that calm had been restored and security forces were conducting operations to eliminate remaining threats.

Security analyst Zahid Hussain noted that the attacks revealed the insurgency remained potent despite recent claims by government officials that it had been contained. He highlighted the lack of political engagement in the province and said the situation had escalated to a dangerous level, pointing to the militants' apparent local support base as essential to launching such coordinated operations.

11:05 AM ET

Romanian filmmaker Cristian Mungiu's English-language debut "Fjord" claimed the top prize at the 79th Cannes Film Festival, marking a significant achievement for the director nearly two decades after his previous Palme d'Or win.

The film stars Renate Reinsve and Sebastian Stan as Romanian religious parents who relocate to Norway and become accused of child abuse. The drama explores cultural tensions in its exploration of a family facing scrutiny in their new home.

Mungiu's victory makes him the 10th director to win the Palme d'Or twice. His earlier win came 19 years ago for "4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days," an abortion drama. He joins an elite group including Francis Ford Coppola, Michael Haneke, Ken Loach, and Ruben Östlund.

The festival's other major prizes went to different filmmakers and genres. Andrey Zvyagintsev's "Minotaur," a dark satire about corruption and infidelity in contemporary Russia, won the Grand Prix. Valeska Grisebach's Bulgarian drama "The Dreamed Adventure," which centers on an archaeologist, received the Jury Prize.

The directing award was split between Pawel Pawlikowski for "Fatherland" and Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi for "The Black Ball." Acting honors were distributed among four performers. Virginie Efira and Tao Okamoto jointly won best actress for Ryusuke Hamaguchi's care drama "All of a Sudden," while Valentin Campagne and Emmanuel Macchia shared the best actor prize for the World War I queer drama "Coward."

The 79th edition proved notable for its relatively muted atmosphere, characterized by disappointing returns from several established directors and a notable absence of major Hollywood productions. Two American films in competition, James Gray's "Paper Tiger" and Ira Sachs' musical "The Man I Love," received no recognition from the judges.

"Fjord" has already been acquired by US distributor Neon, marking the seventh consecutive year the company has taken home the festival's top prize. This continues a winning streak that began with Sean Baker's "Anora," which progressed from winning the Palme two years ago to sweeping the 2025 Oscars.

Park Chan-wook presided over this year's jury, which included actress Demi Moore, actor Stellan Skarsgård, director Chloé Zhao, and screenwriter Paul Laverty.

Barbra Streisand received an honorary Palme d'Or at the festival but could not attend due to a knee injury. She sent a video message to audiences at the closing ceremony, speaking about cinema's capacity to unite people. Peter Jackson and John Travolta also received honorary Palmes d'Or this year.

Colorado Democratic Party censures Governor Polis over Tina Peters clemency

The Colorado Democratic Party censured Governor Jared Polis on Thursday over his decision to commute the prison sentence of Tina Peters, the former Mesa County clerk who allowed unauthorized access to county election equipment. Polis reduced Peters's sentence from eight and a half years to approximately four and a half years, allowing her release on parole beginning June 1. In...

May 21, 10:12 AM ET

The Colorado Democratic Party censured Governor Jared Polis on Thursday over his decision to commute the prison sentence of Tina Peters, the former Mesa County clerk who allowed unauthorized access to county election equipment.

Polis reduced Peters's sentence from eight and a half years to approximately four and a half years, allowing her release on parole beginning June 1. In his clemency letter, Polis described the original sentence as "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes."

Peters was convicted in 2024 of four felonies and three misdemeanors. In 2021, she permitted Conan Hayes, a former pro surfer affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, to access Mesa County's voting equipment, copy it, and attend a sensitive software upgrade. Sensitive passwords and other information from the county's voting systems were later published online by right-wing figures.

The decision prompted immediate backlash from Democratic officials. Jena Griswold, Colorado's secretary of state, said the clemency "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president." She warned that the action signals to election deniers nationwide that "it's open season on our elections and election officials."

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, called the decision "furious, disgusting, and deeply disappointing," adding that election officials across the country view it as a dangerous precedent.

Polis previously signaled his leanings on March 3 when he compared Peters's case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for submitting forged letters to the legislature. Both faced overlapping charges of attempting to influence public officials. Polis stated that "justice in Colorado and America needs to be applied evenly."

Griswold disputed this comparison, noting that while the charges overlapped on one count, "Peters organized the breach of the election equipment, broke the public trust and attacked the very foundations of our democratic process." She said Peters's actions continue to be used to undermine the 2026 election.

The original judge who sentenced Peters in 2024 rejected sympathy for her case. Judge Matthew Barrett stated at sentencing: "You are no hero. You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again."

An appeals court overturned the sentence in April and ordered reconsideration. Former President Trump, who has repeatedly pushed for Peters's release and issued a federal pardon to her, has characterized her case as persecution for attempting to expose supposed election fraud.

The censure carries symbolic weight but does not affect Polis's governing authority. It reflects deepening tensions within Colorado's Democratic Party over how to address election security violations.

The festival's other major prizes went to different filmmakers and genres. Andrey Zvyagintsev's "Minotaur," a dark satire about corruption and infidelity in contemporary Russia, won the Grand Prix. Virginie Efira and Tao Okamoto jointly won best actress for Ryusuke Hamaguchi's care drama "All of a Sudden," while Valentin Campagne and Emmanuel Macchia shared the best actor prize for the World War I queer drama "Coward."

The 79th edition proved notable for its relatively muted atmosphere, characterized by disappointing returns from several established directors and a notable absence of major Hollywood productions. Two American films in competition, James Gray's "Paper Tiger" and Ira Sachs' musical "The Man I Love," received no recognition from the judges.

"Fjord" has already been acquired by US distributor Neon, marking the seventh consecutive year the company has taken home the festival's top prize. Park Chan-wook presided over this year's jury, which included actress Demi Moore, actor Stellan Skarsgård, director Chloé Zhao, and screenwriter Paul Laverty.
