Federal Reserve Holds Rates Steady, Signals Patience Amid Mixed Economic Data

The Fed voted unanimously to keep the benchmark rate unchanged, citing persistent inflation in services alongside a softening labor market. Chair Powell indicated the committee sees no urgency to move in either direction before year-end.

Today, 3:00 PM ET

The Federal Reserve held its benchmark interest rate steady on Wednesday, as policymakers opted for caution amid a mixed set of economic signals that have complicated the outlook for monetary policy this year.

In a unanimous decision, the Federal Open Market Committee left the federal funds rate in its current target range, resisting pressure from both sides of the policy debate. Officials cited continued stickiness in services inflation as a reason not to cut, while acknowledging that labor market conditions have cooled from their peak.

Chair Jerome Powell, speaking at a press conference following the decision, emphasized that the committee remains data-dependent and sees no compelling case to move rates in either direction before gathering more information on inflation trends and economic growth. He pushed back on suggestions that the Fed was behind the curve, noting that the current policy stance remains appropriate given the uncertainty in the environment.

Researchers Identify Mechanism Linking Sleep Disruption to Long-Term Memory Failure

Fragmented sleep interrupts hippocampal replay essential for long-term memory storage.

12:20 PM ET

A study published in Nature Neuroscience identified a specific neurological mechanism by which disrupted sleep interferes with memory consolidation, finding that fragmented sleep prevents the hippocampal replay sequences that transfer experiences into durable long-term storage.

Researchers said the findings help explain why poor sleep quality, not just insufficient sleep duration, has been associated with cognitive decline. The work may point toward targeted interventions for sleep-related memory disorders.
