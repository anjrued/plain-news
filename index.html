A few minutes ago

Officials in Orange County, California announced that the threat of a catastrophic explosion from a chemical tank in Garden Grove has been eliminated following an overnight operation by firefighters.

The crisis began Thursday at a facility owned by GKN Aerospace, where a storage tank containing methyl methacrylate, a highly flammable chemical used to make resins and plastics, began leaking. Initial evacuation orders were lifted that day, but the situation deteriorated when damage to a valve created additional problems that prevented complete mitigation of the crisis.

By Friday, authorities expanded evacuation orders to cover approximately 40,000 residents across six cities: Garden Grove, Cypress, Stanton, Anaheim, Buena Park and Westminster. Orange County fire authority division chief Craig Covey described the severity of the situation at a news conference, saying the tank faced two possible outcomes. "One: the tank fails and spills a total of about 6-7,000 gallons of very bad chemicals into the parking lot in that area. Or two: the tank goes into a thermal runaway and blows up, affecting the tanks that are around them that have fuel or the chemicals in them as well."

Covey emphasized the unpredictability of the situation, telling residents: "This thing is going to fail, and we don't know when. We're doing our best to figure out when or how we can prevent it."

Firefighters worked through the weekend to stabilize the tank through cooling and monitoring. The tank developed a crack over the weekend, which authorities believed would relieve internal pressure and reduce explosion risk. By Monday, officials declared the explosion threat eliminated.

Dr Regina Chinsio-Kwong, the county health officer, warned that if the chemical heated up, it could release harmful vapor that causes respiratory issues, eye irritation, nausea and headaches. Emergency crews established containment barriers using sandbags to prevent any chemical spill from entering storm drains, creeks or nearby ocean waters.

Garden Grove, located about 30 miles southeast of downtown Los Angeles and less than a mile from Disneyland, remained a focus of emergency operations. Police chief Amir El-Farra noted that approximately 15 percent of residents ordered to evacuate were refusing to leave. The city established at least two shelters for those who did comply with evacuation orders.

GKN Aerospace is a division of a British corporation that manufactures airplane engines and aircraft parts. While officials announced the elimination of the explosion threat, tens of thousands of residents remained under evacuation orders as authorities continued monitoring the damaged chemical tank. No casualties or injuries have been reported from the incident.

The United States and Iran are moving closer to a potential agreement to end their conflict, though significant questions remain unresolved and Iranian officials caution against premature declarations of success.

President Trump said a "memorandum of understanding" has been "largely negotiated," according to statements made this week. However, Iran's Foreign Ministry Spokesperson Esmail Baghaei emphasized that while progress has been made on many issues, "to say that this means the signing of an agreement is imminent - no one can make such a claim."

According to a U.S. official who spoke with Axios, the deal framework includes a 60-day ceasefire extension during which several key provisions would take effect. The Strait of Hormuz would reopen for shipping, Iran would be permitted to freely sell oil, and both sides would hold talks on limiting Iran's nuclear program. As part of the agreement, Iran would clear mines it deployed in the strait and refrain from imposing tolls on vessels passing through. In return, the United States would lift its blockade on Iranian ports that has been in place since April 13.

U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Sunday that the deal could result in "a completely open" Strait of Hormuz "without tolls" if successful. Iranian media reports, however, suggest that Tehran views the strait as remaining under Iranian control.

The agreement would reportedly unfreeze some Iranian assets currently held in banks outside the country. Hostilities would halt on all fronts, including between Israel and Lebanon.

What remains unclear is whether Iran's nuclear program will be substantially addressed. A senior Iranian source told Reuters that Tehran had not agreed to hand over its highly enriched uranium stockpile, and that Iran's nuclear issue was not part of the preliminary agreement. This contradicts an earlier New York Times report citing U.S. officials who said Iran had expressed willingness to give up the stockpile. Even if Tehran agrees to surrender some uranium, no details have emerged about how such a transfer would occur in practice.

Also notably absent from discussions are Iran's ballistic missile program and its support for regional allies including Hezbollah in Lebanon and the Houthis in Yemen. These remain largely unaddressed in the emerging framework.

The proposed deal has drawn sharp criticism from Republican hawks and pro-Israel groups who argue the agreement does not go far enough in constraining Iran's military capabilities and regional influence. Trump has defended the approach against these criticisms, indicating he is not rushing into an agreement.

Both sides continue working through remaining differences as international attention centers on Qatar, where diplomatic negotiations are taking place. The talks address nuclear restrictions, sanctions relief, and ceasefire terms as core negotiating points.

The potential deal centers on reopening the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping passage th...

2:54 PM ET

Oil prices fell sharply on Monday following reports that the United States and Iran may be moving toward a diplomatic agreement to end their three-month conflict. Brent crude futures, the global oil benchmark, dropped 6 percent to $97.43 a barrel, marking the lowest level in two weeks.

The potential deal centers on reopening the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping passage that has been effectively closed since the U.S. and Israel launched missile strikes on Iran in late February. The closure sent energy prices soaring and created one of the world's most dangerous energy chokepoints.

However, significant obstacles remain. While a framework has been negotiated, the U.S. and Iran still disagree over key issues, including Iran's blockade of the strait. An Iranian government spokesperson cautioned that an agreement was "not imminent," tempering market enthusiasm.

Stock markets rose on the news, with Japan's Nikkei climbing nearly 3 percent and the pan-European Stoxx 600 index gaining 1 percent. The dollar eased 0.3 percent against a basket of major currencies, while gold climbed 1.46 percent to $4,574 an ounce. Treasury bond futures also rallied as investors positioned for reduced geopolitical tension.

Stephen Innes, an independent analyst, noted the market logic: "Treasury futures rallied, gold climbed and equity futures pushed higher as investors started pricing the possibility that the world's most dangerous energy choke point may soon reopen to something resembling normal flow."

Despite the optimism, analysts warned against overconfidence. Warren Patterson, head of commodities strategy at ING, told Reuters: "We've been at this stage before, only for talks to break down. Therefore, the market will likely be more cautious about overreacting." Giovanni Staunovo, a UBS analyst, emphasized that "the key factors for the oil market to watch should be the physical oil flows, and so far flows through the strait remain restricted."

Even if diplomatic progress leads to an agreement, restoring normal oil supplies will take considerable time. Analysts say damaged energy infrastructure in Qatar and elsewhere requires repair before flows can return to pre-war levels. Two tankers carrying liquefied natural gas exited the strait on Monday, while a supertanker with Iraqi crude left the Gulf after being stranded for nearly three months, suggesting some movement in previously frozen shipping.

The price decline reflects investor relief that reduced energy costs could ease global inflation concerns. Higher oil prices have driven expectations of interest rate increases from central banks worldwide. However, oil prices remain significantly elevated compared to pre-war levels, when Brent crude traded around $70 a barrel. Barclays forecast an average price of $100 this year, though acknowledged risks of further increases.

Google announced Tuesday that it would overhaul its search box with artificial intelligence capabilities, marking the biggest change to the product in nearly 30 years. The tech giant revealed the updates at its annual I/O conference for software developers.

The redesign enables the search box to handle longer and more specific queries that resemble natural conversation rather than traditional search syntax. Instead of returning a list of links, Google's new search will direct users to engage directly with the company's AI chatbot, powered by the newly announced Gemini 3.5 model.

"Google Search is AI search," said Elizabeth Reid, who oversees the product. The changes are now rolling out globally to both desktop and mobile versions.

The updated search results will feature more visual elements and interactive suggestions. Users can now ask Google's AI to create calendar invites, build spreadsheet plans, or perform other actions within Google's products. Results will more closely resemble visually enhanced versions of AI Overviews, the bulleted summaries Google currently provides, rather than traditional link lists. Users who prefer the original format can click a "Web" tab to access it.

Since Google launched "AI Mode" a year ago, allowing users to chat with a Gemini-powered bot instead of browsing links, queries to the chatbot interface have doubled every quarter. Google reported that Search queries reached an all-time high last month.

The company introduced several new AI features. Gemini Spark, an AI agent, can access information from users' Gmail, Google Calendar, and other Google products to research, shop, and plan trips and appointments. For subscribers to its AI Pro and Ultra plans, Search will offer "information agents," autonomous bots that conduct in-depth research and return summaries or action plans. A "generative user interface" feature can create customized visuals and interactive dashboards based on user requests.

Google also announced "intelligent eyewear" in partnership with Samsung, Warby Parker, and Gentle Monster. These voice-activated glasses with embedded cameras will respond to Gemini commands and launch in the fall. The move marks Google's return to wearable glasses after shuttering Google Glass in 2015.

Josh Woodward, who oversees Gemini development, described the goal as creating "a universal assistant that's personal, proactive, persistent." Google reported that 900 million people use the Gemini app monthly, though this remains below ChatGPT's 900 million weekly users.

The integration of AI agents into Google's core search product represents a significant shift in how billions of users access information daily. The expansion brings this type of AI technology beyond tech-focused business users to mainstream consumers.

The film, which brings the popular Disney Plus series to theaters, underperformed compared to previous Star Wars releases. It failed to match the opening weekend of Solo: A Star Wars Story, which debuted on Memorial Day in 2018 with $103 million domestically and $171 million globally. Solo had been considered the franchise's first major flop despite its higher opening numbers.

The movie stars Pedro Pascal as the helmeted warrior traveling the galaxy with the tiny character widely known as Baby Yoda. The series previously aired exclusively on Disney Plus before this theatrical release.

According to industry analyst David Gross, the numbers represent "a piece of the business" for spin-offs. "The movie is a piece of the story, and it's earning a piece of the business," he told Agence France-Presse. He noted the film is "not on the level of the primary Star Wars films," but added these remain substantial box office figures.

The lower opening may signal continuing Star Wars fatigue. The Mandalorian and Grogu is the first Star Wars theatrical release since The Rise of Skywalker in 2019, when Disney paused new Star Wars film releases after determining they had launched too many too quickly.

Disney reportedly expects the film to maintain strength at the box office in coming weeks due to positive audience reception, despite receiving a muted critical response. The studio hopes the release will drive viewers toward related products including merchandise, theme parks, and Disney Plus content.

The Mandalorian remains the most-watched original series on Disney Plus, while more than 13 million Grogu toys have sold since the show debuted in 2019.

The Star Wars franchise has generated more than $10 billion in global ticket sales since its 1977 debut and ranks among the highest-grossing movie franchises of all time. Disney purchased the franchise from creator George Lucas in 2012.

While Disney has focused on launching Star Wars shows on Disney Plus, results have been mixed. Shows like Andor achieved both critical acclaim and strong viewership, but others including The Book of Boba Fett, Obi-Wan Kenobi, and The Acolyte failed to generate significant audience interest.

The next Star Wars theatrical film, Star Wars: Starfighter, which will star Ryan Gosling, is scheduled to release in May 2027.

The Trump administration has temporarily halted deportation flights to the Democratic Republic of Congo during a widening Ebola outbreak, according to reporting by Politico. However, public health experts say the pause will not help prevent the disease from spreading.

The suspension affects the removal of detainees to the DRC, a country facing a spiraling health crisis. The decision reflects concerns about potential exposure to the virus for Immigration and Customs Enforcement officials involved in deportation operations and the possibility that the disease could spread closer to the United States.

Ebola is a severe viral disease transmitted through direct contact with bodily fluids from infected individuals. Previous outbreaks in Central Africa have caused thousands of deaths. ICE typically conducts removal flights with teams of officers who accompany deportees during transport.

The pause has created complications for individuals already affected by removal orders. At least one woman has been left in limbo after officials moved her to Kinshasa, the capital of the DRC. Despite a judge's order for her return, administration officials say they will not bring her back because of the Ebola travel restrictions.

The outbreak has disrupted other international activities in the region. The Democratic Republic of Congo's national soccer team canceled the Kinshasa portion of its pre-World Cup training due to health restrictions. The team still plans to travel to the United States for additional preparation despite the outbreak.

Kate White, a nurse working in the DRC, expressed serious concerns about the situation. She stated she is "extremely concerned about the inability to get resources" to the Democratic Republic of Congo as the outbreak spreads.

Public health experts have noted that neither Ebola nor other recent viral outbreaks pose an immediate threat to most Americans. However, they have raised concerns about erosion of public trust in health institutions, warning that such a deficit could prove damaging when a genuine health crisis requires rapid public response.

The Trump administration has not specified how long the pause will remain in effect or what conditions would need to be met before deportation flights to Congo resume. Federal immigration authorities have not announced alternative plans for individuals who were scheduled for removal to the country.

The temporary suspension represents a shift in immigration enforcement operations targeting Congolese nationals. It comes amid broader scrutiny of the Trump administration's deportation flight program. House Democrats have demanded greater transparency from the Federal Aviation Administration regarding ICE charter operations, citing concerns about due process violations and inhumane conditions on deportation flights.
