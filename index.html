Yesterday, 8:35 AM ET

The World Health Organization has upgraded its risk assessment for the ongoing Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with WHO Director-General warning that the disease is spreading rapidly.

The WHO chief made the announcement following recent developments in the outbreak, which has prompted international health authorities to reassess the threat level. The upgraded risk assessment reflects growing concern about the trajectory of the outbreak and its potential impact on public health in the region.

The Democratic Republic of Congo has faced multiple Ebola outbreaks in recent years, with the disease posing significant challenges to health systems in affected areas. Ebola is a severe, often fatal illness that spreads through contact with bodily fluids of infected individuals.

The WHO's decision to upgrade the risk assessment signals increased international attention to the outbreak and typically precedes expanded response efforts. Such upgrades are based on factors including the number of cases, geographic spread, and the capacity of local health systems to respond.

The rapid spread cited by the WHO chief indicates that transmission rates have accelerated beyond previous projections. This development raises concerns about the potential for the outbreak to extend beyond its current boundaries and affect a larger population.

Previous Ebola outbreaks in the region have required substantial international support and coordination to bring under control. Response efforts typically include case identification and isolation, contact tracing, safe burial practices, and community education about transmission prevention.

The upgraded risk assessment comes as health authorities work to contain the outbreak and prevent further transmission. The WHO coordinates international response efforts during such public health emergencies, working with national governments and partner organizations.

The announcement underscores the ongoing threat that Ebola poses to communities in central Africa and the importance of rapid response mechanisms. The disease can have devastating effects on affected communities, with mortality rates varying depending on the virus strain and the availability of medical care.

Yesterday, 9:38 PM ET

Authorities in Orange County, California have ordered the evacuation of 40,000 people over concerns about a chemical leak that could result in a spill or explosion.

The problem arose on Thursday at a facility owned by GKN Aerospace in the town of Garden Grove, where a storage tank holding methyl methacrylate began off-gassing and threatened to fail. The chemical, which is highly flammable, is used to fabricate resins and plastics.

Local authorities originally responded to the incident with a hazmat team on Thursday, ordering local residents to evacuate. They lifted the order later that day, but the problem worsened due to "damage to a valve on the tank" that "created additional operational challenges, preventing complete mitigation," Garden Grove authorities wrote in an evacuation order.

By Friday, new evacuation orders had expanded to residents in six cities. Schools were shut down as the crisis continued.

"We have a tank that is actively in crisis," Orange County fire authority division chief Craig Covey told reporters at a news conference. "There are literally two options left remaining. One: the tank fails and spills a total of about 6-7,000 gallons of very bad chemicals into the parking lot in that area. Or two: the tank goes into a thermal runaway and blows up, affecting the tanks that are around them that have fuel or the chemicals in them as well."

Garden Grove police chief Amir El-Farra said some 15% of those facing evacuation orders were refusing to leave, according to the Orange County Register. The city has established at least two shelters for evacuees.

Covey, the county fire division chief, urged residents to take the order seriously. "We are setting up these evacuations in preparation for these two options: it fails or it blows up," Covey said. "Please follow our requests and orders for evacuations."

It's possible, however, that the tank could crack rather than explode. Emergency responders have laid out sand barriers to contain the chemicals in that event. "In a weird world, that's the best-case scenario, believe it or not," Covey said, according to the New York Times.

GKN Aerospace is a division of a British corporation that produces airplane engines and other aircraft parts.

Officials have warned that the tank contains a "highly volatile" substance. The storage tank continued to leak the hazardous chemical Friday as authorities worked to manage the crisis and protect public safety in the affected area.

The potential change would mark a significant shift in the governance of one of the world's premier space exploration facilities.

Caltech has managed JPL since the laboratory's founding, maintaining a unique relationship with NASA that has shaped decades of space exploration missions. The laboratory has been responsible for numerous landmark achievements in planetary science and space technology.

The news comes as NASA announced a broader organizational realignment aimed at accelerating mission delivery. The space agency revealed changes to its structure on Friday, though the specific details connecting these changes to JPL's management remain unclear from available information.

JPL, located in Pasadena, California, has been the operational home for many of NASA's most prominent missions. The laboratory has played a central role in Mars exploration, outer planet missions, and Earth science programs. Its engineers and scientists have developed spacecraft and instruments that have explored every planet in the solar system.

The potential management change represents a departure from the long-standing partnership between Caltech and NASA. Universities managing federal laboratories is a common arrangement in the United States, with various academic institutions overseeing Department of Energy facilities and other government research centers.

Caltech's oversight of JPL has allowed the laboratory to blend academic research with practical space mission development. The arrangement has enabled close collaboration between university researchers and JPL engineers, facilitating the transfer of scientific discoveries into space exploration technology.

The timing of this potential change coincides with NASA's stated goal of streamlining its operations and accelerating mission timelines. The space agency has faced pressure to deliver missions more efficiently while managing budget constraints and competing priorities across its various programs.

No official announcement has been made regarding the final decision on JPL's management structure. The implications of any management change would likely affect ongoing missions, future project planning, and the laboratory's workforce of thousands of employees and contractors who support NASA's exploration objectives.
