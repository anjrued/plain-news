Donald Trump announced on Saturday that a peace deal with Iran has been largely negotiated, potentially signaling an end to the conflict that began in February between the United States, Israel and Iran.

The US president wrote on his social media platform that the "final aspects and details" of a memorandum of understanding are still under discussion and will be announced shortly. As part of the agreement, Trump said the Strait of Hormuz will be reopened.

The announcement came after Trump held discussions with Pakistani mediators, Gulf allies and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Iran's Fars news agency, which has ties to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard, reported that the strait would remain under Iranian control.

Earlier in the week, Trump had expressed growing impatience with the pace of negotiations, posting on social media that "the Clock is Ticking" for Iran and warning they "better get moving, FAST, or there won't be anything left of them." He added that "TIME IS OF THE ESSENCE."

According to Axios, Trump is scheduled to meet with his national security team to discuss next steps in the Iran situation. His call with Netanyahu preceded an Israeli security cabinet meeting focused on Iran, Lebanon and Gaza. There has been mounting speculation in Israel that fighting with Iran could resume if no signs of compromise emerge from negotiations.

Three months into the conflict, the Trump administration has alternated between threats of military strikes and diplomatic overtures. Neither approach has produced a clear resolution thus far.

Regional tensions remain high despite talk of a potential agreement. The United Arab Emirates blamed Iran or one of its proxies for a drone attack that caused a fire near its nuclear power plant, calling the incident a dangerous escalation.

Tehran has also signaled progress on the talks but indicated that the critical issue of nuclear weapons is not part of the initial framework being developed. According to reports, any deal would include a two-month negotiation period specifically addressing Iran's nuclear program.

The potential agreement represents a significant development in a conflict that has destabilized the region and threatened global energy supplies. The Strait of Hormuz is a vital waterway through which a substantial portion of the world's oil supply passes, and its closure has had major economic implications.

Trump's announcement of a largely completed deal suggests movement after weeks of diplomatic deadlock, though significant details remain to be finalized. The coming days will reveal whether the framework holds and whether both sides can reach final agreement on outstanding issues.

The situation continues to evolve rapidly, with multiple parties involved in complex negotiations aimed at ending the military conflict while addressing longer-term concerns about Iran's nuclear capabilities and regional influence.

The US Secret Service shot and killed an armed intruder who breached the perimeter of Mar-a-Lago, Donald Trump's Florida residence and private club, early Sunday morning. President Trump was at the White House in Washington during the incident, along with first lady Melania Trump.

At a press conference Sunday morning, Palm Beach County Sheriff Ric Bradshaw said two Secret Service agents and one of his deputies responded to the north gate of the property around 1:30 am ET after security alerted them that someone had entered an inner perimeter. There they confronted a white male carrying a shotgun and a gasoline can.

"He was ordered to drop those two pieces of equipment that he had with him, at which time he put down the gas can [and] raised the shotgun to a shooting position," Bradshaw said. "At that point in time, the deputy and the two Secret Service agents fired their weapons and neutralized the threat. He is deceased at the scene."

None of the law enforcement personnel were injured. Bradshaw could not confirm whether the shotgun was loaded and did not immediately identify the intruder. The Associated Press later reported that investigators identified the man as 21-year-old Austin Tucker Martin, citing an anonymous source familiar with the investigation.

The Secret Service said the suspect was in his early 20s and from North Carolina. He had been reported missing by his family a few days earlier. Investigators believe he traveled south from North Carolina and obtained the shotgun during his journey. The gun's box was recovered in his vehicle.

According to Secret Service spokesman Anthony Guglielmi, the man drove through the north gate of Mar-a-Lago as another vehicle was exiting. He was confronted by Secret Service agents and fatally shot. Investigators are now working to compile a psychological profile and determine a motive.

The incident occurred just inside Mar-a-Lago's main entrance gate on South Ocean Boulevard. The gate at the north end of the estate opens to a driveway leading directly to the main building's entrance lobby.

Brett Skiles, special agent in charge of the FBI's Miami field office, said the bureau's evidence response team was processing the shooting site and collecting evidence. Skiles asked nearby residents to check exterior cameras for suspicious footage from Saturday night or early Sunday.

This episode follows other security concerns at Mar-a-Lago. In 2019, a Chinese woman carrying multiple cellphones and a thumb drive with malware gained access to the main lobby. Trump also survived two assassination attempts during 2024, including a shooting at a Pennsylvania rally in July where he was wounded, and an incident in September when a man with a rifle was captured near Trump's golf course in West Palm Beach.

3:55 PM ET

An Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has rapidly escalated, with health officials reporting a sharp increase in suspected cases and deaths over the past week. The outbreak, centered in the conflict-affected Ituri province, has raised significant concerns among international health authorities.

The World Health Organization revised its threat assessment upward on Friday, describing the outbreak as posing a "very high" risk to the country. The situation has deteriorated quickly, with suspected cases nearly tripling from 246 to almost 750 in just one week. Suspected deaths have also jumped from 65 to 177 during the same period.

The outbreak is concentrated mainly in the Mongwalu and Rwampara health zones of Ituri province, which borders Uganda and South Sudan. Suspected cases have also been reported in neighboring Bunia. The region's high population mobility, with people constantly moving through mining towns, makes containing the infectious disease particularly challenging.

Uganda has confirmed the outbreak has crossed its border, with a 59-year-old man dying in a Kampala hospital after traveling from the DRC. This cross-border spread has prompted urgent action from regional health authorities.

Testing conducted by the DRC's national research laboratory detected Ebola virus in 13 of 20 samples examined. Health officials identified the strain as Bundibugyo, which presents a significant challenge because no licensed vaccine exists for this variant. This differs from the more common Zaire strain, for which vaccines are available. Bundibugyo virus has only caused two previous outbreaks, in 2007 and 2012.

The outbreak has had broader implications beyond public health. The DR Congo's national football team has been told they must isolate for 21 days before entering the United States for the World Cup due to the Ebola situation.

Dr. Jean Kaseya, director general of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention, emphasized the need for rapid regional coordination. "Africa CDC stands in solidarity with the government and people of the Democratic Republic of the Congo as they respond to this outbreak," he said, noting that high population movement between affected areas and neighboring countries makes regional coordination essential.

The Africa CDC convened an urgent meeting on Friday with authorities from the DRC, Uganda, and South Sudan, along with the World Health Organization and pharmaceutical companies to coordinate response efforts.

Dr. Michael Head, a senior research fellow in global health at the University of Southampton, pointed to multiple factors contributing to regular outbreaks in the DRC, including close human contact with animal reservoirs like bats and primates, movement between rural and urban areas, the tropical climate, and extensive rainforest coverage.

This marks the DRC's 16th Ebola outbreak since the virus was first identified there in 1976. Ebola spreads through direct contact with body fluids of infected people or dead bodies during funeral preparations.

An Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has rapidly escalated, with health officials reporting a sharp increase in suspected cases and deaths over the past week. The outbreak, centered in the conflict-affected Ituri province, has raised significant concerns among international health authorities.

The World Health Organization revised its threat assessment upward on Friday, describing the outbreak as posing a "very high" risk to the country. The situation has deteriorated quickly, with suspected cases nearly tripling from 246 to almost 750 in just one week. Suspected deaths have also jumped from 65 to 177 during the same period.

The outbreak is concentrated mainly in the Mongwalu and Rwampara health zones of Ituri province, which borders Uganda and South Sudan. Suspected cases have also been reported in neighboring Bunia. The region's high population mobility, with people constantly moving through mining towns, makes containing the infectious disease particularly challenging.

Uganda has confirmed the outbreak has crossed its border, with a 59-year-old man dying in a Kampala hospital after traveling from the DRC. This cross-border spread has prompted urgent action from regional health authorities.

Testing conducted by the DRC's national research laboratory detected Ebola virus in 13 of 20 samples examined. Health officials identified the strain as Bundibugyo, which presents a significant challenge because no licensed vaccine exists for this variant. This differs from the more common Zaire strain, for which vaccines are available. Bundibugyo virus has only caused two previous outbreaks, in 2007 and 2012.

The outbreak has had broader implications beyond public health. The DR Congo's national football team has been told they must isolate for 21 days before entering the United States for the World Cup due to the Ebola situation.

Dr. Jean Kaseya, director general of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention, emphasized the need for rapid regional coordination. "Africa CDC stands in solidarity with the government and people of the Democratic Republic of the Congo as they respond to this outbreak," he said, noting that high population movement between affected areas and neighboring countries makes regional coordination essential.

The Africa CDC convened an urgent meeting on Friday with authorities from the DRC, Uganda, and South Sudan, along with the World Health Organization and pharmaceutical companies to coordinate response efforts.

Dr. Michael Head, a senior research fellow in global health at the University of Southampton, pointed to multiple factors contributing to regular outbreaks in the DRC, including close human contact with animal reservoirs like bats and primates, movement between rural and urban areas, the tropical climate, and extensive rainforest coverage.

This marks the DRC's 16th Ebola outbreak since the virus was first identified there in 1976. Ebola spreads through direct contact with body fluids of infected people or dead bodies during funeral preparations.
