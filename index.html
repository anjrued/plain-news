The investment represents a major shift in how the government supports strategic technology development. Rather than traditional grant structures, the government will take ownership positions in quantum computing companies, linking taxpayer funding directly to potential commercial outcomes.

Quantum computing represents a fundamental departure from traditional computing, using quantum mechanical phenomena to process information in ways conventional computers cannot. The technology has applications in cryptography, drug discovery, financial modeling, and artificial intelligence, though practical large-scale quantum computers remain in development.

The $2 billion commitment comes as the United States faces increasing competition in quantum technology from China and other nations. Both countries view quantum computing as strategically important for national security and economic competitiveness. China has invested heavily in quantum research and infrastructure over the past decade.

By taking equity stakes rather than providing grants or contracts, the government creates a different risk-reward structure. If the quantum companies succeed commercially, taxpayers could see returns on the investment. If the companies fail or the technology develops more slowly than anticipated, the stakes could lose value.

The investment plan has not been finalized, and details about which companies would receive funding, what percentage stakes the government would take, or how the investment would be structured remain unclear. The initiative would likely require congressional approval and coordination between multiple federal agencies.

Quantum computing has attracted significant private investment in recent years, with major technology companies including Google, IBM, and Microsoft developing quantum research programs. Startups in the sector have also raised substantial venture capital funding. The federal investment would add government resources to this mix of private capital.

The announcement reflects broader government efforts to maintain American leadership in advanced technologies. Similar approaches have been discussed for semiconductor manufacturing, artificial intelligence development, and biotechnology. The quantum investment would test whether direct equity participation can effectively accelerate strategic technology development while protecting taxpayer interests.

The satellite launched Thursday and represents a collaboration between European and Chinese space agencies. Its primary objective is to collect detailed measurement...

Yesterday, 2:23 AM ET

A joint European-Chinese satellite has launched aboard a Vega-C rocket to study Earth's magnetic field. The mission aims to provide new data that could change how scientists understand the planet's magnetic shield.

The satellite launched Thursday and represents a collaboration between European and Chinese space agencies. Its primary objective is to collect detailed measurements of Earth's magnetic field and how it protects the planet from solar radiation.

Earth's magnetic field acts as an invisible shield, deflecting charged particles from the sun and cosmic radiation. Understanding this protective barrier is important for predicting space weather events that can disrupt satellites, power grids, and communication systems.

The satellite carries instruments designed to measure variations in the magnetic field with high precision. These measurements will help scientists map the field's structure and track how it changes over time.

Researchers expect the data to reveal new information about the dynamics of Earth's magnetosphere, the region of space dominated by the planet's magnetic field. This includes how the field interacts with solar wind and how disturbances propagate through the system.

The mission is part of ongoing efforts to monitor Earth's magnetic field, which has been weakening in some regions and strengthening in others. Scientists have observed that the magnetic north pole has been shifting at an accelerating rate in recent decades.

Previous satellite missions have provided foundational data about the magnetic field, but this new mission aims to fill gaps in understanding and provide more detailed observations. The combination of European and Chinese expertise and technology is expected to enhance the quality and scope of the data collected.

The launch on the Vega-C rocket marks another step in international cooperation on space science. The data from this mission will be shared among the international scientific community for research purposes.

Scientists plan to use the satellite's observations to improve models of the magnetic field and better predict how it might evolve in the future. This information has practical applications for navigation systems, which rely on accurate magnetic field data, as well as for understanding the long-term habitability of planets.
