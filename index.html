"While we have made significant progress … I recognize there is still important work to be done," she wrote.

The White House forced Gabbard to resign, the Reuters news agency reported, citing a source familiar with the issue. However, a spokesperson for Gabbard's office said rumors that Gabbard had been forcibly ousted from office were "100% false."

In her resignation letter, Gabbard said her husband, Abraham, "has recently been diagnosed with an extremely rare form of bone cancer." She added: "At this time, I must step away from public service to be by his side and fully support him through this battle."

Gabbard was an unconventional choice for the role. The former Democrat lacked an intelligence background and her foreign policy views at times diverged with Trump's, particularly on questions of overseas military intervention. She also took unusual measures to ingratiate herself to the president by vowing to root out politicization across US spy agencies and embracing his agenda of election denial.

Over time, Gabbard became increasingly sidelined by the president, who excluded her from key national security conversations on Iran and Venezuela, according to people familiar with the office of the director of national intelligence.

Trump was asking cabinet members last month whether he should replace Gabbard, according to two people briefed on the discussions.

"Unfortunately, after having done a great job, Tulsi Gabbard will be leaving the Administration on June 30th," he wrote in a statement on his Truth Social platform on Friday. Gabbard "has done an incredible job, and we will miss her," the president said.

Trump added that Aaron Lukas, principal deputy director of national intelligence, would serve as acting director of national intelligence.

The resignation comes as Trump faces mounting pressure over multiple foreign policy challenges. The US president and Secretary of State Marco Rubio on Thursday again raised the specter of military intervention in Cuba, a renewed threat that takes on greater weight a day after the administration announced criminal charges against Raúl Castro, the island's former leader.

Delivery Hero has confirmed it has received a takeover offer from Uber, marking a potential consolidation in the global food delivery market. The Berlin-based company disclosed the approach in a regulatory filing.

The confirmation comes as the food delivery sector continues to face pressure from slowing growth and increased competition. Delivery Hero operates in over 70 countries across Asia, Europe, Latin America, and the Middle East, making it one of the largest food delivery platforms outside the United States.

Uber already operates its Uber Eats platform in multiple markets and has previously pursued expansion through acquisitions. The company acquired Postmates in 2020 for $2.65 billion and has made several smaller acquisitions to strengthen its delivery business in various regions.

Delivery Hero did not disclose the financial terms of the offer or provide details about the timing of any potential deal. The company stated it is reviewing the proposal and will update shareholders as appropriate.

The food delivery industry has undergone significant consolidation in recent years as companies seek to achieve profitability through scale. Many delivery platforms have struggled to turn consistent profits due to high operating costs, including payments to delivery drivers and restaurant partners, as well as customer acquisition expenses.

A combination of Uber and Delivery Hero would create a delivery giant with operations spanning most major markets globally. However, such a deal would likely face regulatory scrutiny in multiple jurisdictions, particularly in markets where both companies currently operate.

Uber has been working to strengthen its delivery business as a complement to its core ride-hailing operations. The company has reported that its delivery segment has helped offset volatility in ride-hailing demand and provides a more diversified revenue base.

Delivery Hero shares are listed on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange. The company has not indicated whether it views the Uber offer favorably or if it is considering other options. Neither company has commented beyond the initial confirmation from Delivery Hero.

Yesterday, 7:55 PM ET

The California Institute of Technology faces the possibility of losing management control of NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory for the first time in the institution's history. JPL has been operated by Caltech under contract with NASA since the laboratory's founding.

The potential change in management comes as NASA evaluates its relationship with the laboratory. JPL, located in Pasadena, California, has been responsible for managing many of NASA's robotic space missions and developing spacecraft technology. The laboratory has played a central role in planetary exploration, including Mars rover missions and deep space probes.

Caltech has managed JPL since 1936, when the laboratory began as a graduate student rocket project at the university. The institution formalized its relationship with NASA in 1958 when the space agency was established. Under this arrangement, Caltech has overseen the laboratory's operations, staffing, and research direction while receiving federal funding.

The review of JPL's management structure represents a significant shift in how NASA oversees one of its major research facilities. JPL employs thousands of scientists, engineers, and support staff who work on current and future space missions. The laboratory's work includes developing technologies for Mars exploration, studying Earth's climate from space, and planning missions to outer planets and their moons.

NASA has not announced a final decision on JPL's future management structure. The agency conducts periodic reviews of its facilities and contractor relationships as part of standard operations. Any change in management would require NASA to issue a new contract and potentially restructure how the laboratory operates.

Caltech's leadership has not publicly commented on the review process. The university's management of JPL has been part of its identity as a leading science and engineering institution. Beyond managing the laboratory, Caltech faculty and students have collaborated with JPL researchers on space science projects.

The outcome of NASA's review will determine whether Caltech continues its decades-long role managing JPL or whether another organization takes over operations. The decision could affect how the laboratory operates and its relationship with the academic community.

The space agency operates programs including the International Space Station, the Artemis lunar exploration program, and various robotic missions exploring the solar system and beyond. NASA also maintains responsibilities for Earth science research and aeronautics development.

Isaacman's background differs from many previous NASA administrators, as he comes from the private spaceflight sector rather than traditional government or military aviation channels. His appointment reflected a shift toward greater involvement of commercial space companies in NASA operations.

Two-time NASCAR Cup Series champion Kyle Busch has died at age 41 after severe pneumonia progressed into sepsis, resulting in rapid and overwhelming associated complications, according to a statement released by his family.

Dakota Hunter, vice-president of Kyle Busch Companies, said in a news release that the family received the medical evaluation on Saturday.

NASCAR announced Busch's death in a Thursday statement. "We are saddened and heartbroken to share the news of the passing of Kyle Busch, a two-time Cup champion and one of our sport's greatest and fiercest drivers. He was 41 years old," the racing organization wrote on social media. "We extend our deepest condolences to the Busch family, Richard Childress Racing and the entire motorsports community."

Busch's family had announced his hospitalization earlier on Thursday, saying he wouldn't compete as planned at the weekend's NASCAR race in Charlotte, North Carolina.

During a NASCAR Cup Series race on May 10 at Watkins Glen, Busch radioed in to his crew requesting medical aid from Dr. Bill Heisel and a "shot" after the race. According to the TV broadcast, Busch had been struggling with a sinus cold that was exacerbated by the intense G-forces and elevation changes at the upstate New York road course. Busch continued to race and finished eighth.

Busch ranked 24th in the Cup Series standings, with two top-10 finishes in 12 races this season. The Las Vegas native won championships in 2015 and 2019. Busch was in his fourth season at Richard Childress Racing after winning titles with Joe Gibbs Racing.

Busch won 232 races across NASCAR's three national series, more than any driver in history. He won the trucks race at Dover on Friday, six days before his death.

In a joint statement, the Busch family, Richard Childress Racing and NASCAR said: "Our entire NASCAR family is heartbroken by the loss of Kyle Busch. A future Hall of Famer, Kyle was a rare talent, one who comes along once in a generation. He was fierce, he was passionate, he was immensely skilled and he cared deeply about the sport and fans."

The statement continued: "Throughout a career that spanned more than two decades, Kyle set records in national series wins, won championships at NASCAR's highest level and fostered the next generation of drivers as an owner in the Truck Series. His sharp wit and competitive spirit sparked a deep emotional connection with race fans of every age, creating the proud and loyal 'Rowdy Nation.' NASCAR lost a giant of the sport today, far too soon."

A polarizing figure known as "Rowdy" and "Wild Thing" for his post-race fights, regular feuds with other drivers and sometimes outlandish behavior, the multi-talented Busch stormed on the Cup Series scene in 2005 by winning rookie of the year.

Busch is survived by his wife, Samantha, and children Brexton and Lennix.
