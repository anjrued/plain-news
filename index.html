A 21-year-old gunman who opened fire outside the White House on Saturday was shot and killed by federal agents in an exchange of gunfire. The suspect, identified in media reports as Nasire Best, approached a checkpoint near 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue NW shortly after 6pm, removed a pistol from a bag, and fired at officers. Secret Service personnel returned fire, striking the suspect fatally.

A bystander was also struck during the exchange, though it remains unclear whether they were hit by the suspect's shots or by return fire from officers. The bystander was taken to a nearby hospital in serious condition. No Secret Service agents were injured in the incident.

The White House was briefly locked down following the shooting. President Donald Trump was inside the building at the time. Trump later stated on social media that the gunman had "a violent history" and a "possible obsession" with the White House. He praised the "swift and professional action" of law enforcement officials.

Court records reveal that Best was already known to the Secret Service before Saturday's incident. According to an affidavit filed in DC superior court, Best had previously attempted to enter the White House complex. Last summer, he was documented "walking around the White House complex inquiring how to gain access at various entry points" on multiple occasions.

On June 26, 2025, Best was involuntarily committed after obstructing vehicle entry to part of the White House complex. Two weeks later, on July 10, 2025, he was arrested for unlawful entry after multiple officers confronted him for ignoring warning signs and walking into a restricted area. According to court documents, Best claimed to be Jesus and stated that he wanted to get arrested.

A judge issued a "stay away order" at an initial hearing that day, barring Best from the White House area. A bench warrant was later issued after Best failed to appear at a subsequent hearing.

Law enforcement officials described Best as an emotionally disturbed person who was known to authorities. The FBI is assisting the Secret Service and local police in investigating the incident.

This shooting occurred approximately one month after another shooting at the White House Correspondents' Association dinner at a Washington hotel. Following the incident, Trump referenced his plans for enhanced security measures, stating that the event "goes to show how important it is, for all future presidents, to get, what will be, the most safe and secure space of its kind ever built in Washington, DC."

Busch was one of NASCAR's most accomplished drivers, winning 234 races across the sport's top three series over his two-decade career, a record surpassing any other driver in history. NASCAR driver Kyle Busch has died at age 41 from complications related to severe pneumonia that progressed into sepsis, his family announced. The two-time NASCAR champion passed away on Thursday, one day after collapsing in a Chevrolet simulator while preparing for the Coca-Cola 600 at Charlotte Motor Speedway.

According to a statement from Dakota Hunter, vice-president of Kyle Busch Companies, the family received medical evaluation on Saturday. "Severe pneumonia progressed into sepsis, resulting in rapid and overwhelming associated complications," Hunter said.

A 911 call obtained by the Associated Press revealed that on the day before his death, Busch experienced shortness of breath, felt overheated, and was coughing up blood. He was found lying on a bathroom floor inside a complex and was transported to a hospital in Charlotte, North Carolina, where he later died.

Busch had been showing signs of illness earlier in the racing season. While competing at Watkins Glen on May 10, he radioed his team requesting medical attention after the race. Despite feeling unwell, he recovered enough to win a Trucks Series race at Dover the following weekend and finished 17th in the All-Star race on Sunday.

The NASCAR community responded with widespread tributes honoring Busch's legacy. Daniel Suárez, who won the Coca-Cola 600, dedicated his victory to the late driver. Multiple drivers, including William Byron, Kyle Larson, and Chase Elliott, paid their respects ahead of the race. Dale Earnhardt Jr. narrated a tribute video reflecting on Busch's career and his relationships within the racing community.

NASCAR CEO Steve O'Donnell described Busch as "an American badass," while acknowledging their competitive history. "We certainly had our battles but I would give a lot of money to have a few more battles," O'Donnell said. Brad Keselowski confirmed that Busch had not been feeling well recently but declined to provide specifics.

Busch was one of NASCAR's most accomplished drivers, winning 234 races across the sport's top three series over his two-decade career, a record surpassing any other driver in history. His death prompted tributes from beyond the motorsports world. Donald Trump called Busch "a true talent who loved NASCAR and its fans. Kyle was a WINNER!" Vice President JD Vance described him as "one of NASCAR's greatest racers."

All 39 drivers entered in Sunday's race displayed a black number 8 decal on their cars to honor Busch. Sepsis is a life-threatening medical emergency that occurs when the body has an extreme overactive response to infection, causing the immune system to damage its own tissues and organs.
