8:20 AM ET

A blast on a passenger train in Pakistan's southwestern Balochistan province has killed at least 24 people, according to officials. The explosion struck the train while it was traveling through the region, which has experienced repeated violence in recent years.

Balochistan, Pakistan's largest province by area, shares borders with Iran and Afghanistan. The region has faced prolonged security challenges from separatist groups who have launched attacks against security forces, infrastructure, and civilians. Railway targets have been hit multiple times in previous incidents.

Authorities have not yet confirmed what caused the blast or identified those responsible. The death toll of 24 makes this one of the deadlier attacks on civilian transportation in the province this year.

The explosion is part of a broader pattern of security incidents affecting Balochistan. Insurgent groups have operated in the province for decades, citing grievances related to the central government's control of the region's natural resources and what they describe as the marginalization of local populations.

Railway transport remains a vital means of travel for residents of Balochistan despite the ongoing security risks. Previous attacks on trains and railway infrastructure in the province have included bombings and assaults on passengers.

The Pakistani military and paramilitary forces have been deployed throughout Balochistan to counter insurgent activity. However, attacks continue to occur regularly. The province's vast terrain and sparse population make it challenging for security forces to prevent all incidents.

As of the time of reporting, no group had claimed responsibility for the attack. Multiple armed organizations operate within Balochistan, including separatist factions and other militant groups. The attack follows patterns similar to previous bombings attributed to these organizations, though official confirmation of responsibility has not been released.

The incident adds to escalating violence in the region. Recently, the Balochistan Liberation Army carried out attacks that killed approximately 50 people in the southwestern province. In response, security forces reported killing more than 200 suspected militants, according to military statements.

Pakistan has experienced a surge in militant violence in recent months. Beyond Balochistan, attacks have occurred in other areas. In November of the previous year, a suicide bombing outside a court in Islamabad killed 12 people. One of the deadliest attacks on the capital occurred in 2008, when a suicide bombing at a Marriott hotel resulted in 63 deaths and injured more than 250.

The ongoing instability in Balochistan underscores the broader security challenges facing Pakistan as various militant groups continue operations across different regions of the country. The government continues to work on security responses while civilian casualties from these incidents persist.

9:53 AM ET

A 21-year-old man died on Saturday after opening fire at a security checkpoint near the White House and being shot by federal agents who returned fire, according to the U.S. Secret Service. The suspect, identified in media reports as Nasire Best, approached a checkpoint near 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue NW shortly after 6 p.m. and pulled a pistol from a bag before firing at officers.

Secret Service agents immediately returned fire, striking the suspect. A bystander was also struck during the exchange, though it remains unclear whether they were hit by the gunman's shots or by rounds fired by officers. No Secret Service agents were injured. The suspect was transported to a nearby hospital and later pronounced dead.

President Donald Trump was inside the White House at the time of the incident. The complex was briefly locked down following the shooting. Trump praised the "swift and professional action" of law enforcement officials in response to the incident on social media, characterizing the gunman as having "a violent history" and a "possible obsession" with the White House.

Court records show that Best was already known to the Secret Service before Saturday's shooting. According to an affidavit filed in DC Superior Court, Best had been observed "walking around the White House complex inquiring how to gain access at various entry points" on multiple occasions during the previous summer.

In June 2025, Best was involuntarily committed for obstructing vehicle entry to part of the White House complex. The following month, on July 10, 2025, he was arrested for unlawful entry after officers found him ignoring warning signs and walking into a restricted area outside the White House. During that arrest, Best claimed to be Jesus and told officers "that he wanted to get arrested," according to court documents.

A judge issued a stay-away order at an initial hearing, prohibiting Best from entering the White House area. The New York Post reported that a bench warrant was later issued after Best failed to attend a subsequent court hearing.

An unnamed law enforcement official told the Washington Post that Best was considered an emotionally disturbed person known to law enforcement. The FBI is assisting the Secret Service and local police in investigating the incident.

The shooting marked the third incident involving gunfire in the vicinity of President Trump within a month. Trump indicated that the incident underscored the importance of enhanced security measures, referencing plans for what he described as "the most safe and secure space of its kind ever built in Washington, DC."

1:35 PM ET

A sustained selloff in US Treasury bonds is forcing the White House to confront rising borrowing costs that could complicate fiscal policy and economic growth targets.

Treasury yields have climbed in recent trading sessions as investors demand higher returns to hold government debt. The selloff reflects concerns about the federal deficit and inflation expectations, pushing up the interest rates the government must pay to finance its operations.

Higher Treasury yields ripple through the broader economy by increasing borrowing costs for mortgages, car loans, and corporate debt. They also raise the federal government's own debt servicing expenses, which now consume a growing share of the annual budget.

The White House faces pressure to respond as the cost of financing the national debt rises. Higher interest payments could force difficult choices between maintaining spending programs and addressing deficit concerns. The situation also complicates economic policy coordination between the administration and the Federal Reserve.

Market participants are watching for signals about whether policymakers will tolerate the higher rates or take action to support Treasury prices. The government's borrowing needs remain substantial, with regular auctions of new debt securities required to fund operations and refinance maturing obligations.

The Treasury market serves as the foundation for global finance, with yields on US government bonds influencing interest rates worldwide. Sustained volatility in this market carries implications beyond American borders, affecting international capital flows and foreign government debt costs.

Economists note that rising Treasury yields could slow economic growth by making credit more expensive for businesses and consumers. At the same time, higher yields might help the Federal Reserve in its efforts to control inflation by tightening financial conditions without additional rate increases.

The administration has not yet announced specific policy responses to the market moves. Treasury officials continue regular debt issuance schedules while monitoring market conditions. The situation highlights ongoing tensions between fiscal policy needs and market forces in determining government borrowing costs.

Epic Games announced Unreal Engine 6 on Sunday, revealing the next generation of its widely used game development platform alongside a remake of Rocket League built on the new engine.

The announcement marks a significant update to the Unreal Engine series, which powers games and applications across the industry. Epic used Rocket League, its popular car soccer game, to demonstrate the new engine's capabilities.

Unreal Engine has become a standard tool for game developers since its earlier versions, with studios of all sizes using it to create games for consoles, computers, and mobile devices. The engine also sees use in film production, architecture visualization, and other industries requiring real-time 3D graphics.

The decision to showcase Unreal Engine 6 through a Rocket League remake gives players a direct look at what the new technology can produce. Rocket League launched in 2015 and has maintained a large player base through regular updates and competitive play.

Epic has not announced a release date for Unreal Engine 6 or provided technical specifications. The company also did not say when the Rocket League remake would become available to players.

The announcement comes as game engines continue to evolve to support more detailed graphics, larger game worlds, and new technologies. Unreal Engine 5, the current version, introduced features like Nanite virtualized geometry and Lumen global illumination that allowed developers to create more realistic environments.

Game studios typically need time to learn new engine versions and adapt their workflows. Major engine updates can take years for developers to fully adopt across their projects.

Epic Games owns both Unreal Engine and Rocket League developer Psyonix, which it acquired in 2019. The company has previously used its own games to demonstrate engine capabilities, including Fortnite for Unreal Engine 5 features.

Developers and players will likely learn more about Unreal Engine 6 and the Rocket League remake in the coming months as Epic provides additional details about the technology and its release plans.

Romanian director Cristian Mungiu has won the Palme d'Or at the 2026 Cannes Film Festival for his film "Fjord," marking his second victory at the prestigious event. The film stars Renate Reinsve and Sebastian Stan as Romanian religious parents who move to Norway and face accusations of child abuse.

This is Mungiu's English-language debut. Nineteen years earlier, he won the same top prize for "4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days," his acclaimed drama about abortion in communist-era Romania. With this second Palme d'Or, Mungiu joins an exclusive group of ten directors to have received the award twice. The others include Francis Ford Coppola, Michael Haneke, Ken Loach, and Ruben Östlund.

The awards ceremony took place at the conclusion of the 79th Cannes Film Festival. Andrey Zvyagintsev's "Minotaur," described as a dark satire examining corruption and infidelity in contemporary Russia, won the runner-up Grand Prix prize. "The Dreamed Adventure," Valeska Grisebach's drama set in Bulgaria about an archaeologist, took the Jury Prize in third place.

The best director award was split between Pawel Pawlikowski for "Fatherland" and Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi for "The Black Ball." Acting awards were also divided among multiple performers. Virginie Efira and Tao Okamoto both won the best actress prize for their work in Ryusuke Hamaguchi's care drama "All of a Sudden." The best actor prize went jointly to Valentin Campagne and Emmanuel Macchia for the queer World War I drama "Coward."

"Fjord" has already been acquired by US distributor Neon, marking their seventh consecutive year securing the top prize at Cannes. This achievement reflects the film's commercial appeal alongside its critical recognition.

The festival was presided over by a jury led by filmmaker Park Chan-wook, which included actress Demi Moore, actor Stellan Skarsgård, director Chloé Zhao, and screenwriter Paul Laverty.

During the closing ceremony, Isabelle Huppert paid special tribute to Barbra Streisand, who was one of three recipients of honorary Palmes d'Or this year. Streisand was unable to attend due to a knee injury but sent a video message to the audience. She spoke about cinema's ability to unite people across divides, saying: "Film has that magical ability to unite us, opening our hearts and minds."

The festival attracted significant international attention, showcasing films from directors worldwide. Past Palme d'Or winners have gone on to achieve both critical acclaim and commercial success in global release, with Sean Baker's "Anora" being a recent example, having won the prize two years ago before sweeping the 2025 Oscars.

Colorado Democratic Party censures Governor Polis over clemency for election official

Peters, who served as Mesa County Clerk, had her nearly nine-year sentence reduced to approximately four and a half years. She is scheduled for release on p...

May 21, 10:12 AM ET

The Colorado Democratic Party voted to censure Governor Jared Polis following his decision to commute the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former county clerk convicted of allowing unauthorized access to voting equipment.

Peters, who served as Mesa County Clerk, had her nearly nine-year sentence reduced to approximately four and a half years. She is scheduled for release on parole on June 1. In his clemency letter, Polis described the original sentence as "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes."

Peters was found guilty in 2024 of four felonies and three misdemeanors. In 2021, she allowed Conan Hayes, a former professional surfer affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, to access the county's voting equipment and copy sensitive data. Hayes also attended a restricted upgrade of the county's voting software. Sensitive passwords and other information were subsequently published online by right-wing figures.

The censure represents a rare public rebuke of a sitting governor by his own state party and reflects deep divisions within Colorado Democratic ranks. Jena Griswold, Colorado's secretary of state, condemned the decision, stating that it "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president." She warned that the clemency would encourage election denial movements across the country and leave "a dark, dangerous imprint on American democracy for years to come."

Griswold emphasized that Peters should have awaited resentencing through the courts, as ordered by the Court of Appeals, rather than receiving what she characterized as "special treatment" from the governor. She learned about Polis's decision only 45 minutes before it was announced publicly.

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, expressed that the group was "furious, disgusted, and deeply disappointed by the governor's decision." He noted that election officials across the country are now concerned that the clemency signals "it's open season on our elections and election officials."

When sentencing Peters in 2024, Judge Matthew Barrett stated: "You are no hero. You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again."

Polis had signaled in March that he was considering clemency, comparing Peters's case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for submitting forged letters to the legislature. However, Griswold disputed the comparison, noting that while both faced charges of attempting to influence public officials, Peters's actions directly compromised election security and attacked democratic foundations.

The censure carries symbolic weight but does not affect Polis's ability to govern. Trump, who had previously issued a federal pardon to Peters and instructed the Justice Department to seek her release, had repeatedly called for her freedom on social media.
