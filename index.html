The United States military conducted fresh strikes on Iranian missile launch sites and mine-laying vessels in southern Iran, describing the operations as defensive measures to protect American troops. The attacks occurred as Iranian negotiators traveled to Qatar to participate in peace talks aimed at ending the conflict.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio addressed the strategic importance of the Strait of Hormuz while speaking to reporters in India on Tuesday. "The straits have to be open," Rubio stated, adding that they "are going to be open one way or the other." He characterized Iran's actions in the waterway as "unlawful," "illegal," and "unacceptable" to the international community.

Rubio indicated that negotiations would require more time to complete, saying the deal language "could take a few days." He noted that discussions were occurring in Qatar and expressed the administration's willingness to proceed. "The president's expressed his desire to make it. He's either going to make a good deal or no deal," Rubio said.

Iranian officials arriving in Doha included the country's top negotiator and foreign minister, who met with Qatar's prime minister to discuss key issues. According to an official briefed on the discussions, talks focused on the Strait of Hormuz and Iran's stockpile of highly enriched uranium. Iran's central bank governor also attended to address the potential release of frozen Iranian funds as part of any final agreement. Foreign ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei stated that nuclear issues would only be negotiated after a framework agreement was established, noting that "we have reached a conclusion on a large portion of the issues under discussion."

President Trump has attached additional conditions to any potential Iran deal, stating that several Muslim-majority countries should join the Abraham Accords as part of the agreement. Trump identified Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Pakistan, Turkey, Egypt, and Jordan as nations that should "immediately" sign on to normalize relations with Israel. Saudi Arabia has previously maintained that Palestinian statehood must be established before it would consider normalization.

The simultaneous military strikes and diplomatic negotiations reflect the complex dynamics of the current situation. Oil prices showed mixed reactions to the developments. West Texas Intermediate crude fell more than 5 percent on Tuesday to $91.33 per barrel, while North Sea Brent crude rose 1.6 percent to $97.68 per barrel.

Both Iranian and American officials have tempered expectations for immediate breakthroughs in the talks. The involvement of Qatar as a mediator and its role in addressing frozen funds demonstrates the country's significant position in regional diplomacy. The timeline for reaching any final agreement remains uncertain as negotiations proceed alongside continued military operations.

Officials in Orange County, California have allowed approximately 65 percent of evacuees to return home after reducing the evacuation zone surrounding a chemical leak in Garden Grove. Authorities determined that the risk of a catastrophic explosion at the facility has been averted, though officials emphasized the crisis remains ongoing.

The incident began on Thursday at a facility owned by GKN Aerospace, where a storage tank holding methyl methacrylate, a highly flammable chemical used in resin and plastic production, began releasing gas. Local authorities initially responded with a hazmat team and ordered residents to evacuate, but lifted the order later that day. However, the situation deteriorated when damage to a valve on the tank created additional operational challenges that prevented complete mitigation efforts.

By Friday, the evacuation had expanded to cover approximately 40,000 people across six cities. Orange County Fire Authority Division Chief Craig Covey told reporters that the tank faced two possible outcomes: it could fail and spill 6,000 to 7,000 gallons of chemical into the surrounding area, or experience thermal runaway and explode, potentially affecting nearby tanks containing fuel or other hazardous materials.

"This thing is going to fail, and we don't know when," Covey said. "We're doing our best to figure out when or how we can prevent it."

Emergency crews created containment barriers using sandbags to prevent chemical spillage from entering storm drains, creeks, or reaching the nearby ocean. County Health Officer Dr. Regina Chinsio-Kwong warned that if the chemical heats up, it releases harmful vapors that can cause respiratory issues, eye irritation, nausea and headaches.

Garden Grove is located approximately 30 miles southeast of downtown Los Angeles and less than a mile from Disneyland, which was not placed under evacuation orders. The evacuation affected residents in Garden Grove, Cypress, Stanton, Anaheim, Buena Park and Westminster.

Some residents resisted leaving their homes, with Garden Grove Police Chief Amir El-Farra noting that approximately 15 percent of those facing evacuation orders refused to depart. The city established at least two shelters for those who evacuated.

As conditions improved and crews worked to stabilize the chemical tank, officials reduced the evacuation zone, enabling most residents to return to their neighborhoods. However, some restrictions remain in effect for areas closest to the leak site.

GKN Aerospace, a division of a British corporation that manufactures airplane engines and aircraft parts, owned the facility where the incident occurred. Emergency personnel continue monitoring the situation while maintaining safety protocols for the remaining evacuated areas.

Residents returning home were advised to follow local authority instructions and remain alert for further updates regarding the ongoing response efforts.

The company's IPO comes as SpaceX has established itself as the dominant player in commercial spaceflight, launching satellites for government and private customers and developing the Starship rocket system. The filing includes key financial metrics that investors will use to evaluate the company's trajectory.

The disclosure documents show SpaceX spending at levels typically associated with companies building large-scale AI infrastructure, rather than aerospace manufacturers. This spending reflects the capital-intensive nature of rocket development and the company's satellite internet constellation, Starlink.

The company faces competition from traditional aerospace contractors and newer commercial space ventures, though it currently dominates the market for launching payloads to orbit.

The IPO filing does not specify a target date for the stock to begin trading or how many shares the company plans to sell. Typical practice involves companies updating their filings with pricing and timing details as they move through regulatory review.

Google has fundamentally redesigned its search interface by integrating artificial intelligence directly into the product, marking the most significant transformation of the search box since the company's inception nearly 30 years ago.

Elizabeth Reid, who oversees Google Search, announced the changes at Google I/O, describing the shift as the biggest update in the product's history. The redesigned search experience now accommodates longer, more conversational queries than the traditional syntax Google users have relied on. Rather than returning a list of links, search results will increasingly feature visual elements and AI-generated summaries similar to the AI Overviews already available.

The updates, powered by Google's new Gemini 3.5 model, rolled out globally to desktop and mobile versions on Tuesday. Users can still access the original link-based search by clicking a "Web" tab if preferred.

Google has reported record-breaking search activity, with queries reaching an all-time high last month. Since introducing AI Mode a year ago, where users chat directly with a Gemini-powered bot, the company has seen queries to that interface double every quarter. When users upload photos, videos, or documents through Chrome's search bar, the system automatically activates AI mode.

The expansion reflects Google's broader strategy. Subscribers to AI Pro and Ultra plans will gain access to "information agents," autonomous AI bots capable of conducting detailed research and presenting findings or action plans. A new feature called Gemini Spark will access information from users' Gmail, Google Calendar, and other Google products to research topics, shop, and organize upcoming trips and appointments.

Google also unveiled a "generative user interface" that can create customized visuals and interactive dashboards based on user requests. Josh Woodward, overseeing Gemini's development, explained that the goal is building "a universal assistant that's personal, proactive, persistent."

The company reported that Gemini reaches 900 million monthly active users, still trailing ChatGPT's 900 million weekly users.

Beyond search, Google announced "intelligent eyewear" in partnership with Samsung, Warby Parker, and Gentle Monster. The voice-activated glasses, which respond to Gemini commands and feature embedded cameras, will launch in the fall. This marks Google's return to the eyewear market after shuttering Google Glass in 2015, which faced widespread ridicule.

The announcement comes as tech companies race to embed AI into core products. Industry observers view the redesign as a pivotal moment in how people access information online, though reactions remain mixed. Some critics argue the shift represents significant changes to how the internet functions, while others see it as an inevitable evolution of search technology.

The dominant victory Monday night capped a remarkable playoff run that has positioned the team to pursue its first championship since 1973.

The Knicks' path through the postseason has been relentless. After beating the Atlanta Hawks 4-2 in the first round, they swept the Philadelphia 76ers in the conference semifinals before dispatching Cleveland without losing a single game. The team is currently riding an 11-game winning streak, the third longest in a single postseason in NBA history.

Monday's clinching game was a blowout, with the Knicks winning 130-93. The game was essentially decided in the opening minutes when New York went on a 20-0 run late in the first quarter and early in the second. Karl-Anthony Towns led the Knicks with 19 points and 14 rebounds.

Jalen Brunson was named Eastern Conference Finals MVP after averaging 25.5 points and 7.8 assists throughout the series. His leadership proved crucial in steering the team through challenging moments. In Game 1, Cleveland held a 22-point fourth-quarter lead before Brunson orchestrated a thrilling overtime comeback. After Monday's victory, Brunson acknowledged his teammates' contributions. "It means a lot to get to the finals, but I wouldn't be here without my teammates," he said on the ESPN broadcast. "My coach, this staff, the fanbase, without them none of this is possible."

Teammate Josh Hart emphasized Brunson's importance to the team's success. "His leadership is vital for us," Hart said. "He's an even keel, he doesn't let us get too high or too low. Even games like this when we're up 15 or 20, he wants to make sure we're doubling down on our habits."

For Cleveland, the sweep represented a disappointing conclusion to the season. Despite Donovan Mitchell's 31-point performance, the Cavaliers could not mount a serious challenge. Cavaliers coach Kenny Atkinson acknowledged the Knicks' superiority. "They're playing better basketball. You got to give them credit. They're on a heater," Atkinson said.

The Knicks will await the winner of the Western Conference Finals, which features the reigning champion Oklahoma City Thunder against the San Antonio Spurs, led by superstar Victor Wembanyama. The series is tied 2-2 with Game 5 scheduled for Tuesday.

New York's return to the Finals has generated enormous excitement among fans. The team's first home Finals game on June 8 has already seen courtside seats priced at $105,000 on resale sites.

Sonny Rollins, one of the greatest saxophonists in jazz history, has died at the age of 95. His death was announced on his website on Monday, with his publicist confirming the news. He died at his home in Woodstock, New York, on Monday afternoon. No cause of death was provided.

Rollins emerged as a major figure during the bebop era and became known as "the Saxophone Colossus." Over more than six decades, he recorded more than 60 albums and collaborated with jazz legends including Miles Davis, Thelonious Monk, John Coltrane, and Dizzy Gillespie. His technical mastery and innovative approach to improvisation helped transform jazz from primarily a dance and ballad form into a startlingly expressive art form.

Born Walter Theodore Rollins in Harlem in 1930, he earned the nickname Sonny from his grandmother. Inspired by his siblings and jazz heroes like Louis Jordan and Fats Waller, he began learning saxophone at age seven. His high school band included future stars Jackie McLean and Art Taylor.

Rollins approached music intuitively, once describing himself as "primitive" and saying he went "with my feelings more than my brain." This willingness to break with convention helped define his role in jazz's evolution alongside Charlie Parker and Miles Davis. Davis himself noted that Rollins quickly became "a legend, almost a god to a lot of the younger musicians" for his aggressive, innovative style and fresh ideas.

His career faced setbacks when he struggled with heroin addiction. In 1950, he committed armed robbery to support his habit but eventually received treatment in 1955, which helped spark a creative renaissance. He released landmark albums including Saxophone Colossus in 1956, featuring the signature tune St Thomas, and Freedom Suite in 1958.

One of the most famous aspects of Rollins' legend involves his practice routine on the Williamsburg Bridge in New York. Between 1959 and 1962, he practiced for up to 15 hours daily on the bridge's pedestrian walkway, partly to avoid disturbing neighbors. This inspired his 1962 comeback album The Bridge.

Throughout his life, Rollins continued evolving his sound. He took another sabbatical from 1969 to 1971 to study yoga, philosophy, and meditation at an Indian ashram. Later, he incorporated funk, calypso, and R&B influences into his work. He also composed the 1966 film score for Alfie and added uncredited saxophone to the Rolling Stones' 1981 album Tattoo You.

Rollins remained socially conscious throughout his career, campaigning on climate change with benefit concerts and his 1998 album Global Warming. He married Lucille Pearson in 1965, and they remained together until her death in 2004. Rollins' death marks the loss of one of the last surviving figures from jazz's golden era.

Trump pauses deportation flights to Congo as Ebola outbreak spreads

May 21, 5:06 PM ET

The Trump administration has temporarily halted deportation flights to the Democratic Republic of Congo while an Ebola outbreak continues to spread in the country, according to reporting by Politico. However, public health experts say the pause will not prevent the disease from spreading and masks a more troubling reality: the administration is refusing to return people already deported there despite court orders.

The decision to pause new removals came after officials raised concerns that the flights could expose Immigration and Customs Enforcement personnel to the virus. Unnamed officials also told Politico that the flights could help spread Ebola closer to the United States. The administration is additionally motivated by legal concerns, as sending people to a country with an active Ebola outbreak could be used as a defense in deportation cases.

Adriana Zapata, a 55-year-old Colombian refugee, exemplifies the consequences of this policy. Immigration officials moved her to Kinshasa, the capital of the DRC, over a month ago even though the DRC stated it could not meet her complex medical needs. A U.S. judge ordered her return, but American officials now refuse to bring her back, citing the travel ban. "I'm just really worried about losing her," said Zapata's lawyer, Lauren O'Neal. "I don't want her to die before we can get her back here."

Jeremy Konyndyk, president of Refugees International and the former top Ebola response official at the U.S. Agency for International Development, questioned the logic of the administration's position. "By the government's own logic, if it is not safe for people to come from there to here, it is equally unsafe to send people there," Konyndyk said.

At least 37 people have been moved to countries affected by or near the Ebola outbreak in recent months, according to journalist Gillian Brockell, who tracks third-country removals. Brockell suspects the administration is using the travel ban as an excuse to avoid returning Zapata, noting that sending detainees to distant African nations serves as a threat to immigrants. She pointed out that the government has evacuated people from Ebola-affected regions before, including patients with active cases, and that an ICE contractor formerly with the State Department specializes in high-risk medical evacuations.

A Department of Homeland Security spokesperson said ICE "follows all applicable health and safety guidelines" when conducting removals but did not address questions about returning Zapata or whether flights to Uganda, South Sudan, and Rwanda would continue.

Immigration lawyer Camille Mackler warned that sending immigrants to other countries could violate international law. The CDC has announced that all passengers from the DRC, Uganda, and South Sudan will be diverted to Washington-Dulles International Airport for screening, and public health experts say this monitoring makes it unlikely that travelers will bring Ebola into the United States.

Jazz Saxophonist Sonny Rollins Dies at 95

12:00 AM ET

Sonny Rollins, one of the greatest saxophonists in jazz history, has died at the age of 95. His death was announced on his website on Monday, with his publicist confirming the news. He died at his home in Woodstock, New York, on Monday afternoon. No cause of death was provided.

Rollins emerged as a major figure during the bebop era and became known as "the Saxophone Colossus." Over more than six decades, he recorded more than 60 albums and collaborated with jazz legends including Miles Davis, Thelonious Monk, John Coltrane, and Dizzy Gillespie. His technical mastery and innovative approach to improvisation helped transform jazz from primarily a dance and ballad form into a startlingly expressive art form.

Born Walter Theodore Rollins in Harlem in 1930, he earned the nickname Sonny from his grandmother. Inspired by his siblings and jazz heroes like Louis Jordan and Fats Waller, he began learning saxophone at age seven. His high school band included future stars Jackie McLean and Art Taylor.

Rollins approached music intuitively, once describing himself as "primitive" and saying he went "with my feelings more than my brain." This willingness to break with convention helped define his role in jazz's evolution alongside Charlie Parker and Miles Davis. Davis himself noted that Rollins quickly became "a legend, almost a god to a lot of the younger musicians" for his aggressive, innovative style and fresh ideas.

His career faced setbacks when he struggled with heroin addiction. In 1950, he committed armed robbery to support his habit but eventually received treatment in 1955, which helped spark a creative renaissance. He released landmark albums including Saxophone Colossus in 1956, featuring the signature tune St Thomas, and Freedom Suite in 1958.

One of the most famous aspects of Rollins' legend involves his practice routine on the Williamsburg Bridge in New York. Between 1959 and 1962, he practiced for up to 15 hours daily on the bridge's pedestrian walkway, partly to avoid disturbing neighbors. This inspired his 1962 comeback album The Bridge.

Throughout his life, Rollins continued evolving his sound. He took another sabbatical from 1969 to 1971 to study yoga, philosophy, and meditation at an Indian ashram. Later, he incorporated funk, calypso, and R&B influences into his work. He also composed the 1966 film score for Alfie and added uncredited saxophone to the Rolling Stones' 1981 album Tattoo You.

Rollins remained socially conscious throughout his career, campaigning on climate change with benefit concerts and his 1998 album Global Warming. He married Lucille Pearson in 1965, and they remained together until her death in 2004. Rollins' death marks the loss of one of the last surviving figures from jazz's golden era.
