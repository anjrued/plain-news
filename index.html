"We were told to sprint to the press briefing room, where we are holding now," Wang added in her post.

Donald Trump said earlier that he was in the Oval Office but has not yet commented on the reported gunfire.

Julie Tsirkin, an NBC News correspondent, reported that the White House was on lockdown after what sounded like "approximately 20-30" gunshots.

Multiple news organizations confirmed hearing the sounds of gunfire in the area. The incident occurred Saturday evening while reporters were present near the White House grounds.

The Secret Service has not yet provided further details about the source of the gunfire or whether anyone was injured in the incident. The FBI confirmed it was on scene supporting the Secret Service response.

The lockdown of the White House remained in effect as authorities worked to investigate the situation and verify the reports from personnel on the ground near 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue NW.

Wang's video captured the moment the sounds interrupted her standup report, showing the sudden nature of the incident that prompted immediate security protocols. Reporters on the scene were instructed to take shelter in the press briefing room as a precautionary measure.

The incident comes as Trump was confirmed to be at the White House earlier in the day, though no statement from the president about the gunfire had been issued at the time of initial reports.

Authorities have not released information about potential suspects or the circumstances surrounding the reported gunfire. The investigation remained ongoing Saturday evening with both Secret Service and FBI personnel working to gather information about what transpired near the White House grounds.

10:20 AM ET

Astronomers have discovered a white dwarf star being torn apart by its binary companion in an extremely tight orbit that completes every 8.5 minutes. The finding represents one of the most extreme binary star systems ever observed.

The white dwarf is being gravitationally disrupted by its companion star as the two objects orbit each other at extraordinarily close range. The 8.5-minute orbital period is among the shortest known for any binary star system.

White dwarfs are the dense remnants of stars that have exhausted their nuclear fuel. They typically have masses comparable to the Sun but are compressed to roughly the size of Earth. In binary systems, white dwarfs can interact with companion stars in various ways, sometimes transferring mass or being distorted by gravitational forces.

The discovery sheds light on the extreme conditions that can exist in close binary systems. The gravitational interaction between the two objects is severe enough to physically deform and tear apart the white dwarf as it races around its companion.

Binary star systems where objects orbit this rapidly are rare and provide scientists with opportunities to study stellar physics under extreme conditions. The intense gravitational forces and rapid orbital motion allow researchers to test predictions about how matter behaves in strong gravitational fields.

The observation adds to the catalog of known binary systems and helps astronomers understand the range of possible stellar interactions. Systems with such tight orbits are thought to evolve over time, potentially leading to stellar mergers or other dramatic outcomes.

Further observations of the system will help scientists understand how the white dwarf is responding to the gravitational stress and how the binary pair might evolve in the future. The discovery demonstrates the variety of extreme environments that exist in the universe and the ongoing ability of modern telescopes to detect them.

Busch's family had announced his hospitalization earlier on Thursday, saying he wouldn't compete as planned at this weekend's NASCAR race in Charlotte, North Carolina.

During a NASCAR Cup Series race on May 10 at Watkins Glen, Busch radioed in to his crew requesting medical aid from Dr. Bill Heisel and a "shot" after the race. According to the TV broadcast, Busch had been struggling with a sinus cold that was exacerbated by the intense G-forces and elevation changes at the upstate New York road course. Busch continued to race and finished eighth.

Busch ranked 24th in the Cup Series standings, with two top-10 finishes in 12 races this season. The Las Vegas native won championships in 2015 and 2019. Busch was in his fourth season at Richard Childress Racing after winning titles with Joe Gibbs Racing.

Busch won 232 races across NASCAR's three national series, more than any driver in history. He won the trucks race at Dover on Friday, six days before his death.

In a joint statement, the Busch family, Richard Childress Racing and NASCAR said: "Our entire NASCAR family is heartbroken by the loss of Kyle Busch. A future Hall of Famer, Kyle was a rare talent, one who comes along once in a generation. He was fierce, he was passionate, he was immensely skilled and he cared deeply about the sport and fans."

The statement continued: "Throughout a career that spanned more than two decades, Kyle set records in national series wins, won championships at NASCAR's highest level and fostered the next generation of drivers as an owner in the Truck Series. His sharp wit and competitive spirit sparked a deep emotional connection with race fans of every age, creating the proud and loyal 'Rowdy Nation.' NASCAR lost a giant of the sport today, far too soon."

A polarizing figure known as "Rowdy" and "Wild Thing" for his post-race fights, regular feuds with other drivers and sometimes outlandish behavior, the multi-talented Busch stormed on the Cup Series scene in 2005 by winning rookie of the year.

Busch is survived by his wife, Samantha, and children Brexton and Lennix.

WHO Director General Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus announced the decision on Sunday following 88 deaths and more than 300 suspected cases linked to the outbreak of the B...

12:19 PM ET

The World Health Organization has declared an Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Uganda a "public health emergency of international concern" as cases continue to rise across the region.

WHO Director General Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus announced the decision on Sunday following 88 deaths and more than 300 suspected cases linked to the outbreak of the Bundibugyo virus. The declaration came before convening a formal emergency committee, with experts suggesting the speed likely reflects the gravity of the situation.

The outbreak has recorded 80 suspected deaths, eight laboratory-confirmed cases and 246 suspected cases in the DRC's Ituri province, which borders Uganda and South Sudan and is about five days' travel from the capital. Two cases, including one death, have also been reported in Uganda's capital, Kampala, apparently in people who had travelled from the DRC.

On Sunday, a laboratory confirmed a further Ebola case in the city of Goma, in a separate part of the DRC. "A positive case in Goma has been confirmed by tests carried out by the laboratory. It involves the wife of a man who died of Ebola in Bunia, who travelled to Goma after her husband's death whilst already infected," Prof Jean-Jacques Muyembe, director of the Congolese National Institute for Biomedical Research, told AFP.

The Bundibugyo virus is one of three strains that can cause Ebola virus disease and the least common. There are currently no approved vaccines or treatments for the strain, or specific tests. The WHO noted that "there are currently no approved Bundibugyo virus-specific therapeutics or vaccines. As such, this event is considered extraordinary."

Dr Jean Kaseya, director general of Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention, told Sky News: "Currently, I'm on panic mode because people are dying. I don't have medicines. I don't have [a] vaccine to support countries."

The WHO said signs "all point towards a potentially much larger outbreak than what is currently being detected and reported, with significant local and regional risk of spread." Conflict was continuing in the Ituri region, the organization said, with many people moving around and living in close quarters in urban or suburban areas, factors that can make spread more likely.

In Bunia, Ituri's capital, resident Jean Marc Asimwe told Associated Press journalists: "Every day, people are dying and this has been going on for about a week. In a single day, we bury two, three or even more people. At this point, we don't really know what kind of disease it is."

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention said on Sunday it was supporting interagency partners coordinating the safe withdrawal of a small number of Americans directly affected by the outbreak.
