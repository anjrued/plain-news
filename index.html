A 21-year-old gunman was shot and killed by federal agents after opening fire outside the White House on Saturday evening. The suspect, identified as Nasire Best, approached a checkpoint near 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue NW shortly after 6pm, removed a pistol from a bag, and fired at the location. Secret Service agents returned fire, striking the suspect. He was transported to a nearby hospital and later pronounced dead.

Court records show Best had previous interactions with the Secret Service. According to documents filed in DC superior court, Best was known to the Secret Service for "walking around the White House complex inquiring how to gain access at various entry points" on multiple occasions during summer 2025. In June of that year, he was involuntarily committed after obstructing vehicle entry to part of the White House complex. The following month, he was arrested for unlawful entry after ignoring warning signs and walking into a restricted area outside the White House.

During his July arrest, Best claimed to be Jesus and told officers "that he wanted to get arrested," according to court filings. A judge issued a stay-away order barring Best from the White House area. A bench warrant was later issued after he failed to appear at a subsequent hearing.

Law enforcement officials characterized Best as an emotionally disturbed person. President Donald Trump, who was inside the White House at the time, said the gunman had "a violent history" and a "possible obsession" with the White House. Trump praised the "swift and professional action" of law enforcement officials.

During the exchange of gunfire, a bystander was struck. It remains unclear whether they were hit by the suspect's gunfire or by shots fired by officers. No Secret Service agents were injured in the incident.

The FBI is assisting the Secret Service and local police in investigating the shooting.

Trump used the incident to advance his contentious ballroom construction plans for the White House. He stated it "goes to show how important it is, for all future presidents, to get, what will be, the most safe and secure space of its kind ever built in Washington, DC." The Justice Department has filed a request to resume the previously halted ballroom project, directly referencing the security incident in its legal argument. The attorney general's filing represents an effort to strengthen the administration's position for moving the construction forward, which had faced previous delays.

The White House was briefly locked down following the shooting. The incident marks the second known shooting at or near White House grounds in recent weeks, following a shooting at the White House Correspondents' Association dinner at a Washington hotel approximately one month earlier.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the attack targeted the city of Bila Tserkva in the Kyiv region with the Oreshnik missile and caused widespread destruction. This marked the third use of the Oreshnik in the conflict. Klitschko said damage was recorded in every district of Kyiv, with fires started at a school and people trapped in a shelter at a business centre.

Residents described horrific scenes. Svitlana Onofryichuk, who worked for 22 years at the market that was hit, told the Associated Press: "It was a terrible night and there has never been anything like it in the entire war. My job is gone, everything is gone, everything has burned down."

Yevhen Zosin, 74, said he rushed to grab his dog when he heard explosions. "Then there was another explosion and she and I were thrown back like a pin by the shock wave. My apartment was blown to pieces," he said.

Ukraine's National Art Museum sustained damage, with broken windows and shattered glass throughout the building, though staff said the collection itself was not harmed. Ukraine's foreign ministry building was damaged for the first time since World War Two, and government headquarters had windows blown out but no injuries.

The attack followed Putin's vow of revenge after accusing Ukraine of a deadly drone strike on a student dormitory in Luhansk, a Russian-controlled area. Ukraine denied responsibility and said it had struck a drone command unit instead.

International leaders condemned the strikes. French President Emmanuel Macron said the use of the Oreshnik signalled "the dead end of Russia's war of aggression." EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas called the attacks "abhorrent acts of terror" and described the hypersonic weapon use as "reckless nuclear brinkmanship."

Yesterday, 10:12 AM ET

A security flaw in Ghost CMS has been exploited to compromise more than 700 websites, including pages belonging to Harvard University and DuckDuckGo, according to multiple security reports. The vulnerability, tracked as CVE-2026-26980, is an SQL injection flaw that attackers used to hijack sites and deploy fake Cloudflare error pages containing malware.

The compromised websites were used in what researchers call a ClickFix campaign, where visitors are presented with deceptive prompts that appear to be legitimate system messages. When users interact with these fake error pages, they unknowingly trigger malware downloads or execute malicious code on their devices.

Ghost is an open-source content management system used by publishers and organizations to run blogs and websites. The SQL injection vulnerability allowed attackers to insert malicious database commands that gave them control over affected installations. Security researchers discovered the mass compromise as part of an ongoing investigation into ClickFix attack methods.

The scale of the campaign suggests attackers scanned the internet for vulnerable Ghost installations and exploited them systematically. High-profile sites like those associated with Harvard and DuckDuckGo were among the victims, though the attackers appeared to target any vulnerable Ghost installation regardless of the organization running it.

SQL injection remains one of the most common and dangerous web application vulnerabilities. These attacks exploit inadequate input validation in database queries, allowing attackers to manipulate or extract data, modify content, or gain administrative access to affected systems.

The ClickFix technique used in this campaign represents a social engineering approach where attackers leverage trust in familiar error messages and system prompts. By mimicking legitimate Cloudflare error pages, attackers increase the likelihood that visitors will follow instructions that compromise their systems.

Website administrators running Ghost CMS should verify their installations are patched against CVE-2026-26980 and check for signs of compromise. Users who visited affected sites and interacted with unexpected error messages or download prompts should scan their systems for malware.

The incident highlights ongoing security challenges for content management systems, which remain attractive targets due to their widespread use and the potential to reach large audiences through compromised sites.

Suarez's victory was his third of his Cup Series career and came after an 82-race winless streak. The win held special meaning given Suarez's history with Busch. Suarez had driven the No. 51 truck for Kyle Busch Motorsports in NASCAR's truck series in 2015 and 2016 before his Cup Series career.

The race at Charlotte Motor Speedway drew approximately 95,000 attendees who witnessed both competitive racing and tributes to the late driver throughout the day. The event, which is traditionally held over Memorial Day weekend, became an impromptu memorial service for the NASCAR community.

Suarez used a two-tire pit stop strategy during a rain-shortened race to hold off Christopher Bell and Denny Hamlin for the victory. After the win, Suarez paid tribute to his longtime friend. "This one is for him," Suarez said, dedicating the race to Busch.

Busch had been hospitalized earlier in the week with a severe illness, according to a NASCAR statement. No cause of death was initially given, though he had been dealing with health issues during the racing season. During a race on May 10 at Watkins Glen, Busch had requested medical assistance from the track doctor after struggling with a sinus cold that was worsened by the intense physical demands of the road course. He finished eighth in that race.

The Las Vegas native was a fixture in NASCAR for more than two decades. Busch won championships in 2015 and 2019 and had amassed 232 races across NASCAR's three national series, more than any driver in history. He was in his fourth season at Richard Childress Racing when he died. Just six days before his death, Busch had won the trucks race at Dover.

In a joint statement, the Busch family, Richard Childress Racing, and NASCAR described him as a generational talent. "A future Hall of Famer, Kyle was a rare talent, one who comes along once in a generation," they wrote. "He was fierce, he was passionate, he was immensely skilled and he cared deeply about the sport and fans."

Known by the nicknames "Rowdy" and "Wild Thing" for his competitive nature and sometimes controversial behavior, Busch burst onto the Cup Series scene in 2005 by winning rookie of the year. Beyond his driving accomplishments, he also fostered the next generation of drivers as an owner in the truck series.

Busch is survived by his wife, Samantha, and two children, Brexton and Lennix.

The soft opening suggests potential Star Wars fatigue among audiences. The Mandalorian and Grogu marks the first Star Wars theatrical release since The Rise of Skywalker in 2019, when Disney decided to pause new Star Wars movies after concluding the franchise had released too much content too quickly.

Disney believes the film will have stronger staying power in subsequent weekends due to positive audience scores, despite receiving mixed critical reviews. The studio is banking on the movie driving viewers to other Disney products, including merchandise, theme parks, and its streaming service.

Disney's theatrical strategy shifted dramatically after acquiring Lucasfilm, with the company focusing on launching multiple shows on Disney+ rather than theatrical films. Griswold disputed Polis' comparison between Peters' case and that of Lewis, stating that their actions and impacts were fundamentally different.
