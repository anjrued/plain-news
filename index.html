# Tulsi Gabbard Resigns as US Intelligence Chief After Turbulent Tenure

Tulsi Gabbard is resigning from her position as director of national intelligence, ending a rocky 15-month tenure marked by marginalization and controversy, according to reports from The Guardian and other news outlets.

In a letter to President Donald Trump, Gabbard announced she would leave her post on June 30. "While we have made significant progress, I recognize there is still important work to be done," she wrote. However, Reuters reported that the White House forced her resignation, citing a source familiar with the matter. Fox News attributed her departure to her husband's cancer diagnosis.

Trump had been asking cabinet members last month whether he should replace Gabbard, according to two people briefed on the discussions. In a statement on Truth Social Friday, Trump said, "Unfortunately, after having done a great job, Tulsi Gabbard will be leaving the Administration on June 30th." He announced that Aaron Lukas, principal deputy director of national intelligence, would serve as acting director.

Gabbard was largely sidelined from major national security decisions during her tenure. She was excluded from decision-making surrounding the seizure of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro in January and absent from key decisions about military strikes on Iran in February. Last June, Trump publicly contradicted her testimony to Congress that Iran was not building a nuclear weapon, declaring he did not care what she said and dismissing her assessment as "wrong."

The former Democratic congresswoman from Hawaii attempted to regain Trump's favor by calling for the prosecution of Barack Obama and several national security officials from his administration, alleging they conducted a "treasonous conspiracy" regarding Russian interference in the 2016 election. Obama denied the allegations. She also appeared at an FBI raid to seize ballots from the 2020 presidential election, far outside her foreign intelligence responsibilities.

Democratic Senator Mark Warner, vice-chair of the Senate intelligence committee, said he hoped her successor would ensure "the office remain grounded in facts, independence, and the rule of law." Senator Adam Schiff was more critical, stating, "She politicized intelligence. She dismantled critical agencies keeping Americans safe. She weaponized the Intelligence Community to pursue baseless election fraud claims."

Gabbard becomes the fourth woman to depart Trump's cabinet in just over two months, following Kristi Noem, Pam Bondi, and Lori Chavez-DeRemer.

Her appointment following Trump's November 2024 election victory had drawn criticism due to her history of repeating Kremlin talking points about Ukraine and her 2017 meeting with Syrian dictator Bashar al-Assad, where she told him Syria was "not an enemy of the United States."

# Eight Dead in Deadliest US Avalanche in 45 Years as California Rescue Continues

Eight skiers have died and one remains missing after an avalanche struck the Castle Peak area of California's Sierra Nevada mountains on Tuesday morning. Six other skiers who were stranded have been rescued, though two suffered injuries requiring hospitalization.

The avalanche occurred about 10 miles north of Lake Tahoe at approximately 11:30am on Tuesday, engulfing a group of 15 backcountry skiers. The group included four guides and 11 clients on the final day of a three-day skiing expedition.

"This is an ongoing incident and some facts do change," Nevada County Sheriff Shannan Moon said during a Wednesday press conference. She explained that while 12 people had signed up for the ski tour, "one person last minute decided to back out of that trip."

The incident now ranks as the deadliest single avalanche in the United States in 45 years. The previous deadliest avalanche occurred in 1981, when 11 people died on Mount Rainier in Washington state.

Rescue operations began after the ski tour company leading the expedition reported the avalanche to authorities. About 46 emergency first responders joined the search party, using a Sno-Cat vehicle to reach within 2 miles of where six survivors had constructed a makeshift shelter partly from tarpaulin sheets. Search and rescue volunteers skied the remaining distance to reach them.

The survivors had already recovered three bodies before rescuers arrived. First responders located five additional bodies but could not immediately transport them due to severe weather conditions. Two of the six survivors were "not mobile because of the injuries they survived during the avalanche," Moon said.

One injured skier was stabilized and released from the hospital, while the other remained under treatment for non-life-threatening injuries. Sheriff Wayne Woo of Placer County revealed that "one of the nine missing decedents is a spouse of one of our Tahoe Nordic search and rescue team members."

The avalanche struck during extremely dangerous conditions. Heavy snowfall, strong winds, and low visibility created what scientists at the Central Sierra Snow Lab called some of the worst conditions the region has experienced in years. The town of Soda Springs recorded at least 30 inches of snow during a 24-hour period.

The Sierra Avalanche Center had issued a warning before dawn on Tuesday about "high avalanche danger" in the ski region. Chris Feutrier, forest supervisor of the Tahoe National Forest, said the avalanche was "about a football field in length." The terrain has since accumulated another 3 feet of snow, meaning "the hazard remains high" for additional avalanches.

Nevada County Sheriff Captain Russell Greene questioned the ski company's decision to proceed with the expedition under such conditions. "I don't think it was a wise choice," Greene told the Associated Press, "but we don't know all the details yet."

# Kevin Warsh Confirmed as Fed Chair in Most Divisive Vote in History

The US Senate confirmed Kevin Warsh as chair of the Federal Reserve on Wednesday in a 54-45 vote that marked the most divisive confirmation for the position in history. The vote split along party lines, with Pennsylvania Democrat John Fetterman as the lone exception joining the Republican majority.

Warsh will officially take over on May 14 when outgoing chair Jerome Powell's term ends. He has been confirmed for a four-year term as chair and a 14-year appointment on the Fed's rate-setting board.

The new chair faces immediate challenges. Inflation has risen to 3.8%, and the Trump administration is pressuring the Fed to lower interest rates even as prices climb and war continues in the Middle East. Warsh has echoed President Trump's calls for lower rates, but he must convince the other 11 voting members of the Federal Open Market Committee to support such moves.

A White House spokesperson, Kush Desai, called the confirmation "a welcome step towards finally restoring accountability, competence and confidence in Fed decision-making."

The confirmation proved far more contentious than previous appointments. Before Warsh, the most divisive Fed chair confirmation was Ben Bernanke's 70-30 vote in 2010. Senate Majority Leader John Thune criticized Democrats from the Senate floor, saying "I wish I could say I'm surprised, but this is just how bad Trump derangement syndrome has gotten on the other side of the aisle."

Democratic Senator Elizabeth Warren, the top Democrat on the Senate banking committee, accused Trump of nominating Warsh to be his "sock puppet" to control interest rates. She wrote on X that "Warsh's confirmation = another step in Trump's attempt to take over the Fed. That's not good for working families – it's good for Wall Street."

Fetterman defended his vote, saying he believes Warsh will be "transparent and responsive to Congress and the public" and that "his promise to maintain Fed independence in setting interest rates is crucial."

Warsh previously served as a Fed governor from 2006 to 2011, where he was known as an "inflation hawk" who advocated for higher interest rates to combat inflation. He left the Fed board partly due to disagreements over the central bank's post-financial crisis stimulus package. Trump reportedly interviewed Warsh for the Fed chair position in 2018 but ultimately appointed Powell, a decision Trump now calls a "really big mistake."

During his Senate banking committee hearing last month, Warsh pledged to "take politics out of monetary policy and monetary policy out of politics." However, he refused to answer whether Trump lost the 2020 election, raising concerns among Democrats about his independence.

Powell announced he will remain on the Fed board as a voting governor, citing White House scrutiny of headquarters renovations as "pretext" for pressuring the central bank.

# Google Blocks Searches for "Disregard" Following AI Manipulation Issue

Google has temporarily blocked searches for the word "disregard" after its AI Overview feature appeared to malfunction when users searched for the term. The issue was spotted on Friday and quickly spread across social media, highlighting ongoing challenges with the company's AI-powered search features.

When users searched for "disregard," Google's AI Overview section displayed responses that looked like traditional chatbot outputs instead of the typical AI summary. The problem suggests the word may have been exploited in prompt injection attacks, where users embed commands in their queries to manipulate AI systems into ignoring safety guidelines or previous instructions.

The blocking of a common English word marks an unusual step for Google, which processes billions of searches daily without restricting specific terms. The company has not released an official statement explaining the decision or when normal search functionality will be restored.

The incident comes as Google dramatically expands AI integration across its search platform. At its annual I/O developer conference on Tuesday, the company announced what Elizabeth Reid, who oversees Google Search, called "the biggest Google Search had seen in its nearly 30-year history."

The updates introduce longer, more conversational queries powered by the new Gemini 3.5 AI model. Search results will feature more visual elements and suggestions to interact with Google's AI for creating calendar invites, spreadsheets, and other actions within Google's products. "Google Search is AI search," Reid said during the announcement.

Users can still access traditional link-based results by clicking a "Web" tab, but the changes push users toward engaging directly with Google's chatbot. Reid reported that Google Search queries reached an all-time high last month, and queries to the AI Mode interface have doubled every quarter since its debut a year ago.

For subscribers to AI Pro and Ultra plans, Search will include "information agents" that can perform in-depth research and return summaries or action plans. A feature called Gemini Spark will access information from Gmail, Google Calendar, and other Google products to help with research, shopping, and planning tasks.

Josh Woodward, who oversees Gemini development, described the goal as creating "a universal assistant that's personal, proactive, persistent." Google announced that 900 million people use the Gemini app monthly, though this remains below ChatGPT's 900 million weekly users.

The "disregard" blocking incident demonstrates the security vulnerabilities that emerge as AI becomes more deeply integrated into core search functionality. While blocking individual words is a blunt solution, it may serve as a temporary measure while engineers develop more sophisticated protections against adversarial inputs that attempt to manipulate AI behavior.

# NASA Opens JPL Management Contract to Competitive Bidding After 88 Years Under Caltech

NASA has announced it will open the management contract for the Jet Propulsion Laboratory to competitive bidding, ending an 88-year relationship with the California Institute of Technology that dates back to 1936. The decision marks a significant shift for one of the agency's most prominent research centers.

The Jet Propulsion Laboratory in Pasadena, California has been managed by Caltech under a single-source contract since its founding. JPL plays a central role in NASA's planetary science and deep space exploration programs, operating missions including Mars rovers, asteroid studies, and outer solar system probes.

NASA's decision to compete the contract opens the door for other organizations to bid on managing the facility. Caltech will be able to submit a proposal to retain management of the laboratory it has operated for nearly nine decades.

The announcement comes as NASA undergoes broader organizational changes under Administrator Jared Isaacman, who recently issued a memo outlining plans to restructure agency operations. The timing suggests the JPL contract competition is part of a larger effort to reassess how NASA manages its research centers and contracts.

JPL has been responsible for numerous landmark achievements in space exploration. The laboratory managed the Voyager missions to the outer planets, the Mars Curiosity and Perseverance rovers, and the Cassini mission to Saturn. The facility employs thousands of scientists, engineers, and support staff working on active missions and future spacecraft development.

The competitive bidding process will allow NASA to evaluate proposals from different organizations for how JPL should be managed. The agency has not specified the timeline for the bidding process or when a new contract would take effect.

The transition to a competed contract could affect JPL's operational structure, workforce, and relationship with NASA headquarters. The laboratory's role in managing some of NASA's highest-profile missions means the management change will be closely watched within the space science community.

NASA has not disclosed the reasons behind the decision to end the single-source contract arrangement. The move represents one of the most significant changes to NASA's institutional structure in decades, potentially reshaping how one of its premier research facilities operates.

The laboratory's management structure has remained relatively stable throughout its history, even as its mission evolved from early rocket research to managing complex robotic spacecraft exploring the solar system. How the competitive bidding process will affect ongoing missions and future projects remains to be seen.

The decision will likely attract interest from major research universities, private companies, and other organizations with experience managing large-scale scientific facilities. The outcome could set a precedent for how NASA approaches management contracts at its other centers.

The two-time Cup Series champion died Thursday at age 41 after being hospitalized with a severe illness. He had been testing in a Chevrolet racing simulator in Concord, North Carolina on Wednesday when he became unresponsive and was transported to a hospital in Charlotte, according to several people familiar with the situation who spoke to the AP anonymously.

The 911 call revealed that Busch experienced shortness of breath, felt he was overheating, and was coughing up blood before losing consciousness. No official cause of death has been announced by his family or Richard Childress Racing, his team for the past four seasons.

Busch was the winningest driver across NASCAR's three series in history. He won Cup Series championships in 2015 and 2019 with Joe Gibbs Racing. This season, he ranked 24th in the Cup Series standings with two top-10 finishes in 12 races. Just last week, he won a NASCAR Truck Series race at Dover International Speedway and had been scheduled to compete in this weekend's Coca-Cola 600 in Charlotte.

The racing world responded with an outpouring of grief and respect. Denny Hamlin, Busch's former teammate and a 22-year NASCAR veteran, wrote on social media: "Absolutely cannot comprehend this news. We just need to think of his family during this time. We love you KB."

Driver Ricky Stenhouse Jr. said: "I've raced against Kyle for a long time, and anyone who's lined up next to him knows exactly what made him special. He gave you everything he had, every single lap, and he made all of us better for it."

Known as "Rowdy" and "Wild Thing" for his fierce competitive nature and feuds with other drivers, the Las Vegas native won rookie of the year in 2005 after joining the Cup Series. His brother Kurt was also a successful NASCAR driver.

Dale Earnhardt Jr., once a rival who later became a friend, called Busch "one of the greatest drivers in NASCAR history" and credited him with making the effort to resolve their differences.

Hall of Fame driver Jeff Gordon praised Busch as "a fierce competitor who demanded the very best from himself each time he put on the helmet."

Fans honored Busch by donating hundreds of dollars to the Samantha and Kyle Busch Bundle of Joy Fund, a foundation the couple created in 2015 to support couples seeking fertility treatments. Many donations were for $18.08, referencing the number 18 and number 8 cars Busch drove during his 26-year career.

# British Grime Artist Dot Rotten Dies at 37

British rapper and producer Dot Rotten, a key figure in the grime music scene, has died at 37. The musician, whose real name was Joseph Ellis-Stevenson, reportedly died in the Gambia. His family confirmed his death to the BBC.

Born and raised in south London, Ellis-Stevenson began making music as a child. He was still a teenager when he released his debut mixtape, This Is the Beginning, in 2007. He quickly became recognized as one of the most important beat-makers in grime, a genre that was thriving in London at the time.

His tracks including Petrol Bomb and Bazooka provided distinctive heavy yet agile backings for MCs to rap over. He released seven volumes of his Rotten Riddims series along with numerous other mixtapes, establishing himself as a prolific creator in the underground scene.

Dot Rotten was also a skilled MC who wasn't afraid of confrontation. Over the years, he released combative tracks clashing with prominent grime artists including Wiley, P Money, JME, and Stormzy. These musical feuds were a common part of grime culture and helped build his reputation.

His career reached a wider audience after Mercury Records signed him in 2011. That year, he appeared on Ed Sheeran's No 5 Collaborations Project EP and contributed to Teardrop, an all-star charity record benefiting Children in Need. He was nominated for the BBC's prestigious Sound of poll in 2012.

Later in 2012, he released Overload, his only solo Top 20 hit. The track leaned toward dubstep and sampled Robert Miles' trance hit Children, showing his willingness to blend genres and experiment with different sounds.

His debut album for Mercury failed to achieve commercial success, leading Ellis-Stevenson to leave the label. He reinvented himself, changing his artist name to Zeph Ellis and shifting his focus back to production work. His instrumental track XCXD BXMB was used by rapper Kano for the hit song Garage Skank. He also co-produced I See You Shining, which became a Top 40 hit for rapper Nines.

Tributes poured in from the British rap community following news of his death. Lady Leshurr wrote on social media: "My jaw just dropped. Rest in perfect peace Dot Rotten, we've lost another GOAT [greatest of all time]."

Dot Rotten's death marks the loss of an artist who helped define grime's sound during its formative years and later successfully transitioned between underground credibility and mainstream production work. His influence on British urban music remains significant, particularly his contributions as both an MC and a producer during grime's crucial development period.
