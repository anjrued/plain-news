U.S. and Iran Close to Agreement Ending Conflict

Officials in Islamabad said a very basic "interim" deal could be reached as early as this weekend and that Tehran was reviewing a US pr...

6:10 PM ET

The US and Iran are close to a temporary agreement to halt the war in the Middle East, officials in Pakistan claimed on Thursday, as diplomatic activity gathered fresh momentum after a near breakdown of the current ceasefire earlier this week.

Officials in Islamabad said a very basic "interim" deal could be reached as early as this weekend and that Tehran was reviewing a US proposal. However, Trump and Pakistan have consistently suggested a breakthrough was imminent, and weeks of previous efforts to negotiate a permanent end to hostilities have made little real progress.

Despite many observers' skepticism and continuing defiance in Tehran, the possibility of even a partial agreement that could lead to the reopening of the strait of Hormuz sent global stocks to near-record highs on Thursday as oil prices dropped steeply. The strategic waterway in normal times carries a fifth of the world's oil and fossil gas supplies.

Pakistan has been the principal mediator in recent indirect contacts between Washington and Tehran after hosting a round of abortive face-to-face talks last month. "Both sides are now more amenable to suggestions, the distance between their proposals is reducing," said a diplomat in Islamabad with knowledge of the negotiations. "That's natural. They start off with maximalist positions and then soften."

On Monday, Iran launched missiles and drones at the United Arab Emirates when Donald Trump launched a short-lived effort to support shipping stranded by the Iranian closure of the strait of Hormuz. On Wednesday, the US military fired on an Iranian-flagged oil tanker hours after Trump issued a fresh ultimatum to Tehran.

Trump said in an interview with PBS on Wednesday he was optimistic about reaching an agreement with Iran before a scheduled trip to China next week. "I think it's got a very good chance of ending, and if it doesn't end, we have to go back to bombing the hell out of them," Trump told the broadcaster.

Trump also insisted that under any deal Tehran would "export" its highly enriched uranium, necessary for making a nuclear weapon, to the US, a demand that experts say Iran cannot accept.

A spokesperson for Pakistan's foreign ministry told a briefing in Islamabad on Thursday that "we expect an agreement sooner rather than later."

Control of the strait and the threat to restart attacks on nearby countries' oil and other infrastructure in the Gulf are the two main cards Iran can play in negotiations. The US has blockaded Iran, stopping all Iran-linked shipping seeking to exit the Gulf, to put pressure on Tehran.

The Iranian president, Masoud Pezeshkian, said on Thursday he had met the country's supreme leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, who has not been seen in public since his appointment in early March. Analysts saw the meeting as part of the attempt to align different factions and institutions within Iran behind a unified position for the negotiations.

The latest deal proposed by the US would be in two phases, with the initial accord ending the war and reopening the strait of Hormuz. Iran would also like to see its overseas assets unfrozen, including about $6 billion held in Qatar.

Yesterday, 10:07 AM ET

Consumer sentiment in the United States plummeted to a near record low in November 2025, driven by the ongoing federal government shutdown and mounting economic worries, according to the University of Michigan's monthly survey.

The consumer sentiment index dropped approximately 6% to 50.3 in November, down from 53.6 in October. The reading fell nearly three points below economists' expectations of 53.0, according to a Wall Street Journal poll.

This marks the lowest consumer sentiment since June 2022, when the index hit 50.0 amid pandemic-era inflation. The November 2025 reading represents the lowest level reported by the index since it began tracking data in 1978.

"With the federal government shutdown dragging on for over a month, consumers are now expressing worries about potential negative consequences for the economy," said Joanne Hsu, director of the survey, in a statement. "This month's decline in sentiment was widespread throughout the population, seen across age, income, and political affiliation."

The deteriorating sentiment comes during a blackout on federal economic data. The monthly jobs report, which was scheduled for Friday, was not released due to the government shutdown, forcing investors to rely on privately funded research reports instead.

ADP, the nation's largest payroll supplier, reported that private employers added 42,000 new jobs in October. While this exceeded expectations, it represents a dramatic slowdown from the three-month moving average of 188,000 jobs between November and January.

More troubling news came from Challenger, Gray & Christmas, an outplacement and executive coaching firm. The company reported that US-based employers announced 153,074 job cuts in October, a 175% increase from the 55,597 cuts announced in October 2024. This marked the highest level of October layoffs since 2003.

Alex Jacquez, chief of policy and advocacy at the thinktank Groundwork Collaborative, criticized the administration's economic policies in response to the Michigan survey results. "Americans are losing faith in the economy because they're losing ground. Every day it becomes clearer that President Trump has no real interest in improving the lives of American families," Jacquez said in a statement.

"His economic mismanagement has left households buried under record debt and rising prices. It's no surprise consumer sentiment is at its lowest point since 2022 and households are turning to leaders who didn't just learn the word 'affordability'," he added.

The decline in sentiment occurred across all demographic groups, suggesting widespread economic anxiety among American consumers regardless of their age, income level, or political affiliation.

The facility's current projects include missions to Jupiter's moons, asteroid sample return efforts, and development of next-generation propulsion systems.

NASCAR lost a giant of the sport today, far too soon."

Three Red Cross Workers Die of Ebola as Uganda Outbreak Grows

The WHO made its declaration on Sunday after 88 deaths and more than 300 suspected cases linked to the outbreak of t...

6:29 PM ET

An Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Uganda has been declared a "public health emergency of international concern" by the World Health Organization, with the situation prompting Africa's top health official to say he was "on panic mode".

The WHO made its declaration on Sunday after 88 deaths and more than 300 suspected cases linked to the outbreak of the Bundibugyo virus. Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, the director general of the WHO, announced the decision before convening a formal emergency committee at the organization.

The Bundibugyo virus is one of three strains that can cause Ebola virus disease and the least common. There are no approved vaccines or treatments for the strain, or specific tests. Ebola is a highly contagious disease spread via bodily fluids such as blood and vomit. It has a high fatality rate, particularly in low-resourced settings.

The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention announced the outbreak publicly on Friday, though it remains unclear when it began. Dr Jean Kaseya, the director general of Africa CDC, told Sky News: "Currently, I'm on panic mode because people are dying. I don't have medicines. I don't have [a] vaccine to support countries."

He warned that the outbreak affects everyone, saying: "Western countries, they don't understand that when Africa is affected, they are also at risk because people are flying every day."

There have been 80 suspected deaths, eight laboratory-confirmed cases and 246 suspected cases in the DRC's Ituri province in the east of the country, bordering Uganda and South Sudan. Two cases, including one death, have been reported in Uganda's capital, Kampala, in people who had travelled from the DRC.

The WHO said signs "all point towards a potentially much larger outbreak than what is currently being detected and reported, with significant local and regional risk of spread".

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention said on Sunday it was supporting the safe withdrawal of a small number of Americans directly affected by the outbreak. CDC officials said the agency had activated its emergency response center and planned to send more people to its offices in the DRC and Uganda.

On Sunday, a laboratory confirmed a further case in the city of Goma in a separate part of the DRC. "A positive case in Goma has been confirmed by tests carried out by the laboratory. It involves the wife of a man who died of Ebola in Bunia, who travelled to Goma after her husband's death whilst already infected," said Prof Jean-Jacques Muyembe, director of the Congolese National Institute for Biomedical Research.

Journalists in Ituri's capital, Bunia, interviewed locals who reported unusually high numbers of burials. "Every day, people are dying and this has been going on for about a week. In a single day, we bury two, three or even more people," said Jean Marc Asimwe, a Bunia resident.

1:52 AM ET

A fossil site in North Dakota appears to have captured evidence from the day an asteroid struck Earth 66 million years ago, ending the age of dinosaurs. Researchers have found tiny glass beads lodged in the gills of fossilized fish at the site, suggesting the animals died within hours of the impact.

The glass beads are believed to be impact spherules, formed when molten rock from the asteroid collision was ejected into the atmosphere and fell back to Earth. The spherules' presence in fish gills indicates the animals were alive when the material rained down. The site may provide a rare direct record of the immediate aftermath of the impact event, preserving a moment in the mass extinction event that reshaped life on Earth.
