Yesterday, 8:59 AM ET

Russia launched a major assault on Kyiv using its advanced Oreshnik hypersonic ballistic missile for the third time in the conflict, killing at least four people and injuring around 100 across the country.

The attack involved approximately 600 drones and 90 missiles of various types, with 36 ballistic missiles among them, according to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. The Oreshnik missile struck the city of Bila Tserkva in the Kyiv region. Zelenskyy stated that not all ballistic missiles were intercepted, with Kyiv bearing the primary impact of the assault.

The damage across the capital proved extensive. A water supply facility was hit, a market was burned down, and dozens of residential buildings sustained damage. Several schools were also targeted. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko reported two deaths and 56 wounded in the capital itself, while authorities in the surrounding Kyiv region confirmed two additional deaths and nine injured based on preliminary estimates.

Damage was recorded in every district of Kyiv. One school attack sparked a fire, while another strike on a business center trapped people in a shelter. The National Art Museum, which houses one of Ukraine's largest collections, sustained damage to ceilings, windows, and interior spaces, though officials said the collection itself remained unharmed. Ukraine's foreign ministry building was damaged for the first time since World War II, with nearby explosions causing light structural harm. The government headquarters also sustained damage, with windows blown out, though no one was injured.

Residents described harrowing experiences during the bombardment. One market worker who had been employed there for 22 years told the Associated Press the attack was unlike anything during the entire war, forcing her to leave Kyiv. A 74-year-old witness said he was thrown back by shock waves while trying to protect his dog, and his apartment was destroyed.

Russia's defense ministry confirmed using the Oreshnik alongside Iskander, Kinzhal, and Zircon missiles. The Russian government claimed the strikes targeted military command facilities, airbases, and military enterprises, describing the attack as retaliation for what it said were Ukrainian strikes on civilian facilities in Russian territory. Ukraine rejected accusations of targeting civilians and said it had struck an elite drone command unit.

International leaders condemned the assault. French President Emmanuel Macron called the use of the Oreshnik a sign of "the dead end of Russia's war of aggression." German Chancellor Friedrich Merz characterized it as a "reckless escalation." The EU's foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas described the strikes as "abhorrent acts of terror" and called the Oreshnik deployment "political scare tactic and reckless nuclear brinkmanship."

The attack followed Russian President Vladimir Putin's vow to retaliate for what Moscow claimed was a Ukrainian drone strike on a student dormitory in Luhansk that killed 21 people.

Lower oil prices typically benefit Japan's economy, which imports nearly all of its petroleum needs. The index's gains follow a period of volatility driven by geopolitical uncertainty. Traders had been closely monitoring developments in the Middle East, particularly any signs of progress in negotiations between Washington and Tehran.

Market analysts point to the potential peace deal as a catalyst for the rally. If tensions continue to ease and shipping resumes normal operations through the strait, energy markets could see sustained downward pressure on prices.

The milestone also reflects strong corporate earnings and continued strength in Japanese exporters. Technology and automotive stocks have contributed to the index's climb in recent months.

Investors remain cautious about the durability of the gains. While optimism about Middle East diplomacy has driven recent advances, any setback in negotiations could quickly reverse market sentiment. Crowdfunding for the game continues, with supporters able to purchase ships and other in-game items before the full release. Polis reduced her sentence to approximately four and a half years, making her eligible f...

May 21, 10:12 AM ET

Colorado Governor Jared Polis commuted the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former Mesa County clerk, sparking formal disapproval from his own party. Peters had been sentenced to eight and a half years in prison for allowing unauthorized individuals to access her county's voting equipment. Polis reduced her sentence to approximately four and a half years, making her eligible for parole on June 1.

Peters was convicted in 2024 of four felonies and three misdemeanors. In 2021, she allowed Conan Hayes, a former pro surfer affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, to access Mesa County's voting systems, copy them, and observe a sensitive software upgrade. Sensitive passwords and other information from the Dominion voting equipment were later published online by right-wing figures.

In his clemency letter, Polis wrote that Peters received "an extremely unusual and lengthy sentence for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes." However, Colorado election officials disputed this characterization. Jena Griswold, Colorado's secretary of state, said the clemency decision "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president." She warned that the decision signals election officials across the country that "it's open season on our elections."

Griswold noted that an appeals court had ordered a judge to reconsider Peters's sentence in April, but Polis granted clemency before that process concluded. "Instead of waiting for Peters to be resentenced in the courts, she will be released from incarceration in the coming weeks," Griswold stated. She learned of Polis's decision only 45 minutes before it became public.

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, called the decision deeply troubling. "We're starting to hear from election officials across the country that this signals that it's open season on our elections and election officials," he said.

Polis had signaled his intentions in March when he compared Peters's case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who was convicted of submitting forged letters and received probation and community service. Polis cited sentencing disparities as a consideration in his decision. Election officials rejected this comparison, noting that Peters's actions directly attacked election infrastructure and the democratic process itself.

Peters's case has become prominent among Republicans who continue to falsely claim the 2020 election was stolen. Former President Trump repeatedly called for her release and issued a federal pardon, though it carried limited effect since Peters faced state charges. Trump also instructed the Justice Department to attempt securing her release.

The censure from Colorado Democrats represents a rare public rebuke of a sitting Democratic governor from party leadership, highlighting significant internal disagreement over the clemency decision and its broader implications for election security.
