Officials declared the threat of a catastrophic explosion eliminated at a Garden Grove chemical facility in Orange County, California, after a cracked tank prompted a state of emergency and the evacuation of thousands of residents.

The chemical leak began when a tank at the facility developed a crack. Authorities declared a state of emergency as the situation posed an explosion risk to the surrounding community. Thousands of people were ordered to evacuate their homes as emergency crews worked to contain the hazard.

Officials confirmed the explosion threat has been eliminated, though the chemical leak itself required ongoing response efforts. The Orange County Register issued a correction stating that FEMA was not offering hotel stays to evacuees, contrary to earlier reports.

The incident occurred in Garden Grove, a city in Orange County with a population of approximately 170,000 people. The evacuation affected thousands of residents in the immediate vicinity of the chemical facility.

Emergency response teams worked to stabilize the cracked tank and prevent the release of additional chemicals. The nature of the chemicals involved was not specified in available reports, nor was the cause of the tank failure.

Authorities have not yet announced when evacuated residents will be permitted to return to their homes. The state of emergency declaration remains in effect as crews continue cleanup and assessment operations at the site.

The elimination of the explosion threat marks a significant development in an incident that posed serious danger to a densely populated area of Southern California. No injuries or fatalities were reported in connection with the leak or evacuation.

Further details about the timeline for residents to return home and the full scope of the environmental impact were not immediately available.

Yesterday, 10:12 AM ET

A security vulnerability in Ghost CMS has been exploited to compromise more than 700 websites in a widespread attack campaign. The flaw, identified as CVE-2026-26980, is an SQL injection vulnerability that attackers used to inject malicious code into affected sites.

The compromised websites include high-profile domains such as Harvard University and DuckDuckGo. Attackers used the vulnerability to deliver fake Cloudflare security warnings through a technique known as ClickFix. When visitors encounter these fake warnings, they are tricked into running malicious commands that can compromise their systems.

SQL injection vulnerabilities allow attackers to manipulate database queries by inserting malicious code. In this case, the flaw in Ghost CMS gave attackers the ability to modify website content and inject their own code into legitimate pages. The ClickFix technique specifically targets users by displaying convincing fake error messages that prompt them to take actions that install malware.

Ghost CMS is an open-source publishing platform used by thousands of websites for content management. The platform markets itself as a simpler alternative to WordPress, focusing on modern publishing needs. The widespread nature of this attack demonstrates how a single vulnerability in popular content management software can affect numerous organizations simultaneously.

Security researchers have documented the attack campaign and identified the specific vulnerability being exploited. The attacks appear to be part of a coordinated effort targeting Ghost CMS installations across the internet. Website administrators using Ghost CMS should check whether their installations have been compromised and ensure they are running patched versions of the software.

The incident highlights ongoing security challenges facing content management systems. As these platforms power millions of websites, vulnerabilities can quickly become targets for large-scale exploitation. Organizations running Ghost CMS need to implement security updates promptly and monitor their sites for signs of compromise. Visitors to affected websites may have been exposed to malware through the fake Cloudflare warnings displayed during the attack period.

Kyle Busch, a two-time NASCAR Cup Series champion, has died at age 41 following hospitalization with a severe illness, NASCAR announced on Thursday. The racing organization released a statement expressing deep sadness over the loss of one of the sport's greatest competitors.

Busch's family announced his hospitalization earlier that day, noting he would miss the scheduled NASCAR race in Charlotte, North Carolina that weekend. A cause of death was not initially disclosed in NASCAR's statement, though the family later confirmed he died from severe pneumonia and sepsis.

The Las Vegas native achieved considerable success throughout his racing career. He won NASCAR Cup Series championships in 2015 and 2019 and accumulated 232 wins across NASCAR's three national series, more than any driver in history. He claimed Rookie of the Year honors when he entered the Cup Series in 2005 and was in his fourth season with Richard Childress Racing after spending many years with Joe Gibbs Racing.

In his final weeks, Busch had been dealing with health challenges. During a May 10 Cup Series race at Watkins Glen, he radioed his crew requesting medical assistance after struggling with a sinus cold that was aggravated by the intense G-forces and elevation changes of the road course. Despite his condition, Busch finished eighth in that race.

In a joint statement, the Busch family, Richard Childress Racing, and NASCAR described him as "a rare talent, one who comes along once in a generation." They highlighted his fierce competitiveness, passion for the sport, and deep care for his fans. The statement noted that throughout his more than two-decade career, he set records in national series wins and fostered the next generation of drivers as an owner in the Truck Series.

Known by nicknames including "Rowdy," Busch was a polarizing figure in NASCAR, recognized for his aggressive driving style and competitive spirit. His personality created a devoted fan base referred to as "Rowdy Nation."

Busch is survived by his wife, Samantha, and two children, Brexton and Lennix. The couple were known advocates for fertility treatment, having funded in vitro fertilization procedures for numerous families through their charitable foundation.

The racing community mourned his loss deeply. Daniel Suárez, who had competed in Busch's truck series team in the past, honored his friend by winning the emotional Coca-Cola 600 race held the following Sunday, dedicating the victory to Busch's memory. NASCAR continued its season while the sport processed the sudden loss of one of its biggest names and a future Hall of Famer.

The Mandalorian and Grogu claimed first place at the box office during the Memorial Day weekend, though its performance marked a significant milestone for the wrong reasons. The film earned $102 million at the domestic box office and $165 million globally, representing the lowest opening for a Star Wars film since Disney acquired the franchise in 2012.

The movie, starring Pedro Pascal as a helmeted bounty hunter traveling the galaxy with a small alien companion known to fans as "Baby Yoda," builds on Disney's popular streaming series. Despite its connection to one of Disney's most-watched original shows on Disney+, the film's opening fell short of even Solo: A Star Wars Story, which launched in 2018 and earned $103 million domestically over the same holiday weekend.

This underperformance suggests possible Star Wars fatigue among audiences. The Mandalorian and Grogu marks the first Star Wars theatrical release since The Rise of Skywalker in 2019, when Disney paused Star Wars film production after determining the studio had released movies too frequently. The franchise has instead focused on Disney+ series, with varied results. While The Mandalorian and Andor have found critical acclaim and large audiences, other shows like The Book of Boba Fett, Obi-Wan Kenobi, and The Acolyte have struggled to gain traction.

Despite the disappointing opening weekend, Disney executives expressed confidence in the film's future performance based on positive audience responses, even as critics offered more measured reviews. The studio views the theatrical release as part of a broader strategy to determine which Star Wars properties can draw significant cinema audiences versus which perform better on streaming platforms.

According to industry analyst David Gross, while the numbers may not match primary Star Wars films, they reflect typical spin-off performance. "The movie is a piece of the story, and it's earning a piece of the business," Gross told Agence France-Presse.

The film's more modest budget of $165 million, compared to Solo's bloated $275 million production cost, could help it achieve profitability despite the lower opening. Disney also expects the film to drive engagement across other company properties, including merchandise, theme parks, and its streaming service. The Mandalorian remains the platform's most-watched original series, and Grogu merchandise has sold over 13 million units since the show debuted in 2019.

The Star Wars franchise has generated more than $10 billion in global ticket sales since its 1977 debut. Looking ahead, Disney plans to release Star Wars: Starfighter, featuring Ryan Gosling, in May 2027. The studio's theatrical strategy appears to be shifting toward selective releases rather than the rapid rollout that characterized the previous decade.

May 21, 10:12 AM ET

The Colorado Democratic Party censured Governor Jared Polis after he signaled his intention to commute the nine-year prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former county clerk whose case has become central to Republican efforts to question the validity of the 2020 presidential election.

Peters served as clerk in Mesa County in western Colorado during the 2020 election. She allowed an unauthorized person to access her county's voting equipment using a security badge. Sensitive passwords and information related to the election equipment subsequently became public and were used by election deniers to challenge the legitimacy of the 2020 results.

A jury convicted Peters in 2024 on multiple charges including attempting to influence a public servant, conspiracy to commit criminal impersonation, and first-degree official misconduct. She was sentenced to nine years in prison.

Polis signaled his openness to clemency on Tuesday by posting on social media that Peters's case raised concerns about sentencing disparities. He compared Peters's conviction to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who was convicted of the same felony charge of attempting to influence a public official but received probation and community service instead of prison time.

"Justice in Colorado and America needs to be applied evenly," Polis wrote. "This is the context I am using as I consider cases like this that have sentencing disparities."

The governor's statement prompted swift backlash from Democratic officials and election administrators. Jena Griswold, Colorado's Democratic secretary of state, countered that the cases were not comparable. While Lewis was convicted of one count of attempting to influence a public servant and three counts of forgery, Peters organized a breach of election equipment and undermined democratic processes, Griswold said.

"Her actions are still being used to try to undermine the 2026 election," Griswold stated. "She should get no special treatment by the Governor, and his statement is shocking and worrisome."

When asked by local media whether Polis should reduce Peters's sentence, no Democrat in the state legislature said he should. State Representative Karen McCormick called it "a ridiculous idea."

U.S. Senator Michael Bennet, a Democrat running for Colorado governor, also opposed clemency. "Tina Peters knowingly broke the law, undermined our elections, and was rightfully convicted by a jury of her peers," he said. "We can't capitulate to a lawless Administration. We have to stand strong for our institutions, our democracy, and the rule of law."

Dan Rubinstein, the district attorney who prosecuted Peters, warned that modifying her sentence would constitute a "gross injustice" to the citizens he represents. He noted that sentencing ranges exist precisely because the same offense can be committed in different ways with different consequences.

Peters remains the only person currently serving a prison sentence for attempting to overturn the 2020 election, according to her lawyer.
