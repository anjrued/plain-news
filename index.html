9:38 PM ET

Authorities in Orange County, California have ordered the evacuation of 40,000 people over concerns about a chemical leak that threatened to spill or explode.

The problem arose on Thursday at a facility owned by GKN Aerospace in the town of Garden Grove, where a storage tank holding methyl methacrylate began off-gassing and threatened to fail. The chemical, which is highly flammable, is used to fabricate resins and plastics.

Local authorities originally responded to the incident with a hazmat team on Thursday, ordering local residents to evacuate. They lifted the order later that day, but the problem worsened due to "damage to a valve on the tank" that "created additional operational challenges, preventing complete mitigation," Garden Grove authorities wrote in an evacuation order.

By Friday, new evacuation orders had expanded to residents in six cities. Schools shut down across the affected areas as the situation deteriorated.

"We have a tank that is actively in crisis," Orange County Fire Authority Division Chief Craig Covey told reporters at a news conference. "There are literally two options left remaining. One: the tank fails and spills a total of about 6-7,000 gallons of very bad chemicals into the parking lot in that area. Or two: the tank goes into a thermal runaway and blows up, affecting the tanks that are around them that have fuel or the chemicals in them as well."

Garden Grove Police Chief Amir El-Farra said some 15% of those facing evacuation orders were refusing to leave, according to the Orange County Register. The city has established at least two shelters for evacuees.

Covey, the county fire division chief, urged residents to take the order seriously. "We are setting up these evacuations in preparation for these two options: it fails or it blows up," Covey said. "Please follow our requests and orders for evacuations."

It's possible, however, that the tank could crack rather than explode. Emergency responders have laid out sand barriers to contain the chemicals in that event. "In a weird world, that's the best-case scenario, believe it or not," Covey said, according to the New York Times.

Officials have warned that the tank contains a highly volatile substance. The methyl methacrylate storage tank continued to leak the hazardous chemical throughout Friday as authorities worked to prevent a catastrophic failure.

GKN Aerospace is a division of a British corporation that produces airplane engines and other aircraft parts. The company's Garden Grove facility became the center of the emergency response as officials worked to manage the crisis.

The World Health Organization has upgraded its risk assessment for an Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with WHO Director-General describing the situation as spreading rapidly. The announcement signals growing international concern over the disease's transmission rate in the central African nation.

The WHO chief's statement marks an escalation in the organization's evaluation of the outbreak's severity. Risk assessment upgrades typically indicate that an outbreak has exceeded initial containment projections or that transmission patterns have changed in ways that increase potential for wider spread.

The Democratic Republic of Congo has experienced multiple Ebola outbreaks over the past decade, with varying degrees of severity. The country's eastern regions, which have faced persistent security challenges and displacement of populations, have proven particularly difficult environments for containing infectious disease outbreaks.

Ebola is a viral hemorrhagic fever that can cause severe illness and death in humans. The virus spreads through direct contact with bodily fluids of infected individuals or contaminated materials. Healthcare workers and family members caring for the sick face elevated exposure risks.

The WHO's decision to upgrade the risk assessment reflects the organization's role in coordinating international response to disease outbreaks. Such assessments inform decisions by national governments, aid organizations, and international health agencies about resource allocation and response measures.

Previous Ebola outbreaks in Congo have required sustained international support for containment. Response efforts typically involve contact tracing, isolation of confirmed cases, safe burial practices, and vaccination campaigns using available Ebola vaccines. The success of containment measures depends heavily on community cooperation and access to affected areas.

The current outbreak's rapid spread suggests challenges in one or more aspects of the response effort. Factors that can accelerate transmission include delayed case identification, insufficient isolation capacity, community resistance to health measures, or difficulty reaching affected populations due to security concerns or infrastructure limitations.

International health authorities will now assess whether additional resources, personnel, or coordination mechanisms are needed to support Congo's national response. The upgraded risk assessment may also trigger increased surveillance in neighboring countries to detect any cross-border transmission early.

Kevin Warsh Sworn In as Federal Reserve Chair

Kevin Warsh has been sworn in as chair of the US Federal Reserve, taking over one of the most powerful roles in the federal government that holds enormous sway over the economy. 2:16 PM ET

Kevin Warsh has been sworn in as chair of the US Federal Reserve, taking over one of the most powerful roles in the federal government that holds enormous sway over the economy.

The US Senate confirmed Warsh on Wednesday in a 54-45 vote that was split along party lines, with the exception of Democratic senator John Fetterman from Pennsylvania, who joined the Republican majority. It was the most divisive confirmation vote for the position in history.

Warsh was confirmed for a four-year term as chair and a 14-year appointment on the Fed's rate-setting board. He officially stepped into the role on 14 May, when the term of outgoing Fed chair Jerome Powell ended.

The new Fed chair is taking over leadership at a time when the central bank faces immense pressure from the Trump administration to lower rates, even as inflation climbs and war in the Middle East continues. With inflation rising to 3.8%, making the case for lower rates could be difficult.

A White House spokesperson, Kush Desai, called the Senate's confirmation of Warsh "a welcome step towards finally restoring accountability, competence and confidence in Fed decision-making."

Before Warsh, the most divisive Fed chair confirmation was in 2010, when Ben Bernanke was confirmed by a 70-30 vote.

Speaking from the Senate floor on Wednesday, John Thune, the Senate majority leader, derided Democrats for opposing Warsh's nomination. "I wish I could say I'm surprised, but this is just how bad Trump derangement syndrome has gotten on the other side of the aisle," he said.

Elizabeth Warren, the top Democratic member on the Senate banking committee, said Trump nominated Warsh to be his "sock puppet" to help him control interest rates. "Warsh's confirmation = another step in Trump's attempt to take over the Fed," she wrote on X. "That's not good for working families – it's good for Wall Street."

Fetterman, the lone Democrat to vote for Warsh, said in a statement he believes Warsh will be "transparent and responsive to Congress and the public." "His promise to maintain Fed independence in setting interest rates is crucial, and I look forward to working with him," he said.

An Ivy League economist and former Wall Street banker, Warsh previously served as a Fed governor from 2006 to 2011. During his time on the board, he was known as an "inflation hawk," advocating for higher interest rates to combat high inflation. He left the Fed board in 2011 due in part to disagreements over the Fed's post-financial crisis stimulus package.

At a hearing before the Senate banking committee last month, Warsh said he would maintain Fed independence and "take politics out of monetary policy and monetary policy out of politics." But he refused to answer whether Trump lost the 2020 election, raising alarm among Democrats.

Powell announced last month that he will stay on the Fed board as a voting governor.

The space agency recently announced a realignment aimed at accelerating mission delivery across its programs. JPL employs thousands of scientists, engineers, and support staff at its facility in Pasadena, California. The laboratory currently manages multiple active space missions and continues to develop new projects for future launches. The facility's work spans planetary science, Earth observation, and deep space exploration.

Caltech has not released detailed comments on the potential change in management structure. NASA has also not provided a timeline for any decision regarding JPL's future governance. The current management contract terms and any discussions about renewal or modification remain under review.

The laboratory has been integral to many of NASA's most visible achievements in space exploration. JPL teams have guided rovers across the Martian surface, sent probes to distant planets, and maintained spacecraft traveling beyond the solar system. The facility also plays a key role in Earth science missions that monitor climate and environmental changes.

Any change in JPL's management would require careful coordination to maintain continuity in ongoing missions and projects. The laboratory's unique position in the American space program makes its governance structure a matter of significant interest to the scientific community and space policy experts. The outcome of current discussions will shape the future of one of NASA's most important research centers.

8:18 AM ET

The James Webb Space Telescope has created the first detailed weather map of a planet located 690 light-years away. The observations captured morning weather patterns and revealed clouds composed of sand-like particles in the planet's atmosphere.

The findings have exposed what researchers describe as a significant bias in how exoplanet atmospheres have been interpreted for more than a decade.
