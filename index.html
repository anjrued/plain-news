5:22 PM ET

Efforts to contain a chemical leak in Orange County, California have shifted to a new approach as 50,000 residents remain displaced from their homes. The incident began Thursday at a GKN Aerospace facility in Garden Grove, where a storage tank holding methyl methacrylate started releasing gas and threatened to fail catastrophically.

Methyl methacrylate is a highly flammable chemical used in the production of resins and plastics. When authorities first responded Thursday with a hazmat team, they ordered evacuations but lifted the order later that day. However, damage to a valve on the tank created additional problems that prevented complete mitigation of the threat, and new evacuation orders were issued Friday.

Orange County Fire Authority Division Chief Craig Covey described the urgency of the situation at a news conference. "We have a tank that is actively in crisis," he said. "There are literally two options left remaining. One: the tank fails and spills a total of about 6-7,000 gallons of very bad chemicals into the parking lot in that area. Or two: the tank goes into a thermal runaway and blows up, affecting the tanks that are around them that have fuel or the chemicals in them as well."

Covey emphasized the unpredictability of the threat, stating "This thing is going to fail, and we don't know when."

If the chemical is exposed to high heat, it releases a vapor that poses health risks to residents. County Health Officer Dr Regina Chinsio-Kwong warned that exposure can cause respiratory issues, eye irritation, nausea and headaches.

Crews have constructed containment barriers using sandbags to prevent any chemical spill from reaching storm drains, nearby creeks or the ocean. The evacuation orders cover Garden Grove and five surrounding cities: Cypress, Stanton, Anaheim, Buena Park and Westminster. Garden Grove is located about 30 miles southeast of downtown Los Angeles and less than a mile from Disneyland's theme parks, which remain open and were not subject to evacuation orders.

Despite the evacuation orders, some residents have refused to leave. According to reports, approximately 15 percent of people facing evacuation orders would not comply with the directive. Garden Grove police and city officials have established shelters for displaced residents.

Covey urged residents to heed the evacuation warnings. "We are setting up these evacuations in preparation for these two options: it fails or it blows up," he said. "Please follow our requests and orders for evacuations."

GKN Aerospace is a division of a British corporation that manufactures airplane engines and aircraft components. Authorities have not yet determined the cause of the initial tank failure and said investigations will begin once the immediate threat has been contained.

Rescue operations began immediately following the blast, with emergency workers transporting the injured to nearby medical facilities. The exact number of wounded has not been confirmed, though officials are continuing to assess the full scope of casualties.

Balochistan separatist groups have conducted numerous attacks over the years, pursuing greater autonomy or independence from the central government. This attack represents one of the deadliest incidents targeting civilian transportation in Pakistan in recent months. Railway officials have periodically strengthened security measures along vulnerable routes, implementing armed escorts and increased surveillance.

Pakistan's railway system serves millions of passengers annually across the country. Investigators have been examining the explosion site to determine whether the blast resulted from an improvised explosive device or other means. The attack reflects broader security concerns in Pakistan, where militant violence has surged in recent months. Pakistan's government has consistently worked to address security threats through military operations and law enforcement efforts. May 21, 10:12 AM ET

The Colorado Democratic Party censured Governor Jared Polis for commuting the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former county clerk whose case has become a flashpoint in ongoing disputes about the 2020 election.

Polis reduced Peters' sentence from eight and a half years to approximately four and a half years. She is scheduled for release on parole on June 1. In his clemency letter, Polis wrote that the sentence was "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes."

Peters was convicted in 2024 of four felonies and three misdemeanors. In 2021, while serving as clerk in Mesa County, Peters allowed an individual named Conan Hayes to access the county's voting equipment, copy it, and attend a sensitive software upgrade. Hayes is affiliated with Mike Lindell, CEO of MyPillow and a prominent figure in spreading false claims about the 2020 election. Sensitive passwords and information from the county's voting systems were subsequently published online by right-wing personalities.

State election officials sharply criticized the decision. Jena Griswold, Colorado's secretary of state, called the clemency a validation of "Trump's basest impulses" that would "embolden the election denial movement across the country." She said the decision signals that tampering with elections carries minimal consequences.

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, said the group was "furious, disgusted, and deeply disappointed." He noted that election officials nationwide are now concerned the decision suggests "it's open season on our elections and election officials."

Griswold disputed Polis' earlier comparison of Peters' case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for submitting forged letters during a legislative inquiry. While both faced some overlapping felony charges, Griswold said the cases were fundamentally different. "Peters organized the breach of the election equipment, broke the public trust and attacked the very foundations of our democratic process," she stated.

The original judge, Matthew Barrett, had sentenced Peters harshly in 2024, telling her "You are no hero. You're a charlatan." However, an appeals court overturned the sentence in April and ordered reconsideration.

Peters has become a rallying figure for those claiming the 2020 election was stolen, despite these claims being repeatedly debunked. Former President Donald Trump, who issued a federal pardon for Peters that had limited effect since she faces state charges, has repeatedly called for her release and criticized both Polis and local prosecutors.

Polis, a Democrat in his second term, had remained noncommittal about clemency for months before announcing the commutation on March 3. The censure represents a significant rift between the state party leadership and its sitting governor, signaling disapproval of his decision.
