A coal mine explosion in China has killed 90 people, marking the deadliest mining disaster in the country since 2009. The blast occurred at a mine facility, though the exact location has not been specified in initial reports.

The death toll makes this the most severe coal mining accident China has experienced in over 15 years. The country's coal mining industry has historically faced safety challenges, with previous major incidents claiming dozens of lives.

China remains the world's largest coal producer and consumer, with thousands of mines operating across the country. The industry employs millions of workers and supplies the majority of the nation's energy needs.

Authorities have not yet released information about the cause of the explosion or whether any miners remain trapped underground. The scale of the casualty count suggests the blast occurred in a section of the mine where many workers were present at the time.

Previous major mining disasters in China have typically led to safety reviews and regulatory changes. A 2009 incident resulted in similar casualties and prompted government oversight reforms in the mining sector.

Coal mine explosions are often caused by buildups of methane gas or coal dust in underground shafts, which can ignite from sparks or equipment malfunctions. Modern safety protocols require gas monitoring systems and ventilation to prevent such accumulations.

The mining industry has seen improvements in safety standards over the past two decades, with the Chinese government implementing stricter regulations following a series of deadly accidents in the early 2000s. Annual mining fatalities in China have decreased significantly since that period, though incidents continue to occur.

This explosion represents a significant setback for workplace safety in Chinese coal operations and raises questions about enforcement of existing safety measures at the affected facility.

In a letter to the US president, she said she would resign and leave her post on 30 June. "While we have made significant progress … I recognize there is still important work to be done," she wrote.

The White House forced Gabbard to resign, the Reuters news agency reported, citing a source familiar with the issue. However, a spokesperson for Gabbard's office said rumors that Gabbard had been forcibly ousted from office were "100% false".

In her resignation letter, Gabbard said her husband, Abraham, "has recently been diagnosed with an extremely rare form of bone cancer". She added: "At this time, I must step away from public service to be by his side and fully support him through this battle."

Gabbard was an unconventional choice for the role. The former Democrat lacked an intelligence background and her foreign policy views at times diverged with Trump's, particularly on questions of overseas military intervention. She also took unusual measures to ingratiate herself to the president by vowing to root out politicization across US spy agencies and embracing his agenda of election denial.

Over time, Gabbard became increasingly sidelined by the president, who excluded her from key national security conversations on Iran and Venezuela, according to people familiar with the office of the director of national intelligence.

Trump was asking cabinet members last month whether he should replace Gabbard, according to two people briefed on the discussions.

"Unfortunately, after having done a great job, Tulsi Gabbard will be leaving the Administration on June 30th," Trump wrote in a statement on his Truth Social platform on Friday. Gabbard "has done an incredible job, and we will miss her", the president said, adding that Aaron Lukas, principal deputy director of national intelligence, would serve as acting director of national intelligence.

Gabbard's tumultuous 15-month tenure as the nation's top intelligence official ended Friday when she submitted her resignation. Over those 15 months, the ex-Democrat without an intelligence background took actions seemingly meant to flatter Trump.

The resignation marks another departure from Trump's cabinet during a period of significant foreign policy activity, including ongoing operations against Iran and Venezuela. Her exclusion from key national security conversations about these operations highlighted the tensions within the administration regarding her role.

Aaron Lukas will now take over as acting director of national intelligence as the administration continues to handle multiple international crises and military operations.

The song's infectious beat and Base's call-and-response style made it a staple at parties and sporting events for decades. Its hook "It takes two to make a thing go right, it takes two to make it out of sight" became embedded in popular culture, frequently sampled and referenced by other artists.

Base and DJ E-Z Rock, who died in 2014, released their debut album of the same name in 1988. While they never replicated the massive success of their signature hit, Base continued performing the song at shows throughout his career.

Born in Harlem, New York, Base began his music career in the mid-1980s during hip-hop's formative years. His work came during a period when the genre was transitioning from underground phenomenon to mainstream cultural force.

"It Takes Two" has been featured in numerous films, television shows, and commercials over the years. The song has been sampled by artists across multiple genres and remains a fixture on oldies and hip-hop radio stations.

Base's contribution to hip-hop extended beyond chart success. The song's production techniques and sampling methods influenced subsequent generations of producers and helped establish hip-hop's place in mainstream American music.

After his initial success, Base released additional albums in the 1990s and early 2000s, though none achieved the commercial impact of his debut. He remained active on the nostalgia circuit, performing at hip-hop revival shows and festivals celebrating 1980s music.

Base is survived by family members whose names have not been publicly released. Tributes from fellow artists and fans have been appearing on social media since news of his death broke.
