Yesterday, 11:36 PM ET

Donald Trump announced on Saturday that a peace deal with Iran "has been largely negotiated" following discussions with Pakistani mediators, Gulf allies, and Israel. The agreement could potentially end the war launched by the United States and Israel in February.

Writing on his social media platform, Trump stated that "final aspects and details" of a "memorandum of understanding" were still under discussion and would be "announced shortly." He indicated the Strait of Hormuz would reopen as part of the deal.

"An agreement has been largely negotiated, subject to finalization between the United States of America, the Islamic Republic of Iran, and the various other Countries," Trump posted.

However, Iran's Fars news agency, which maintains close ties to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, disputed Trump's characterization. The outlet reported that the Strait of Hormuz would remain under Iranian control, describing this as a red line for the United States. Fars stated that "the management of the Strait, determining the route, time, method of passage, and issuing permits will continue to be the monopoly and discretion of the Islamic Republic of Iran." The agency called Trump's assertion that an agreement was nearly finalized "inconsistent with reality."

Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif later congratulated Trump on his peace efforts and expressed hope to host another round of talks between Washington and Tehran "very soon." Pakistan's army chief, Syed Asim Munir, has emerged as a key figure in negotiations, recently meeting with Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian and parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf in Tehran.

According to a regional official with direct knowledge of the mediation efforts, the potential deal would include an official declaration ending the war, followed by two months of negotiations on Iran's nuclear program. It would also involve Iran opening the crucial shipping lane and the United States ending its blockade of Iranian ports.

Three senior Iranian officials told The New York Times the agreement would halt fighting in Iran and Lebanon and could release $25 billion in frozen Iranian assets overseas, with nuclear negotiations to be completed within 30 to 60 days.

Trump said he had held phone discussions with leaders from Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Egypt, Jordan, Bahrain, Pakistan, and Turkey. "Separately, I had a call with Prime Minister Bibi Netanyahu, of Israel, which, likewise, went very well," Trump stated.

The announcement drew criticism from Republican hawks. Mike Pompeo, who served as CIA director and secretary of state during Trump's first term, denounced the rumored terms as too similar to the Obama-era nuclear deal. Trump had withdrawn from that international agreement, known as the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action, in 2018.

A gunman died after approaching a White House security checkpoint and opening fire on officers, federal officials confirmed Saturday. The incident prompted a brief lockdown of the White House, where President Donald Trump was present at the time.

The shooting occurred shortly after 6pm local time when a man approached a checkpoint at a White House gate near 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue NW. According to the Secret Service, the suspect drew a pistol and fired at officers. Secret Service personnel returned fire, striking the gunman, who was transported to a hospital where he later died.

A bystander was also shot during the incident. The Secret Service stated it remains unclear whether the bystander was struck by the suspect's initial gunfire or during the subsequent exchange between the gunman and officers. No Secret Service personnel were injured.

The sustained volley of gunshots sent journalists in the area running for cover. Selina Wang, a White House correspondent for ABC News, shared video on social media showing the moment gunfire interrupted her as she recorded a report. In the clip, Wang dove for cover as what sounded like dozens of shots rang out. Journalists were told to sprint to the press briefing room for safety.

Chris Flanagan, an anchor for DC News Now who took cover in the White House briefing room, reported that about 30 shots were fired. A CBS News producer estimated at least 20 shots had been fired. The security lockdown was later lifted.

Hours after the incident, Trump praised the swift and professional action of law enforcement officials in a social media post. He noted the incident came a month after a shooting at the White House Correspondents' Association dinner at a Washington hotel, adding that it shows how important it is for all future presidents to get the most safe and secure space of its kind ever built in Washington, in an apparent reference to his ballroom plans.

The suspect has not been officially named, but multiple US media outlets reported he had a history of mental health issues. According to these reports, he had been arrested last year trying to enter via a different White House checkpoint without authorization and had been issued a stay away order.

FBI director Kash Patel posted that the FBI was on the scene supporting the Secret Service response to shots fired near White House grounds.

The shooting comes less than three weeks after another incident in which a man brandishing a gun fired at a Secret Service officer near the route of JD Vance's motorcade outside the White House. In that incident, a civilian witness was wounded in the leg, and officers returned fire, striking the suspect.

SpaceX has filed for an initial public offering, with documents showing the company is betting its future on its Starlink satellite internet business rather than its rocket operations. The filing provides a rare look into the priorities of Elon Musk's space company as it prepares to go public.

The IPO filing indicates that SpaceX views Starlink, its satellite-based internet service, as its primary growth driver. The company has been deploying thousands of satellites into low Earth orbit to provide broadband internet access globally, particularly to underserved areas.

Separately, SpaceX conducted the first flight of its Starship V3 vehicle on Saturday. The test flight was described as mostly successful, though the company characterized the rocket as still being a work in progress. The Starship program is designed to be a fully reusable transportation system for missions to the Moon, Mars, and beyond.

The timing of the IPO filing comes as SpaceX continues to expand both its launch services and its Starlink network. The company has been launching regular batches of Starlink satellites while also conducting commercial and government launches through its Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy rockets.

Starlink currently operates thousands of satellites and serves customers across multiple continents. The service has gained particular attention for its ability to provide internet access in remote locations and during natural disasters when traditional infrastructure fails.

The shift in focus toward Starlink as the primary business represents a strategic pivot for a company long associated with ambitious rocket development. While SpaceX will continue its launch operations, the IPO filing signals that the satellite internet service is expected to drive the majority of future revenue growth.

The filing does not specify a timeline for the public offering or an initial valuation target. SpaceX has been one of the most valuable private companies in the world, with previous funding rounds valuing it at over $100 billion.

Investors will be watching closely to see how SpaceX balances its ongoing rocket development programs with the capital-intensive expansion of the Starlink constellation. The company continues to develop Starship while maintaining its existing launch business and scaling up satellite production and deployment.

Newly confirmed NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman has released a comprehensive memo outlining his vision for the space agency's future direction. The billionaire private astronaut was confirmed by the U.S. Senate in a 67-30 vote, becoming the 15th leader of NASA under President Donald Trump's administration.

Isaacman, a strong advocate of Mars missions and ally of SpaceX CEO Elon Musk, told senators during his second confirmation hearing that NASA must accelerate its efforts to beat China back to the moon this decade. His confirmation came after an unusual process in which Trump initially nominated him, then withdrew the nomination, before naming him again for the position.

The new administrator made his fortune founding Shift4, a payment processing startup that now handles billions of transactions annually. His credentials include flying on two SpaceX missions, both self-funded with all-civilian crews. The most recent mission in 2024 featured the first spacewalk conducted by a private company.

Acting NASA chief Sean Duffy, who also leads the U.S. Transportation Department, congratulated Isaacman on social media and wished him success "as he begins his tenure and leads NASA as we go back to the Moon in 2028 and beat China." Duffy had been pushing to move NASA under his department's permanent control, an effort that ended with Isaacman's confirmation.

The consolidation attempt sparked conflict with Musk around the same time Duffy stated that SpaceX had fallen behind on its NASA contracts. The transportation secretary looked to competitors, particularly Jeff Bezos's Blue Origin, for alternatives.

Isaacman's path to confirmation faced obstacles earlier this year. Trump withdrew his nomination in May during a public dispute with Musk, posting on Truth Social that Isaacman's donations to Democratic candidates were behind the decision. However, Isaacman re-emerged as a candidate in recent months and, according to the Wall Street Journal, enlisted allies within the administration to support his appointment.

The confirmation places a private space industry veteran at the helm of NASA during a critical period. The agency faces pressure to maintain American leadership in space exploration while competing with China's growing space program. Isaacman's experience with commercial spaceflight and his connections to the private space sector position him uniquely to navigate the relationship between NASA and commercial partners like SpaceX.

With the 2028 moon landing goal established, Isaacman now faces the challenge of translating his vision into action while managing the agency's complex relationships with both government entities and private space companies.

Yesterday, 8:13 PM ET

Romanian director Cristian Mungiu has won the Palme d'Or at the Cannes Film Festival for his English-language debut "Fjord," making him only the tenth filmmaker to receive the prestigious award twice. This victory comes nineteen years after his abortion drama "4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days" won the same honor.

The film stars Renate Reinsve and Sebastian Stan as Romanian religious parents who relocate to Norway, where they face accusations of child abuse. With this win, the 58-year-old director joins an exclusive group of two-time Palme d'Or winners that includes Francis Ford Coppola, Michael Haneke, Ken Loach, and Ruben Östlund, among others.

US distributor Neon has already acquired "Fjord," marking their seventh consecutive year distributing the top prize winner from Cannes. The company's previous acquisition, Sean Baker's "Anora," won the Palme two years ago before sweeping the 2025 Oscars.

The 79th Cannes Film Festival was described as slightly muted, marked by disappointing returns from various auteurs and a notable absence of Hollywood glamour. The two American films in competition, James Gray's "Paper Tiger" starring Adam Driver and Scarlett Johansson, and Ira Sachs' AIDS musical "The Man I Love" starring Rami Malek, were both overlooked by the judges.

Russian director Andrey Zvyagintsev's "Minotaur," a dark satire about corruption and infidelity in contemporary Russia, won the runner-up Grand Prix. Valeska Grisebach's "The Dreamed Adventure," a Bulgaria-set drama about an archaeologist, took third place with the Jury prize.

The director award was shared between Pawel Pawlikowski for "Fatherland" and Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi for "The Black Ball." Both acting awards were split between pairs of performers. Virginie Efira and Tao Okamoto shared the best actress prize for Ryusuke Hamaguchi's care drama "All of a Sudden," while Valentin Campagne and Emmanuel Macchia won best actor for the queer World War I drama "Coward."

Park Chan-wook presided over this year's jury, which included Demi Moore, Stellan Skarsgård, Chloé Zhao, and Paul Laverty. During the closing ceremony, Isabelle Huppert paid special tribute to Barbra Streisand, one of three honorary Palme d'Or recipients this year alongside Peter Jackson and John Travolta. Streisand was unable to attend due to a knee injury but sent a video message. "In this crazy, volatile world that seems more fractured every day, it's reassuring to see the compelling movies at this festival, by artists from many countries," Streisand said. "Film has that magical ability to unite us, opening our hearts and minds."

Secret Service Fatally Shoots Person Near White House

A shooting near the White House on Monday left one person dead and a juvenile bystander injured after an armed individual opened fire on Secret Service agents. According to Matthew Quinn, deputy director of t...

12:30 AM ET

A shooting near the White House on Monday left one person dead and a juvenile bystander injured after an armed individual opened fire on Secret Service agents.

The incident occurred around 15th Street and Independence Avenue, near the Washington Monument, shortly after Vice President JD Vance's motorcade passed through the area. According to Matthew Quinn, deputy director of the Secret Service, plainclothes officers first identified the individual as looking suspicious and believed he was carrying a firearm.

The plainclothes officers called for support from uniformed agents. When the individual saw the uniformed officers approaching, he fled on foot, drew his weapon, and began firing at the agents, Quinn said.

"They returned fire and engaged," Quinn told reporters. "That individual was hit, [and] he's since been transported to the hospital."

Multiple news outlets later confirmed that the suspect died from his injuries. Quinn initially declined to comment on the person's condition immediately following the shooting.

A juvenile bystander was also shot during the exchange of gunfire but did not sustain life-threatening injuries, according to Quinn. The bystander received treatment at a hospital. Officials said it remains unclear whether the bystander was hit by gunfire from the suspect or from Secret Service agents.

The shooting prompted a brief lockdown of the White House, where President Donald Trump was hosting a small business event at the time. Secret Service agents ushered journalists who were outside into the briefing room as a precaution. The president continued his event without interruption.

Quinn said investigators have not yet determined whether Vice President Vance was a target of the attack. The circumstances surrounding why the individual was in the area and what motivated the shooting were not immediately known.

The Secret Service urged the public to avoid the area as emergency crews responded to the scene. The location of the shooting is not far from the White House and near a security checkpoint in the vicinity of the Washington Monument.

Law enforcement officials continued investigating the incident throughout the day. The sustained volley of gunshots sent journalists and others in the area running for cover before authorities secured the scene.

The shooting marks another security incident in the heavily protected area surrounding the White House and national monuments. Secret Service agents are responsible for protecting the president, vice president, and the White House complex, and maintain checkpoints and patrols throughout the surrounding streets.

No additional suspects were being sought in connection with the shooting, and authorities said there was no ongoing threat to public safety in the area.

Communities near the epicenter of an ongoing Ebola crisis have expressed fear and concern about the spread of misinformation as the outbreak continues. Local residents in the affected area are dealing with both the health threat and confusion over accurate information about the disease.

The Ebola outbreak has prompted a public health response that now includes expanded screening measures in the United States. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention announced that Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport will begin screening travelers for Ebola. The Atlanta airport joins other U.S. facilities conducting health screenings as officials work to prevent the virus from spreading beyond the outbreak zone.

Ebola is a severe viral disease that spreads through direct contact with bodily fluids of infected individuals. Symptoms include fever, severe headache, muscle pain, and in later stages, bleeding. The virus has a high fatality rate and requires immediate medical intervention.

The current outbreak has created conditions where misinformation can spread rapidly, particularly in communities with limited access to reliable health information. Public health experts have emphasized the importance of clear communication from trusted sources during disease outbreaks to prevent panic and ensure people take appropriate protective measures.

The CDC's decision to expand screening to Atlanta reflects the airport's role as a major international hub. Health officials at designated airports conduct temperature checks and health questionnaires for travelers arriving from affected regions. These screening measures aim to identify potentially infected individuals before they travel further into the United States.

Local health departments near the outbreak epicenter are working to provide accurate information to residents while managing fear within the community. Public health campaigns typically focus on explaining how the virus spreads, what symptoms to watch for, and when to seek medical care.

The outbreak response involves coordination between international health organizations, national governments, and local health authorities. Previous Ebola outbreaks have demonstrated the importance of rapid response, contact tracing, and community cooperation in containing the virus.
