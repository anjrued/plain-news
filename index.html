8:20 PM ET

A man who was shot and killed near the White House on Saturday had previously attempted to breach the complex, according to court records.

The incident occurred when Cole Tomas Allen, 31, a tutor from Torrance, California, approached a security checkpoint outside the White House while carrying a 12-gauge shotgun. The checkpoint was in place because Donald Trump was attending the White House correspondents' dinner at the time.

Allen charged through the checkpoint at approximately 8:40 p.m., prompting an immediate response from Secret Service agents. One officer drew his weapon within two seconds of Allen's arrival and fired four times. During the exchange of fire, one officer was shot but remained uninjured because of a bulletproof vest, according to Washington police chief Jeffery Carroll.

According to court filings, Allen was previously known to the Secret Service. He had attempted to enter the White House complex during an incident in 2025 that resulted in his arrest nearby and a trespassing charge. This prior contact with authorities preceded the Saturday shooting by several months.

Federal prosecutors described Allen's actions as part of an alleged weeks-long plan. In court documents filed by U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro, prosecutors detailed selfies found showing Allen in his hotel room wearing a black suit and armed with two firearms and multiple knives. Prosecutors say these are the same weapons confiscated after the attack.

Allen's alleged motivations emerged through emails prosecutors say he sent minutes before the attack. In one message, he wrote: "I am no longer willing to permit a pedophile, rapist, and traitor to coat my hands with his crimes." Prosecutors claim he also intended to target members of Trump's cabinet.

A letter apparently written from Allen's hotel room described weak security protocols at the establishment and reflected on how staff there had not considered him a threat. He signed the letter using aliases including "Cole 'coldForce' 'Friendly Federal Assassin' Allen."

Allen had maintained online presence under the pseudonyms "coldForce" and "@CForce3000" on platforms including X, YouTube, Twitch and Steam. His posts initially focused on gaming content, but shifted toward political commentary following Trump's 2024 election victory. Archived tweets compared Trump to Adolf Hitler and encouraged gun purchases among critics of his presidency.

Firearm records show Allen purchased his first gun in October 2023 and the shotgun used in the attack in August 2025.

Allen faces a possible life sentence with a mandatory minimum of 10 years in prison. He has not yet entered a plea. Prosecutors asked the court to keep him detained before trial, citing what they describe as "overwhelming" evidence of his guilt.

8:59 AM ET

Russia deployed its advanced Oreshnik hypersonic ballistic missile against Kyiv and surrounding areas in a major assault that killed at least four people and injured approximately 100, according to Ukrainian officials.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed the strike on the city of Bila Tserkva in the Kyiv region, describing what he called a "heavy Russian assault." The attack damaged critical infrastructure including a water supply facility, destroyed a market, and damaged dozens of residential buildings and schools across multiple districts of the capital. Zelenskyy described the assault on social media as particularly intense, stating "They are genuinely deranged."

The broader attack involved around 600 drones and 90 missiles of various types, with 36 ballistic missiles in total. Zelenskyy noted that not all ballistic missiles were intercepted, with Kyiv bearing the heaviest impact as the primary target of the operation.

This marks the third time Russia has used the Oreshnik missile in the conflict. The weapon system can carry nuclear or conventional warheads and travels at hypersonic speeds, making it significantly more difficult to intercept than traditional missiles. Russia's defense ministry confirmed the strike and claimed it targeted Ukrainian military command facilities, airbases, and military enterprises. The ministry said the attack used a combination of Oreshnik, Iskander, Kinzhal, and Zircon missiles.

Kyiv's mayor, Vitali Klitschko, reported that two people were killed in the capital with 56 wounded, while officials in the surrounding Kyiv region confirmed two additional deaths and nine injuries based on preliminary counts. Damage extended across every district of the city.

The assault struck multiple significant sites. A school fire was started by one impact, and a business center experienced direct damage that trapped people in an underground shelter. Ukraine's National Art Museum, housing one of the country's most important cultural collections, sustained damage with broken windows, shattered glass, and debris throughout the building, though staff confirmed the collection itself remained unharmed.

Other targeted locations included Ukraine's Foreign Ministry building, which experienced its first damage since World War Two, and the government headquarters, where windows were blown out though no one was injured.

International leaders condemned the strikes. French President Emmanuel Macron said the use of the Oreshnik signaled "the dead end of Russia's war of aggression." German Chancellor Friedrich Merz called it a "reckless escalation." European Union foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas described the missile deployment as "political scare tactic and reckless nuclear brinkmanship," announcing that EU foreign ministers would discuss increasing international pressure on Russia.

The Russian government claimed the attack was retaliation for Ukrainian strikes on what it described as civilian facilities in Russian territory, specifically citing an alleged drone attack on a student dormitory in Luhansk. Ukraine denied these accusations and stated it had targeted a military command unit instead.

He warned, however, that if Iran does not agree, "the bombing starts" at "a much higher level and intensity than...

6:07 PM ET

Oil prices fell sharply as markets responded to signs that the United States and Iran are moving toward an agreement to end their conflict. President Donald Trump said that if Tehran agrees to a deal, the war would end and the Strait of Hormuz would be "open to all." He warned, however, that if Iran does not agree, "the bombing starts" at "a much higher level and intensity than it was before."

Brent crude oil tumbled 11 percent to as low as $97 a barrel following the announcement, marking its first drop below $100 since April 22. The price had reached $126 a barrel the previous week after Trump said the US blockade of Iranian ports could last for months. Wholesale gas prices also declined, with the British June contract falling 6.3 percent.

Trump said he would pause his "Project Freedom" operation, which has been escorting ships through the strait, for a brief period to finalize negotiations. The blockade has disrupted global energy supplies since late February, as the strait carries roughly one-fifth of the world's oil. Trump maintained that his blockade of Iranian ports would remain in place during talks.

According to Iranian state media, the Revolutionary Guards' Navy said safe transit through the strait would be ensured once US threats end and new procedures are implemented. However, Iran later tempered expectations, calling reports of a near-final agreement an "American wishlist and not a reality." Oil prices partly recovered on this skepticism, trading down 7.3 percent at $101.83 a barrel.

Financial markets rallied broadly on the diplomatic progress. European stock markets surged, with the UK's FTSE 100 rising 2 percent, France's Cac 40 up 3 percent, and Germany's Dax gaining 2.1 percent. On Wall Street, the S&P 500 rose nearly 1.5 percent. South Korea's Kospi jumped 6.6 percent to clear the 7,000 mark for the first time, led by a 14.8 percent surge in Samsung Electronics.

Gold, traditionally seen as a safe haven asset, rose 3 percent to $4,695 an ounce. UK long-term gilt yields decreased slightly, with 30-year gilts falling about 9 basis points to 5.63 percent after reaching their highest level since 1998.

Wealth Club's chief investment strategist noted that "a dam of tension has eased with relief flooding into financial markets, amid hopes that hostilities will cease in the Middle East." Bond market improvements reflected expectations that inflation might not rise as steeply if a longer-term Middle East resolution can be negotiated.

The developments come as the French shipping company CMA CGM reported that one of its vessels was attacked while crossing the strait on Tuesday, injuring crew members and damaging the ship.

Backers who have pledged money can access the current alpha build and test new features as the studio adds them. The game runs a persistent universe where players can interact, though server stability and performance remain works in progress according to the ongoing development updates.

His teams were noted for their aesthetic approach to football, evolving from the brittle, transitional style of the early years through hyper-engineered possession-based football to a more adaptive later period.

The atmosphere at the Etihad reflected the emotional weight of the occasion. The crowd played iconic songs from the club's greatest hits, honouring the transformation Guardiola had overseen.

The defeat, while disappointing for City fans hoping to send their manager off with a victory, did not diminish the recognition of Guardiola's achievements. The manager had built chemistry and demonstrated elite coaching through abstract planning, creating what supporters recognised as an exceptional era for the club.

Guardiola's legacy includes developing young talent, including recent work with Nico O'Reilly, and implementing tactical innovations that influenced modern football. His intensity during matches and press conferences became legendary, reflecting his philosophy of never relaxing regardless of circumstances.

The Manchester City ownership had geared the club's direction towards Guardiola's demands and needs throughout his decade in charge. This period ended a particularly successful modern era, following years when the club had faced significant difficulties before his arrival.

Supporters at the Etihad recognised that Guardiola had taken them to what many described as "a galaxy only a handful of teams in world football experience." Despite the final result, the occasion represented a significant transition point for Manchester City as the club prepared to appoint his successor and continue competing at the highest level.

Guardiola's next destination remained unconfirmed following his departure.

May 21, 10:12 AM ET

The Colorado Democratic Party voted to censure Governor Jared Polis over his decision to commute the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former Mesa County clerk who allowed unauthorized access to voting equipment. The censure represents a formal rebuke from within Polis's own party, though it carries no legal consequences for the governor.

Peters was convicted of four felonies and three misdemeanors in 2024 for her role in a 2021 breach of county voting systems. She allowed a former pro surfer named Conan Hayes to access and copy voting equipment and attend a sensitive software upgrade. Hayes is affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, a prominent figure in spreading false claims about the 2020 election. Sensitive passwords and other information from the equipment later appeared online through right-wing personalities.

Polis reduced Peters's sentence from eight and a half years to approximately four and a half years, making her eligible for parole on June 1. In his clemency letter, the governor wrote that the sentence was "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes."

The decision sparked immediate backlash from Colorado election officials. Secretary of State Jena Griswold, a Democrat, said the clemency validates "Trump's basest impulses" and will embolden election denial movements across the country. "Instead of waiting for Peters to be resentenced in the courts as directed by the Court of Appeals, she will be released from incarceration in the coming weeks," Griswold said.

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, stated the group was "furious, disgusted, and deeply disappointed." He warned that election officials across the country are hearing that "it's open season on our elections and election officials."

Polis had signaled his leanings toward clemency in March when he compared Peters's case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for submitting forged letters to the legislature. The governor said justice "needs to be applied evenly" and cited sentencing disparities.

However, election officials rejected the comparison. Griswold noted that while both had overlapping felony counts, "Peters organized the breach of the election equipment, broke the public trust and attacked the very foundations of our democratic process."

A judge who sentenced Peters in 2024 had told her, "You are no hero. You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again." An appeals court had overturned that sentence in April, ordering reconsideration.

Peters has become a rallying point for those who falsely claim the 2020 election was stolen. Former President Donald Trump has repeatedly called for her release and previously issued a federal pardon, though it held limited effect since her convictions are state crimes.
