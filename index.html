A Secret Service officer fatally shot a person who opened fire near a White House security checkpoint early Monday morning. A bystander was seriously injured during the exchange.

The shooting occurred when the suspect approached a checkpoint and fired at Secret Service agents. Officers returned fire and killed the suspect. The wounded bystander was taken to a nearby hospital in serious condition.

According to court records, the gunman was already known to the Secret Service. The 21-year-old suspect had previously attempted to enter the White House complex, resulting in an arrest nearby in 2025, according to an affidavit filed in DC superior court.

The incident marks another security breach at the nation's most heavily protected residence. Secret Service protocols require officers to respond with lethal force when shots are fired at security checkpoints around the executive mansion.

The White House maintains multiple armed checkpoints where Secret Service officers screen vehicles and pedestrians attempting to access restricted areas. These positions serve as the first line of defense and require officers to make rapid decisions when threats emerge.

Authorities have not disclosed the specific motive behind the shooting or additional details about how the bystander was injured. The investigation into the incident remains ongoing, with federal law enforcement conducting the standard investigation that follows any discharge of weapons by Secret Service personnel.

No White House staff or residents faced danger during the incident, and normal operations at the complex continued without disruption. The area surrounding the checkpoint was secured following the shooting as investigators collected evidence.

The shooting adds to a recent pattern of security incidents near the White House. Last week, police in Washington arrested a man from Georgia who was armed with a loaded shotgun and ran toward the west side of the Capitol building.

In September, a man with a rifle was captured after waiting near Trump's golf course in West Palm Beach while the president played. He was sentenced to life in prison earlier this month. Additionally, a Chinese woman gained unauthorized access to Mar-a-Lago's main lobby in 2019 while carrying multiple cellphones and a computer thumb drive with malware, raising questions about security protocols at the private club.

The Secret Service has not indicated whether the suspect breached any security barriers before the shooting occurred. Training for Secret Service officers emphasizes threat assessment and rapid response procedures for active threats against protected facilities.

Federal officials are continuing to investigate the circumstances surrounding the shooting and the suspect's identity and background.

Air raid sirens sounded for hours as Ukraine's air defense systems engaged the incoming threats. The attack represents a significant escalation in strikes on the capital, which had seen relatively fewer large-scale attacks in recent months compared to earlier phases of the conflict.

The barrage came as diplomatic efforts to end the war have stalled and winter approaches. Russia has intensified attacks on Ukrainian energy infrastructure in recent weeks, following a pattern seen in previous winters of the conflict. Ukrainian air defense units worked through the night to intercept incoming missiles and drones. Emergency services responded to multiple impact sites across the capital as cleanup operations began.

The Oreshnik missile system used in the attack can carry conventional or nuclear warheads and travels at speeds that make interception difficult with current air defense technology. Russia has used the system sparingly since introducing it to the battlefield, typically reserving it for high-priority targets or symbolic strikes.

Zelensky's call for consequences signals Ukraine's expectation that Western allies will respond to the escalation. The attack comes as Ukraine continues to seek additional air defense systems from partner nations. Monday's barrage demonstrates Russia's continued ability to strike deep into Ukrainian territory despite nearly three years of war and international sanctions aimed at limiting its military capabilities.

The escalation in Kyiv follows a pattern of intensified attacks across the broader conflict. In the Kharkiv region, three people were wounded in drone strikes over the weekend. Crude oil prices fell sharply on Monday after President Trump indicated that peace negotiations with Iran were progressing constructively, raising hopes that the Strait of Hormuz could reopen to commercial shipping.

Benchmark crude dropped as much as 6% during Asian trading hours following Trump's comments about the status of talks. Shipping sources reported that vessels were beginning to position themselves near the strait in preparation for resumed transit through this critical waterway.

The Strait of Hormuz connects the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman and handles roughly one-fifth of global oil supplies. The passage has been effectively closed to most commercial traffic during the recent conflict between the US and Iran, pushing oil prices higher due to supply concerns.

Trump claimed on his social media platform that an agreement "has been largely negotiated" and said the strait would reopen as part of the deal. However, he cautioned that "final aspects and details" were still being discussed and would be announced shortly. He also stated that "the US blockade of Iran's ports will remain in full force and effect until an agreement is reached, certified, and signed."

According to reports, the proposed deal would offer Iran sanctions relief and access to as much as $20 billion in frozen assets in exchange for reopening the strait and agreeing to negotiate its nuclear programme over 60 days. At least $6 billion of those assets are held by Qatar, with a key point of dispute being whether unfreezing these assets should be conditional on Iran handing over enriched uranium.

Iran has agreed that shipping should return to prewar levels within 30 days, though Iran's state media indicated that the strait would remain under Iranian control. The Iranian government said the future governance of the strait was a matter for negotiation between Iran and Oman, not something the US could determine.

Iran's supreme leader and national security council still need to approve the proposed agreement before it can be finalized. Officials said one or two clauses needed clarification before the memorandum could be sent for ratification.

Asian stock markets responded positively to the news, with Japan's Nikkei 225 index surpassing 65,000 points for the first time. Lower energy costs reduce input expenses for manufacturers and other businesses across the region.

The dollar weakened against major currencies as traders adjusted positions based on reduced geopolitical risk in the Middle East. Analysts cautioned that oil prices could reverse if negotiations break down or if reopening the strait takes longer than expected.

Market observers noted that shipping companies and insurers would need assurance that vessels could safely transit the passage before resuming normal operations through the waterway.

Yesterday, 11:34 AM ET

Google introduced its biggest search redesign in 25 years, fundamentally altering how users interact with its search box. The company announced the changes at its I/O 2026 conference on Saturday, integrating AI-powered features directly into the search interface.

The redesign marks the first major change to Google's search box since the company launched in 1998. Users will now see AI-generated responses alongside traditional search results, representing a shift in how the company presents information.

Google also launched Gemini Spark, a new AI agent designed to perform tasks beyond simple searches. The announcement comes after OpenClaw, an open-source AI project, surpassed 300,000 stars on GitHub, highlighting growing interest in AI development tools.

The changes reflect Google's push into what it calls the "agentic Gemini era," where AI assistants can handle more complex user requests. The company framed the updates as an evolution of search rather than a replacement of existing features.

Some users have already found ways to bypass the AI features. A simple modifier allows people to turn off AI-generated results and return to traditional search listings. The workaround spread quickly after the announcement.

Google has also encountered technical issues with the rollout. The search engine currently struggles to define basic words like "disregard," "stop," and "ignore." Users attempting to search for these terms receive inconsistent or broken results.

The redesign has sparked debate about the future of web search. Some observers view the changes as marking the end of the internet's traditional era, while others see them as a natural evolution of information retrieval.

Several alternative search engines have gained attention as users explore options beyond Google. TechCrunch and CNET both published guides recommending other free search engines for people who want to avoid the new AI features.

Google has offered cheaper access to its Gemini AI service as part of the push to deepen its ecosystem. The company partnered with Xreal on smart glasses that integrate Google's AI technology, with products expected this fall.

The search changes represent Google's largest bet yet on AI technology reshaping how people find information online.

The Mandalorian and Grogu topped the box office during its opening weekend but achieved the lowest debut for a Star Wars film released under Disney's ownership of the franchise. The movie earned $102 million domestically over the four-day Memorial Day weekend, contributing to a global total of $165 million.

Despite building on the success of the popular Disney Plus series, the film's opening fell short of previous Star Wars theatrical releases. It underperformed compared to 2018's Solo: A Star Wars Story, which earned $103 million domestically and $171 million globally during its Memorial Day weekend debut. Solo was previously considered the franchise's first major box office disappointment under Disney, hampered by its enormous $275 million budget.

The Mandalorian and Grogu, which stars Pedro Pascal as the armored protagonist traveling with the character known as Baby Yoda or Grogu, carries a more modest $165 million budget. This lower production cost means the opening weekend figures could still lead to profitability, unlike Solo's troubled performance.

Industry analyst David Gross noted that while the numbers are substantial, they represent typical spin-off performance rather than main franchise entries. "The movie is a piece of the story, and it's earning a piece of the business," Gross told Agence France-Presse. The opening also fell below other Disney Star Wars spin-offs like 2016's Rogue One.

The film's opening reflects broader questions about Star Wars fatigue in theatrical releases. This marks the first Star Wars film released in cinemas since The Rise of Skywalker in 2019, when Disney paused its theatrical Star Wars strategy after determining it had released too many films too quickly.

Disney executives expressed confidence that positive audience reception scores would support the film's performance in coming weeks, despite muted critical reviews. The studio is banking on the film driving interest toward related products and experiences, including merchandise, theme park offerings, and Disney Plus viewership. The Mandalorian remains the most-watched original series on Disney Plus, while over 13 million Grogu toys have sold since the show's 2019 debut.

Disney has shifted its Star Wars strategy toward streaming series in recent years, producing multiple shows set in the franchise universe. While The Mandalorian and Andor have received strong critical and audience reception, other series including The Book of Boba Fett, Obi-Wan Kenobi, and The Acolyte generated less significant viewership.

The franchise faces an uncertain theatrical future after more than a decade of aggressive release schedules under Disney's ownership. The next Star Wars film, Star Wars: Starfighter starring Ryan Gosling, is scheduled for May 2027, leaving a considerable gap in theatrical releases.

May 21, 10:12 AM ET

The Colorado Democratic Party voted to censure Governor Jared Polis for commuting the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former county clerk who allowed unauthorized access to her county's voting equipment in a case that became central to efforts to overturn the 2020 election.

Polis reduced Peters's sentence from eight and a half years to approximately four and a half years. She is now set for release on parole in early June. In his clemency letter, Polis wrote that "this is an extremely unusual and lengthy sentence for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes."

Peters, the former clerk in Mesa County in western Colorado, was convicted in 2024 of four felonies and three misdemeanors. In 2021, she allowed a pro surfer named Conan Hayes to access the county's voting equipment and make copies of it while attending a sensitive software upgrade. Hayes is affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, a prominent figure in spreading misinformation about the 2020 election. Sensitive information from the county's voting equipment later appeared online through rightwing personalities.

The censure represents a significant rebuke from Colorado's Democratic establishment. State election officials and party leaders expressed outrage at the decision, viewing it as undermining accountability for those who tamper with elections and spread false claims about voting systems.

Jena Griswold, Colorado's top election official, said the clemency "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president." She warned that the decision would "embolden the election denial movement across the country and will leave a dark, dangerous imprint on American democracy for years to come."

Griswold noted that Peters could have waited for resentencing through the courts, as directed by the Court of Appeals, but instead received what she called "special treatment."

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, said the group was "furious, disgusted, and deeply disappointed by the governor's decision." He added that election officials across the country are now concerned that "it's open season on our elections and election officials."

Polis had been noncommittal about clemency for months before making his decision. In March, he suggested the case warranted reconsideration by comparing Peters's sentencing to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for forging letters to the legislature.

Election officials disputed this comparison. Griswold stated that "beyond one count in common, it is not accurate to suggest that Peters's and Sonya Jaquez Lewis's actions or impacts are the same." She emphasized that Peters organized the equipment breach and "attacked the very foundations of our democratic process."

The censure carries no legal weight but signals serious displeasure within the state party. Colorado governors have broad clemency powers, including the ability to commute sentences and grant pardons.

Google introduced its biggest search redesign in 25 years, fundamentally altering how users interact with its search box. The company announced the changes at its I/O 2026 conference on Saturday, integrating AI-powered features directly into the search interface.

The redesign marks the first major change to Google's search box since the company launched in 1998. Users will now see AI-generated responses alongside traditional search results, representing a shift in how the company presents information.

Google also launched Gemini Spark, a new AI agent designed to perform tasks beyond simple searches. The announcement comes after OpenClaw, an open-source AI project, surpassed 300,000 stars on GitHub, highlighting growing interest in AI development tools.

The changes reflect Google's push into what it calls the "agentic Gemini era," where AI assistants can handle more complex user requests. The company framed the updates as an evolution of search rather than a replacement of existing features.

Some users have already found ways to bypass the AI features. A simple modifier allows people to turn off AI-generated results and return to traditional search listings. The workaround spread quickly after the announcement.

Google has also encountered technical issues with the rollout. The search engine currently struggles to define basic words like "disregard," "stop," and "ignore." Users attempting to search for these terms receive inconsistent or broken results.

The redesign has sparked debate about the future of web search. Some observers view the changes as marking the end of the internet's traditional era, while others see them as a natural evolution of information retrieval.

Several alternative search engines have gained attention as users explore options beyond Google. TechCrunch and CNET both published guides recommending other free search engines for people who want to avoid the new AI features.

Google has offered cheaper access to its Gemini AI service as part of the push to deepen its ecosystem. The company partnered with Xreal on smart glasses that integrate Google's AI technology, with products expected this fall.

The search changes represent Google's largest bet yet on AI technology reshaping how people find information online.

The movie earned $102 million domestically over the four-day Memorial Day weekend, contributing to a global total of $165 million.

Despite building on the success of the popular Disney Plus series, the film's opening fell short of previous Star Wars theatrical releases. It underperformed compared to 2018's Solo: A Star Wars Story, which earned $103 million domestically and $171 million globally during its Memorial Day weekend debut. Solo was previously considered the franchise's first major box office disappointment under Disney, hampered by its enormous $275 million budget.

The Mandalorian and Grogu, which stars Pedro Pascal as the armored protagonist traveling with the character known as Baby Yoda or Grogu, carries a more modest $165 million budget. This lower production cost means the opening weekend figures could still lead to profitability, unlike Solo's troubled performance.

Industry analyst David Gross noted that while the numbers are substantial, they represent typical spin-off performance rather than main franchise entries. "The movie is a piece of the story, and it's earning a piece of the business," Gross told Agence France-Presse. The opening also fell below other Disney Star Wars spin-offs like 2016's Rogue One.

The film's opening reflects broader questions about Star Wars fatigue in theatrical releases. This marks the first Star Wars film released in cinemas since The Rise of Skywalker in 2019, when Disney paused its theatrical Star Wars strategy after determining it had released too many films too quickly.

Disney executives expressed confidence that positive audience reception scores would support the film's performance in coming weeks, despite muted critical reviews. The studio is banking on the film driving interest toward related products and experiences, including merchandise, theme park offerings, and Disney Plus viewership. The Mandalorian remains the most-watched original series on Disney Plus, while over 13 million Grogu toys have sold since the show's 2019 debut.

Disney has shifted its Star Wars strategy toward streaming series in recent years, producing multiple shows set in the franchise universe. While The Mandalorian and Andor have received strong critical and audience reception, other series including The Book of Boba Fett, Obi-Wan Kenobi, and The Acolyte generated less significant viewership.

The franchise faces an uncertain theatrical future after more than a decade of aggressive release schedules under Disney's ownership. The next Star Wars film, Star Wars: Starfighter starring Ryan Gosling, is scheduled for May 2027, leaving a considerable gap in theatrical releases.
