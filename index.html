An Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has intensified in recent days, with protesters setting fire to a treatment facility and clashing with health workers over access to bodies of deceased relatives. The violence reflects growing community resistance to containment efforts as case numbers rise.

Protestors in the affected region demanded the return of bodies of family members who died from the disease. Traditional burial practices, which involve washing and touching the deceased, have been identified as a significant transmission route for Ebola. Health authorities typically require safe burial protocols to prevent further spread.

The attack on the medical facility disrupts treatment for current patients and hampers efforts to trace contacts of confirmed cases. Health workers have faced similar resistance during previous Ebola outbreaks in the region, where mistrust of outside medical teams and unfamiliar procedures has sometimes turned violent.

Questions remain about how the current outbreak began. Scientists are investigating potential sources, including whether the virus emerged from an animal reservoir or represents a resurgence from a previous epidemic. Ebola can persist in the bodies of survivors for months or years, occasionally sparking new transmission chains.

The DRC has experienced multiple Ebola outbreaks over the past decade. The 2018-2020 epidemic in the eastern provinces killed more than 2,200 people and was the second-largest Ebola outbreak in history. That crisis was complicated by armed conflict, population displacement, and community distrust of health workers.

Ebola spreads through direct contact with bodily fluids of infected individuals. Early symptoms include fever, fatigue, and muscle pain, followed by vomiting, diarrhea, and in severe cases, internal and external bleeding. The virus has a high fatality rate, though early treatment and supportive care improve survival chances.

International health organizations have not yet declared the current situation a public health emergency. The World Health Organization typically monitors such outbreaks closely and coordinates with local governments on containment strategies, including vaccination campaigns using available Ebola vaccines.

The violence against health facilities represents a critical obstacle to controlling the outbreak. Effective response requires cooperation between medical teams and affected communities, along with sufficient resources for treatment, contact tracing, and safe burials. Without community trust and participation, containment efforts face severe limitations regardless of available medical technology.

Today, 10:00 PM ET

Tennessee officials failed to execute Tony Carruthers on Thursday after prison staff could not establish intravenous access for the lethal injection. The state has announced it will not attempt another execution for at least one year.

The execution was scheduled to proceed at 7:00 PM at Riverbend Maximum Security Institution, but the execution team was unable to insert the IV lines necessary to administer the fatal drugs. After multiple attempts, officials called off the procedure.

Carruthers, 59, was convicted of the 1981 murder of Patty Johnson. He had exhausted his appeals, and Governor Bill Lee declined to grant clemency. The case proceeded despite objections from civil rights groups who argued the lethal injection protocol carries risks of painful complications.

Tennessee Department of Correction officials said the difficulty stemmed from challenges accessing Carruthers' veins. The state uses a three-drug protocol for executions, but administration requires successful IV placement. Officials did not provide details about how long the team attempted to establish access before abandoning the procedure.

The state's announcement that it will not attempt another execution for a year marks an unusual admission of operational failure. Tennessee has carried out seven executions since 2018 after a nine-year pause. The state had scheduled multiple executions for 2025.

Difficulties with IV access during executions have sparked legal challenges nationwide. In several states, execution teams have taken over an hour to insert lines, with witnesses describing condemned prisoners in visible distress. Medical professionals generally do not participate in executions due to ethical guidelines, leaving the technical procedures to corrections staff without medical training.

The failed execution raises questions about Tennessee's execution protocol and staff training. State officials have not announced whether they will review procedures before resuming executions. Legal experts expect defense attorneys in future cases to cite this incident as evidence that the state cannot reliably carry out executions without risking constitutional violations related to cruel and unusual punishment.

Carruthers remains on death row. Under Tennessee law, his death sentence remains active, and the state can reschedule the execution after the announced pause. His attorneys have not yet commented on whether they will file new legal challenges based on Thursday's events.

Today, 10:00 PM ET

The United States government announced plans to invest $2 billion in quantum computing technology, with IBM and other American firms positioned to receive significant funding. The investment represents a major federal commitment to maintain technological leadership in a field that promises to revolutionize computing power and national security capabilities.

Quantum computers use the principles of quantum mechanics to process information in ways that traditional computers cannot, potentially solving complex problems in minutes that would take conventional supercomputers thousands of years. The technology has applications in drug discovery, climate modeling, cryptography, and artificial intelligence.

The funding comes as the US faces increasing competition from China and other nations racing to achieve quantum advantage. China has invested heavily in quantum research over the past decade, building dedicated research facilities and launching quantum communication satellites. European nations have also committed billions to quantum initiatives through coordinated programs.

IBM, one of the primary beneficiaries, has been developing quantum systems for years and currently offers cloud-based access to quantum computers for researchers and businesses. The company recently unveiled its most advanced quantum processor, though practical quantum computers capable of outperforming classical systems for useful applications remain under development.

The investment will likely support both hardware development and the creation of quantum algorithms and software. Building stable quantum computers requires maintaining extremely cold temperatures and isolating quantum bits from environmental interference, presenting significant engineering challenges that require sustained research funding.

Industry experts view the announcement as recognition that quantum computing has moved from theoretical research to a strategic technology requiring national investment. The funding may accelerate the timeline for practical quantum applications, though most analysts expect commercially viable quantum computers for widespread use remain several years away.

The initiative also aims to develop a skilled quantum workforce and establish US standards for quantum technology. As quantum computers could potentially break current encryption methods, the investment includes work on quantum-resistant cryptography to protect sensitive data and communications infrastructure.

Google Publishes Exploit Code for Unpatched Chromium Vulnerability

Today, 10:00 PM ET

Google has released public exploit code for a security vulnerability in Chromium before millions of users have received patches, raising concerns about the timing and potential risks of the disclosure.

The vulnerability affects all Chromium-based browsers, including Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Brave, and Opera. By publishing proof-of-concept exploit code through its security research division, Google has made the technical details of the flaw available to anyone, including potential attackers.

Security researchers typically withhold exploit code until a significant majority of users have updated their software. The practice, known as coordinated disclosure, gives software vendors and users time to apply patches before attackers can weaponize vulnerabilities. Google's decision to publish working exploit code breaks from this standard approach.

The vulnerability involves a type confusion bug, a class of security flaw that allows attackers to trick software into treating data as the wrong type. These bugs can potentially be exploited to execute malicious code on affected systems. Type confusion vulnerabilities are considered serious security issues in web browsers because they can be triggered simply by visiting a compromised website.

Google has issued a patch for Chrome users, but the update process takes time to reach all installations. Chromium-based browsers from other vendors must incorporate the fix into their own builds and distribute updates through their respective channels. This means millions of users remain vulnerable during the window between public disclosure and widespread patch deployment.

The company has not publicly explained its reasoning for publishing the exploit code ahead of broad patch coverage. Traditional disclosure policies balance transparency with user safety by ensuring fixes reach most systems before attack methods become public knowledge.

Browser security experts have expressed concern about the disclosure timeline. With functional exploit code now available, the barrier to attacking unpatched systems drops significantly. Attackers no longer need to independently discover and develop exploitation techniques, they can adapt Google's published proof of concept.

Users of Chromium-based browsers should update to the latest version immediately. Chrome typically updates automatically, but users can manually check for updates through the browser's settings menu. Other Chromium-based browsers will release their own patched versions on different schedules.

The incident highlights ongoing debates in cybersecurity about disclosure practices. While transparency helps inform users and security professionals, premature publication of exploit code can create windows of heightened risk for those who have not yet received patches.

Sea Level Rise Accelerating Since 1960, New Analysis Confirms

Scientists have refined measurements of global sea level rise and confirmed that the rate has been accelerating continuously since 1960, according to new research published this week. Today, 10:00 PM ET

Scientists have refined measurements of global sea level rise and confirmed that the rate has been accelerating continuously since 1960, according to new research published this week. The findings provide the clearest picture yet of how quickly oceans are rising and underscore the mounting impact of climate change on coastal communities worldwide.

The study used improved satellite data and historical tide gauge records to reduce uncertainty in sea level measurements. Researchers found that the acceleration has been consistent over six decades, with the current rate of rise roughly double what it was in the 1960s. This acceleration matches predictions from climate models and correlates with increasing global temperatures.

The research addresses a critical gap in climate science. While scientists have long known sea levels are rising, quantifying the exact rate of acceleration has been challenging due to incomplete historical data and natural variability in ocean heights. The new analysis reconciles different measurement methods and extends the record further back in time with greater confidence.

Current sea level rise stands at approximately 3.4 millimeters per year, compared to roughly 1.5 millimeters per year in the 1960s. This acceleration is driven primarily by two factors: thermal expansion of warming ocean water and increased meltwater from glaciers and ice sheets in Greenland and Antarctica. Both processes are intensifying as global temperatures continue to climb.

The findings have direct implications for coastal planning and infrastructure. More than 600 million people live in coastal areas less than 10 meters above sea level. Accurate projections of future sea level rise depend on understanding not just the current rate, but how quickly that rate is changing. The confirmed acceleration means previous estimates based on steady rates likely underestimate future impacts.

Researchers emphasize that the acceleration is expected to continue as greenhouse gas concentrations increase. Even if emissions were to stabilize immediately, thermal inertia in the ocean system means sea levels would continue rising for centuries. The study provides coastal communities and policymakers with improved data for adaptation planning, from building seawalls to relocating vulnerable infrastructure.

The work represents a collaboration among multiple research institutions using decades of observational data. By reducing uncertainty in historical measurements, the team has given scientists and planners a more reliable foundation for projecting what lies ahead for the world's coastlines.
