A few minutes ago

President Trump approved a federal emergency declaration on Saturday for a major sewage spill near Washington DC that threatens to disrupt the nation's 250th anniversary celebrations this summer.

The declaration was prompted by a rupture in the Potomac Interceptor, a 72-inch pipe that carries wastewater across a 54-mile stretch. The break, which occurred last month, has released millions of gallons of raw, untreated sewage into the Potomac River. The Washington Post described the spill as "one of the largest sewage spills in US history," with local utility company DC Water estimating that 243 million gallons of wastewater had leaked before a temporary bypass was established.

The Federal Emergency Management Agency stated that Trump's action "authorizes Fema to coordinate all disaster relief efforts to alleviate the hardship and suffering caused by the emergency on the local population and to provide appropriate assistance to save lives, to protect property, public health and safety, and to lessen the threat of catastrophe."

The emergency declaration enables Fema to mobilize resources to support cleanup and recovery efforts across Washington DC and surrounding areas in Maryland and Virginia. The sewage spill has created significant environmental concerns, with dangerous levels of E coli bacteria detected in the Potomac River. Local authorities have issued recreational advisories and the DC department of energy and environment recommends residents avoid contact with the river.

Washington Mayor Muriel Bowser declared a local public emergency earlier in the week and requested that Trump declare a major disaster for the capital. The spill has sparked ongoing disputes about responsibility for the infrastructure failure.

Jay Apperson, a spokesperson for the Maryland department of the environment, noted that while "the portion of the pipe that broke is in Maryland, DC Water owns and operates the Potomac Interceptor, and it is regulated and overseen by the federal government, specifically the US EPA." He added that the break occurred within the C&O Canal National Historical Park, which is National Park Service property.

Trump blamed local and state officials for the spill and called it an "ecological disaster," directing federal authorities to protect the Potomac. However, officials have disputed who bears responsibility for the maintenance failures.

Ammar Moussa, a spokesperson for Maryland Governor Wes Moore, countered Trump's claims, stating that "since the last century, the federal government has been responsible for the Potomac Interceptor, which is the origin of the sewage leak. For the last four weeks, the Trump Administration has failed to act, shirking its responsibility and putting people's health at risk."

The infrastructure failure has raised broader questions about the maintenance and condition of America's aging water systems.

5:40 PM ET

Oil prices fell sharply on Monday as markets responded to growing optimism about a potential peace agreement between the United States and Iran. Brent crude futures, the global oil benchmark, declined 6 percent to $97.43 a barrel, reaching their lowest level in two weeks.

The price drop reflects investor expectations that an agreement could end the nearly three-month US-Israeli conflict with Iran and lead to the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping passage that Iran had effectively closed. The strait's blockade had sent energy prices soaring following initial US and Israeli missile strikes on Tehran in late February.

Stock markets also responded positively to the news. Japan's Nikkei index rose nearly 3 percent while the pan-European Stoxx 600 index climbed 1 percent. The dollar dipped 0.3 percent against a basket of major currencies, while gold prices increased 1.46 percent to $4,574 an ounce.

However, significant obstacles remain before any final agreement takes effect. US and Iran negotiators continue disagreeing over key issues, with an Iranian government spokesperson cautioning that an accord was "not imminent." While a framework has been negotiated, implementation faces substantial hurdles.

Even if the strait reopens quickly, energy analysts warn that normal oil flows will take months to resume. Damaged energy infrastructure in Qatar and other regional facilities requires extensive repairs. Warren Patterson, head of commodities strategy at ING, cautioned that markets should remain cautious about the negotiations. "We've been at this stage before, only for talks to break down," he told Reuters.

Some signs of improved shipping activity have already emerged. Two tankers carrying liquefied natural gas exited the strait on Monday bound for Pakistan and China. A supertanker carrying Iraqi crude also left the Gulf for China after being stranded for nearly three months.

Despite Monday's decline, oil prices remain substantially higher than before the conflict began. Brent crude was trading around $70 per barrel before the war started. According to UBS analyst Giovanni Staunovo, the key factor to monitor is actual physical oil flows through the strait, which remain restricted despite recent price movements.

The oil market's performance comes as inflation concerns continue affecting global economies. Higher energy costs have spurred expectations of interest rate increases from central banks. Markets currently expect the Bank of England to raise rates twice this year. Lower oil prices could help ease inflation pressures that have concerned policymakers and households worldwide, though sustained relief depends on whether a deal materializes and shipping through the Strait of Hormuz eventually returns to normal levels.

The film generated $102 million domestically and $165 million globally, making it the lowest-grossing opening for a Star Wars film since Disney acquired the franchise in 2012.

The movie brings Pedro Pascal's character from the Disney Plus series to the big screen alongside Grogu, the character affectionately known as "Baby Yoda" by audiences. Despite the streaming show's popularity, the theatrical release failed to match previous Star Wars openings, falling short of even 2018's Solo: A Star Wars Story, which earned $103 million domestically during its Memorial Day weekend debut.

The comparison to Solo is instructive. While Solo opened slightly higher and made $171 million globally in its opening weekend, The Mandalorian and Grogu's more modest budget of $165 million means it has better prospects for profitability. Solo, by contrast, was considered a box office disappointment due to its enormous $275 million production cost, despite its larger opening numbers.

Industry analyst David Gross told Agence France-Presse that the results reflect normal performance patterns for spin-offs. "The movie is a piece of the story, and it's earning a piece of the business," he said. "These are big numbers, but not on the level of the primary Star Wars films."

The opening weekend figures suggest ongoing audience fatigue with Star Wars content. Disney had paused theatrical Star Wars releases after 2019's The Rise of Skywalker, concluding that too many films had been released too quickly. The studio subsequently shifted its focus toward streaming, releasing shows including The Mandalorian, Andor, and others on Disney Plus.

However, not all streaming ventures have succeeded equally. While The Mandalorian ranks as the most-watched original series on Disney Plus and Andor has earned critical acclaim, other shows including The Book of Boba Fett, Obi-Wan Kenobi, and The Acolyte have struggled to gain significant audiences.

Disney reportedly believes The Mandalorian and Grogu will sustain box office performance in coming weeks, bolstered by positive audience scores despite mixed critical reception. The studio hopes the film will drive viewers toward related products including merchandise, theme park attractions, and its streaming service. Grogu merchandise alone has sold more than 13 million units since the series debuted in 2019.

The Star Wars franchise has generated more than $10 billion in global ticket sales since its 1977 debut. Disney's next Star Wars theatrical film, Star Wars: Starfighter, featuring Ryan Gosling, is scheduled for May 2027.

May 21, 5:06 PM ET

The Trump administration has temporarily halted the removal of detainees to the Democratic Republic of Congo due to a widening Ebola outbreak, according to reporting by Politico. Officials cited concerns about exposing Immigration and Customs Enforcement personnel to the virus during deportation operations.

However, the pause does not extend to detainees already transferred to third countries in the disease-stricken region. At least one woman remains in limbo after officials moved her to Kinshasa, the capital of the DRC. Despite a judge's order for her return, authorities say they will not bring her back because of travel restrictions related to the Ebola outbreak.

Public health experts question whether the temporary pause will effectively prevent disease spread. Ebola transmission requires direct contact with bodily fluids of infected individuals and does not spread through the air like some other pathogens. The current outbreak in Congo has prompted health officials to implement restrictions on travel and gatherings in affected areas.

The decision comes as the Trump administration has dramatically expanded its deportation operations. Human rights groups tracking ICE flights documented an 84 percent increase in the number of deportation flights during 2025 compared to 2024. The administration has transported detained immigrants using both commercial airliners and charter jets.

This pause represents one element of a broader controversy surrounding the administration's immigration enforcement program. A group of 40 House Democrats recently sent a letter to the Federal Aviation Administration expressing grave concerns about what they describe as secretive deportation flights and inhumane conditions on ICE charter jets. The lawmakers referenced credible reports indicating that individuals have been placed on flights without notice to counsel or family members, effectively disappearing from public view when flights are shielded from tracking systems.

The Democrats cited an investigation revealing that the Trump administration transported detained immigrants in ways that routinely violated constitutional rights. They also referenced allegations of abuse at a private detention center in Alexandria, Louisiana, which serves as a central node in the administration's deportation program.

The FAA has granted unusual permission to dozens of charter jets used for deportation flights to block certain data, including tail numbers, from public flight tracking sites. This makes it harder for the public and Congress to monitor ICE air operations.

The administration has previously dismissed claims of hidden or weaponized transfer flights as categorically false and argued its detention centers maintain higher standards than most U.S. prisons. It has also defended its use of full body restraints during deportation flights as long-standing standard protocol designed to ensure the safety of both detainees and officers.

The timing of the Congo pause coincides with broader questions about transparency and oversight of the administration's expanded immigration enforcement activities.

The Mandalorian and Grogu claimed the top spot at the box office during its opening weekend, but the performance marked a significant milestone for the wrong reasons. The film generated $102 million domestically and $165 million globally, making it the lowest-grossing opening for a Star Wars film since Disney acquired the franchise in 2012.

The movie brings Pedro Pascal's character from the Disney Plus series to the big screen alongside Grogu, the character affectionately known as "Baby Yoda" by audiences. Despite the streaming show's popularity, the theatrical release failed to match previous Star Wars openings, falling short of even 2018's Solo: A Star Wars Story, which earned $103 million domestically during its Memorial Day weekend debut.

The comparison to Solo is instructive. While Solo opened slightly higher and made $171 million globally in its opening weekend, The Mandalorian and Grogu's more modest budget of $165 million means it has better prospects for profitability. Solo, by contrast, was considered a box office disappointment due to its enormous $275 million production cost, despite its larger opening numbers.

Industry analyst David Gross told Agence France-Presse that the results reflect normal performance patterns for spin-offs. "The movie is a piece of the story, and it's earning a piece of the business," he said. "These are big numbers, but not on the level of the primary Star Wars films."

The opening weekend figures suggest ongoing audience fatigue with Star Wars content. Disney had paused theatrical Star Wars releases after 2019's The Rise of Skywalker, concluding that too many films had been released too quickly. The studio subsequently shifted its focus toward streaming, releasing shows including The Mandalorian, Andor, and others on Disney Plus.

However, not all streaming ventures have succeeded equally. While The Mandalorian ranks as the most-watched original series on Disney Plus and Andor has earned critical acclaim, other shows including The Book of Boba Fett, Obi-Wan Kenobi, and The Acolyte have struggled to gain significant audiences.

Disney reportedly believes The Mandalorian and Grogu will sustain box office performance in coming weeks, bolstered by positive audience scores despite mixed critical reception. The studio hopes the film will drive viewers toward related products including merchandise, theme park attractions, and its streaming service. Grogu merchandise alone has sold more than 13 million units since the series debuted in 2019.

The Star Wars franchise has generated more than $10 billion in global ticket sales since its 1977 debut. Disney's next Star Wars theatrical film, Star Wars: Starfighter, featuring Ryan Gosling, is scheduled for May 2027.
