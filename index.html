Oil Prices Fall as Tensions Ease in Strait of Hormuz

9:23 PM ET

Oil futures declined as Donald Trump signaled progress toward a peace deal with Iran that would reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway for global energy supplies. The price drop reflects growing market optimism that regional tensions may be easing.

West Texas Intermediate and Brent crude both fell sharply in trading. Brent crude tumbled 11 percent to as low as $97 a barrel, marking the first time it has fallen below $100 since April 22. The decline came after Trump stated on social media that if Iran agrees to a deal, "the highly effective Blockade will allow the Hormuz Strait to be OPEN TO ALL." The strait handles roughly one-fifth of the world's petroleum traffic.

Trump said he would briefly pause his operation escorting ships through the waterway, which Iran has blockaded since late February. He maintained that his blockade of Iranian ports would remain in place during negotiations. According to Iranian state media, the Revolutionary Guards' Navy responded by saying safe transit would be ensured once US threats end and new procedures are implemented.

News reports suggesting the White House believed it was close to finalizing a one-page agreement accelerated the oil price decline. However, oil prices recovered partially later in the day after Iran's Fars news agency dismissed the reports as an "American wishlist and not a reality."

The oil price had hit $126 a barrel the previous week after Trump indicated the US blockade could last for months and peace talks remained stalled. The recent decline represents a significant reversal.

Stock markets rallied globally on the optimistic news. European indices rose substantially, with France's Cac 40 up 3 percent and Germany's Dax rising 2.1 percent. The UK's FTSE 100 increased 2 percent. Wall Street's S&P 500 rose nearly 1.5 percent, while Asian markets also gained, with South Korea's Kospi surging 6.6 percent.

Wholesale gas prices fell as well, with the British June contract declining 6.3 percent. Airline stocks rose on improved prospects for international travel. Gold, traditionally viewed as a safe asset, increased 3 percent to $4,695 an ounce.

Long-term UK government borrowing costs eased slightly, with yields on 30-year gilts declining about 9 basis points to 5.63 percent after reaching their highest level since 1998.

The price movement reflects how geopolitical developments directly influence energy markets. Oil market volatility affects broader economic conditions, from gasoline prices to inflation rates. Energy analysts caution that Middle East tensions remain fluid and could shift market dynamics quickly if diplomatic negotiations falter.

A bomb exploded on a passenger train in Pakistan's southwestern Balochistan province, killing at least 24 people. Officials said the train had been transporting military personnel home for Eid when armed separatists attacked it.

The blast represents one of the deadliest recent incidents on Pakistan's rail network. Emergency responders worked to evacuate survivors and recover bodies from the damaged train as security forces intensified operations against the militants responsible.

The Balochistan Liberation Army (BLA), a proscribed separatist group, claimed responsibility for the attack. The organization has been behind a series of coordinated assaults across the province, with simultaneous strikes in more than 10 cities described by analysts as unprecedented in scale. Those attacks killed at least 31 civilians and 17 security personnel on Saturday.

The broader assault on Balochistan demonstrated the militant group's reach and organizational capacity. Before dawn on Saturday, scores of insurgents attacked military installations, police stations and banks. They blocked main roads, destroyed railway tracks and carried out suicide bombings in the provincial capital Quetta and the port city of Gwadar.

In Mastung, dozens of insurgents attacked a prison and freed almost 30 inmates. In Gwadar, where China has built a deep-sea port, militants attempted to penetrate the facility's perimeter security but were repelled. In Pasni, five militants were killed during an attack on Pakistan's coastguard headquarters after a suicide bomber detonated explosives at the gate.

The BLA claimed in statements to have killed 84 security personnel and taken 18 security and civilian officials prisoner, though these claims could not be verified independently.

Pakistan's chief minister of Balochistan said 145 people described as militants had been killed in 40 hours of military operations, with their bodies in authorities' custody. Pakistan's defence minister told local media that calm had been restored and security forces were conducting mopping-up operations.

Balochistan has experienced decades of armed insurgency by separatist groups seeking independence from Pakistan. The province, despite being resource-rich, remains one of the country's poorest and least developed regions. Government and provincial officials had claimed in recent months that the insurgency had been contained, but the scale of Saturday's coordinated attacks contradicted those assertions.

Security analyst Zahid Hussain said the attacks proved insurgents maintained significant local support and had access to safe sanctuaries outside Pakistan's borders. He argued that the military's dominant role in provincial governance, with little political apparatus to address grievances, had worsened the situation.

Pakistan has repeatedly blamed Afghanistan for providing safe haven to Baloch insurgents and other militant groups, though Kabul has denied these claims. Mobile internet services were suspended across several cities including Quetta, Gwadar and Noshki following the attacks.

A 31-year-old man who opened fire near the White House during a high-profile event was already known to the Secret Service, according to court records released this week.

Cole Tomas Allen, a tutor from Torrance, California, allegedly charged through a Secret Service checkpoint outside the White House Correspondents' Association dinner on Saturday night carrying a 12-gauge shotgun. The incident occurred around 8:40 p.m. as more than 2,500 members of the Washington press corps attended the annual black-tie event, which was also attended by Donald Trump.

A Secret Service officer drew his weapon within two seconds of Allen's arrival at the checkpoint and fired four times, according to surveillance footage reviewed by the Washington Post. One officer was struck during the exchange but remained uninjured due to his bulletproof vest, according to Jeffrey Carroll, a Washington police chief. Allen was fatally shot during the confrontation.

Court filings reveal that Allen had previously attempted to enter the White House complex. An affidavit filed in DC superior court in 2025 documents a prior arrest nearby on trespassing charges.

Federal prosecutors have described what they characterize as a weeks-long plan by Allen to kill Trump and members of his cabinet. The prosecution's motion to detain Allen before trial includes selfies showing him in a hotel room wearing a black suit, armed with two firearms and multiple knives. Prosecutors say these are the same weapons confiscated after the attack.

Emails allegedly sent minutes before the shooting provide insight into Allen's stated motivations. In one message, he wrote: "I am no longer willing to permit a pedophile, rapist, and traitor to coat my hands with his crimes." He outlined a set of rules to follow during the attack and later signed correspondence with the aliases "coldForce" and "Friendly Federal Assassin."

Online records show Allen frequently used the pseudonyms "coldForce" and "@CForce3000" on platforms including X, YouTube, Twitch, and Steam. Since Trump's 2024 election victory, his social media posts shifted from gaming discussions to political commentary. Archived tweets compared Trump to Adolf Hitler and encouraged others critical of his presidency to purchase firearms. Allen also advocated for nullifying the 2024 election results.

Firearm records detailed in court filings show Allen purchased his first weapon in October 2023 and acquired the 12-gauge shotgun in August 2025, two years later.

Prosecutors argue that evidence of Allen's guilt is "overwhelming" and have asked the court to detain him before trial. He faces a possible life sentence and a mandatory minimum of 10 years in prison. Allen has not yet entered a plea in the case and first appeared in federal court on Monday.

The game allows players to purchase spacecraft and equipment before the game's full release, with some virtual ships selling for hundreds or thousands of dollars.

The $1 billion milestone represents contributions from backers worldwide who have supported the project's development. The game has attracted both dedicated supporters and critics who question the extended development timeline and business model.

Cloud Imperium Games has released playable alpha versions of Star Citizen that allow backers to test various features and systems. The company continues to add new content and mechanics to these test versions while working toward a full commercial release.

Squadron 42 features a single-player story campaign set in the Star Citizen universe. The game has faced multiple delays since its original announced release date. Cloud Imperium Games has shown gameplay footage and trailers for Squadron 42 over the years, featuring motion capture performances from actors including Mark Hamill and Gary Oldman.

The development studio has expanded significantly since the project's launch, with offices in multiple countries working on both Star Citizen and Squadron 42. The company has not announced a release date for the main Star Citizen multiplayer experience, which remains in alpha testing.

The crowdfunding total continues to grow as the company sells additional virtual items and accepts new backer pledges. Star Citizen represents one of the most ambitious crowdfunded projects in entertainment history, both in terms of funding raised and scope of the planned game.

The Mandalorian and Grogu has topped the weekend box office as Disney makes a significant push to revive Star Wars on the theatrical big screen. The film brings characters from the popular Disney Plus streaming series to movie theaters, marking a notable shift in the company's strategy for the franchise.

The movie stars Pedro Pascal as the Mandalorian, a bounty hunter operating in the Star Wars galaxy after the fall of the Empire. His character serves as guardian to Grogu, a small creature of the Yoda species with telekinetic abilities. Grogu became a cultural phenomenon when The Mandalorian premiered on Disney Plus in 2019, driving subscriber growth for the streaming service through merchandise sales and fan enthusiasm.

The film's plot centers on a mission to rescue Rotta the Hutt, son of the infamous Jabba the Hutt, from an imperial warlord. The New Republic's Colonel Ward, portrayed by Sigourney Weaver, hires the Mandalorian and Grogu to complete this task in exchange for valuable intelligence about Empire schemers planning a return to power. Director Jon Favreau fills the movie with exotic creatures and action sequences typical of the Star Wars universe, including an aerial combat scene with X-wing fighters.

The cast includes various supporting characters, from street vendors to imperial antagonists. The Mandalorian's helmet remains on throughout most of the film, with body-double actors Lateef Crowder and Brendan Wayne handling much of the physical performance while Pascal provides the voice work.

Disney acquired Lucasfilm in 2012 for more than four billion dollars. In recent years, the Star Wars franchise has experienced mixed commercial results at theaters. Earlier theatrical releases saw varying box office performance, while the company found more consistent audiences through Disney Plus series. The transition from streaming to cinema for these characters represents a test of whether audiences will support Star Wars content in traditional movie theaters.

The film serves as a bridge between the streaming television series and theatrical cinema. As one reviewer noted, it is "perfectly fine matinee fodder," though opinions differ on whether it captures the full depth of Star Wars storytelling. The movie demonstrates Disney's effort to leverage the popularity of streaming characters for broader theatrical appeal.

This release reflects a broader strategic recalibration at Disney. The company has invested heavily in Star Wars content across multiple platforms, producing series like Andor, Obi-Wan Kenobi, and Ahsoka alongside theatrical films. Studio executives are evaluating how to balance future Star Wars content between streaming and traditional cinema releases.

The box office performance will help determine Disney's next steps for the franchise. The company continues developing additional Star Wars projects and weighing whether developing characters on streaming platforms before bringing them to theaters proves financially viable for future releases.

Colorado Democratic Party censures Governor Polis over clemency for election official

May 21, 10:12 AM ET

The Colorado Democratic Party voted to censure Governor Jared Polis for commuting the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former county clerk whose security breach of voting equipment became central to election denial efforts.

Peters, the former clerk in Mesa County in western Colorado, was convicted of four felonies and three misdemeanors in 2024. In 2021, she allowed an unauthorized person named Conan Hayes to access the county's voting equipment, copy it, and attend a sensitive software upgrade. Hayes is affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, a prominent figure in spreading false claims about the 2020 election.

Polis reduced Peters' sentence from eight and a half years to approximately four and a half years. She will be released on parole in June. In his clemency letter, Polis wrote that the sentence was "extremely unusual and lengthy for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes."

The decision drew sharp criticism from within Polis' own party. Jena Griswold, Colorado's secretary of state, said the clemency "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president." She warned that the decision would "embolden the election denial movement across the country and will leave a dark, dangerous imprint on American democracy for years to come."

Griswold disputed Polis' reasoning for the clemency. The governor had compared Peters' case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who received probation and community service for submitting forged letters to the legislature. Polis argued both cases involved sentencing disparities that needed correction.

Griswold rejected this comparison. "Peters organized the breach of the election equipment, broke the public trust and attacked the very foundations of our democratic process," she said. "Her actions are still being used to try to undermine the 2026 election."

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, called the decision "fueled, disgusted, and deeply disappointed." He said election officials nationwide are concerned the clemency signals "it's open season on our elections and election officials."

Peters' case gained prominence among Republicans and election deniers. Former President Donald Trump repeatedly called for her release, issued her a federal pardon, and instructed the Justice Department to seek her release. Trump has continued spreading false claims about the 2020 election and Dominion voting systems.

The original judge who sentenced Peters rejected her claims, telling her in 2024: "You are no hero. You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again."

An appeals court overturned the original sentence in April, ordering reconsideration. The censure represents a formal statement of disapproval from Colorado Democrats over Polis' decision, though it carries no legal consequences. Polis has not publicly detailed his reasoning beyond the clemency letter.
