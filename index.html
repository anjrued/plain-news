8:18 PM ET

Authorities in Orange County, California have shifted their approach to containing a dangerous chemical leak at an aerospace facility in Garden Grove as approximately 50,000 residents remain under evacuation orders across six cities.

The crisis began Thursday when a storage tank at GKN Aerospace, a division of a British corporation that manufactures aircraft parts, began releasing methyl methacrylate, a highly flammable chemical used in manufacturing resins and plastics. Initial evacuation orders were lifted that day, but the situation deteriorated when damage to a valve on the tank created additional operational challenges that prevented complete mitigation.

By Friday, authorities had expanded evacuation orders to residents in Garden Grove, Cypress, Stanton, Anaheim, Buena Park and Westminster. The tank currently holds approximately 6,000 to 7,000 gallons of the chemical.

Orange County fire authority division chief Craig Covey explained the severity of the situation at a news conference. "We have a tank that is actively in crisis," he said. "There are literally two options left remaining. One: the tank fails and spills a total of about 6-7,000 gallons of very bad chemicals into the parking lot in that area. Or two: the tank goes into a thermal runaway and blows up, affecting the tanks that are around them that have fuel or the chemicals in them as well."

Covey added that crews are uncertain when the tank will fail. "This thing is going to fail, and we don't know when," he said. "We're doing our best to figure out when or how we can prevent it."

The chemical poses significant health risks if it heats up and releases vapor. According to county health officer Dr. Regina Chinsio-Kwong, exposure can cause respiratory issues, itching and burning eyes, nausea and headaches.

Emergency crews have constructed containment barriers using sandbags around the facility to prevent any chemical spill from entering storm drains or nearby waterways and the ocean. Local authorities have established at least two shelters for evacuees and urged residents to comply with evacuation orders.

Garden Grove, located approximately 30 miles southeast of downtown Los Angeles, sits less than a mile from Disneyland's two theme parks, which were not under evacuation orders. Garden Grove police chief Amir El-Farra reported that some 15 percent of residents facing evacuation orders were refusing to leave.

EPA administrator Lee Zeldin acknowledged the uncertainty surrounding the tank's failure, telling CNN that officials have been warned the tank will fail but different scenarios exist regarding what that means. Local officials have promised to provide updates as the containment efforts progress and the situation develops.

6:47 PM ET

Oil prices dropped to their lowest level in two weeks as investors grew optimistic about a potential agreement between the United States and Iran. The development triggered significant market movements across multiple asset classes, with crude oil declining sharply while stock markets rallied globally.

Benchmark crude oil futures fell substantially as traders responded to diplomatic progress. Brent crude tumbled 11 percent to around $97 a barrel, marking the first time it has fallen below $100 since late April. The price decline accelerated after reports that the White House believed it was approaching agreement on a memorandum of understanding to end the conflict with Iran. The two sides were reportedly moving toward a framework for more detailed nuclear negotiations.

The market reaction reflects expectations that a deal could resolve the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, which carries about one-fifth of the world's oil supplies. President Trump stated that if Iran agreed to terms, the strait would be "open to all" and military operations would end. He also warned that without an agreement, military action would resume at greater intensity. Trump indicated he was pausing his "Project Freedom" operation temporarily to finalize negotiations, though he maintained the blockade of Iranian ports.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy responded by stating that safe transit through the strait would be ensured once US threats ceased and new procedures took effect. However, Iranian officials later disputed the progress, describing the reported agreement as an "American wishlist and not a reality." Oil prices pared some losses following this statement, settling down 7.3 percent at around $101.83 a barrel.

The optimism extended beyond energy markets. European stock indexes surged, with France's CAC 40 rising 3 percent and Germany's DAX gaining 2.1 percent. The UK's FTSE 100 climbed 2 percent. Asia-Pacific markets also rallied, with South Korea's Kospi clearing 7,000 for the first time and Samsung Electronics surging 14.8 percent as it reached a $1 trillion market value. On Wall Street, the S&P 500 rose nearly 1.5 percent to approach record levels.

Bond markets showed mixed signals. UK gilt yields declined slightly from their highest levels since 1998, with 30-year gilt yields falling roughly 9 basis points to 5.63 percent. Gold, traditionally viewed as a safe haven asset, rose 3 percent to $4,695 an ounce.

Financial analysts cautioned that while markets have priced in expectations of improved relations, details of any final agreement remain unclear. The economic impact will depend on whether diplomatic progress translates into an actual deal and how quickly it affects oil production and exports. Relief in energy prices could help American consumers facing elevated gasoline costs, though analysts warn that prewar price levels may not return quickly even with a successful agreement.
