Tulsi Gabbard is leaving her post as US director of national intelligence following a tumultuous stint in which she was largely sidelined as Donald Trump launched attacks on Venezuela and Iran.

In a letter to the US president, she said she would resign and leave her post on 30 June. "While we have made significant progress … I recognize there is still important work to be done," she wrote.

The White House forced Gabbard to resign, the Reuters news agency reported, citing a source familiar with the issue. However, a spokesperson for Gabbard's office said rumors that Gabbard had been forcibly ousted from office were "100% false".

In her resignation letter, Gabbard said her husband, Abraham, "has recently been diagnosed with an extremely rare form of bone cancer". She added: "At this time, I must step away from public service to be by his side and fully support him through this battle."

Gabbard was an unconventional choice for the role. The former Democrat lacked an intelligence background and her foreign policy views at times diverged with Trump's, particularly on questions of overseas military intervention. She also took unusual measures to ingratiate herself to the president by vowing to root out politicization across US spy agencies and embracing his agenda of election denial.

Over time, Gabbard became increasingly sidelined by the president, who excluded her from key national security conversations on Iran and Venezuela, according to people familiar with the office of the director of national intelligence.

Trump was asking cabinet members last month whether he should replace Gabbard, according to two people briefed on the discussions.

"Unfortunately, after having done a great job, Tulsi Gabbard will be leaving the Administration on June 30th," he wrote in a statement on his Truth Social platform on Friday. Gabbard "has done an incredible job, and we will miss her", the president said, adding that Aaron Lukas, principal deputy director of national intelligence, would serve as acting director of national intelligence.

Gabbard's tumultuous 15-month tenure as the nation's top intelligence official ended Friday when she submitted her resignation. Over those 15 months, the former Democrat without an intelligence background took actions seemingly meant to flatter Trump.

Her departure marks another shift in Trump's national security team during a period of heightened international tensions, with ongoing US operations against Iran and Venezuela. The acting director, Aaron Lukas, will now lead the intelligence community as these operations continue.

The resignation comes amid broader changes in Trump's cabinet and follows mounting pressure on the administration regarding foreign policy decisions and military operations in the Middle East and Latin America.

Yesterday, 10:32 PM ET

Blue Origin has completed its investigation into the failure of its New Glenn rocket launch, according to a statement from the company. The investigation examined what went wrong during the mission, which marked a setback for the company's orbital launch ambitions.

New Glenn is Blue Origin's heavy-lift orbital rocket, designed to compete with SpaceX's Falcon 9 and other commercial launch vehicles. The rocket represents the company's entry into the orbital launch market after years of suborbital flights with its New Shepard vehicle.

The launch failure occurred during what was intended to be a demonstration mission for the new rocket system. Blue Origin, founded by Amazon's Jeff Bezos, has been developing New Glenn for several years as part of its strategy to capture a share of the commercial satellite launch market.

The company has now concluded its review of the incident. Blue Origin examined flight data, hardware systems, and operational procedures to determine the cause of the failure. The investigation process is standard practice in the aerospace industry following any launch anomaly.

Blue Origin has not yet announced when it plans to attempt another New Glenn launch. The company will need to implement any recommended changes from the investigation before proceeding with future missions. The rocket is designed to be partially reusable, with a first stage that returns to Earth for landing, similar to SpaceX's approach.

The completion of the investigation marks an important step in Blue Origin's effort to establish itself as a reliable provider of orbital launch services. The company has contracts to launch satellites for various customers and is also developing a lunar lander for NASA's Artemis program.

New Glenn is named after John Glenn, the first American to orbit Earth. The rocket stands 98 meters tall and is powered by seven BE-4 engines, which Blue Origin also supplies to United Launch Alliance for use on the Vulcan Centaur rocket.

Throughout his career, Base continued to perform, often appearing at nostalgia tours and 1980s-themed events. He remained a recognizable figure in hip-hop history, representing an era when the genre was establishing itself as a permanent force in American music.

The rapper's influence extended beyond his own recordings. "It Takes Two" has been sampled by numerous artists and remains a frequent choice for commercials, movie soundtracks, and sporting events. The song's immediate recognizability speaks to its lasting impact on popular culture.

Base's death removes another figure from the generation of artists who built hip-hop's foundation in the 1980s. That era saw the genre transition from underground phenomenon to mainstream commercial success, with "It Takes Two" serving as one of the bridges between those worlds.

No cause of death has been announced.
