A few minutes ago

Multiple people died and at least 10 were injured in a chemical incident at a packaging plant in Longview, Washington. Some workers remained unaccounted for following the incident early Tuesday morning.

The hazardous materials incident occurred at Nippon Dynawave Packaging when a tank containing white liquor ruptured. White liquor is an alkaline chemical solution used in paper manufacturing to break down wood chips into pulp. The solution is composed primarily of sodium sulfide, sodium hydroxide, and disisodium carbonate.

The 80,000-gallon tank, which was approximately 60 percent full at the time of rupture, released the caustic chemical. Several people sustained critical injuries, including chemical burns. According to fire officials, nine employees and one firefighter were hospitalized with critical injuries. Fire Chief Scott Goldstein described some injuries as life-threatening.

The Longview fire department responded to the scene along with multiple agencies. Battalion Chief Michael Gorsuch noted that people remained unaccounted for following the incident. Authorities confirmed fatalities related to the incident but withheld identifying information pending notification of family members.

The Nippon Dynawave facility operates as a kraft pulp and paper mill with a liquid packaging division. The plant employs approximately 1,000 workers. The incident occurred during operating hours when staff were present at the facility.

Emergency responders continued recovery operations at the scene. The fire department stated there was "no immediate threat to the public" from chemical fallout affecting neighboring communities. Crews worked with Nippon employees to address containment of the product and damaged containers.

Authorities have not yet released information about the cause of the tank rupture. Investigation into what led to the structural failure remains active. Federal workplace safety officials typically examine fatal industrial accidents to determine whether safety violations played a role in the incident.

The incident drew attention as it followed a similar chemical safety situation in southern California. Orange County authorities had ordered the evacuation of approximately 50,000 people in Garden Grove the previous week over concerns that a damaged chemical tank might explode. Officials later lifted those evacuation orders after determining the immediate explosion threat had been eliminated, though ongoing safety risks required continued mitigation efforts.

Longview, a city of roughly 37,000 people, has historically served as a center for timber and paper production. The mill represents a significant regional employer in the manufacturing sector. Family members of workers gathered near the facility seeking information about their loved ones as the incident unfolded.

The fire department urged people to avoid the area as recovery operations and investigation continued at the Nippon Dynawave facility.

The warning comes as Russia prepares what it describes as the next stage of its ongoing four-year conflict with Ukraine. By alerting the US government to forthcoming military operations, Lavrov appears to be attempting to prevent American casualties while Russia proceeds with attacks on Ukrainian targets.

The communication between the two foreign ministers represents direct contact between Moscow and Washington regarding Russian military intentions. It marks a relatively unusual instance of Russia providing advance notice to the United States about planned operations.

The warning has prompted international concern. The EU summoned Russia's top diplomat in Brussels to respond to the threat. Anitta Hipper, the EU's foreign policy spokesperson, called the warning "an unacceptable escalation" and said the EU delegation to Ukraine would remain in Kyiv. She added that the threat demonstrates Russia's lack of interest in genuine peace negotiations.

The warning follows one of the largest aerial assaults on Kyiv during the war, which occurred overnight on Saturday. Previous Russian strikes on the Ukrainian capital have targeted both military installations and civilian infrastructure, including power facilities and residential areas.

The State Department has not yet publicly confirmed whether it will issue an evacuation order for Americans in Kyiv. Such orders are typically issued when the US government determines that American citizens face imminent danger in a foreign country.

The exact number of American citizens currently in Kyiv remains unclear. Americans in Ukraine have included diplomats, journalists, aid workers, and private citizens. The Kremlin said Monday that the fuel situation in Cuba was critical and that US attempts to "suffocate" the island were causing many difficulties. Russia's foreign minister separately said Moscow stood in solidarity with Cuba and was ready to provide support together with other like-minded partners.

Countries regularly issue evacuation advisories when they anticipate military escalation in conflict zones. Such warnings typically suggest that authorities believe civilian areas may be targeted or that collateral damage to civilian infrastructure is likely.

The ongoing conflict between Russia and Ukraine continues with no resolution in sight. The warning to American nationals underscores the persistent threat of military operations in and around Ukraine's major cities.
