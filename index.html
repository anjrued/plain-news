Yesterday, 9:30 PM ET

A 21-year-old man was fatally shot by federal agents after opening fire near a White House security checkpoint on Saturday evening, according to the Secret Service.

The suspect approached a checkpoint at a gate near 17th Street and Pennsylvania Avenue NW shortly after 6pm and fired a pistol after removing it from a bag. Secret Service officers returned fire, striking the individual. The White House was briefly locked down following the incident.

According to court records, the gunman was already known to the Secret Service. The suspect, identified in media reports as Nasire Best, had previously attempted to enter the White House complex. Court filings from 2025 indicate that Best was known to the agency for "walking around the White House complex inquiring how to gain access at various entry points" on multiple occasions during summer 2025.

Best was involuntarily committed on June 26, 2025 for "obstructing vehicle entry" to part of the White House complex. On July 10, 2025, he was arrested for unlawful entry after ignoring warning signs and walking into a restricted area outside the White House. According to court documents, Best claimed to be Jesus and said he wanted to get arrested. A judge issued a "stay away order" barring him from the White House area, though a bench warrant was later issued after he failed to attend a subsequent hearing.

An unnamed law enforcement official described Best as an emotionally disturbed person known to law enforcement, according to the Washington Post.

One bystander was struck during the exchange of gunfire. It remained unclear whether the bystander was shot by the suspect or by Secret Service officers returning fire. No Secret Service agents were injured in the incident.

President Donald Trump, who was inside the White House during the shooting, said the gunman had "a violent history" and a "possible obsession" with the White House. Trump praised the "swift and professional action" of law enforcement officials on social media.

The shooting represents another security incident near the White House complex. The Secret Service maintains multiple layers of security around the grounds, with checkpoints positioned at strategic locations to screen vehicles and pedestrians before allowing access closer to the building.

The FBI is assisting the Secret Service and local police in investigating the incident. The Secret Service has not yet released additional details about the circumstances leading to the exchange of fire or the suspect's specific motivations.

Yesterday, 9:23 PM ET

Russia launched a massive aerial assault on Kyiv and surrounding regions, deploying approximately 600 drones and 90 missiles in one of the war's heaviest bombardments. The coordinated attack killed at least four people and injured roughly 100 across the country, with Kyiv bearing the brunt of the strikes.

The operation included multiple deployments of Russia's Oreshnik hypersonic missile, marking the third use of this weapon in the conflict. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed the strike on the city of Bila Tserkva in the Kyiv region and described extensive damage throughout the capital. "They are genuinely deranged," Zelenskyy said on Telegram, noting that 36 of the missiles were ballistic weapons. He stated that not all ballistic missiles were intercepted and that Kyiv was the primary target.

Ukrainian air defense systems engaged the incoming weapons, but the scale of the assault overwhelmed some defenses. Kyiv's mayor, Vitali Klitschko, reported two deaths and 56 wounded in the capital, while regional officials documented two additional deaths and nine wounded in surrounding areas. Klitschko noted that damage extended to every district of the city.

The strikes caused widespread destruction. A fire started at a school, a business center collapsed with people trapped in a shelter, and a market burned down completely. Ukraine's National Art Museum sustained damage to ceilings, windows, and interior spaces, though staff confirmed the art collection itself remained unharmed. The Foreign Ministry building, damaged for the first time since World War II, sustained light damage from nearby explosions. Government headquarters also suffered damage, with windows blown out but no injuries reported.

Residents described harrowing experiences. One market worker of 22 years told the Associated Press the attack was unprecedented in the war and that she was leaving Kyiv. A 74-year-old man recounted being thrown backward by shock waves alongside his dog while his apartment was destroyed.

Russia's defense ministry confirmed using Oreshnik, Iskander, Kinzhal, and Zircon missiles, claiming successful strikes on Ukrainian military command facilities and airbases. Moscow characterized the attack as retaliation for Ukrainian strikes on Russian territory, specifically citing a drone attack on a student dormitory in Luhansk that Russia said killed 21 people.

International condemnation followed quickly. French President Emmanuel Macron condemned the strikes as signaling "the dead end of Russia's war of aggression." German Chancellor Friedrich Merz called the Oreshnik deployment a "reckless escalation." EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas described the assault as deliberate strikes on civilian centers meant to kill as many civilians as possible, calling the Oreshnik use "political scare tactic and reckless nuclear brinkmanship."

7:57 AM ET

Google has changed its search box interface for the first time since the company's founding, integrating artificial intelligence directly into the search experience. The redesign introduces what the company calls "agentic Gemini era" functionality.

The change represents a fundamental shift in how Google's search engine operates. Users now encounter AI-powered features as a core part of the search interface rather than as an optional add-on. The company presented the changes at its I/O 2026 developer conference.

The redesign comes as Google faces competitive pressure from AI-focused search alternatives. The company is repositioning its search product before competitors can capture significant market share with AI-native search experiences.

Google's implementation has encountered technical issues. The search engine currently struggles to define basic words including "disregard," "stop," and "ignore." Users have reported that searching for these terms produces problematic results.

A workaround exists for users who prefer traditional search results. Adding a specific modifier to search queries disables AI-generated overviews and returns conventional link-based results. The modifier method allows users to access what some are calling "the old Google."

The company describes the update as its biggest upgrade in 25 years. The previous search interface had remained largely unchanged since Google's launch, aside from cosmetic refinements and feature additions around the core search box.

Industry observers view the change as marking a turning point for the internet. The shift from link-based search results to AI-generated answers changes how information is discovered and consumed online. Critics argue the redesign ends what they characterize as the internet's golden age, when search engines primarily directed users to websites rather than generating answers directly.

Google launched a project called Spark in connection with the redesign. The timing coincided with OpenClaw surpassing 300,000 stars on GitHub, suggesting competitive dynamics in the AI search space.

The company maintains a blog post detailing the changes and explaining the vision behind the agentic AI approach. The redesign affects billions of daily searches across Google's global user base.

Yesterday, 8:52 PM ET

Two-time NASCAR Cup Series champion Kyle Busch has died at age 41, NASCAR announced Thursday. The racing organization confirmed his death but did not initially disclose the cause. His family later revealed that Busch died from severe pneumonia and sepsis.

"We are saddened and heartbroken to share the news of the passing of Kyle Busch, a two-time Cup champion and one of our sport's greatest and fiercest drivers," NASCAR said in a statement on social media.

Busch's family had announced his hospitalization earlier Thursday, saying he would not compete in the scheduled NASCAR race in Charlotte, North Carolina that weekend. The Las Vegas native had been struggling with health issues leading up to his death. During a Cup Series race on May 10 at Watkins Glen, Busch requested medical assistance from the track doctor after experiencing difficulties with a sinus cold that worsened due to the intense G-forces and elevation changes at the upstate New York road course. Despite his condition, Busch finished eighth in that race.

In a joint statement, the Busch family, Richard Childress Racing, and NASCAR described him as a generational talent. "A future Hall of Famer, Kyle was a rare talent, one who comes along once in a generation. He was fierce, he was passionate, he was immensely skilled and he cared deeply about the sport and fans," the statement read.

Busch's accomplishments in racing were historic. He won 232 races across NASCAR's three national series, more than any driver in history. He captured championships in 2015 and 2019 while driving for Joe Gibbs Racing before joining Richard Childress Racing for his fourth season. He had won a truck series race at Dover just six days before his death.

The NASCAR community gathered for an emotional tribute before the Coca-Cola 600 race at Charlotte Motor Speedway. Approximately 95,000 people attended the ceremony, which became a memorial service. Drivers and team owners surrounded Busch's family during the heartfelt tribute.

Daniel Suarez, driving for Spire Motorsports and Kyle Busch Motorsports, won the Coca-Cola 600 and dedicated his victory to Busch. "This one is for Kyle," Suarez said after the race. Suarez's connection to Busch ran deep through Kyle Busch Motorsports, the team Busch had founded in the truck series.

Busch, known by nicknames "Rowdy" and "Wild Thing," became a polarizing figure in racing known for his competitive spirit and occasionally outlandish behavior. He won rookie of the year in 2005 when he entered the Cup Series. Throughout his career spanning more than two decades, Busch mentored younger drivers and fostered the next generation of racing talent.

He is survived by his wife, Samantha, and children Brexton and Lennix.

Yesterday, 11:28 AM ET

Russian filmmaker Andrei Zvyagintsev has emerged from nearly a decade of absence to premiere his new film at the Cannes Film Festival and make a direct appeal to Vladimir Putin to stop the war in Ukraine.

Zvyagintsev's nine-year absence from filmmaking followed a severe bout with Covid-19 that left him hospitalized across multiple countries. The infection caused 90% lung damage and temporary paralysis, forcing him to relearn basic movements like walking and holding a spoon. He was still recovering in a German clinic when Russia invaded Ukraine in February 2022. "It was a shock; I felt immense pain and deep despair," he said in a recent interview.

His new film, "Minotaur," adapts French director Claude Chabrol's 1969 thriller "The Unfaithful Wife" and relocates the story to a provincial Russian town. The narrative follows a business executive confronting both his wife's infidelity and the prospect of laying off employees. The film is competing for the Palme d'Or against heavyweight directors including Pedro Almodóvar, László Nemes, and Asghar Farhadi.

Zvyagintsev's return to cinema represents a significant moment for Russian cinema. Julian Graffy, a professor of Russian literature and film at University College London, noted that Zvyagintsev was the most important director of the wave of filmmakers that emerged in the early 21st century. "Many artistic careers have been broken by the political turn that Russia has taken," Graffy said, "but the loss of his voice has been felt most keenly of them all."

The director has long used film to explore themes of corruption and moral decay in Russian society. His 2014 film "Leviathan" depicts a corrupt mayor and complicit government officials crushing an ordinary citizen's attempts at justice. Though Zvyagintsev has sometimes claimed his films drew inspiration from non-Russian stories, viewers widely understood them as damning commentaries on the Putin era.

Russian authorities initially supported his work through funding, but later grew critical. After "Leviathan" received 35% of its budget from the Russian ministry of culture, then-minister Vladimir Medinsky said he disliked the film and criticized the director's approach.

Zvyagintsev's decision to speak publicly against the war at an international festival carries significant risk. Russia has criminalized public criticism of what the Kremlin calls its "special military operation" in Ukraine. By choosing Cannes as his platform and speaking directly to Putin, Zvyagintsev has positioned himself as one of Russia's most prominent artistic voices opposing the conflict.

The Cannes Film Festival has become a venue for addressing the war since the 2022 invasion, hosting Ukrainian filmmakers and others affected by the conflict. Zvyagintsev's appearance adds weight to international criticism of the war from within Russia's cultural community.

May 21, 10:12 AM ET

Governor Jared Polis commuted the prison sentence of Tina Peters, a former Colorado county clerk convicted of allowing unauthorized access to voting equipment, drawing sharp criticism from within his own party and state election officials.

Peters, who was serving nearly nine years in prison, will be released on parole on June 1 after Polis reduced her sentence from eight and a half years to approximately four and a half years. In his clemency letter, Polis wrote that Peters received "an extremely unusual and lengthy sentence for a first time offender who committed non-violent crimes."

Peters, the former clerk in Mesa County in western Colorado, was convicted in 2024 of four felonies and three misdemeanors. In 2021, she allowed Conan Hayes, a former pro surfer affiliated with MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell, to access the county's voting equipment, copy it, and attend a software upgrade for the voting systems. Sensitive passwords and other information from the Dominion voting equipment were later published online by right-wing figures.

The decision immediately prompted a formal censure from the Colorado Democratic Party, reflecting significant internal party divisions. Colorado Secretary of State Jena Griswold, a Democrat, said the clemency "validates Trump's basest impulses and emboldens this lawless president." She added that the decision would "embolden the election denial movement across the country and will leave a dark, dangerous imprint on American democracy for years to come."

Griswold disputed Polis's comparison of Peters's case to that of former state senator Sonya Jaquez Lewis, who was convicted of submitting forged letters to the legislature and sentenced to probation and community service. Polis had cited sentencing disparities between the two cases as context for his clemency decision. However, Griswold stated that beyond one overlapping felony count, "it is not accurate to suggest that Peters's and Sonya Jaquez Lewis's actions or impacts are the same."

Matt Crane, executive director of the Colorado Clerks Association, said the group was "furious, disgusted, and deeply disappointed by the governor's decision." He warned that election officials across the country were interpreting the clemency as a signal that "it's open season on our elections and election officials."

At trial, Judge Matthew Barrett told Peters: "You are no hero. You're a charlatan who used, and is still using, your prior position in office to peddle a snake oil that's been proven to be junk time and time again."

Peters's case has become central to Republican claims about election integrity. Donald Trump repeatedly called for her release, issuing a federal pardon and instructing the Justice Department to seek her release, though the pardon carried little weight since Peters faced state charges.

The Democratic Party censure represents a formal rebuke but carries no legal consequences for the governor's authority or position.
