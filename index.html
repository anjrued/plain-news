9:38 PM ET

Authorities in Orange County, California have ordered the evacuation of 40,000 people after a storage tank containing a highly flammable chemical began leaking and threatening to explode.

The crisis started Thursday at a GKN Aerospace facility in Garden Grove, where a tank holding methyl methacrylate began off-gassing and showing signs of failure. The chemical is used to make resins and plastics and is extremely flammable.

Emergency teams initially responded with a hazmat unit on Thursday and ordered local evacuations. Officials lifted that order later the same day, but the situation got worse overnight. According to Garden Grove authorities, damage to a valve on the tank "created additional operational challenges, preventing complete mitigation." By Friday, new evacuation orders had expanded to cover residents in six cities.

"We have a tank that is actively in crisis," Orange County Fire Authority Division Chief Craig Covey told reporters at a news conference. He explained there are only two possible outcomes. "One: the tank fails and spills a total of about 6,000 to 7,000 gallons of very bad chemicals into the parking lot in that area. Or two: the tank goes into a thermal runaway and blows up, affecting the tanks that are around them that have fuel or the chemicals in them as well."

Covey urged residents to take the evacuation seriously. "We are setting up these evacuations in preparation for these two options: it fails or it blows up. Please follow our requests and orders for evacuations."

However, Garden Grove Police Chief Amir El-Farra said about 15 percent of those facing evacuation orders were refusing to leave, according to the Orange County Register. The city has opened at least two shelters for evacuees.

Emergency responders have laid out sand barriers to contain the chemicals if the tank cracks rather than explodes. "In a weird world, that's the best-case scenario, believe it or not," Covey said, according to the New York Times.

The evacuation of 40,000 people represents one of the largest emergency evacuations in recent California history. While the state regularly sees mass evacuations due to wildfires, industrial chemical incidents requiring evacuations of this scale are far less common.

GKN Aerospace is a division of a British corporation that manufactures airplane engines and other aircraft parts. Orange County officials are coordinating with state emergency management agencies to handle the response. The timeline for when residents might be able to return home remains unclear as authorities focus on stabilizing the tank and preventing a potential explosion.

Australia Faces Largest Diphtheria Outbreak in Decades as Cases Spread Across Northern States

Australia is grappling with its largest diphtheria outbreak in more than 20 years, with Health Minister Mark Butler warning it is "the biggest diphtheria outbreak we've seen, certainly for decades."

The outbreak began in late 2023 in the Northern Territory and was officially declared in March. It has primarily affected Indigenous communities and is now spreading beyond the Territory's borders into neighboring states.

The latest data from the National Notifiable Disease Surveillance System shows 133 cases in the Northern Territory, 79 in Western Australia, six in South Australia, and up to five in Queensland. The cases include both cutaneous (skin) and respiratory forms of the disease.

"There's no question this is serious," Butler told ABC Radio National on Tuesday. "It's serious in the NT. It's spreading across other parts of the Top End. It's crept below the South Australian border into the APY Lands."

Diphtheria was once a leading cause of childhood death globally but became rare in Australia after widespread vaccination beginning in the 1930s. Before the COVID-19 pandemic, most Australian cases were imported from overseas.

Dr John Boffa, a public health medical officer at the Central Australian Aboriginal Health Congress in Alice Springs, said approximately one third of cases are respiratory diphtheria, the more dangerous form of the disease. "We're seeing about 20 new diphtheria cases a week," he said. "That hasn't slowed down yet, and of the new cases, about a third are being hospitalized."

The outbreak has caused one suspected death, which would mark the first diphtheria death in Australia since 2018. "It's 95% certain that this unfortunate person died because of diphtheria," Boffa said, explaining the adult patient had both skin and respiratory infections and developed heart inflammation, a known complication.

Respiratory diphtheria is significantly more deadly, killing up to one in 10 infected people even with treatment. It spreads through respiratory droplets and causes sore throat, fever, grey covering over the tonsils, and enlarged lymph nodes.

Health officials attribute the outbreak to falling vaccination rates since the pandemic, combined with people missing booster shots as adults. Routine childhood immunization coverage fell to its lowest level in five years in 2023, dropping to about 90% for two-year-olds, well below the 95% target needed for herd immunity.

Butler said authorities are working with the Northern Territory government and Aboriginal-controlled health sector to increase vaccine access. Pop-up clinics have started this week, though Boffa noted ongoing workforce shortages of nurses and GPs are limiting vaccination capacity.

Dr Milena Dalton from the Burnet Institute said the outbreak "highlights how quickly vaccine-preventable diseases can re-emerge when there are immunity gaps."

2:16 PM ET

Kevin Warsh has been confirmed as chair of the Federal Reserve following a contentious Senate vote that split largely along party lines. The 54-45 vote on Wednesday was the most divisive confirmation for the position in history.

All Republicans and one Democrat, Senator John Fetterman of Pennsylvania, voted to confirm Warsh for a four-year term as chair and a 14-year appointment on the Fed's rate-setting board. He will officially take over on May 14, when current chair Jerome Powell's term ends.

The confirmation comes as the Federal Reserve faces mounting pressure from the Trump administration to lower interest rates, even as inflation rises to 3.8%. The Fed sets interest rates, which determine the cost of borrowing money across the economy. Higher rates typically cool spending and prices but risk increasing unemployment. Lower rates can boost the economy but may also drive prices higher.

A White House spokesperson, Kush Desai, called the confirmation "a welcome step towards finally restoring accountability, competence and confidence in Fed decision-making."

Senate Majority Leader John Thune criticized Democrats who opposed the nomination. "I wish I could say I'm surprised, but this is just how bad Trump derangement syndrome has gotten on the other side of the aisle," he said from the Senate floor.

Senator Elizabeth Warren, the top Democrat on the Senate banking committee, sharply disagreed. She said Trump nominated Warsh to be his "sock puppet" to help control interest rates. "Warsh's confirmation equals another step in Trump's attempt to take over the Fed," Warren wrote on X. "That's not good for working families. It's good for Wall Street."

Fetterman defended his vote, saying he believes Warsh will be "transparent and responsive to Congress and the public." He added that Warsh's "promise to maintain Fed independence in setting interest rates is crucial."

Warsh, an Ivy League economist and former Wall Street banker, previously served as a Fed governor from 2006 to 2011. During that time, he was known as an "inflation hawk," advocating for higher interest rates to combat inflation. He left the Fed board partly due to disagreements over the central bank's post-financial crisis stimulus package.

At a Senate banking committee hearing last month, Warsh pledged to "take politics out of monetary policy and monetary policy out of politics." However, he refused to answer whether Trump lost the 2020 election, raising concerns among Democrats about his independence.

The previous record for a divisive Fed chair confirmation was in 2010, when Ben Bernanke was confirmed by a 70-30 vote.

Powell announced he will remain on the Fed board as a voting governor, citing White House scrutiny of headquarters renovations that went over budget, which he called "pretext" for pressuring the central bank.

JPL has been managed by the California Institute of Technology since its establishment. The potential shift represents one of the most significant organizational changes in NASA's recent history. The laboratory has served as a center for robotic space exploration, managing missions including Mars rovers and deep space probes.

The realignment comes as NASA works to streamline operations and improve the pace of mission development. The agency has not specified what entity would assume management responsibilities if the change proceeds, or what timeline such a transition would follow.

JPL currently plays a central role in NASA's planetary science program and has been responsible for some of the agency's most high-profile achievements in solar system exploration. The laboratory employs thousands of scientists and engineers in Pasadena, California.

The announcement marks a departure from decades of continuity in how NASA operates one of its flagship research centers. Caltech has overseen JPL since the laboratory's early days, providing academic partnership and administrative structure for NASA's West Coast operations.

NASA officials indicated the realignment is part of a broader effort to optimize the agency's organizational structure. The changes would affect how the space agency coordinates its various centers and manages relationships with external partners.

The potential management change raises questions about how JPL's research priorities and operational culture might evolve under different leadership. The laboratory has maintained a distinct identity within NASA's network of centers, partly due to its unique management arrangement with Caltech.

No specific date has been set for implementing the realignment. NASA has not detailed what factors led to consideration of changing JPL's management structure at this time.

He became unresponsive Wednesday while testing in a Chevrolet racing simulator in Concord, North Carolina, and was transported to a hospital in Charlotte. No official cause of death has been announced.

Busch was the winningest driver across NASCAR's three series in history. He won Cup Series championships in 2015 and 2019 with Joe Gibbs Racing and was in his fourth season at Richard Childress Racing. He ranked 24th in the Cup Series standings this season with two top-10 finishes in 12 races. Just last week, he won a NASCAR Truck Series race at Dover International Speedway and was scheduled to compete in this weekend's Coca-Cola 600 in Charlotte.

The Las Vegas native, known by the nicknames "Rowdy" and "Wild Thing," won rookie of the year in 2005. His brother Kurt is also a successful NASCAR driver.

Tributes poured in from across the racing world. Denny Hamlin, Busch's former teammate and a 22-year NASCAR veteran, wrote on social media: "Absolutely cannot comprehend this news. We just need to think of his family during this time. We love you KB."

Driver Ricky Stenhouse Jr said: "I've raced against Kyle for a long time, and anyone who's lined up next to him knows exactly what made him special. He gave you everything he had, every single lap, and he made all of us better for it. But more than the wins and the records, I keep thinking about Samantha, Brexton, and Lennix, and the entire Busch family right now."

Dale Earnhardt Jr, who had a complicated relationship with Busch before the two became friends, called him "one of the greatest drivers in NASCAR history." He said: "Kyle and I had a really challenging existence for many years. But we luckily took the time to figure out our differences and that was something he instigated."

Joey Logano, who famously brawled with Busch in a 2017 post-race incident that left Busch with a bloodied face, said: "The bottom line is he is one of the best drivers to ever sit in a race car."

The NHL's Carolina Hurricanes held a moment of silence before Thursday night's game. Fans honored Busch by donating to the Samantha and Kyle Busch Bundle of Joy Fund, a foundation the couple created in 2015 to support couples seeking fertility treatments.
