Yesterday, 11:10 PM ET

The US military conducted strikes against Iranian targets including missile launch sites, describing the action as self-defense. The strikes targeted locations along Iran's Gulf coast near the Strait of Hormuz while diplomatic delegations gathered in Qatar to negotiate an end to the conflict.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio stated that a potential peace deal could take days to finalize. He emphasized that the Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway for global oil shipments, will reopen either through diplomatic agreement or other means, signaling pressure on Iran to end its disruption of this strategic chokepoint.

The simultaneous military action and active negotiations reflect the dual approach US officials are taking. American forces targeted sites they said posed threats to US personnel and interests in the region, with strikes focusing on Iranian military infrastructure along the country's southern coastline.

According to diplomatic sources, negotiators in Qatar are working through several major points of disagreement. The proposed deal includes provisions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz, which Iran has disrupted during the conflict. Other issues under discussion include sanctions relief, security guarantees, and withdrawal of military forces from the region.

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei acknowledged progress in talks, stating that negotiators "have reached a conclusion on a large portion of the issues under discussion." However, he cautioned against expectations of an imminent agreement, saying "no one can make such a claim" about signing an accord.

Rubio's comments suggested momentum in negotiations despite continued military operations. He emphasized that the waterway, which serves as a chokepoint for approximately one-fifth of the world's petroleum supply, would be cleared of Iranian interference through diplomatic means or otherwise.

The latest strikes represent continued military pressure even as both sides engage diplomatically. US officials characterized the operations as necessary defensive measures rather than an expansion of hostilities. Iranian officials have not yet publicly responded to the strikes.

The negotiations come after weeks of military confrontation between the countries. President Trump has pushed for a peace agreement while maintaining military pressure on Iran, with the talks in Qatar representing significant diplomatic engagement between the two nations.

Separately, Western officials indicated that the UK has not ruled out joining future strikes against Iranian ballistic missile launch sites. They expressed concern that Iran could deplete allied air defense interceptors if its missile capabilities remain intact. US heavy bombers are expected to arrive at UK bases in the coming days to target Iranian underground missile facilities.

Yesterday, 11:24 PM ET

Firefighters in Orange County, California have eliminated the threat of an explosion at a chemical storage facility in Garden Grove after an overnight operation, officials announced. The development allows thousands of evacuated residents to return to their homes following days of heightened danger.

The incident centered on a storage tank containing methyl methacrylate, a highly flammable chemical used in resin and plastic manufacturing. The tank, located at a GKN Aerospace facility, began leaking after damage to a valve created operational challenges that prevented complete mitigation of the problem.

The crisis escalated dramatically when authorities expanded evacuation orders to cover approximately 40,000 people across six Orange County cities: Garden Grove, Cypress, Stanton, Anaheim, Buena Park and Westminster. Orange County fire authority division chief Craig Covey described the severity of the situation at a news conference, explaining that officials faced two possible outcomes: the tank could fail and spill thousands of gallons of toxic chemicals, or it could experience a thermal runaway and explode, potentially triggering failures in nearby tanks containing fuel and other hazardous materials.

"This thing is going to fail, and we don't know when," Covey said. "We're doing our best to figure out when or how we can prevent it."

County health officer Dr Regina Chinsio-Kwong warned that if the chemical heated up, it could release harmful vapors causing respiratory issues, eye irritation, nausea and headaches. Emergency responders prepared for potential spillage by creating containment barriers with sandbags to prevent the toxic chemical from reaching storm drains, creeks or the nearby ocean.

The tank cracked over the weekend, which authorities believed would relieve pressure and reduce explosion risk. Crews continued monitoring and cooling operations overnight to stabilize the situation.

During the emergency, some residents initially resisted evacuation orders. Garden Grove police chief Amir El-Farra reported that approximately 15 percent of those facing evacuation orders were refusing to leave. The city established at least two shelters to accommodate evacuees during the crisis.

The facility's location raised particular concerns, as Garden Grove sits approximately 30 miles southeast of downtown Los Angeles and less than a mile from Disneyland's theme parks, which were not placed under evacuation orders.

Fire officials urged residents to follow evacuation orders seriously, emphasizing that precautions were necessary given the potential for catastrophic failure or explosion. With the immediate danger now averted, authorities continue assessing the site and conducting remediation work. Officials have not yet released details about what preventive measures will be implemented to avoid similar incidents in the future.

Yesterday, 7:57 AM ET

Google has fundamentally redesigned its search box for the first time in nearly 30 years, integrating artificial intelligence directly into the search interface. The changes represent a major shift in how billions of people access information online.

The company announced the overhaul at its I/O conference, introducing what executives call the "agentic Gemini era." Elizabeth Reid, who oversees Google Search, described the changes as "the biggest Google Search had seen in its nearly 30-year history."

The new design accommodates longer and more specific queries than the traditional search box allowed. Rather than requiring users to type search-specific syntax, the interface now accepts questions phrased naturally, as people would ask one another. The results page has also changed substantially, moving away from lists of links toward visually augmented summaries powered by AI.

The updated search experience is built on Gemini 3.5, Google's latest artificial intelligence model. When users submit queries involving photos, videos, or documents through Chrome's search bar, the system automatically switches into AI mode. Results now feature visual elements and interactive options that allow users to complete tasks directly within Google's ecosystem, such as creating calendar invites or building spreadsheets.

Google has made its AI Mode accessible to all users. Since the feature debuted a year ago, queries to the chatbot-specific interface have doubled every quarter. Users can still access traditional link-based search results by clicking a "Web" tab.

The company is rolling out these features globally to both desktop and mobile versions of Google Search. The transformation marks a departure from the directory-style search engine that has defined Google since its founding, instead positioning the company as a system that processes and presents information directly to users.

For paid subscribers to AI Pro and Ultra plans, Google is introducing additional features. Users will be able to create "information agents," autonomous AI bots capable of performing in-depth research and returning summaries or action plans. A "generative user interface" feature can create customized visuals and interactive dashboards based on user requests.

Google Spark, a new agent feature, can access information from Gmail, Google Calendar, and other Google products to research topics, shop online, and plan trips and appointments.

The company also announced new intelligent eyewear developed with Samsung, Warby Parker, and Gentle Monster. These voice-activated glasses with embedded cameras will launch in the fall and respond to commands sent to Gemini.

According to Google, 900 million people use the Gemini app monthly. The expansion of AI agents from specialized business applications to mainstream search represents a significant evolution in consumer-facing artificial intelligence technology.

The Knicks have been in exceptional form throughout their playoff run. They swept the Philadelphia 76ers in the second round and defeated the Atlanta Hawks 4-1 in the first round, extending their current winning streak to 11 games, the third longest in a single postseason in NBA history.

Jalen Brunson was the standout performer in the series, averaging 25.5 points and 7.8 assists across the four games and earning the Larry Bird Trophy as Eastern Conference finals MVP. After the Game 4 victory, Brunson acknowledged the contributions of his teammates and coaching staff. "It means a lot to get to the finals, but I wouldn't be here without my teammates, my coach, this staff, the fanbase," he said on the ESPN broadcast.

Karl-Anthony Towns contributed 19 points and 14 rebounds in the sweep-clinching game. The Knicks went on a devastating 20-0 run late in the first quarter and early in the second, essentially putting the game out of reach.

For Cleveland, the sweep marked a disappointing end to the season. Donovan Mitchell scored 31 points in the final game but could not lead the Cavaliers to a serious challenge. Coach Kenny Atkinson acknowledged the Knicks' superior play. "They're playing better basketball. You got to give them credit. They're on a heater," Atkinson said.

The Knicks have not won an NBA championship since 1973, and they will face a formidable opponent in the Finals. Their Western Conference opponent will be determined by the outcome of the Spurs-Thunder series, which is currently tied at 2-2. If the Oklahoma City Thunder advance, the Knicks will face the reigning champions. If San Antonio wins, they will contend with the Spurs and their 7-foot-4-inch superstar Victor Wembanyama.

The Finals matchup has generated significant excitement among Knicks fans. High-profile supporters including filmmaker Spike Lee and actor Timothée Chalamet traveled to Cleveland for Game 4. Guard Landry Shamet marveled at the fanbase's dedication. "Knicks fans are a specific species of human that should be studied, they're crazy. They fly out to Cleveland on a Monday, they're everywhere."

The Knicks' first home game of the NBA Finals is scheduled for June 8, with courtside seats already priced at $105,000 on resale sites.

Yesterday, 9:41 PM ET

Sonny Rollins, one of the greatest jazz saxophonists of all time, has died at 95. His death was announced Monday on his website and confirmed by his publicist Terri Hinte. He died at his home in Woodstock, New York, on Monday afternoon. No cause of death was provided.

Rollins emerged as a towering figure in jazz during the bebop era and remained one of the last surviving giants from that transformative period. Over more than seven decades, he released more than 60 albums and collaborated with many of jazz's greatest musicians, including Miles Davis, Thelonious Monk, and John Coltrane. His influence extended across generations of musicians and genres.

Born Walter Theodore Rollins in Harlem in 1930, he earned the nickname Sonny from his grandmother. He began learning saxophone at seven years old, inspired by his musically talented siblings and jazz legends like Louis Jordan and Fats Waller. Early in his career, he played alongside future stars Jackie McLean and Kenny Drew, quickly establishing himself as an innovative voice in jazz.

Rollins was known for his genius with melody and his ability to transform standard tunes into complex, spontaneous solos. Saxophonist Branford Marsalis called him "the greatest improviser in the history of jazz" alongside Louis Armstrong. When presenting Rollins with the National Medal of the Arts in 2011, President Barack Obama said the musician had inspired him to "take risks that I might not otherwise have taken."

His career was marked by both remarkable creativity and personal struggle. He battled heroin addiction and served time on Rikers Island, but kicked his habit in 1955 through rehabilitation. This turning point sparked an extraordinary creative period. His celebrated albums from the 1950s and 1960s included "Saxophone Colossus" (1956), featuring his signature tune "St Thomas," and "Freedom Suite" (1958), a 20-minute composition that became an elegant argument for freedom during the civil rights movement.

Rollins took extended breaks from performing to refine his craft. Most famously, between 1959 and 1962, he practiced for up to 15 hours daily on the Williamsburg Bridge pedestrian walkway to avoid disturbing neighbors. This sabbatical inspired his 1962 comeback album "The Bridge."

Beyond music, Rollins was an environmental activist who campaigned around climate change through benefit concerts and his 1998 album "Global Warming." He continued performing well into his eighties, maintaining the physical stamina required for his demanding style of play.

Rollins married Lucille Pearson in 1965, and they remained together until her death in 2004. His extensive recorded legacy and revolutionary approach to saxophone improvisation established the instrument as central to jazz expression and continue to inspire musicians across genres.

Trump pauses deportation flights to Congo over Ebola outbreak

According to reporting by Politico, the pause aims to limit exposure risks to Immigration and Customs Enforcement officials involved in the transfer process. The decision represents a public health-driven interruption...

May 21, 5:06 PM ET

The Trump administration has temporarily suspended deportation flights to the Democratic Republic of Congo following an escalating Ebola outbreak in the country. According to reporting by Politico, the pause aims to limit exposure risks to Immigration and Customs Enforcement officials involved in the transfer process.

The decision represents a public health-driven interruption to the administration's aggressive deportation operations. ICE officers conducting deportation flights face direct contact exposure when transferring individuals to Congolese authorities in areas affected by the outbreak.

Ebola spreads through direct contact with bodily fluids from infected individuals. The virus does not transmit through casual contact or airborne transmission, though past outbreaks in Central Africa have resulted in high mortality rates.

However, the pause has created complications for some detainees. According to the Guardian, at least one woman was moved to Kinshasa, the capital of the DRC, before the travel restrictions took effect. Officials now refuse to return her despite a judge's order for her return, citing the Ebola travel ban. This situation highlights how the pause has left some individuals in legal limbo.

The suspension affects only flights to Congo and does not extend to other deportation destinations. The administration has not disclosed how long the pause will remain in effect or what conditions would trigger its resumption.

Public health experts have noted that the move, while cautious, does not actually prevent disease spread as effectively as it might suggest. Kate White, a nurse cited by the BBC, expressed extreme concern about the inability to get resources to the DRC during the outbreak, emphasizing the broader public health challenges facing the region.

The Congo decision occurs as the country's national soccer team continues planning to travel to the United States for pre-World Cup training despite the outbreak. The team canceled its training sessions in Kinshasa due to health restrictions but has not altered its plans for the American leg of its preparation.

The temporary halt comes amid broader scrutiny of the Trump administration's deportation operations. A group of 40 House Democrats recently demanded transparency from the Federal Aviation Administration regarding ICE charter flights, citing credible reports that individuals have been placed on flights without notice to counsel or family members. Lawmakers raised concerns about due process violations and the administration's efforts to shield these flights from public tracking systems.

The administration has previously described allegations of mistreatment on ICE flights and hidden operations as "categorically false," arguing its detention centers maintain standards comparable to or exceeding those in most U.S. prisons.

The administration has not disclosed whether it consulted with the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention before implementing the pause or provided details on how many deportations to Congo were affected by the suspension.

Rollins emerged as a towering figure in jazz during the bebop era and remained one of the last surviving giants from that transformative period. Over more than seven decades, he released more than 60 albums and collaborated with many of jazz's greatest musicians, including Miles Davis, Thelonious Monk, and John Coltrane. His influence extended across generations of musicians and genres.

Born Walter Theodore Rollins in Harlem in 1930, he earned the nickname Sonny from his grandmother. He began learning saxophone at seven years old, inspired by his musically talented siblings and jazz legends like Louis Jordan and Fats Waller. Early in his career, he played alongside future stars Jackie McLean and Kenny Drew, quickly establishing himself as an innovative voice in jazz.

Rollins was known for his genius with melody and his ability to transform standard tunes into complex, spontaneous solos. Saxophonist Branford Marsalis called him "the greatest improviser in the history of jazz" alongside Louis Armstrong. When presenting Rollins with the National Medal of the Arts in 2011, President Barack Obama said the musician had inspired him to "take risks that I might not otherwise have taken."

His career was marked by both remarkable creativity and personal struggle. He battled heroin addiction and served time on Rikers Island, but kicked his habit in 1955 through rehabilitation. This turning point sparked an extraordinary creative period. His celebrated albums from the 1950s and 1960s included "Saxophone Colossus" (1956), featuring his signature tune "St Thomas," and "Freedom Suite" (1958), a 20-minute composition that became an elegant argument for freedom during the civil rights movement.

Rollins took extended breaks from performing to refine his craft. Most famously, between 1959 and 1962, he practiced for up to 15 hours daily on the Williamsburg Bridge pedestrian walkway to avoid disturbing neighbors. This sabbatical inspired his 1962 comeback album "The Bridge."

Beyond music, Rollins was an environmental activist who campaigned around climate change through benefit concerts and his 1998 album "Global Warming." He continued performing well into his eighties, maintaining the physical stamina required for his demanding style of play.

Rollins married Lucille Pearson in 1965, and they remained together until her death in 2004. His extensive recorded legacy and revolutionary approach to saxophone improvisation established the instrument as central to jazz expression and continue to inspire musicians across genres.
